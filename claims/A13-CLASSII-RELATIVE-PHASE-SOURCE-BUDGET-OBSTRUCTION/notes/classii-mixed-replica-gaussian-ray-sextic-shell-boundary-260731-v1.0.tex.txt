\section*{1. Purpose and scope}

R-131 left two apparently separate problems.  First, diagonal sample Grams
do not determine the mixed conditional response.  Second, a fixed heat law
does not by itself give a state-uniform $\Xi$ gap along large singlet rays,
although the once-owned terminal sextic grows strongly there.  R-132 resolves
the exact algebraic relation between these issues and identifies one
production-relevant positive model.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The complete square-of-conditional-mean term and trace term admit an
exact two-future-replica polarization, provided the heat variable is kept
common and same-root visits are recombined first.
\item The mixed six-row contraction has an exact Pauli--Fierz formula.  Every
floor denominator is localized to the two rational scalars $\tau,\tau'$, so
the polynomial and rational shell problems can be distinguished without
splitting the owner.
\item The diagonal heat Gram plus the once-owned sextic is globally coercive.
For the exact 64-atom product-Rademacher comparison law, the raw diagonal form
has the explicit constant $9/10240$.  A half-normalized comparison still has
the exact constant $1/2250$.
\item That diagonal theorem cannot be promoted to the physical mixed response:
at the origin the same symmetric law has $\E\Xi=0$ and zero sextic Hessian.
This is an exact square-before-average boundary, not a numerical shortfall.
\item An exact two-point rational-ray law makes the mixed current--trace
Hessian diverge like $-10c_1/(81\delta)$.  Hence no law-free floor-uniform
theorem is possible from the six-row algebra alone.
\item In contrast, a standard-Gaussian rational-ray packet has a

\section*{3. Exact mixed-replica response representation}

For owner $k$, cluster $\cC$, and spatial direction $i$, put
\[
 X_f=P_{\cC}C_6(W_{f,r})\partial_iW_{f,r},\qquad
 Y_{f,g}=P_{\cC}C_6(W_{f,r})\partial_iG'_{k,g},       \qquad\text{(3.1)}
\]
where $f,f'$ are conditionally independent copies of the complete future,
and $G'_k$ is the independent Gaussian trace probe with the prescribed
covariance.  For fixed-law physical variations $z,w$, define
\[
\begin{split}
X_{f;z}&=P_{\cC}\{DC_6(W_f)[z_f]\partial_iW_f
                 +C_6(W_f)\partial_i z_f\},\\
X_{f;zw}&=P_{\cC}\{D^2C_6(W_f)[z_f,w_f]\partial_iW_f
 +DC_6(W_f)[z_f]\partial_iw_f
 +DC_6(W_f)[w_f]\partial_i z_f\},                  \qquad\text{(3.2)}
\end{split}
\]
and define $Y_{f,g;z},Y_{f,g;zw}$ by the same coefficient derivatives with
the trace probe held fixed.

\paragraph{Theorem 3.1 (paired-replica polarization).}
Under the firewall above,
\[
\boxed{\begin{split}
q_{\rm comp}(z,w)=\sum_{k,\cC,i}\E_r\E\big[&
 \E_{f,f'}\Rea\{\ip{X_{f;z}}{X_{f';w}}
                    +\ip{X_f}{X_{f';zw}}\}\\
&-\E_{f,g}\Rea\{\ip{Y_{f,g;z}}{Y_{f,g;w}}
 +\ip{Y_{f,g}}{Y_{f,g;zw}}\}\big].          \qquad\text{(3.3)}
\end{split}}\]

Indeed,
\[
 \|\E_fX_f\|^2=\E_{f,f'}\Rea\ip{X_f}{X_{f'}},       \qquad\text{(3.4)}
\]
and differentiating one half of (3.4) produces four product-rule terms.
Replica exchange pairs them into the two terms displayed in (3.3).  The trace
probe has the identical calculation.  Thus (3.3) is an exact representation
of the R-131 physical form.  It is not a lower bound: the mixed contractions
may have either sign and require the full joint law.

\section*{4. Mixed Pauli--Fierz algebra}

For the doublet Pauli matrices $\sigma_A$, put
\[
 m_A(u)=u^\dagger\sigma_Au,\qquad
 j_A(u,v)=2\Rea(u^\dagger\sigma_Av),                 \qquad\text{(4.1)}
\]
\[
 \tau={2\Rea(u^\dagger v+\bar\chi w)
             \over |u|^2+|\chi|^2+e},\qquad
 \ell_A=j_A-\alpha\tau m_A,\quad \alpha={5\over9}. \qquad\text{(4.2)}
\]
For two replicas define $\mathrm{JJ},\mathrm{JM},\mathrm{MJ},\mathrm{MM}$
by summing the corresponding Pauli products.  Completeness gives
\[
\boxed{
\ip{C_6(u)v}{C_6(u')v'}
=c_0\mathrm{JJ}+c_1\{\mathrm{JJ}-\alpha\tau'\mathrm{JM}
-\alpha\tau\mathrm{MJ}+\alpha^2\tau\tau'\mathrm{MM}\}.}       \qquad\text{(4.3)}
\]
The invariant contractions are
\[
\begin{split}
\mathrm{MM}&=2|\ip{u}{u'}|^2-|u|^2|u'|^2,\\
\mathrm{JM}&=4\Rea\{\ip{v}{u'}\ip{u'}{u}\}
             -2|u'|^2\Rea\ip{u}{v},\\
\mathrm{MJ}&=\mathrm{JM}\text{ with primed and unprimed variables exchanged},\\
\mathrm{JJ}&=2\Rea\{2\ip{u}{v'}\ip{u'}{v}
 -\ip{u}{v}\ip{u'}{v'}+2\ip{u}{u'}\ip{v'}{v}
 -\ip{u}{v}\ip{v'}{u'}\}.
\end{split}                                                     \qquad\text{(4.4)}
\]
On the diagonal these reduce to the R-130 radial-plus-wedge norm.  Formula
(4.3) is the cancellation obtained only after all Pauli rows are summed.
Every floor denominator is confined to $\tau,\tau'$, but it has not
disappeared.

If the four raw polynomial inputs are $Q_r$-supported, the audited raw
$\mathrm{JJ},\mathrm{JM},\mathrm{MJ},\mathrm{MM}$ products are supported in
$Q_{r+2}$, so the corresponding raw projection vanishes for $m\ge r+4$.
This is a localization candidate.  Common heat, replica conditioning, trace,
forest, and all visits still have to be intertwined before it becomes a
production stopped-response theorem.

\section*{5. Diagonal heat--sextic comparison}

Write $z=(u,\chi)$, $V=(v,w)$, $\rho=|u|^2+|\chi|^2$, and
\[
{1\over4}\|\Xi(z,V)\|^2
=c_0a^2+c_1\{(1-\lambda)a-\lambda s\}^2
+(c_0+c_1)|h|^2,                                   \qquad\text{(5.1)}
\]
where $a=\Rea(u^\dagger v)$, $s=\Rea(\bar\chi w)$,
$h=u_1v_2-u_2v_1$, and
$\lambda=\alpha|u|^2/(\rho+e)$.  The once-owned terminal sextic is
$F_6=3\rho^3/20$, with exact Hessian
\[
 Q_6(z)[V,V]
 ={9\over10}\rho^2\|V\|^2
 +{18\over5}\rho\{\Rea\ip{z}{V}\}^2.             \qquad\text{(5.2)}
\]

For a full-support heat law $\mu$ with finite second moment, define the
diagonal comparison form
\[
 G_\mu(z)[V,V]=\int\|\Xi(z+A,V)\|^2\,\mu(\mathrm dA).             \qquad\text{(5.3)}
\]
If (5.3) vanishes, continuity and full support imply
$\Xi(z+A,V)=0$ for every $A$.  Varying the doublet in the first row gives
$v=0$.  With $v=0$, varying a nonzero doublet and then the singlet in the
second row gives $w=0$.  Thus $G_\mu(z)$ is positive definite pointwise.
Continuity gives a positive minimum $m_{\mu,R}$ on every compact ball
$\rho\le R$, while (5.2) gives $9R^2/10$ outside.  Hence
\[
 G_\mu(z)+Q_6(z)\succeq
 \min\{m_{\mu,R},9R^2/10\}I.                       \qquad\text{(5.4)}
\]
This is a diagonal theorem, not yet the mixed response theorem (3.3).

\subsection*{The exact 64-atom comparison}

Let every one of the six real heat coordinates be an independent sign.

 D_R=(\sqrt R+\sqrt6)^2+e.                          \qquad\text{(5.5)}

\[
 \E|h_A|^2=|h|^2+2|v|^2.                            \qquad\text{(5.6)}

Using the wedge row for $v$ leaves the complete radial pair for the Schur

\[
 {c_0c_1\over c_0+c_1}={243\over28250P},\qquad
 \E|u+U|^4=|u|^4+12|u|^2+16,                       \qquad\text{(5.7)}

independence of doublet and singlet signs yields

\boxed{
 \E_A\|\Xi(z+A,V)\|^2
 \ge {339\over1000P}|v|^2
 +{96\over565P D_R^2}|w|^2,\qquad \rho\le R.}      \qquad\text{(5.8)}

At $R=1/32$,
\[
 D_R={193\over32}+{\sqrt3\over2}+10^{-12},          \qquad\text{(5.9)}

\boxed{

\section*{6. Exact square-before-average boundary}

The response in (3.3) contains $\|\E_f\dot J_V\|^2$, whereas (5.3) is
$\E_f\|\dot J_V\|^2$.  Jensen has the wrong direction for the required
lower bound.  At $z=0$ and for the symmetric 64-atom law,
\[
                  \Xi(-A,V)=-\Xi(A,V)               \qquad\text{(6.1)}
\]
for every $V$, because the rational denominator is even while all four
outputs are odd.  Therefore
\[
 \E_A\Xi(A,V)=0,\qquad Q_6(0)[V,V]=0,               \qquad\text{(6.2)}
\]
although (5.8) is strictly positive.  The diagonal-to-mixed promotion is
therefore false even after the sextic is appended.  A square-before-average
realized owner, a matching trace--forest identity, or the separately owned
source/low channels are indispensable.

\section*{7. Law-free floor-uniformity fails}

The only active rows are $2\sqrt{c_0}s$ and

\[
 \cP^{\rm comp}=-{1\over2}\Var(C_6(u)V).             \qquad\text{(7.2)}
\]
The linear-row Hessian vanishes.  Direct differentiation of the rational row

\[
\boxed{\begin{split}
{\mathrm d^2\over\mathrm dt^2}\cP^{\rm comp}\big|_{t=0}
=-{5c_1(\delta-1)^2\over324\delta(1+\delta^2)^4}
(&7\delta^7+188\delta^6+61\delta^5+100\delta^4\\
 &+57\delta^3+40\delta^2+3\delta+8).

Every displayed polynomial coefficient is positive, and

\section*{8. Standard-Gaussian score transfer repairs the scalar ray}

Let $X$ be standard Gaussian and keep the same $f_\delta$.  Define
\[
 P_\delta(t)=-{1\over2}\Var\{C_6((X+t)e_1)e_1\}
              =-2c_1\Var\{f_\delta(X+t)\}+\hbox{constant}.       \qquad\text{(8.1)}
\]
The linear-row variance is translation independent.  Since $0<\alpha<1$,
\[
                  |f_\delta(x)|\le |x|              \qquad\text{(8.2)}
\]
uniformly in $\delta>0$.  Put
$m(t)=\E f_\delta(X+t)$, $q(t)=\E f_\delta(X+t)^2$.  Gaussian score transfer
uses values rather than the singular pointwise second derivative:
\[
\begin{split}
m'&=\E[Xf_\delta(X+t)],\qquad
m''=\E[(X^2-1)f_\delta(X+t)],\\
q''&=\E[(X^2-1)f_\delta(X+t)^2],\\
\Var(f_\delta(X+t))''&=q''-2(m')^2-2mm''.          \qquad\text{(8.3)}
\end{split}
\]
Using (8.2), Cauchy--Schwarz, $\E X^2=1$, $\E X^4=3$, and
$\E(X^2-1)^2=2$ gives
\[
 |\Var(f_\delta(X+t))''|
 \le 6+2\sqrt2+(4+2\sqrt2)t^2.                    \qquad\text{(8.4)}
\]
Consequently
\[
 P_\delta''(t)\ge-A-Bt^2,\quad
 A=4c_1(3+\sqrt2),\quad B=4c_1(2+\sqrt2).           \qquad\text{(8.5)}
\]

The source Hessian is $9/10$.  The once-owned terminal sextic contributes
$(9/2)\E(X+t)^4\ge(9/2)t^4$.  Completing the square in $y=t^2$ yields
\[
\begin{split}
{9\over10}+P_\delta''(t)+{9\over2}\E(X+t)^4
&\ge {9\over10}-A-By+{9\over2}y^2\\
&\ge \boxed{m_G:={9\over10}-A-{B^2\over18}}
  >{3\over4}.                                      \qquad\text{(8.6)}
\end{split}
\]
The exact executed value is $m_G=0.7653207581\ldots$.  The estimate is
uniform in the floor $e=\delta^2$ and uses source and sextic once.  It is an
actual square-of-mean/current--trace scalar Gaussian packet, not the diagonal
comparison of Section 5.  It remains one real active ray and contains no
spatial shell, chart, low, random-forest, or full A1 owner theorem.
