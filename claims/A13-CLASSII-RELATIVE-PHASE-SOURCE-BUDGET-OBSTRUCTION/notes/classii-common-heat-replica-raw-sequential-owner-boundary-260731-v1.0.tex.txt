\section*{1. Purpose and scope}

R-135 left a seemingly small operator question between its proved uncentred
R-088 atom estimate and its conditional variance summation: could one commute
legal future conditioning through the spatial and root-OU operators and then
centre the R-088 atom?  The operator contraction is valid, but an index audit
shows that this is not the production bridge.  The literal R-088 atom has
already been averaged over roots later than its current root.  It is therefore
measurable at the retained sigma-field and its future-centred part is zero.
The missing issue is information loss, not a difficult commutator.

This note makes five exact advances and one route correction.
\begin{enumerate}
\item It proves the Hilbert conditional-centring contraction, records the
exact random-operator commutator, and distinguishes current-root Doob
centring from future-owner centring.
\item It proves that the post-heat R-088 sequential atom belongs to the
retained sigma-field.  Future-centring it is identically zero.  A two-point
fixture proves that post-heat mean data cannot recover future variance.
\item It puts the R-125 future variance into a common-heat two-replica form.
The heat dummy is a common Hilbert coordinate; only the legal future is
replicated.
\item It gives the exact pathwise raw sequential telescope in each replica
and then subtracts the two telescopes.  This is the correct algebraic owner
intertwiner before future heat averaging.  It keeps the complete low endpoint
and recalculates all later feedback in each replica.
\item It rewrites the same variance by the original forward-filtration Doob
increments and the root OU resolvent.  The generic Hilbert identity and the
regular R-102 cross-Doob transpose are prior authorities; they are reused and
not advertised as a new R-136 theorem.
\item It records that full product-space Douglas--Loewner positivity is only
a sufficient acceptance test on the actual production tangent graph.  A
graph-compressed direct Taylor estimate is a weaker live route and spends the
source and sextic squares once.  This observation does not repair the missing
raw spatial estimate.
\end{enumerate}

The scope is one finite product chart, one finite cutoff, one positive floor,
one legal retained sigma-field per owner, deterministic R-088 target heat for
the post-heat degeneracy, common heat in the replica Hilbert coordinate, and an
orthogonal exhaustive output-cluster direct sum.  Same-root visits are
recombined before a variance, replica difference, or shell norm is formed.
The result does not prove the production raw-current spatial atom bound, the
once-owned $q_k$ sum, the signed forest-current inequality, positive collar

The binding authorities are R-079, R-084, R-088, R-099, R-102, R-104,

\section*{2. Two orthogonal centring directions}

Let
\[
 \cF_{j-1}\subset\cF_j\subset\cF_J,
 \qquad P_jX=\E[X\mid\cF_j].                         \tag{2.1}
\]
There are two different projections adjacent to $P_j$:
\[
 d_j=P_j-P_{j-1},\qquad R_j=\Id-P_j.                 \tag{2.2}
\]
The first extracts the current-root innovation.  The second removes the
retained data and extracts the entire future residual.  Orthogonal projection
algebra gives
\[
 d_j^2=d_j,\qquad R_j^2=R_j,\qquad d_jR_j=R_jd_j=0. \tag{2.3}
\]
R-084 applies the Gaussian OU resolvent to a $d_j$ current-root innovation.
R-125 defines its future variance by $R_j$ after retaining the current root.
These are orthogonal coordinates and cannot be renamed as each other.

\subsection*{Theorem 2.1 (conditional-centring contraction)}

Let $P_{\cH}=\E[\,\cdot\mid\cH]$ on a Hilbert-valued $L^2$ space and put
$C_{\cH}=\Id-P_{\cH}$.  If $L$ is a deterministic bounded contraction, or a
bounded operator measurable with respect to $\cH$, then
\[
 P_{\cH}(LY)=LP_{\cH}Y,
 \qquad C_{\cH}(LY)=LC_{\cH}Y.                       \tag{2.4}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 \E\|LC_{\cH}Y\|^2
 =\E\|LY\|^2-\E\|P_{\cH}(LY)\|^2
 \le \E\|LY\|^2.}                                  \tag{2.5}
\]

\emph{Proof.}
Bochner conditional expectation commutes with a deterministic bounded linear
map.  An $\cH$-measurable bounded multiplier may likewise be pulled out.
Conditional expectation is the orthogonal projection onto
$L^2(\cH;\cK)$, so its range and kernel obey Pythagoras.  This proves
(2.4)--(2.5). \hfill$\square$

For a random operator $Q$ for which every displayed product and conditional
expectation is square-integrable (in particular, for essentially bounded
$Q$), the exact defect is
\[
 \boxed{
 C_{\cH}(QZ)-QC_{\cH}Z
 =Q P_{\cH}Z-P_{\cH}(QZ).}                            \tag{2.6}
\]
Thus a future-dependent output multiplier need not commute with future
conditioning and cannot be moved through it without an additional
measurability or commutation hypothesis.  A current-root-dependent multiplier
may commute with future conditioning and still fail to commute with the
current-root OU semigroup.
Deterministic spatial Fourier projections are safe for both probability
operations.  In R-135 equation (5.1), the OU and output operations have
already been applied before the variable is centred, so (2.5) needs no OU
commutator at all.

The four-point audit makes the boundary exact.  Let $X,Y$ be independent
Rademachers, retain $X$, put $Q={\bf1}_{\{Y=1\}}$, and set $Z=Y$.  Then
\[
 C_{\cH}(QZ)=Y/2,\qquad QC_{\cH}Z=Q,
 \qquad C_{\cH}(QZ)-QC_{\cH}Z=-1/2,                 \tag{2.7}
\]
so the squared commutator norm is $1/4$.

\subsection*{Theorem 4.1 (paired-replica variance and owner identity)}

For every square-integrable owner current,
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\E_fX_o^f\|^2
 &=\E_{f,f'}\operatorname{Re}\ip{X_o^f}{X_o^{f'}},\\
 \cV_o
 &=\E_f\|X_o^f-\E_fX_o^f\|^2\\
 &={1\over2}\E_{f,f'}\|X_o^f-X_o^{f'}\|^2.
 \end{aligned}}                                      \tag{4.2}
\]
If $\Theta_o$ is the once-owned primitive trace and
\[
 \Forest_{063,o}^{\rm ad}=\E_f\|X_o^f\|^2-\Theta_o, \tag{4.3}
\]
then the R-123 direct owner is
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \cP_{{\rm comp},o}
 &=\frac12(\|\E_fX_o^f\|^2-\Theta_o)\\
 &=\frac12\Forest_{063,o}^{\rm ad}
   -\frac14\E_{f,f'}\|X_o^f-X_o^{f'}\|^2.
 \end{aligned}}                                      \tag{4.4}
\]

\emph{Proof.}
Conditional independence gives the first line of (4.2).  Expanding the
replica difference gives twice the conditional second moment minus twice the
conditional mean square, which is twice the variance.  Substitution into the
R-125 identity
$2\cP_{{\rm comp},o}=\Forest_{063,o}^{\rm ad}-\cV_o$
proves (4.4). \hfill$\square$

The factor $1/4$ in (4.4) is the product of the owner half and the replica
half.  Common heat is load bearing.  Independently replicating the heat dummy
would add a heat variance that does not belong to the R-125 future owner.
Freezing later feedback in one or both replicas would remove part of the raw
current and is likewise illegal.

The exact vector fixture used by both executables has future samples

\]
Its mean-square, variance, and second moment are $5$, $2$, and $7$.
With $\Theta=3$, the forest is $4$ and both forms of (4.4) give

\section*{5. The raw sequential replica telescope}

The replica identity becomes useful only before future heat has discarded
the raw path.  Fix integers $k_0<K$.  For each replica $q\in\{f,f'\}$, let
$X_o^q[A]$ denote the complete owner current evaluated with all legal field
and control feedback of that replica.  For $k_0\le k\le K$, let
\[
 A^{(k),q}=A^{(k_0),q}+\sum_{k_0<s\le k}a_s^q.        \tag{5.1}
\]
Every future control is recomputed from the history of its own replica.  Put
\[
 Y_{o,k_0}^q=X_o^q[A^{(k_0),q}],
 \qquad
 Y_{o,k}^q=X_o^q[A^{(k),q}]-X_o^q[A^{(k-1),q}],
 \quad k_0<k\le K.                                   \tag{5.2}
\]

\subsection*{Theorem 5.1 (raw owner-to-sequential intertwiner)}

At every finite cutoff, with $X_o^q:=X_o^q[A^{(K),q}]$,
\[
 \boxed{
 X_o^q=Y_{o,k_0}^q+\sum_{k_0<k\le K}Y_{o,k}^q,}      \tag{5.3}
\]
and therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 X_o^f-X_o^{f'}
 =(Y_{o,k_0}^f-Y_{o,k_0}^{f'})
  +\sum_{k_0<k\le K}(Y_{o,k}^f-Y_{o,k}^{f'}).}       \tag{5.4}
\]
For every deterministic bounded operator $L$ applied identically in both
replicas,
\[
 \boxed{
 \Var_f(LX_o)
 ={1\over2}\E_{f,f'}\left\|
 L\left[(Y_{o,k_0}^f-Y_{o,k_0}^{f'})
 +\sum_{k_0<k\le K}(Y_{o,k}^f-Y_{o,k}^{f'})\right]\right\|^2.} \tag{5.5}
\]

\emph{Proof.}
Equation (5.3) is the pathwise endpoint telescope in a fixed replica.
Subtract the two exact telescopes and then apply Theorem 4.1 to $LX_o$.
No conditional-expectation commutator is used. \hfill$\square$

For one raw atom the same calculation gives
\[
 {1\over2}\E\|L(Y_{o,k}^f-Y_{o,k}^{f'})\|^2
 =\E\Var_f(LY_{o,k})
 \le\E\|LY_{o,k}\|^2.                               \tag{5.6}
\]
Thus a valid uncentred spatial estimate for the \emph{raw complete atoms}
would pass to their replica variance with no extra constant.  R-135 proves
the corresponding estimate only for the post-heat R-088 physical atom.
Identifying the raw atoms in (5.2) with an R-088-type connection-complete
family is the remaining production theorem, not a consequence of (5.6).

The complete-low atom in (5.4) may vanish only when it is genuinely the same
in both futures.  It cannot be dropped by naming it a baseline.  Output
clusters and visits must be recombined before (5.2); otherwise one replica
