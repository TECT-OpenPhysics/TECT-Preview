\section*{2. Round one: the legal adapted production chart}

Let $E_1,E_2$ be independent finite Gaussian source blocks, with
$\xi_j\sim N(0,\Id_{E_j})$ and covariance-root maps $S_j$.  Let
$h_1$ be measurable with respect to the strict past before either new block,
and let $h_2=h_2(\xi_1)$ be measurable after the first reveal but before the
second.  The minimal genuinely adapted chart is

\[
 \begin{split}
 Z_0&=S_1\xi_1+S_2\xi_2,\\
 Z_1&=Z_0+S_1h_1,\\
 Z_*&=Z_1+S_2h_2(\xi_1).
 \end{split}                                             \tag{2.1}
\]

One fresh root with two visits is enough for the algebraic R-116 endpoint
quotient.  It is not enough to test genuinely future feedback: a second
independent reveal block is needed so that a later legal visit can depend on
the first fresh root while remaining strict-past for its own root.  This is a
minimality statement about the filtration pattern, not a canonical claim
about the real dimension of a covariance factorization.

For each endpoint $H\in\{0,1,*\}$ form the full production current

\[
 \mathcal J_H
 =\bigl(\Xi_\Sigma(Z_H,\partial_iZ_H)\bigr)_{i=1}^3,
 \qquad
 x_H=\Pi_{\mathcal C}d_1\mathcal J_H .                  \tag{2.2}
\]

Here $d_1$ is the root-one martingale difference and $\Pi_{\mathcal C}$ is
not an arbitrary singleton projection.  Put a graph on retained output
modes and connect two modes whenever they share a coordinate of the same
fresh root, are joined by an R-063 contraction, or have a realized nonzero
off-diagonal conditional covariance.  A legal $\mathcal C$ is a union of
connected components.  In the scalar linear-row diagnostic this rule gives
$\{0,+2k,-2k\}$.  The rational $r/(\rho+e)$ row can generate all retained
even harmonics, so the full production component may be much larger.

The current used in (2.2) is the R-082 full four-row current.  For
$\Psi=(u_1,u_2,\chi)$ and $\dot\Psi=(v_1,v_2,w)$, write

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 r=|u|^2,\quad \rho=r+|\chi|^2,\quad d=\rho+e,\\
 \dot r=2\operatorname{Re}(u^*v),\qquad
 \dot\rho=2\operatorname{Re}(u^*v+\bar\chi w),\\
 h=u_1v_2-u_2v_1,
 \end{gathered}
\]

and

\[
 \Xi(\Psi,\dot\Psi)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \sqrt{c_0}\,\dot r\\
 \sqrt{c_1}\left(\dot r-\frac59\frac r d\dot\rho\right)\\

 \end{pmatrix},
 \quad
 c_0=\frac3{250P},\quad c_1=\frac{243}{8000P}.          \tag{2.3}
\]

The heat lift is $\Xi_\Sigma(z,y)(r')=C(z+r')y$; averaging $C$ before
squaring is not the same operation.

\subsection*{Proposition 2.1 (complete two-visit telescope)}

Let $t_H$ denote the once-owned trace scalar at endpoint $H$.  With
$a=x_0$, $f=x_1-x_0$, and $\iota=x_*-x_1$, the legal packet is

\[
 \begin{split}
 F_{\rm 2v}={}&\operatorname{Re}\langle a,f\rangle
 +\frac12\|f\|^2
 +\operatorname{Re}\langle a+f,\iota\rangle
 +\frac12\|\iota\|^2\\
 &-\frac12\{(t_1-t_0)+(t_*-t_1)\}\\
 ={}&\frac12(\|x_*\|^2-\|x_0\|^2)-\frac12(t_*-t_0).
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{2.4}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Expand the two squares.  The $f$--$\iota$ cross is generated exactly once
by $\langle a+f,\iota\rangle$; all intermediate $\|x_1\|^2$ and $t_1$
terms cancel.  This is an identity before expectation. \qed

The raw $t_H$ is the safe owner.  Expanding its coefficient curvature by
R-063 produces the $P_3/P_1$ and
$P_4/P_2^{\rm cross}/\Sigma Q/P_0^{\rm cross}/\mathcal R_3$ forest.
That forest is an alternative realization of the same trace increment and
must not be added a second time.

\section*{3. Round two: exact score--trace cancellation}

Condition on all strict-past data.  Let $\xi$ be a standard Gaussian vector
in a finite-dimensional real Hilbert space $E$, and let $H$ be a finite
output Hilbert space.  Decompose the complete endpoint output canonically as

\[
 Y=b+A\xi+R,
 \qquad \E R=0,
 \qquad \E(R\otimes\xi)=0.                              \tag{3.1}
\]

The affine packet is handled by the frozen whole-output determinant.  Put

\[
 \delta\tau=\tau-\|A\|_{\rm HS}^2,
 \qquad
 \cR=\operatorname{Re}\langle b+A\xi,R\rangle
       +\frac12\|R\|^2-\frac12\delta\tau .             \tag{3.2}
\]

\subsection*{Theorem 3.1 (aggregate zero and first chaos)}

The zero and first Wiener projections of (3.2) are

\[
 \Pi_0\cR
 =\frac12\bigl(\E\|R\|^2-\E\delta\tau\bigr),          \tag{3.3}
\]

\[
 \Pi_1\cR=\langle m,\xi\rangle,
 \qquad
 m=\E\left[(DR)^*Y-\frac12D\delta\tau\right].          \tag{3.4}
\]

Consequently $\Pi_0\cR=\Pi_1\cR=0$ if and only if

\[
 \boxed{\E\delta\tau=\E\|R\|^2},                     \tag{3.5}
\]

\[
 \boxed{\E D\delta\tau
 =2\Contr(A,\E D^2R)+2\E[(DR)^*R]}.                    \tag{3.6}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
The two orthogonality conditions in (3.1) delete the expectation of both
$\langle b,R\rangle$ and $\langle A\xi,R\rangle$, proving (3.3).  Gaussian
integration by parts gives the first-chaos coefficient as $\E D\cR$.
Differentiating (3.2) yields

\[
 D\cR=A^*R+(DR)^*Y-\frac12D\delta\tau.                 \tag{3.7}
\]

The expectation of $A^*R$ vanishes, which proves (3.4).  Expanding
$(DR)^*Y$ and integrating the affine term by parts gives (3.6). \qed

This calculation needs aggregated physical-space objects
$Y,R,DR,\tau,D\tau$, not a separate Fourier expansion of every output mode.
It does, however, need the actual production coefficients and all companions.

\subsection*{Corollary 3.2 (chaos-kernel form)}

Write

\[
 R=\sum_{n\ge2}I_n(r_n),
 \qquad
 \delta\tau=\sum_{n\ge0}I_n(t_n),                     \tag{3.8}
\]

with symmetric kernels and the convention
$\E\|I_n(r_n)\|^2=n!\|r_n\|^2$.  Then (3.5)--(3.6) are equivalent to

\[
 t_0=\sum_{n\ge2}n!\|r_n\|^2,                         \tag{3.9}
\]

\[
 (t_1)_i
 =4\sum_j\langle Ae_j,r_2(i,j)\rangle
  +2\sum_{n\ge3}n!\langle r_{n-1},r_n(\,cdot\,,i)\rangle .
                                                               \tag{3.10}
\]

The second sum is the adjacent-chaos debt.  It is invisible if one inspects
only the affine--quadratic contraction.

\subsection*{Proposition 3.3 (score representation)}

Let $u=A^*R$ and define

\[
 S=\delta\tau-2\operatorname{Re}\langle b,R\rangle
       -\|R\|^2-2\operatorname{Re}\Tr(A^*DR).           \tag{3.11}
\]

Then

\[
                       \cR=\delta(u)-\frac12S.          \tag{3.12}
\]

Indeed $\delta(A^*R)=\langle A\xi,R\rangle-\Tr(A^*DR)$.
Formula (3.12) shows why a signed score route remains natural, while
(3.5)--(3.6) show that its low chaoses cannot be assumed away.

\section*{4. Round three: exact failed routes and positive diagnostics}

\subsection*{Theorem 4.1 (bare Jacobian heat no-go)}

Suppose $R$ has no zero or first chaos and use only the bare Jacobian heat

\[
 \delta\tau_{\rm bare}
 =2\operatorname{Re}\Tr(A^*DR)+\|DR\|_{\rm HS}^2.       \tag{4.1}
\]

Then the residual (3.2) has

\[
 \boxed{\Pi_0\cR_{\rm bare}
 =-\frac12\sum_{n\ge2}(n-1)n!\|r_n\|^2}.               \tag{4.2}
\]

In particular it is strictly negative for every nonlinear $R\ne0$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Orthogonality kills the affine cross in expectation.  The Gaussian derivative
isometry gives

\[
 \E\|R\|^2=\sum_{n\ge2}n!\|r_n\|^2,
 \qquad
 \E\|DR\|_{\rm HS}^2=\sum_{n\ge2}n\,n!\|r_n\|^2,
\]

and $\E\Tr(A^*DR)=0$.  Substitute in (3.3). \qed

Thus the output, low, and R-063 forest companions are not optional cosmetic
terms.  They must supply the exact missing debts if the complete A1 packet is
to cancel.

Two one-dimensional fixtures separate the two low-chaos equations.  With
$A=1$, $R=\epsilon H_2$, and
$\delta\tau=2\epsilon^2$, the mean vanishes but

\[
                       \Pi_1\cR=2\epsilon H_1.          \tag{4.3}
\]

With $A=0$, $R=\alpha H_2+\beta H_3$, and
$\delta\tau=2\alpha^2+6\beta^2$, the mean again vanishes but

\[
                       \Pi_1\cR=6\alpha\beta H_1.       \tag{4.4}
\]

These are method counterfixtures, not A1 counterexamples.

\subsection*{Proposition 4.2 (one-pair PSD coefficient)}

For a standard two-dimensional Gaussian $x=(x_1,x_2)$, let
$s=|x|^2$ and

\[
 P_\sigma=\frac{\sigma^4}{16}(s^2-4s).                 \tag{4.5}
\]

With $H_2(u)=u^2-1$ and $H_4(u)=u^4-6u^2+3$, exact expansion gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{sigma,2}&=\frac{\sigma^4}{4}\{H_2(x_1)+H_2(x_2)\},\\
 P_{\sigma,4}&=\frac{\sigma^4}{16}
 \{H_4(x_1)+H_4(x_2)+2H_2(x_1)H_2(x_2)\},\\
 P_\sigma&=P_{\sigma,2}+P_{\sigma,4}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

The R-118 canonical chaos inverse
$W_\sigma=D^2P_{\sigma,2}+D^2P_{\sigma,4}/6$ therefore gives

\[
 W_\sigma(x)=\frac{\sigma^4}{24}
              \{2xx^T+(s+8)\Id_2\}                    \tag{4.6}
\]

satisfies

\[
 \delta^2W_\sigma=2P_\sigma,
 \qquad
 \E(\delta^2W_\sigma)^2=2\sigma^8.                    \tag{4.7}
\]

Its tangential and radial eigenvalues are respectively

\[
 \lambda_{\rm tan}=\frac{\sigma^4(s+8)}{24},
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\rm rad}=\frac{\sigma^4(3s+8)}{24}.          \tag{4.8}
\]

Hence $W_\sigma$ is strictly positive for $\sigma>0$.  The displayed chaos
split proves canonicality, direct differentiation proves (4.7), and the fourth
radial Gaussian moment gives the stated cost.  This
explains why the R-107 one-pair output cluster is a useful positive diagnostic
even though universal PSD representation was refuted by R-118.  It does not
identify the complete rational production cluster.

On $c=1$, the $y$ block vanishes and the $x$ block has

\section*{5. Round four: global terminal Hessian and quotient geometry}

Fix a finite cutoff, positive rational floor, and one R-104 source chart.

 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{5.1}

\subsection*{Theorem 5.1 (global terminal-Hessian factorization)}

For cylindrical directions $u,v$,

\[
 D\cU(h)[u]=\E DV_J^{\rm ren}(Z_h)[L_\pi u],           \tag{5.2}
\]

\[
 \boxed{D^2\cU(h)[u,v]
 =\E D^2V_J^{\rm ren}(Z_h)[L_\pi u,L_\pi v]}.          \tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \Ker L_\pi\subseteq\Ker D^2\cU(h),
 \qquad
 D^2\cU(h)=L_\pi^*B_{J,h}L_\pi                      \tag{5.4}
\]

as a quadratic form on the realized endpoint range.  Moreover

\[
 \cU(h)-\cU(0)
 =D\cU(0)[h]
  +\int_0^1(1-t)D^2\cU(th)[h,h]\dd t.                 \tag{5.5}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
At fixed cutoff and positive floor, $V_J^{\rm ren}$ is a smooth
finite-dimensional function with integrable polynomial/rational derivatives.
Differentiate (5.1) under the Gaussian expectation.  Since $Z_h$ depends
affinely on $h$, no second derivative of the synthesis map appears, proving
(5.2)--(5.4).  Apply the scalar integral Taylor formula to
$t\mapsto\cU(th)$ for (5.5). \qed

This theorem changes the route diagnosis.  The complete terminal action is
globally vertically basic without separately proving that each current,
trace, or forest owner is basic.  Termwise owners can still fail basicness;
only their complete recombination is covered by (5.3).

\subsection*{Theorem 5.2 (realized quotient source norm)}

For a bounded linear $L:\mathcal S\to E$, define on $\Ran L$

\[
 \|z\|_{q,L}=\inf_{Lh=z}\|h\|_{\mathcal S}            \tag{5.6}
\]

and complete this range to $\mathcal Z_L$.  If $N=\Ker L$, then

\[
 \|Lh\|_{q,L}=\|P_{N^\perp}h\|_{\mathcal S},
 \qquad
 U_L:N^\perp\to\mathcal Z_L,\qquad U_Lh=Lh             \tag{5.7}
\]

is unitary.  If a symmetric form $K$ kills $N$, its quotient transport is

\[
 B=U_L(K|_{N^\perp})U_L^{-1},
 \qquad
 \|B\|=\|K|_{N^\perp}\|.                              \tag{5.8}
\]

Absolute operator variation is preserved under the same unitary conjugation.
Thus the algebraic quotient introduces no Moore--Penrose, small-singular-value,
cutoff, or visit-count constant.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Every fibre has the unique minimum-norm representative in $N^\perp$, which
proves (5.7).  Formula (5.8) is unitary conjugation. \qed

The limitation is analytic: the quotient need not commute with spatial
derivatives or sharp cubes.  R-104 covariance-root maps smooth spatially.
Using R-118's $H^2/L^6$ estimate still requires either commutation or a
uniformly $H^2/L^6$-stable horizontal synthesis theorem.
