\textit{Proof.}

\section*{3. Exact common-terminal signed Doob theorem}
\hypertarget{section-3-signed-doob}{ }

Set
\[
 F^\Sigma=F_h+F_0,\qquad F^\Delta=F_h-F_0,                  \tag{3.1}
\]
and define the unique common-terminal martingales
\[
 M_b^a=P_bF^a,\qquad d_b^a=M_b^a-M_{b-1}^a,\qquad
 a\in\{\Sigma,\Delta\}.                                  \tag{3.2}
\]

\subsection*{Theorem 3.1 (discrete signed terminal telescope)}

At every fixed cutoff and finite canonical source chart,
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&\E\{\ip{F_h}{S_2F_h}-\ip{F_0}{S_2F_0}\}\\
&\quad=\E\Rea\ip{M_0^\Sigma}{S_2M_0^\Delta}
 +\sum_{b=1}^N\E\Rea\ip{d_b^\Sigma}{S_2d_b^\Delta}
\end{aligned}}                                             \tag{3.3}
\]
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Delta\cP_{\rm comp}
 &=-{1\over2}\left[
 \E\Rea\ip{M_0^\Sigma}{S_2M_0^\Delta}
 +\sum_b\E\Rea\ip{d_b^\Sigma}{S_2d_b^\Delta}\right]
\end{aligned}}.                                            \tag{3.4}
\]
The same formulas hold for \((Z,S_3)\).

\textit{Proof.}
Polarization gives
\(\ip{F_h}{SF_h}-\ip{F_0}{SF_0}
=\Rea\ip{F^\Sigma}{SF^\Delta}\).
Expand each terminal martingale as \(M_0+\sum_bd_b\).  When \(b<c\),
\(d_b^\Sigma\) is \(\cF_{c-1}\)-measurable and
\(\E[Sd_c^\Delta\mid\cF_{c-1}]=0\) by (2.4).  Every off-diagonal reveal
pair vanishes in expectation, which proves (3.3).  Equation (3.4) follows
from (2.9). \hfill\(\square\)

Define the conditional signed future remainder
\[
 V_b^S=\E\!\left[
 \Rea\ip{F^\Sigma-M_b^\Sigma}{S(F^\Delta-M_b^\Delta)}
 \middle|\cF_b\right].                                    \tag{3.5}
\]
The same calculation gives
\[
 \boxed{
 V_{b-1}^S=\E[V_b^S\mid\cF_{b-1}]
 +\E[\Rea\ip{d_b^\Sigma}{Sd_b^\Delta}\mid\cF_{b-1}],
 \qquad V_N^S=0.}                                         \tag{3.6}
\]
Because \(S\) is indefinite, neither (3.3) nor (3.6) has a sign.  Their
value is exact aggregate terminal-feature accounting without deleting
favourable current correlations.

\subsection*{Theorem 3.2 (continuous Brownian form)}

Assume that \(\cK\) is separable and that the completed, right-continuous
canonical Brownian source filtration has the martingale-representation
property.  For an \(L^2(\cK)\)-valued terminal feature, Hilbert-space
martingale representation then gives predictable Hilbert--Schmidt integrands
\(H_s^a\) with
\[
 M_t^a=M_0^a+\int_0^tH_s^a\,\dd B_s.                       \tag{3.7}
\]
Define
\[
 \Delta Q_S=\Rea\ip{F_h}{SF_h}-\Rea\ip{F_0}{SF_0}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 \E\Delta Q_S
 =\E\Rea\ip{M_0^\Sigma}{SM_0^\Delta}
 +\E\int_0^T\Rea\Tr[(H_s^\Sigma)^*SH_s^\Delta]\,\dd s.} \tag{3.8}
\]
Moreover
\[
 V_t^S=\E\!\left[
 \int_t^T\Rea\Tr[(H_s^\Sigma)^*SH_s^\Delta]\,\dd s
 \middle|\cF_t\right].                                   \tag{3.9}
\]

Martingale representation is sufficient for (3.8).  The stronger
Clark--Ocone identification
\(H_s=\E[D_sF\mid\cF_s]\) requires \(F\in\mathbb D^{1,2}\).  For the defined
shifted terminal
\[
 X_T=\int_0^TK_{\rm can}(t)(\dd B_t+h_t\,\dd t),
\]
with deterministic \(K_{\rm can}\) and adapted, square-integrable,
Malliavin-differentiable \(h\),
\[
 D_sX_T=K_{\rm can}(s)+\int_s^TK_{\rm can}(t)D_sh_t\,\dd t. \tag{3.10}
\]
Deleting the integral in (3.10) is precisely the future-feedback error that
the R-136 raw sequential owner forbids.

\subsection*{Theorem 4.1 (conditional-expectation nest intertwining)}

implies
\[
                         WP_b^{(1)}=P_b^{(2)}W.              \tag{4.2}
\]
Thus \(WF_*^{(1)}=F_*^{(2)}\) transports every martingale row and signed
bracket.  Conversely, transport of conditional projections for every
terminal \(L^2\) feature forces (4.1).

\textit{Proof.}
Conditional expectation is the orthogonal projection onto the declared

ranges. \hfill\(\square\)

For Gaussian linear source processes, a causal physical intertwiner with

\section*{6. Common terminal does not imply scalar sign}

\section*{7. Exact production affine collinear sign}

\subsection*{Theorem 7.1 (uniform affine-ray production sign)}

For every \(e>0\) and production normalization \(P>0\),
\[
\boxed{
 H''(x)={3\over1000P}
 {113e^4-88e^3x^2+243e^2x^4+192ex^6+48x^8
  \over(e+x^2)^4}
 \ge {2223\over25000P}.}                                  \tag{7.3}
\]
Consequently, for every \(m\in L^2(\cF_{\rm past})\), every \(\sigma>0\),
and a fresh standard Gaussian \(g\) independent of that past,
\[
 \boxed{
 \E\{\sigma^2H(m+\sigma g)(1-g^2)\}
 =-\sigma^4\E H''(m+\sigma g)
 \le-{2223\over25000P}\sigma^4<0.}                        \tag{7.4}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
Direct differentiation gives (7.3).  With \(y=x^2/e\), the rational
numerator ratio is
\[
 R(y)={113-88y+243y^2+192y^3+48y^4\over(1+y)^4},           \tag{7.5}
\]
and
\[
 R'(y)={30(y+9)(3y-2)\over(1+y)^5}.                         \tag{7.6}
\]
Its unique nonnegative minimum occurs at \(y=2/3\), where
\(R=741/25\).  This proves (7.3).  Gaussian integration by parts gives
\(\E[H(m+\sigma g)(1-g^2)]=-\sigma^2\E H''(m+\sigma g)\),
which proves (7.4). \hfill\(\square\)

At zero background the rows are separately favourable:
\[
 \E H_P(g)(1-g^2)=-{12\over125P},\qquad

\]
so their pair is at most \(-18/(125P)\).  The \(P\) row is load bearing for
translated lines.  The normalized \(L\)-row curvature at

\[

\]

For completeness, the separate \(L\)-row inequality in (7.7) is not a
curvature consequence.  Put \(u=g^2\), \(b=1-a\), and
\[
 w_e(u)={u\{2au+(1+a)e\}\over(u+e)^2}.
\]
The pointwise decomposition

 =a^2u+be\,w_e(u)
\]
and the Gaussian moments \(\E u=1\), \(\E u^2=3\) give
\[
 \E\!\left[
 u\left({au+e\over u+e}\right)^2(1-u)\right]
 =-2a^2+be\,\E[w_e(u)(1-u)].                              \tag{7.7a}

For \(a=4/9\),
\[

 ={e\{(3a-1)u+(1+a)e\}\over(u+e)^3}\ge0.
\]
Since \(u\) and \(w_e(u)\) are increasing,
\(\E[w_e(u)(1-u)]=-\operatorname{Cov}(w_e(u),u)\le0\).
Multiplication by \(4c_1\) gives
\(-8c_1a^2=-6/(125P)\), proving the second inequality in (7.7).
