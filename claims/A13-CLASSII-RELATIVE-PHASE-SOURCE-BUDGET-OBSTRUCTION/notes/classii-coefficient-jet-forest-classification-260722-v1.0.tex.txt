\section*{Purpose and scope}

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.91\textwidth}{
\textbf{The coefficient-jet forest is classified exactly at finite cutoff,
without closing the coefficient-jet model.}
For the covariance-matched six-real A1 production Gaussian, the complete
non-aliased sharp-cube Bony forests reproduce the literal monomials.  The
summed value--derivative and double-cross contractions vanish because the
full matrix $\E[X\otimes\partial_iX]$ is zero.  For an uncentred coefficient
pair, the only remaining lower-chaos Wick-conversion term is the finite
tensor $\Sigma_\Lambda^{cd}Q_{\Lambda,i}^{ab}$.

\medskip
This result explains how a fixed-sign cone-localized contraction can coexist
with zero net complete-sector correction.  It does not make an individual
resonant jet convergent, does not improve the literal full product's spatial
regularity, and does not identify a rational-$B$ reconstruction with the A7
composite.  A13 remains T4.  The selected coefficient-jet gate is only
partially advanced.
}}
\end{center}

The scope is the fixed $L=16$ three-torus, a centred stationary six-real
finite Fourier Gaussian with the A1 production covariance, a common
entrywise real-even cutoff, exact Fourier convolution with no intermediate
projection, and Wick subtractions evaluated with the same cutoff law.  A
single deterministic root projection after the complete product is allowed.
Node-dependent projections, circular aliasing, adapted shifts, continuum jet
convergence, floor removal, and infinite volume are excluded.

\section*{1. Pinned Gaussian and parity identity}

Write $V=L^3$, $\omega=2\pi/L$, and
\[
 X_\Lambda(x)=V^{-1/2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z^3}
 R_\Lambda(n)\widehat X(n)e^{i\omega n\cdot x},
 \qquad
 \E[\widehat X^a(n)\widehat X^b(m)]
 =\mathbf1_{n+m=0}C^{ab}(n).
 \tag{1.1}
\]
Here $a,b\in\{1,\ldots,6\}$.  The production complex symbol is a scalar
even function of $|n|^2$ times the identity plus a real symmetric internal
mass matrix.  Its inverse is therefore entrywise real and even.  The A7
six-real covariance is one half of its realification.  With a common real-even
$R_\Lambda$, every entry satisfies
\[
 C_\Lambda^{ab}(-n)=C_\Lambda^{ab}(n).
 \tag{1.2}
\]

Let $Y_{\Lambda,i}=\partial_iX_\Lambda$.  The complete mixed covariance
matrix is
\[
 K_{\Lambda,i}^{ca}
 :=\E[X_\Lambda^c(x)Y_{\Lambda,i}^a(x)]
 ={i\omega\over V}\sum_n n_i C_\Lambda^{ca}(n)=0.
 \tag{1.3}
\]
The conclusion requires the matrix identity (1.3), not merely Hermiticity of
the covariance.  An odd imaginary off-diagonal covariance can be Hermitian
and compatible with a real field while making $K_{\Lambda,i}\ne0$.

Define the covariance-matched tensor
\[
 Q_{\Lambda,i}^{ab}
 =:Y_{\Lambda,i}^aY_{\Lambda,i}^b:
 =Y_{\Lambda,i}^aY_{\Lambda,i}^b
  -\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}^{ab},
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{\Lambda,i}^{ab}
 =\E[Y_{\Lambda,i}^aY_{\Lambda,i}^b].
 \tag{1.4}
\]
A subtraction from a different cutoff would leave an additional zero-chaos
term and is not admitted.

\section*{2. Exact first-jet forest}

The componentwise Wiener product formula gives
\begin{align}
 X^c Q_i^{ab}
 ={}&:X^cY_i^aY_i^b:
   +K_i^{ca}Y_i^b+K_i^{cb}Y_i^a.
 \tag{2.1}
\end{align}
Thus the raw first jet has one third-chaos term and two first-chaos cross
contractions.  Under (1.3), its complete monomial is pure third chaos.

For a deterministic bilinear Fourier multiplier $m(p,r)$, the first-chaos
part at output $n$ is, with the convention $\partial_i\leftrightarrow
i\omega n_i$,
\[
 (\cP_1J_1)^b(n)
 =\omega^2n_i\sum_p p_i m(p,n-p)C^{ca}(p)X^b(n)
  +(a\leftrightarrow b).
 \tag{2.2}
\]
The sign is positive because the contracted derivative mode is $-p$.
When the complete multiplier sum is one, (2.2) reduces to (1.3) and vanishes.
An individual resonant multiplier need not vanish.

\section*{3. Exact second-jet forest}

Let $\Sigma_\Lambda^{cd}=\E[X_\Lambda^cX_\Lambda^d]$.  For the Wick-centred
coefficient pair,
\begin{align}
 :X^cX^d:Q_i^{ab}={}&:X^cX^dY_i^aY_i^b:
 +K_i^{ca}:X^dY_i^b:
 +K_i^{cb}:X^dY_i^a: \notag\\
 &+K_i^{da}:X^cY_i^b:
 +K_i^{db}:X^cY_i^a:
 +K_i^{ca}K_i^{db}+K_i^{cb}K_i^{da}.
 \tag{3.1}
\end{align}
There are exactly four single-cross second-chaos families and two
double-cross constants.  They all vanish when the full matrix $K_i$ vanishes.

For an uncentred pair,
\[
 X^cX^dQ_i^{ab}
 =:X^cX^d:Q_i^{ab}+\Sigma_\Lambda^{cd}Q_i^{ab}
 =:X^cX^dY_i^aY_i^b:
  +\Sigma_\Lambda^{cd}Q_i^{ab}.
 \tag{3.2}
\]
Before the parity cancellation, the raw forest therefore has five
second-chaos families and two zero-chaos families.  If the inner $XQ$ is first
projected to pure third chaos, multiplication by the outer $X$ has three
second-chaos contractions and no double-cross constant before its own
subtraction.  These are distinct forest conventions:
\[
 2,\qquad 4+2,\qquad 5+2,\qquad 3+0.
 \tag{3.3}
\]
The scalar multiplicities are independently recovered by
\begin{align*}
 H_1H_2&=H_3+2H_1, &
 H_2H_2&=H_4+4H_2+2H_0,\\
 H_1^2H_2&=H_4+5H_2+2H_0, &
 H_1H_3&=H_4+3H_2.
\end{align*}
They verify the combinatorics but do not replace the six-real tensor formula.

In Fourier space, a scalar double cross at zero output contains
\[
 -{\omega^2\over V^2}\sum_{p,q}p_iq_iw_\sigma(p,q,-p,-q)
 \left[C^{ca}(p)C^{db}(q)+C^{cb}(p)C^{da}(q)\right].
 \tag{3.4}
\]
The minus sign comes from the two Fourier derivatives, not from Wick's
pairing rule.  A positive cone can give a fixed negative contribution, as in
the preceding A13 note.  Equation (3.4) does not have a definite sign after
all angular and Bony sectors are restored.

\section*{4. Complete sharp-cube reconstruction}

Let $S_j=\mathbf1_{\{\|n\|_\infty\le2^j\}}$ and
$\Delta_j=S_j-S_{j-1}$, with a declared low block.  Every scale pair belongs
to exactly one region
\[
 j\le k-2,\qquad |j-k|\le1,\qquad k\le j-2,
 \tag{4.1}
\]
which defines the low--high, resonant, and high--low multipliers.  Their sum
is one on every Fourier pair.  Deterministic multipliers commute with Wiener
chaos projection, so summing the forest correctors over all three sectors
recovers (2.1).

For the two second-jet parenthesisations, use the full nine-channel sums
\[
 \sum_{\alpha,\beta}\alpha\bigl(X^c,
     \beta(X^d,Q_i^{ab})\bigr),
 \qquad
 \sum_{\alpha,\beta}\alpha\bigl(
     \beta(X^c,X^d),Q_i^{ab}\bigr).
 \tag{4.2}
\]
Each sum separately equals the exact triple convolution.  Their complete
sums agree by associativity, but corresponding individual channels generally
do not; their difference is an associator.  Adding the two complete trees
would double-count the monomial.

If $X_J$ is supported through scale $J$, an $XXQ$ output can reach the
$J+2$ block.  Hence the resolution in (4.2) must cover the full convolution
support.  Intermediate projections such as
$S_J[X S_J(XQ)]$ and $S_J[S_J(XX)Q]$ are not interchangeable.  The theorem
uses exact sparse convolution, or equivalently sufficient zero padding,
followed at most by one common root projection.

Combining (1.3), (3.1), and (4.2) proves that the net complete-sector
value--derivative and double-cross corrections vanish.  This is compatible
with the localized negative cone in (3.4): the omitted complementary sectors
carry the opposite net coefficient.  It does not license subtracting only
the resonant correction while leaving the complementary sectors unchanged.

\section*{5. The finite Wick-conversion term}

The production covariance behaves as $|n|^{-4}$.  A three-dimensional shell
contains $O(r^2)$ modes, so
\[
 \sum_n\|C(n)\|<\infty,
 \qquad
 \Sigma_\Lambda\longrightarrow\Sigma.
 \tag{5.1}
\]
Consequently $\Sigma_\Lambda^{cd}Q_{\Lambda,i}^{ab}$ in (3.2) is not a new
divergent interaction counterterm at this monomial/full-partition level.  It
is nevertheless a load-bearing finite tensor Wick-conversion term.  A later
rational-$B$ Taylor reconstruction must retain it and compare it with the
exact A7 covariance-normal prescription.

The complete monomials in (2.1) and (3.2) retain the low--high spectrum of
$Q$.  The previous A13 analysis therefore still applies: literal full $XQ$
and $XXQ$ do not have the improved coefficient-jet regularities required by
the translated-current model.  The next proof must construct balanced or
base-point-increment top-chaos jets, prove their coupled-cutoff convergence,
and supply explicit compensating reconstruction terms.

\section*{Devil's-advocate}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{VALID WITH MITIGATION: a real-even scalar regulator alone is
enough for every matrix covariance.}  It is not.  The proof uses the
entrywise-even A1 covariance, equivalently the complete matrix identity
$K_i=0$.  An odd imaginary off-diagonal covariance is excluded.

\item \textbf{DISMISSED: the cone-localized logarithmic coefficient
contradicts complete cancellation.}  The cone is one sector and angular
subdiagram.  The exact complete partition adds complementary coefficients;
only their sum is constrained by $K_i=0$.

\item \textbf{VALID WITH MITIGATION: the two parenthesisations agree channel
by channel.}  They do not.  The integer fixture records different channels
with equal complete sums.  Each tree must be reconstructed separately.

\item \textbf{UPHELD AGAINST AN INTERMEDIATE-PROJECTION READING: Galerkin
projection may be inserted at every node.}  Such projections break
associativity and are outside this theorem.  Only exact convolution and an
optional common root projection are admitted.

\item \textbf{VALID WITH MITIGATION: $\Sigma_\Lambda Q_\Lambda$ can be
dropped because it is finite.}  Finiteness means it is not a new divergent
counterterm.  It remains part of the exact Wick conversion and must enter the
A7 comparison.

\item \textbf{UPHELD AGAINST CLOSURE: the forest classification proves the
coefficient-jet model.}  It does not prove improved regularity, continuum
convergence, rational coefficient reconstruction, adapted translation, or
the Nelson estimate.  The selected A13 gate remains open.
\end{enumerate}
