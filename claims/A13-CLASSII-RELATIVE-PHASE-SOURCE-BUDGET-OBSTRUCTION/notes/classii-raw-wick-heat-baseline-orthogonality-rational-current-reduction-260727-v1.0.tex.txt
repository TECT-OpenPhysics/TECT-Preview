\section{Purpose and scope}

R-100 wrote the regular coefficient martingale as $F_j=L_j+H_j$, paid the
future-control residual $H_j$ by one full-Wick R-094 secant allocation, and
left the moving heat baseline $L_j$ inside the complete covariance-normal
row.  This note identifies an exact centering which was missed in that
ledger.  It applies only to the isolated raw-Wick cross and therefore does
not contradict R-100's warning about the complete row.

There are five advances.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For a pointwise coefficient, real-even Gaussian covariance, and a
strict-past control, the moving heat coefficient $L_j$ is conditionally
orthogonal to the fresh full-Wick increment $\Delta Q_j$.

\item Cross-Doob terminalization therefore reduces the complete terminal
raw-Wick block to one fixed low endpoint plus exactly the R-100
future-control residual.  A second R-094 charge would duplicate ownership.

\item The derivative-current increment has an exact raw-Wick plus current
decomposition.  Its control-only half telescopes to the terminal rational
control square.

\item At the rational endpoint, the old R-085 shifted-Hessian-plus-square
frontier is reduced to the strictly smaller current packet
\[
 \mathcal K_R=G^TLc+{1\over2}c^TB_1c.                    \tag{1.1}
\]
The $L:Q/2$ member is not estimated separately: it is already inside the
complete raw-Wick block.

\item The production rational vector obeys an exact range identity and a
pointwise norm contraction.  This gives a useful Gram upper bound but no sign
for a Wick-centred form.
\end{enumerate}

The theorem is finite-cutoff and fixed-positive-floor, and its quantitative
consequence uses the regular mutually orthogonal strict-past one-shot class.
It does not prove a lower bound for $\mathcal K_R$, arbitrary progressive or
revisit controls, complete rational $H_N$, REG, $H_A$,
$\mathrm{OVERLAP}_{\rm src}$, the $q=10/9$ Nelson estimate, cutoff or floor
removal, an interacting measure, or Sector A.  Tier stays T4.

\section{Finite-cutoff setting and hypotheses}

Let $g_j$ be independent centred Gaussian value roots and let
$d_{j,i}=D_i g_j$ be the derivative of the $j$th root in direction $i$.
Write
\[
 U_j=\sum_{s\le j}g_s,\qquad
 G_{j,i}=D_iU_j=\sum_{s\le j}d_{s,i}.                    \tag{2.1}
\]
With
\[
 \Delta\Gamma_{j,i}=\Cov(d_{j,i}),\qquad
 \Gamma_{j,i}=\sum_{s\le j}\Delta\Gamma_{s,i},          \tag{2.2}
\]
define the quadratic Wick martingale
\[
 Q_{j,i}=G_{j,i}^{\otimes2}-\Gamma_{j,i}.                \tag{2.3}
\]
Its increment is
\[
 \Delta Q_{j,i}
 =2G_{j-1,i}\mathbin\odot d_{j,i}
 +(d_{j,i}^{\otimes2}-\Delta\Gamma_{j,i}).              \tag{2.4}
\]
Thus $\E_{j-1}\Delta Q_{j,i}=0$.

The regulator is real and even.  At every point $x$ this gives
\[
 \Cov(g_j(x),d_{j,i}(x))=0.                              \tag{2.5}
\]
Joint Gaussianity turns (2.5) into pointwise independence.  This is a
same-point statement; $g_j(y)$ and $d_{j,i}(x)$ need not be independent when
$x\ne y$.

Let
\[
 a_j\in\cF_{j-1},\qquad
 A^{(j)}=A^0+\sum_{j0\le s\le j}a_s,\qquad A^*=A^{(J)},  \tag{2.6}
\]
and put
\[
 Z_*=U_J+A^*,\qquad Z_j=U_J+A^{(j)}.                     \tag{2.7}
\]
For a pointwise matrix coefficient
$B:\mathbb R^N\to\operatorname{Sym}_N$ of sufficient polynomial growth,
define
\[
 F_j=\E_jB(Z_*),                                         \tag{2.8}
\]
\[
 L_j=\E_jB(Z_j)
 =P_{\Sigma[j+1:J]}B(U_j+A^{(j)}),                       \tag{2.9}
\]
and
\[
 H_j=\E_j[B(Z_*)-B(Z_j)].                               \tag{2.10}
\]
Then $F_j=L_j+H_j$ exactly.  For matrix fields, set
\[
 \langle C,Q\rangle=\sum_i\int C(x):Q_i(x)\dd x.        \tag{2.11}
\]

The exact theorem uses finite cutoff, conditional Fubini, independent
Gaussian roots, (2.5), strict-past measurability in (2.6), and spatial
locality of $B(Z)(x)$ in the value $Z(x)$.  The R-100 quantitative residual
bound additionally uses regular mutually orthogonal one-shot controls.

\section{Moving raw-Wick heat-baseline orthogonality}

% evidence-anchor: theorem-3.1-moving-raw-wick-heat-baseline-orthogonality
\subsection*{Theorem 3.1 (fresh value--gradient orthogonality)}

For every $j$ and derivative direction $i$,
\[
 \boxed{\E_{j-1}\langle L_j,\Delta Q_{j,i}\rangle=0.}   \tag{3.1}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 \E\langle L_j-L_{j-1},\Delta Q_{j,i}\rangle=0.}        \tag{3.2}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Fix $x$ and condition on $\cF_{j-1}$.  Then $U_{j-1}(x)$,
$A^{(j)}(x)$, and $G_{j-1,i}(x)$ are fixed.  There is a deterministic
matrix-valued function
\[
 \Phi_{j,x}(v)=P_{\Sigma[j+1:J]}B
 (U_{j-1}(x)+A^{(j)}(x)+v)                               \tag{3.3}
\]
such that $L_j(x)=\Phi_{j,x}(g_j(x))$.  By (2.5), $g_j(x)$ is independent of
$d_{j,i}(x)$.  Hence
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\E_{j-1}\!\left[L_j(x):
 2G_{j-1,i}(x)\mathbin\odot d_{j,i}(x)\right]=0,\\
 &\E_{j-1}\!\left[L_j(x):
 (d_{j,i}(x)^{\otimes2}-\Delta\Gamma_{j,i})\right]=0.
 \end{split}                                             \tag{3.4}
\]
Equation (2.4) and conditional spatial Fubini prove (3.1).  Since
$L_{j-1}$ is $\cF_{j-1}$-measurable,
\[
 \E\langle L_{j-1},\Delta Q_{j,i}\rangle
 =\E\langle L_{j-1},\E_{j-1}\Delta Q_{j,i}\rangle=0,   \tag{3.5}
\]
which proves (3.2). \qed

The scope is exact and narrow.  The theorem centres the isolated cross
$\langle d_jL,d_jQ\rangle$.  It does not centre the complete moving
covariance-normal row, which also contains control derivatives, the terminal
square, heat and trace corrections, the fixed-low subtraction, payment, and
the R-063 reconstruction forest.  R-100's broader warning remains correct
for that complete row; its phrase ``no standalone centering'' is
superseded only for the raw-Wick cross (3.2).

The hypotheses are necessary in the following sense.  If value and gradient
coordinates are correlated, a coefficient quadratic in the value has a
nonzero cross with the centred derivative square.  If the coefficient is
spatially nonlocal or depends directly on the fresh derivative, the
factorisations in (3.4) are unavailable.  Same-root and revisit controls are
also outside (2.6).

\section{Cross-Doob terminalization without duplicate payment}

% evidence-anchor: theorem-4.1-cross-doob-raw-wick-terminalization
\subsection*{Theorem 4.1 (terminal raw-Wick identity)}

For every starting root $\ell<J$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \E\langle B(Z_*),Q_J\rangle
 =\E\langle F_\ell,Q_\ell\rangle
 +\sum_{j>\ell}\E\langle B(Z_*)-B(Z_j),\Delta Q_j\rangle.}
                                                               \tag{4.1}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Both $F_j=\E_jB(Z_*)$ and $Q_j$ are martingales.  Their cross-Doob identity is
\[
 \E\langle F_j,Q_j\rangle-
 \E\langle F_{j-1},Q_{j-1}\rangle
 =\E\langle F_j-F_{j-1},\Delta Q_j\rangle.               \tag{4.2}
\]
Sum (4.2), write $F=L+H$, and use Theorem 3.1 on the $L$ increment.  The
$H_{j-1}$ cross vanishes by prediction, while $\Delta Q_j$ is
$\cF_j$-measurable, so
\[
 \E\langle H_j,\Delta Q_j\rangle
 =\E\langle B(Z_*)-B(Z_j),\Delta Q_j\rangle.              \tag{4.3}
\]
Finally $F_J=B(Z_*)$. \qed

Take $\ell=j_0-1$.  R-100 Theorem 5.1 proves
\[

 \le C_{\rm full}2^{-j0}X^{1/2}(1+Y)^{1/6},              \tag{4.4}

and hence, for every $\eta,\zeta\in(0,1]$,

 \le\eta\E X+\zeta\E Y+C_{\eta,\zeta}.                \tag{4.5}

Thus the entire terminal raw-Wick coefficient is assigned once: its fixed

particular, applying another R-094 secant to a shifted $L:Q$ component would

\section{Exact derivative-current remainder}

Let $B_-$ and $B_+$ be symmetric matrices at two consecutive coefficient
endpoints, let $G$ be the Gaussian derivative prefix, let $C$ be the old

$\Gamma$ be the Gaussian derivative covariance.  Put
\[

% evidence-anchor: lemma-5.1-exact-derivative-current-remainder
\subsection*{Lemma 5.1 (raw-Wick plus current partition)}

The full quadratic increment obeys
\[
 \begin{split}
 &{1\over2}\left\{
 B_+:((G+C+b)^{\otimes2}-\Gamma)
 -B_-:((G+C)^{\otimes2}-\Gamma)\right\}\\
 &\qquad={1\over2}\Delta B:Q+K,
 \end{split}                                             \tag{5.2}
\]
where
\[
 \begin{split}
 K={}&G^T\Delta B C+{1\over2}C^T\Delta B C
 +(G+C)^TB_+b+{1\over2}b^TB_+b.
 \end{split}                                             \tag{5.3}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{
 K=G^T\{B_+(C+b)-B_-C\}
 +{1\over2}\{(C+b)^TB_+(C+b)-C^TB_-C\}.}               \tag{5.4}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Expand both symmetric quadratic forms.  The $G^{\otimes2}-\Gamma$ terms give
$\Delta B:Q/2$; collecting the remaining mixed and control-only monomials
gives (5.3).  Expanding (5.4) reproduces (5.3) term by term. \qed

The square telescope is an expectation identity across different heat levels,
not a pathwise identification.  Fix the mandatory target heat
$\Sigma_{\rm tar}$ and put
\[
 \overline B_k^{(J)}=P_{\Sigma_{\rm tar}+\Sigma_{k+1:J}}B,
 \qquad C^{(k)}=DA^{(k)}.                                \tag{5.5}
\]
For a predictable insertion, $A^{(k)},C^{(k)}\in\mathcal F_{k-1}$, and heat
composition gives the exact conditional matching
\[
 \begin{split}
 \E_{k-1}\!\left[
 \overline B_k^{(J)}(U_k+A^{(k)})\right]
 & =P_{\Delta\Sigma_k}P_{\Sigma_{\rm tar}+\Sigma_{k+1:J}}B
       (U_{k-1}+A^{(k)})\\
 & =\overline B_{k-1}^{(J)}(U_{k-1}+A^{(k)}).
 \end{split}                                             \tag{5.6}
\]
Since $C^{(k)}$ is predictable, multiplication by its quadratic form and
conditional Fubini show that the positive endpoint at level $k$ equals the
matching negative endpoint in expectation.  Consequently the control-square
parts of (5.4) telescope to
\[
 \begin{split}
 {1\over2}\E\sum_i\int &(C_i^{(J)})^T
 (P_{\Sigma_{\rm tar}}B)(U_J+A^*)C_i^{(J)}\dd x\\
 -{1\over2}\E\sum_i\int &(C_i^{(j0-1)})^T
 \overline B_{j0-1}^{(J)}(U_{j0-1}+A^{(j0-1)})
 C_i^{(j0-1)}\dd x.
 \end{split}                                             \tag{5.7}
\]
The fixed-low term is retained unless its control derivative is zero; the
terminal member is nonnegative.  The only signed new part of (5.4) is therefore
the current cross
\[
 G^T\{B_+(C+b)-B_-C\}.                                   \tag{5.8}
\]
This algebra does not assert that (5.8) has a sign.
