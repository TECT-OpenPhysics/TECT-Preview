\section{Finite temporal source factorisation}

\[

\]
Let the control be $u_t=u_k$ on $I_k$, where $u_k$ is bounded, smooth

\[

% evidence-anchor: theorem-2.1-strict-past-source-factorisation
\subsection*{Theorem 2.1 (strict-past source factorisation and cost direction)}

For every $k$ there is a contraction coordinate $R_k$ such that
\[
 L_k=\Delta C_k^{1/2}R_k,
 \qquad \|R_k\|\le |I_k|^{1/2}.                        \tag{2.3}
\]
Consequently, with $S_k=\Delta C_k^{1/2}$ and $h_k=R_ku_k$,
\[
 \boxed{a_k=S_kh_k,
 \qquad \|h_k\|^2\le |I_k|\|u_k\|^2.}                \tag{2.4}
\]

\emph{Proof.}
Operator Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 L_kL_k^*\preceq |I_k|\Delta C_k.                      \tag{2.5}
\]
Douglas range factorisation gives (2.3), including singular
$\Delta C_k$.  Applying $R_k$ to $u_k$ proves (2.4). \hfill$\square$

Let $\xi_k$ be the independent standard source block for $S_k$.  Add any
kernel or auxiliary coordinates which the controller observes but the
physical terminal map discards.  Then $h_k$ is a function only of earlier
source and auxiliary blocks, and is therefore strictly triangular.  The
physical terminal field and shift are
\[
 X_J\stackrel{\rm law}=S_\pi\xi:=\sum_{k=1}^KS_k\xi_k,
 \qquad T_Ju=\sum_{k=1}^Ka_k=\sum_{k=1}^KS_kh_k.        \tag{2.6}
\]
Taking expectations in (2.4) yields
\[
 \boxed{

\]

control to a source chart, not chartwise equality of the two costs.

\section{Exact predictable versus anticipative heat boundary}

Let $G$ be a standard real Gaussian and let
\[
 Q(G)=G^2-1.                                           \tag{6.1}
\]

field independent of $G$, then
\[
 \mathbb E[\Sigma Q(G)]=0.                             \tag{6.2}
\]
Thus a deterministic-heat identity may be disintegrated over genuinely
predictable heat, provided the fresh-root conditional law and the required
uniform constants are retained.

This argument fails for same-root heat.  Take the positive-semidefinite
choice
\[
 \Sigma(G)=G^2.                                        \tag{6.3}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E[\Sigma(G)Q(G)]
 =\mathbb E[G^4]-\mathbb E[G^2]=3-1=2.}               \tag{6.4}

The missing term is exactly a coefficient/heat--Wick covariance.  It has no
reason to vanish when the heat sees the same innovation.
