\section*{1. Purpose and scope: affine source reuse at the origin}
\hypertarget{section-1-result}{ }

R-154 proved a global origin-Hessian gap after requiring all source and
target classes to be pairwise disjoint.  R-155 removes exactly that
restriction for the fixed-law affine innovation chart.  Let $\mathcal P$ be
any finite set of distinct nonzero retained dual-lattice momentum classes
modulo sign.  Retain every doubled class $[2p]$, but allow
$\mathcal P\cap2\mathcal P\ne\varnothing$.  Define the zero-control
Gaussian law once on the union $\mathcal P\cup2\mathcal P$: one whitened
raw innovation $\xi_{[r]}$ is attached to each antipodal class $[r]$.
There is no duplicate Gaussian when a target is later reused as a source.

Order the roots predictably and use the R-128 fixed-law convention
\[
 h_{[2p]}=t_pH_p\xi_{[p]},\quad H_p\in\mathbb R^{12\times12}.           \tag{1.1}
\]
The feedback functions in (1.1) are held fixed during the control
variation; in particular, the already shifted state at $[p]$ is not inserted
in place of the raw $\xi_{[p]}$.

At the centered stationary common-even covariance-matched A1/A6/A7 law,
with fixed finite cutoff, positive floor, exact nonaliased torus integration,
and the registered scalar regulator envelopes, the complete action Hessian
obeys
\[
 \boxed{
 D^2\cA(0)[H,H]>{7\over250}
       \sum_{[p]\in\mathcal P}\|H_p\|_{\HS}^2,\quad H\ne0 .}           \tag{1.2}
\]
For the pure dyadic source-reuse family
$\mathcal P\subset\{[2^jp_0]:j\in\mathbb Z\}$, with no extra vertices,
the sharper bound is
\[
 \boxed{
 D^2\cA(0)[H,H]>{147\over1000}
       \sum_{[p]\in\mathcal P}\|H_p\|_{\HS}^2.}                       \tag{1.3}
\]

The graph assertion below concerns the complete mixed covariance-normal
endpoint/loss Hessian.  The full Hessian is the diagonal Cameron--Martin
form plus that endpoint Hessian plus one global positive-semidefinite sextic
form.  No sparsity is asserted for individual sextic mixed entries.

R-155 is an origin-only theorem for a canonical affine chart.  It does not
cover nonzero past, nonlinear or revisit feedback, finite-amplitude
convexity, aliasing, an independent historical low coordinate, cutoff or
floor removal, T-050 or A13 closure, Nelson, an interacting measure, any
phase/PDE verdict, or Sector-A closure.

\section*{2. General source-target reuse algebra}
\hypertarget{section-2-source}{ }

The field and current along (1.1) are affine:
\[
 W_t=W_0+\sum_p t_pS_{2p}H_p\xi_p,\quad
 V_{t,i}=V_{0,i}+\sum_p t_pD_{2p,i}H_p\xi_p.           \tag{2.1}
\]
Multiplication by two is injective on the torsion-free dual lattice, also
after quotienting by antipodes:
\[
 [2p]=[2q]\Longrightarrow 2p=\pm2q
 \Longrightarrow p=\pm q\Longrightarrow[p]=[q].                     \tag{2.2}
\]
Thus distinct controls have distinct target blocks.  More importantly,
their raw source innovations are independent even when $[2p]$ is itself a
member of $\mathcal P$.  Pair blocks are not called independent: they share
a class in that case.  What remains independent are the distinct raw source
directions entering (2.1).

For arbitrary $p,q\in\mathcal P$, whitened independence and (2.2) give the
index identity
\[
 \E\langle H_p\xi_p,H_q\xi_q\rangle
 =\delta_{[p],[q]}\langle H_p,H_q\rangle_{\HS},                         \tag{2.3}
\]
in the distinct doubled target blocks.  Hence the complete source cost is
\[
 \cA_{\rm CM}(t)={9\over20}\sum_p t_p^2\|H_p\|_{\HS}^2,\quad
 D^2\cA_{\rm CM}(0)={9\over10}\sum_p\|H_p\|_{\HS}^2.                 \tag{2.4}
\]
It is paid once.  Source-target reuse creates no second payment and no
off-diagonal source Gram entry.

Because (2.1) is affine and the raw sources are distinct, every mixed second
covariance jet for $p\ne q$ vanishes:
\[
 C_{pq}=Q_{pq}=K_{pq,i}=0.                                               \tag{2.5}
\]
This conclusion would fail for a chart whose second control reads a shifted
state.  That different chart is treated separately in Section 6.

\section*{3. Complete mixed endpoint and legal adjoint}
\hypertarget{section-3-endpoint}{ }

Let $C=\operatorname{Cov}(W)$,
$Q=\sum_i\operatorname{Cov}(V_i)$, and
$K_i=\operatorname{Cov}(W,V_i)$.  The first jets are exactly the R-151
expressions
\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_p&=S_{2p}H_pS_p^*+S_pH_p^*S_{2p}^*,\\
 Q_p&=\sum_i(D_{2p,i}H_pD_{p,i}^*+D_{p,i}H_p^*D_{2p,i}^*),\\
 K_{p,i}&=S_{2p}H_pD_{p,i}^*+S_pH_p^*D_{2p,i}^* .
\end{aligned}                                                            \tag{3.1}
\]
At $Q(0)=Q_{\rm A7}$ and $K_i(0)=0$, differentiation of the one A7 scalar
gives, for two distinct controls,
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
D^2_{pq}\cR(0)=\int\!\bigg\{&{1\over2}\Tr(Q_{pq}\overline B)\\
&+{1\over4}[(Q_p)_{mn}(C_q)_{ab}+(Q_q)_{mn}(C_p)_{ab}]
 \overline T_{abmn}\\
&+{1\over2}\sum_i[(K_{p,i})_{am}(K_{q,i})_{bn}
 +(K_{q,i})_{am}(K_{p,i})_{bn}]\overline T_{abmn}\bigg\}dx .
\end{aligned}}                                                            \tag{3.2}
\]
The $Q_{pq}$ term is displayed because it is required in a general curved or
recursive chart.  It vanishes by (2.5) in the canonical fixed-law chart.

Equation (3.2) contains both orientations already.  The legal adjoint is
$D^2_{qp}\cR=(D^2_{pq}\cR)^*$; it is not a second reverse control, and it
does not change the physical composition order.  In particular, for
$q=2p$ both the forward block and its legal adjoint are zero by the Fourier
calculation below.

\section*{4. Factor-two overlap is silent; factor-three remains}
\hypertarget{section-4-fourier}{ }

The support of every first response in (3.1) is derived from the target and
source carriers:
\[
 \{2\epsilon-\epsilon':\epsilon,\epsilon'\in\{-1,1\}\}p
 =\{\pm p,\pm3p\}.                                                        \tag{4.1}
\]
For the reused source $q=2p$, the second response has support
$\{\pm2p,\pm6p\}$, disjoint from (4.1).  Exact torus integration therefore
gives
\[
                         D^2_{p,2p}\cR(0)=0.                             \tag{4.2}
\]
For arbitrary distinct classes, cancellation between two copies of (4.1)
leaves only
\[
                         [q]=[3p]\quad\hbox{or}\quad[p]=[3q].            \tag{4.3}
\]
Consequently the complete mixed endpoint/loss graph is again a union of
finite factor-three paths.  Source-target overlap by a factor two creates no
new endpoint edge.  This is not a claim that the complete origin Hessian is
sparse: the terminal sextic Hessian remains one undecomposed global PSD form.

The complete mixed coefficient and R-130/R-151 envelopes are unchanged from
R-154.  The $Q$--$C$ and $K$--$K$ contributions at a factor-three edge are
$240$ and $324$; antipodal normalization gives
\[
                         4(240+324)=2256.                                \tag{4.4}
\]
Writing
\[
 f(x)=x^2+Zx+r+{7\over250},\quad x=|p|^2,                                \tag{4.5}
\]
the diagonal loss and one child-edge norm obey

 L(x)={624(7083/2000)x\over4096f(x)f(4x)},\quad

\section*{5. Sharpened exact row certificates}
\hypertarget{section-5-gap}{ }

Every nonzero lattice mode has $x>3/20$.  For a vertex without a
factor-three parent, the sharpened target is
\[
                         L(x)+E(x)<{109\over125}.                         \tag{5.1}
\]
Put $A=624(7083/2000)/4096$, $D=2256(7083/2000)/4096$,
$F_{14}=f(x)f(4x)$, and $F_{936}=f(9x)f(36x)$.  The exact sign guard is
\[
                  {109\over125}F_{14}-Ax>0.                              \tag{5.2}
\]
After (5.2), squaring (5.1) reduces to positivity of
\[
 \left\{{109\over125}F_{14}-Ax\right\}^2F_{936}
      -D^2x^2F_{14}.                                                      \tag{5.3}
\]
The polynomial in (5.3) has degree twelve.  Its rational Sturm chain has
length thirteen, six sign variations both at $3/20$ and at positive
infinity, and a positive lower value.  Thus it has no half-line root.

An independent standard-library implementation uses exact Fraction
arithmetic, compactifies the half-line, and proves positivity by Bernstein
coefficients on
\[
 [0,1/64], [1/64,1/32], [1/32,1/16], [1/16,1/8],
 [1/8,1/4], [1/4,1/2], [1/2,1].                                         \tag{5.4}
\]
The parent-row certificates imported from and rechecked against R-154 give
\[
 L(x)+E(x)+E(x/9)<{1\over30}+{1\over1000}+{1\over6}
 ={201\over1000},                                                        \tag{5.5}
\]
so that row has margin $699/1000$ and is not the worst case.  Combining
(2.4), (5.1), and (5.5), and dropping the complete global sextic Hessian as
one PSD form, proves (1.2):
\[
                         {9\over10}-{109\over125}={7\over250}.            \tag{5.6}
\]

For a pure dyadic family, three is not a power of two; hence (4.3) produces
no edge at all.  A separate quartic Sturm certificate and independent
Bernstein cover prove $L(x)<753/1000$ for all $x\ge0$, and therefore
\[
                         {9\over10}-{753\over1000}={147\over1000},        \tag{5.7}
\]
which is (1.3).  This is an envelope certificate, not an assertion about an
uncomputed exact tensor eigenvalue.

\section*{6. Separate controlled-state recursive companion}
\hypertarget{section-6-recursive}{ }

For clarity, R-155 also audits a different, explicitly declared chart:
\[
 \eta_{2p}=\xi_{2p}+tH\xi_p,\quad
 \eta_{4p}=\xi_{4p}+sG\eta_{2p}.                                        \tag{6.1}
\]
Then
\[
 W_{t,s}=W_0+tS_{2p}H\xi_p+sS_{4p}G\xi_{2p}
             +tsS_{4p}GH\xi_p.                                         \tag{6.2}
\]
The bilinear chart connection is
$z_{\rm conn}=S_{4p}GH\xi_p$.  On a single-parameter diagonal path the
second derivative contains $2z_{\rm conn}$; that factor two is not a second
connection block and never reverses $GH$ to $HG$.

The connection response has the derived support
\[
 \{4\epsilon-\epsilon':\epsilon,\epsilon'\in\{-1,1\}\}p
 =\{\pm3p,\pm5p\}.                                                       \tag{6.3}
\]
The source cost is
\[
 {9\over20}\left(t^2\|H\|_{\HS}^2+s^2\|G\|_{\HS}^2
                  +t^2s^2\|GH\|_{\HS}^2\right),                       \tag{6.4}
\]
so its origin Hessian remains diagonal.  In (3.2) the connection contributes
$\frac12\int\Tr(Q_{ts}\overline B)dx$, but (6.3) makes it zero at this
centered stationary covariance-matched, translation-invariant, common-even,
exactly integrated zero-control origin.  Gaussian integration by parts and
the same support make the sextic first-variation connection zero there.

These vanishings are not a nonlinear-feedback theorem.  Away from the
declared origin, with nonzero past, covariance mismatch, aliasing, or a
general revisit law, the connection terms must be retained and re-estimated.

\section*{7. Devil's-advocate review}
\hypertarget{section-7-review}{ }

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Objection: a reused class receives two independent Gaussians.}
\textbf{DISMISSED.}  The base law is defined once on
$\mathcal P\cup2\mathcal P$.  Reuse changes incidence, not the raw variable.

\item \textbf{Objection: overlap destroys the source Gram.}
\textbf{DISMISSED IN THE FIXED-LAW CHART.}  Equation (2.3) uses distinct raw
sources and injective doubled targets.  It does not assert independence of
whole overlapping pair blocks.

\item \textbf{Objection: factor two creates a hidden forward or reverse
edge.}  \textbf{DISMISSED.}  The derived supports in (4.1)--(4.2) are
disjoint.  The only legal reverse is the adjoint of the same zero block.

\item \textbf{Objection: the factor-three graph describes every sextic mixed
entry.}  \textbf{UPHELD AS A WORDING CORRECTION.}  The graph describes only
the complete mixed endpoint/loss Hessian.  The global sextic Hessian is kept
as one PSD form and then dropped wholesale.

\item \textbf{Objection: a seven-root numerical chain proves an arbitrary
finite theorem.}  \textbf{DISMISSED AFTER REPAIR.}  Both executables now use
the generic two-index injection/independence algebra; carrier supports are
derived, not assigned.  The proof of the arbitrary-finite quantifier is the
index calculation (2.2)--(2.5) plus the degree-two path row estimate.

\item \textbf{Objection: the recursive companion proves nonlinear or
finite-amplitude reuse.}  \textbf{UPHELD AS A BOUNDARY.}  Section 6 is a
separate two-control all-zero-origin audit.  Its higher-order $t^2s^2$ source
term is precisely where genuine reuse first appears beyond the Hessian.

\item \textbf{Objection: this favours BCC or validates the current PDE.}
\textbf{UPHELD AS PROHIBITED.}  R-155 is phase-neutral Hessian mathematics.
It neither selects nor excludes BCC, a uniform condensate, another modulated
state, metastability, or any effective equation.
\end{enumerate}
