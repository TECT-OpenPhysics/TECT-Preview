}}

The scope is the fixed $L=16$ three-torus, the six-real form of the A1
production Gaussian, the fixed positive density floor inherited by the
Class-II coefficients, common real-even scalar spectral multipliers coupled
through the same Gaussian modes, and exact or sufficiently de-aliased

Adapted random shifts are outside this theorem.

\section*{1. The universal centered tensor}

Let $\widehat C(k)$ denote the six-real covariance symbol.  The production
ellipticity bounds used by A6 and A7 give constants $0<c<C<\infty$ such that
at high momentum
\[
 c\langle k\rangle^{-4}\Id
 \le \widehat C(k)
 \le C\langle k\rangle^{-4}\Id.                     \tag{1.1}
\]
Let $R_\Lambda$ be an admitted common scalar multiplier and put
$X_\Lambda=R_\Lambda X$.  For a spatial direction $i$ and real component
indices $a,b$, define
\[
 \Gamma_{\Lambda,i}^{ab}
 =\E[\partial_iX_\Lambda^a(x)\partial_iX_\Lambda^b(x)],
 \qquad
 Q_{\Lambda,i}^{ab}
 =\partial_iX_\Lambda^a\partial_iX_\Lambda^b
  -\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}^{ab}.                          \tag{1.2}
\]

\textbf{Theorem 1.1.}  For every $\kappa>0$ and every finite $p$,
there is a regulator-independent tensor $Q_i^{ab}$ such that
\[
 Q_{\Lambda,i}^{ab}\longrightarrow Q_i^{ab}
 \quad\hbox{in}\quad
 L^p\bigl(\Omega;H^{-1-\kappa}(\T_L^3)\bigr).        \tag{1.3}
\]
The convergence is joint over the finite family of directions and component
indices and with the A6 enhancement $(X,\mathbb X)$, where
$\mathbb X_i^{ab}=X^a\circ\partial_iX^b$.  A subsequence selected from the
dyadic cutoffs converges almost surely.  The A6 area theorem is inherited
rather than restated as a new result here.

\subsection*{1.1 Fourier--Wick estimate}

Apart from harmless torus normalisations, the Fourier coefficient of (1.2)
is
\[
 \widehat Q_{\Lambda,i}^{ab}(n)
 =-\sum_{k+\ell=n}k_i\ell_i
   :\widehat X_\Lambda^a(k)\widehat X_\Lambda^b(\ell): .
                                                               \tag{1.4}
\]
Wick isometry, the finite internal dimension, and (1.1) imply
\[
 \E|\widehat Q_{\Lambda,i}^{ab}(n)|^2
 \le C\sum_{k+\ell=n}
       \langle k\rangle^{-2}\langle\ell\rangle^{-2}.
                                                               \tag{1.5}
\]
The discrete convolution bound in three dimensions is
\[
 \sum_k\langle k\rangle^{-2}\langle n-k\rangle^{-2}
 \le C\langle n\rangle^{-1}.                         \tag{1.6}
\]
Indeed, on $|k|\le|n|/2$ the second factor is
$O(\langle n\rangle^{-2})$ and the first sum is
$O(\langle n\rangle)$; the region $|n-k|\le|n|/2$ is symmetric.  On the
remaining region both factors are at most their common-scale bounds, and
dyadic summation again gives $O(\langle n\rangle^{-1})$.

Consequently
\[
 \E\|Q_\Lambda\|_{H^{-1-\kappa}}^2
 \le C\sum_n\langle n\rangle^{-2-2\kappa}
                    \langle n\rangle^{-1}<\infty.     \tag{1.7}
\]
The radial summand is $O(r^{-1-2\kappa})$, explaining both convergence for
every positive $\kappa$ and exclusion of the $H^{-1}$ endpoint.

Couple two cutoffs through the same Gaussian modes.  At every fixed Fourier
configuration their multiplier difference tends to zero, while (1.5)--(1.7)
give a summable cutoff-independent majorant.  Dominated convergence proves
the $L^2(H^{-1-\kappa})$ Cauchy property and regulator independence.

\subsection*{1.2 Every finite probability moment}

Each $Q_\Lambda-Q_{\Lambda'}$ lies in the second homogeneous Wiener chaos.
For a Hilbert-valued chaos of degree $m$ and $p\ge2$, Nelson
hypercontractivity gives
\[
 \|Z\|_{L^p(\Omega;\cH)}
 \le(p-1)^{m/2}\|Z\|_{L^2(\Omega;\cH)}.              \tag{1.8}
\]
Apply (1.8) with $m=2$ and
$\cH=H^{-1-\kappa}$.  For $p<2$, probability monotonicity suffices.  Thus
(1.3) holds for every finite $p$, not merely for $p=3$.  This distinction is
load-bearing: in the coefficient-model application range $0<\kappa<1/2$,
the translation-model estimate asks for
\[
 p_\kappa={6\over2-\kappa}>3.                         \tag{1.9}
\]
At the audited $\kappa=0.1$, $p_\kappa=60/19$.

\section*{2. Cameron--Martin translation}

For deterministic $h\in H^2(\T_L^3;\R^6)$ define
\[
 \begin{split}
 (\tau_hQ)_i^{ab}={}&Q_i^{ab}
 +\partial_ih^a\,\partial_iX^b
 +\partial_iX^a\,\partial_ih^b\\
 &+\partial_ih^a\,\partial_ih^b .
 \end{split}                                         \tag{2.1}
\]
At every finite cutoff this is the exact algebraic identity obtained by
replacing $X_\Lambda$ with $X_\Lambda+h_\Lambda$ while keeping the same
Gaussian covariance subtraction $\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}$.

\textbf{Theorem 2.1.}  For every finite $p$ and every $\kappa>0$, (2.1) is
an element of $L^p(\Omega;H^{-1-\kappa})$.  On each $H^2$ ball,
\[
 \|\tau_hQ-\tau_gQ\|_{L^p(\Omega;H^{-1-\kappa})}
 \le C_{p,\kappa,R}
      \bigl(1+\|h\|_{H^2}+\|g\|_{H^2}\bigr)
      \|h-g\|_{H^2}.                                 \tag{2.2}
\]
For each fixed $h$, if $h_\Lambda=R_\Lambda h$, then
$\tau_{h_\Lambda}Q_\Lambda\to\tau_hQ$ in the same space.

\emph{Proof.}
It is enough first to take
$0<\bar\kappa=\min\{\kappa,1/4\}$.  For
$R_h=\partial h\,\partial X$, Fourier orthogonality and (1.1) give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \E\|R_h\|_{H^{-1-\bar\kappa}}^2
 &\le C\sum_k |k|^2|\widehat h(k)|^2
   \sum_m\langle m+k\rangle^{-2-2\bar\kappa}
          \langle m\rangle^{-2}\\
 &\le C_{\bar\kappa}\|h\|_{H^2}^2.
 \end{split}                                         \tag{2.3}
\]
The inner convolution is uniformly bounded because the two decay exponents
sum to $4+2\bar\kappa>3$.  The random term is first chaos, so (1.8) gives
every finite $p$.  Moreover $H^1\hookrightarrow L^4$ on the finite torus,
and hence
\[
 \|\partial h\,\partial g\|_{L^2}
 \le C\|h\|_{H^2}\|g\|_{H^2}.                       \tag{2.4}
\]
Equations (2.3)--(2.4), applied to differences, prove (2.2).  If
$\kappa>1/4$, embed the already constructed
$H^{-1-1/4}$ object into $H^{-1-\kappa}$.  Finally
$R_\Lambda h\to h$ strongly in $H^2$ for each fixed $h$, and the coupled
Gaussian cutoff convergence follows from the same dominated Fourier
estimate. \hfill$\square$

This theorem is deterministic in $h$.  Substituting an adapted random
$h=h(X)$ requires joint moments and measurability estimates not supplied by
(2.2).

\section*{3. Counterterm classification at the closed subgate}

The value covariance is summable.  On unit radial shells,
\[
 \sum_{r\le |k|<r+1}\langle k\rangle^{-4}=O(r^{-2}), \tag{3.1}
\]
whereas the derivative covariance has order one per unit radial shell:
\[
 \sum_{r\le |k|<r+1}|k|^2\langle k\rangle^{-4}=O(1).\tag{3.2}
\]
Thus $\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}$ is the primitive divergent local contraction in
$Q$.  Common real-even multipliers also give the exact full same-point
identity
\[
 \E[X_\Lambda^c(x)\partial_iX_\Lambda^a(x)]
 =\sum_k ik_i\widehat C^{ca}(k)|R_\Lambda(k)|^2=0.   \tag{3.3}
\]
Translation by deterministic $h$ introduces no new stochastic pairing.
Hence $\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}$ is the only divergent \emph{$Q$-level} local
subtraction.

Equation (3.3) must not be overextended.  A Littlewood--Paley resonance
selector need not preserve the global $k\mapsto-k$ cancellation inside each
localized contraction.  Such lower-chaos terms belong to the next model
classification, not to the closed $Q$ theorem.

\section*{4. Why full coefficient products do not have the target gain}

Put $D_i(k)=k_i^2\widehat C(k)$.  On a fixed cone, ellipticity gives
$D_i(k)\asymp|k|^{-2}$.  Restricting the convolution to a ball of order
$|n|$ around $n/2$ gives the matching lower bound
\[
 \E|\widehat Q_i(n)|^2\ge c|n|^{-1}.                 \tag{4.1}
\]
The value covariance is in $\ell^1(\mathbb Z^3)$ and has a positive zero
mode.  Therefore the full Wick products retain the same low--high tail:
\[
 \E|\widehat{{:XQ:}}(n)|^2\ge c|n|^{-1},\qquad
 \E|\widehat{{:XXQ:}}(n)|^2\ge c|n|^{-1}.            \tag{4.2}
\]
A spectrum $|n|^{-1}$ in dimension three has finite
$L^2(\Omega;H^s)$ model norm only for $s<-1$.  For small
$\kappa<\alpha$, this contradicts both advertised full-product targets
\[
 s=\alpha-1-\kappa>-1,
 \qquad s=2\alpha-1-\kappa>-1.                       \tag{4.3}
\]
Thus the diamonds in the parent gate cannot mean literal full products.
This is an analytic no-go, not a failure of the centered $Q$ tensor.

\section*{5. Cone-localized raw nested-resonance certificate}

It is also unsafe to assume that renaming the diamonds as unnormalised nested
Bony products removes every lower-chaos contraction.  Consider the natural
tree
\[
 \Pi\bigl(X,\Pi(X,Q)\bigr).                           \tag{5.1}
\]
For a dyadic integer $r$, let
\[
 S_r=\{k\in\mathbb Z^3:r\le k_j<2r,\ j=1,2,3\}.     \tag{5.2}
\]
Insert a broad balanced angular localization which is one on the following
configurations.  Choose the two value-field momenta $\ell,j\in S_r$ and pair the two
derivative legs of $Q$ with $-\ell,-j$.  The inner $Q$ output is
$-(\ell+j)$, the inner product output is $-\ell$, and the outer product has
zero output.  All inputs are comparable in scale, so this is a legitimate
cone-localized balanced-resonance subdiagram.  No assertion about an
unspecified global Littlewood--Paley multiplier is made.

For the scalar covariance $(1+|k|^2)^{-2}$ used by the executable certificate,
the magnitude of one perfect matching is
\[
 A_r=\left(\sum_{k\in S_r}k_1(1+|k|^2)^{-2}\right)^2. \tag{5.3}
\]
Since $|S_r|=r^3$, $k_1\ge r$, and $|k|^2<12r^2$,
\[
 A_r\ge
 \left({r^4\over(1+12r^2)^2}\right)^2
 \longrightarrow {1\over12^4}>0.                   \tag{5.4}
\]
With the sign convention in (1.4), the scalar double-cross coefficient from
the two perfect matchings is $-2A_r$, not $+A_r$.  Thus its absolute magnitude
has a scale-independent positive lower bound.  The production ellipticity
(1.1) gives the same conclusion, with a changed constant, for a diagonal
production component.  Summing this cone-localized component over the
$\asymp\log_2\Lambda$ admitted scales gives magnitude at least
$c\log\Lambda$.

This does \emph{not} prove that the total symmetric Littlewood--Paley tree has
the same logarithmic coefficient: other angular sectors may cancel it.  It
proves that global same-point evenness and superficial power counting do not
classify the lower-chaos terms.  The next gate must fix the actual multiplier
and calculate the total coefficient, or use base-point/homogeneous-chaos
correctors for which the cancellation is explicit.

The first raw resonance $\Pi(X,Q)$ similarly has two possible
value--derivative contractions.  A localized selector generally leaves a
finite, nonzero first-chaos Fourier multiplier even though the sum in (3.3)
vanishes.  At second order there are four single cross contractions and two
double cross contractions.  A valid coefficient model must specify their
homogeneous-chaos projections or equivalent correctors explicitly.

This does not prove that the Class-II action, or even the final specified
total tree, needs a new physical counterterm.  A7 proves convergence of one
exact $B(X)$-contracted scalar
with the derivative covariance subtraction.  The open task is to show that
all model-level lower-chaos normalisations cancel under the translated
coefficient reconstruction, or else to register the surviving term instead
of hiding it.

\section*{Devil's-advocate}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{DISMISSED: A7's $L^2$ result is enough because every translated
coefficient is fixed chaos.}  The rational $B(X)$ has infinitely many chaos
components.  Hypercontractivity is applied here only to the universal
second-chaos tensor $Q$, not directly to A7's nonlinear contraction.

\item \textbf{UPHELD AS A CORRECTION: exact $L^3$ is the required endpoint.}
For the application range $0<\kappa<1/2$, (1.9) is strictly above three.
The theorem therefore proves all finite moments.

\item \textbf{DISMISSED: $H^2\hookrightarrow C^{1/2}$ permits an ordinary
Holder product with $\partial X$.}  That endpoint multiplication is not used.
The mixed Fourier--Sobolev estimate (2.3) constructs the product directly.

\item \textbf{VALID WITH MITIGATION: local Lipschitz continuity for
deterministic $h$ proves the adapted case.}  It does not.  Joint moments for
$h(X)$ and the signed stochastic form estimate remain open.

\item \textbf{UPHELD AS A NO-GO: the diamonds may be literal full Wick
products.}  Equations (4.1)--(4.3) show that their low--high spectra miss the
advertised regularities.

\item \textbf{UPHELD AS A NO-GO: changing ``full'' to ``resonant'' removes
all contractions by evenness.}  The explicit cone (5.2)--(5.4) gives a
fixed-sign zero-chaos subdiagram with logarithmically growing magnitude, and
the first-chaos localized witness is nonzero.  Total-tree noncancellation is
not claimed.

\item \textbf{VALID WITH MITIGATION: the logarithm proves a new physical
Class-II counterterm is necessary.}  It proves only that a cone-localized
subdiagram is not absolutely summable and must be included in the contraction
classification.  Whether it cancels across the specified total tree and the
exact translated Class-II reconstruction is precisely the next gate.

\item \textbf{DISMISSED: the finite cone and FFT fixtures are presented as a
continuum proof.}  They are only independent regression certificates.  The
analytic majorants and lower bound are written explicitly in the note.
\end{enumerate}
