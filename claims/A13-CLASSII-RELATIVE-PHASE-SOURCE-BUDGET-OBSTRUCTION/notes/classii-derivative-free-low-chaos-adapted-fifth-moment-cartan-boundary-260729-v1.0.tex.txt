\[

\[

\]
The heat variable $r$ remains a coordinate in the output Hilbert space; it

\[
 b=\E\Phi_h,\qquad Y=\Phi_h-b,\qquad
 A=\E[Y\otimes\xi],\qquad R=Y-A\xi.                 \tag{2.3}

Then $R$ is orthogonal to zero and first chaos.

Let $\Theta_h(\xi)$ denote the once-owned complete trace.  In the R-119

\subsection*{Theorem 2.1 (derivative-free $D_0,D_1$ reconstruction)}

Assume the displayed moments are finite and put
$H_2(\xi)=\xi\otimes\xi-\Id$.  Then
\[
 r_2=\frac12\E[R\otimes H_2(\xi)],\qquad
 t_0=\E\Theta_h-\|A\|_{\rm HS}^2,
 \qquad t_1=\E[\xi\Theta_h],                         \tag{2.5}
\]
and the R-119 aggregate low-chaos defects are
\[
 \boxed{D_0=\E\Theta_h-\|A\|_{\rm HS}^2-\E\|R\|^2,} \tag{2.6}
\]
\[
 \boxed{D_1=\E[\xi\Theta_h]
 -2\Contr\!\left(A,\E[R\otimes H_2(\xi)]\right)
 -\E[\xi\|R\|^2].}                                  \tag{2.7}
\]
Here
$(\Contr(A,T))_i=\sum_j\langle Ae_j,T(i,j)\rangle$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
At finite cutoff, Gaussian integration by parts gives
\[
 \E[R\otimes H_2(\xi)]=\E D^2R,qquad
 \E[\xi_i\|R\|^2]=2\E\langle D_iR,R\rangle.        \tag{2.8}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \sum_{n\ge3}n!\langle r_{n-1},r_n(\cdot,i)\rangle
 =\frac12\E[\xi_i\|R\|^2].                         \tag{2.9}
\]
Insert (2.8)--(2.9) into R-119 (3.9)--(3.10), using
$\delta\tau=\Theta_h-\|A\|_{\rm HS}^2$.  This proves
(2.5)--(2.7). \qed

The right sides of (2.6)--(2.7) use only the endpoint law.  Gaussian
integration by parts proves the identity, but evaluating it does not require
differentiating the feedback map.  Passing to a limiting graph still needs
uniform integrability of the listed products.

\section*{3. Round two: identifiability and graph topology}

The derivative-free representation prevents a false inference in the other
direction: chart geometry and source cost do not determine its values.
Consider the normalized circle basis
\[
 e_0=1,\quad e_s=\sqrt2\sin x,\quad e_c=\sqrt2\cos x,
 \quad e_{s2}=\sqrt2\sin2x,
\]
and the legal bounded first-linear-row fixture
\[
 u_h=\xi e_s+(\eta+h(\xi))e_0,qquad
 J_h=\kappa u_h\partial_xu_h,qquad \kappa=2\sqrt{c_0}. \tag{3.1}
\]
Its complete row cluster is
\[
 Y_h=\kappa\left[(\xi h-\E\xi h)e_c
 +\frac{H_2(\xi)}{\sqrt2}e_{s2}\right],\qquad
 \Theta_h=\kappa^2\left(\frac{\xi^2}{2}+h^2+1\right). \tag{3.2}
\]

Take
\[
 h_\pm(\xi)=a\sin\xi\pm d\cos\xi.                  \tag{3.3}
\]
Both controls are bounded, smooth, and strict-past.  They have the same cost
\[
 \E h_\pm^2=\frac{a^2(1-e^{-2})}{2}
             +\frac{d^2(1+e^{-2})}{2},              \tag{3.4}
\]
the same $A=0$, $r_2$, $t_0$, and $D_0$, with
\[
 r_2=\kappa\left[\frac{ae^{-1/2}}2e_c
                  +\frac1{\sqrt2}e_{s2}\right],     \tag{3.5}
\]
\[
 \frac{D_0}{\kappa^2}=\frac12+a^2(e^{-1}-2e^{-2})
                         +2d^2e^{-2}.                \tag{3.6}
\]
Nevertheless
\[
 t_{1,\pm}=\pm2\kappa^2ad e^{-2},\qquad
 \frac12\E[\xi\|R_\pm\|^2]=\mp\kappa^2ad e^{-2}, \tag{3.7}
\]
and hence
\[
                         D_{1,\pm}=\pm4\kappa^2ad e^{-2}. \tag{3.8}
\]
Thus even the source cost together with $A,r_2,t_0,D_0$ does not identify
the first-chaos trace, adjacent-chaos contraction, or $D_1$.

There is also a topology obstruction to the old derivative-by-derivative
route.  For $\xi\sim N(0,1)$ set
\[
                         h_n(\xi)=\frac{\sin(n\xi)}n. \tag{3.9}

Then

\[
 \E|Dh_n|^2=\frac{1+e^{-2n^2}}2\longrightarrow\frac12,
 \qquad
 \E|D^2h_n|^2=\frac{n^2(1-e^{-2n^2})}{2}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{3.12}

Therefore R-075 graph convergence cannot justify separate limits of the four

\subsection*{Proposition 4.1 (a valid conditional shifted theorem)}

Let $U$ be a fresh R-071 Gaussian field, let $h$ be measurable with respect
to a past sigma-field $\cG$, and assume that $U$ is conditionally independent
of $\cG$.  For every $r>1$,
\[
 \E[\|J(U+h)\|_{H^{-3/5}}^5\mid\cG]
 \le \|J(U)\|_{L^{5r}(H^{-3/5})}^5
 \exp\!\left(\frac{\|h\|_{\rm CM}^2}{2(r-1)}\right). \tag{4.1}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Apply H\"older to the Cameron--Martin density
$L_h=\exp(\langle h,U\rangle-\|h\|_{\rm CM}^2/2)$ with conjugate exponent
$r'=r/(r-1)$.  Its required norm is
\[
 (\E L_h^{r'})^{1/r'}
 =\exp\!\left(\frac{\|h\|_{\rm CM}^2}{2(r-1)}\right). \qed
\]

Thus a positive exponential moment of $\|h\|_{\rm CM}^2$ is sufficient.
The R-093 progressive/revisit union supplies only quadratic source energy and
does not meet this hypothesis; same-root later feedback is not conditionally
fresh.

For an owner-complete fresh forest of chaos order at most four, the $p=5$
hypercontractive factors are $4^{3/2}=8$ and $4^2=16$.  Hilbert BDG then
reduces the fifth moment to the missing bracket datum
\[
 \E\left(\sum_k\E_{k-1}\|D_k\|_{H^{-3/5}}^2\right)^{5/2}<\infty, \tag{4.2}
\]
up to the BDG constant and the worst fifth-power factor
$16^5=2^{20}$.  Neither (4.2) nor an equivalent tenth coherent-amplitude
bound is presently available.

\subsection*{Theorem 4.2 (coherent-amplitude graph nontransfer)}

On normalized $\T^3$, let $\xi\sim N(0,1)$ and
\[
 A_t=e^{t\xi-3t^2},\qquad
 z_t=A_t(\cos x_1,\sin x_1),\qquad
 J(v)=v_1\partial_1v_2.                              \tag{4.3}
\]
For every $p>0$,
\[
                         \E A_t^p=e^{p(p-6)t^2/2}.    \tag{4.4}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \E\|z_t\|_{H^2}^2=3e^{-4t^2}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \E\|z_t\|_6^6=1,                           \tag{4.5}
\]
but, since $J(z_t)=A_t^2\cos^2x_1$,
\[
 \E\|J(z_t)\|_{H^{-3/5}}^5=c_J^5e^{20t^2}
 \longrightarrow\infty,                            \tag{4.6}
\]
where
\[
 c_J^2=\|\cos^2x_1\|_{H^{-3/5}}^2
      =\frac14+\frac18\,5^{-3/5}>0.                 \tag{4.7}
\]

Smooth caps $0\le\chi_N(s)\le s$ can be chosen to agree with $s$ below
$N$.  For each $t$, choose $N(t)$ large enough to retain at least one half of
the tenth moment.  The capped amplitudes are bounded smooth Gaussian
cylinders, their second and sixth moments do not increase, and (4.6) still
diverges along a sequence.  Hence existing graph budgets do not imply the
standalone fifth current moment even on bounded cylindrical cores. \qed

\section*{5. The production rational coefficient has the same quadratic ray}

The preceding theorem is not merely an arbitrary scalar integrability trick.
Use the active A1 doublet generator $S=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ and the

\[

For $u_H=H\phi$, the R-120 rational one-form is
\[

 \qquad \alpha=\frac59.                             \tag{5.2}

Write $j_H=\theta(u_H)(\partial_xu_H)$ and let $\omega_H$ be the scalar
coefficient of its curvature.  Directly,
\[
 \frac{j_H}{H^2}\longrightarrow
 m_\phi'-\alpha\frac{m_\phi}{\rho_\phi}\rho_\phi',
 \qquad
 \omega_H\longrightarrow
 -\frac{8\alpha(2+\cos x)\sin x}{\rho_\phi}.        \tag{5.3}
\]
At $x=\pi/2$ these limits are
\[
 -\frac83,\qquad -\frac{16}{9},\qquad
 \boxed{\lim_{H\to\infty}\frac{j_H\omega_H}{H^2}=\frac{128}{27}\ne0.} \tag{5.4}
\]
Because $\rho_\phi\ge1$, convergence holds smoothly and the limiting
coefficient is a nonzero $H^{-3/5}$ function.  Therefore the corresponding

This does not refute the complete signed A1 Hessian.  The $D_z\Omega$ term,

\section*{6. Round four: complete Euler--Lagrange selfadjointness}

The remaining proposed shortcut was that the complete scalar endpoint
Hessian might cancel the first-order Cartan block automatically.  Let
\[
 \cE[U]=\frac12\sum_i\int B(U):
  (G_i\otimes G_i-\Gamma_i),\qquad G_i=\partial_iU,  \tag{6.1}
\]
with $B=B^T$, and define
\[
 Q_i=G_i\otimes G_i-\Gamma_i,qquad
 (C_i)_{ac}=\partial_cB_{ab}G_i^b.                  \tag{6.2}
\]
The unintegrated symmetric Hessian is
\[
 \begin{split}
 H_U(h,k)=\sum_i\int\{&
 (\partial_i h)^TB\partial_i k
 +(\partial_i k)^TC_i h+(\partial_i h)^TC_i k\\
 &+\tfrac12D^2B[h,k]:Q_i\}.
 \end{split}                                        \tag{6.3}
\]
After integration by parts its Jacobi operator is
\[
 \cL_Uh=-\sum_i\partial_i(B\partial_i h)
          +\sum_i(A_i\partial_i h+V_i h),           \tag{6.4}
\]
where
\[
 A_i=C_i^T-C_i,qquad
 (A_i)_{dc}=(\partial_dB_{cb}-\partial_cB_{db})G_i^b. \tag{6.5}
\]
The exact adjoint relation is
\[
 A_i^T=-A_i,qquad V_i-V_i^T=\partial_iA_i.          \tag{6.6}
\]
Thus
\[
 \boxed{\cL_U=-\partial_i(B\partial_i)
 +A_i\partial_i+\frac12\partial_iA_i+S_i,qquad S_i=S_i^T.} \tag{6.7}
\]
Selfadjointness is achieved by the skew first-order completion, not by
$A_i=0$.  The covariance trace $\Gamma_i$ appears only in the symmetric
zeroth-order term and cannot alter $A_i$.

\subsection*{Theorem 6.1 (exact normalized R-102 noncancellation)}

On the R-102 active slice use
\[
 g=\left(x-\frac59\frac{x^3}{1+x^2+y^2},
 -\frac59\frac{x^2y}{1+x^2+y^2}\right),qquad

\]
At $u_2=(2,1)$ and $G=e_1$,
\[
                         A_2=\frac{2680}{729}J.       \tag{6.9}
\]
Factor $B=ff^T$ with $f=2g$.  Then
\[
 f=\frac1{27}(68,-20),\qquad
 \ell=(Df)^TG=\frac1{81}(62,40),\qquad
 \Omega=\frac{20}{27}J,                             \tag{6.10}
\]
and the full first-order coefficient splits as
\[
 A_2=j\Omega+\ell\otimes f-f\otimes\ell
 =\frac{1360}{729}J+\frac{1320}{729}J
 =\frac{2680}{729}J.                                \tag{6.11}
\]
The two contributions reinforce.  At $u_0=(1,1)$,
\[
 A_0=\frac{1480}{729}J,qquad
 A_2-A_0=\frac{400}{243}J\ne0.                      \tag{6.12}
\]
Thus even co-varying both endpoints does not cancel the coefficient; in the
actual fixed-$U_0$ problem all of $A_2$ remains.

As a convention-independent check, take
$\theta=(-y/2)\dd x+(x/2)\dd y$ and
$u(t)=(\cos t,\sin t)$ on $[0,2\pi]$.  Along the radial variation $z=u$,
\[
 \cE((1+s)u)=\frac\pi4(1+s)^4,qquad
 \cE''(0)=3\pi=2\pi+\pi,                            \tag{6.13}
\]
where $2\pi$ is the square term and $\pi$ is the Cartan term.  The latter is
nonzero and necessary for the symmetric Hessian.

The theorem rules out cancellation inferred solely from scalar exactness,
endpoint telescoping, formal selfadjointness, torus integration, or the
covariance trace.  It does not rule out a separately proved identity after
the full control projection or an expectation-level owner cancellation.

\section*{7. Round five: correlation-preserving boundary}

The failure of the isolated fifth moment reveals where the old proof spends
too much.  Along a coherent amplitude ray, the current coefficient and its
fixed-skew payload both scale as $H^2$.  Their joint signed pairing therefore
scales as $H^4$, not as the tenth power introduced by taking a separate
fifth coefficient moment.  For $x=H^2$ and $c,\zeta>0$,
\[
 \boxed{c x^2\le\zeta x^3+\frac{4c^3}{27\zeta^2}.}   \tag{7.1}
\]
Indeed the maximum of $cx^2-\zeta x^3$ occurs at
$x=2c/(3\zeta)$.  Hence the terminal sextic can pay the correlated coherent
amplitude after the coefficient and payload have been multiplied.

Equation (7.1) is not the full A13 theorem.  It is a sharp route boundary:
separating the coefficient before Young can demand a false tenth-amplitude
datum, whereas retaining the complete signed owner can remain within the
existing sextic scale.  The next target is therefore a derivative-free form
estimate of the schematic type
\[
 \left|\E\int\cP_{\rm complete}(h)\right|
 \le \eta\E\|L_\pi h\|_{H^2}^2
    +\zeta\E\|Z_h\|_6^6+C_{\eta,\zeta},             \tag{7.2}
\]
where $\cP_{\rm complete}$ contains the current, square, trace, finite-low,
score, and R-063 forest owners before absolute values.  It must be uniform
in cutoff, chart, visit, and revisit and must spend the R-093 directed source
union and terminal sextic only once.

Two alternatives remain logically valid but require genuinely new data:
the conditional Cameron--Martin exponential moment in (4.1), or the
$L^{5/2}$ predictable-bracket estimate in (4.2).  Neither follows from the
current authorities.
