\section*{Purpose and scope}

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.91\textwidth}{
\textbf{A genuine reduction is closed, but the Nelson theorem is not.}
For the hash-pinned A1 production coefficients and every finite common-even
spectral cutoff, this note proves the exact Class-II Cameron--Martin
translation identity, its Pauli-current frame form, deterministic-shift
expectation positivity, and a Cartan identity which preserves the
antisymmetric curvature of the non-exact $K$ current.  It also removes the
unnecessarily restrictive half-sextic budget: the sufficient one-use range
is $\epsilon_6<\gamma/6$, not $\epsilon_6<\gamma/12$.

\medskip
These results do not prove the cutoff-uniform Nelson moment.  The translated
random-coefficient terms require a larger enhanced model than A7 states, and
an $L^3$ model-norm moment appears already at the endpoint of the simplest
Sobolev estimate.  A homogeneous fixture refutes only a cutoff-independent
deterministic constant remainder; it does not refute an integrable random
remainder.  A13 therefore remains T4 and the umbrella gate
\texttt{A13-CLASSII-CONTROLLED-SHELL-ENERGY-ONE-USE} remains open.
}}
\end{center}

The scope is the fixed $L=16$ torus, three complex components in the six-real
convention, the A1 production covariance and coefficients, fixed
$\rho_{\rm floor}=10^{-12}$, common real-even sharp spectral cutoffs, and
exact covariance-normal subtraction from A7.  All finite-cutoff identities
hold for arbitrary fields in the cutoff space.  The executable manufactured
fields are algebraic regression tests, not continuum convergence evidence.

\section*{1. Production definitions}

Write the complex field as a six-real vector $X\in\R^6$.  For the three
embedded Pauli generators $S_A$, put
\[
 \rho=X^TX,\qquad m_A=X^TS_AX,\qquad
 q_A={m_A\over\rho+\epsilon_\rho},                     \tag{1.1}
\]
\[
 p_A=2S_AX,\qquad v_A=2(S_A-q_A\Id)X.                 \tag{1.2}
\]
The production coefficient matrix is
\[
 B(X)=\sum_A M_A(X)Q_{\rm II}M_A(X)^T,
 \quad M_A=[p_A,v_A],\quad
 Q_{\rm II}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{pmatrix}.   \tag{1.3}
\]
Here
\[
 a=0.004499999999998875,\quad
 b=0.0018749999999995311,\quad
 c=0.002343749999999414,                               \tag{1.4}
\]
and the eigenvalues of $Q_{\rm II}$ are
\[
 0.00125901150092606,\qquad 0.00558473849907223.        \tag{1.5}
\]
Consequently $B(X)\ge0$ for every $X$, including the floor region.

For $Y_i=\partial_iX$ and the exact six-real derivative covariance
$\Gamma_{J,i}$, A7 defines
\[
 V_J^{\rm ren}(X)
 ={1\over2}\sum_i\int_{\T_L^3}
 \left\{Y_i^TB(X)Y_i-\operatorname{Tr}
 [B(X)\Gamma_{J,i}]\right\}\dd x.                    \tag{1.6}
\]
The subtraction in (1.6) is regulator dependent through the exact
$\Gamma_{J,i}$; a leading asymptotic scalar is not substituted for it.

\section*{2. Flexible production-potential coercivity}

The local scalar potential is
\[
 U(r)={\lambda\over4}r^2+{\gamma\over6}r^3,\qquad
 r=|z|^2,\quad \lambda=-0.43,\quad\gamma=1.62.         \tag{2.1}
\]

\textbf{Lemma 2.1.}  For every $\delta>0$ and every $r\ge0$,
\[
 U(r)\ge
 \left({\gamma\over6}-\delta\right)r^3
 -{ |\lambda|^3\over432\delta^2}.                    \tag{2.2}
\]

\emph{Proof.}
Put $A=|\lambda|/4$.  The minimum of
$\delta r^3-Ar^2$ occurs at
\[
 r_*= {2A\over3\delta}={|\lambda|\over6\delta},       \tag{2.3}
\]
and its value is $-|\lambda|^3/(432\delta^2)$.  Adding
$(\gamma/6-\delta)r^3$ proves (2.2).  Equality holds in the auxiliary
inequality at (2.3). \hfill$\square$

The production stress choice used in this package is
\[
 \epsilon_6=0.15,\qquad \delta=0.06,\qquad
 {\gamma\over6}-\delta-\epsilon_6=0.06.               \tag{2.4}
\]
The constant density and the $L=16$ finite-volume constant are
\[
 { |\lambda|^3\over432\delta^2}
 =0.0511233281893004\ldots,\qquad
 L^3{ |\lambda|^3\over432\delta^2}
 =209.401152263374\ldots.                              \tag{2.5}
\]
Thus any one-use estimate with field charge
$\epsilon_6<\gamma/6=0.27$ leaves a coercive sextic after choosing a
sufficiently small positive $\delta<\gamma/6-\epsilon_6$.  The previous
$\gamma/12=0.135$ requirement was a convenient equal split, not the sharp
sufficient range.  This correction is registered as
\texttt{AUDIT-2026-07-22-A13-HALF-SEXTIC-OVERRESTRICTION}.

At $p=1.1$, the retained control candidate is
\[
 \epsilon_v=0.45<{1\over2p}=0.454545\ldots.           \tag{2.6}
\]
The separated direct Ramer-square carrier charge
$0.14136616176932618$ now fits below $0.15$.  This does not revive the

\section*{3. Exact finite-cutoff translation identity}

Let $h$ belong to the same finite cutoff space and set
\[
 H_i=\partial_i h,\qquad B_+=B(X+h),\qquad
 \Delta B=B_+-B(X).                                   \tag{3.1}
\]

\textbf{Theorem 3.1.}  At every finite cutoff,
\[
\begin{split}
 V_J^{\rm ren}(X+h)-V_J^{\rm ren}(X)
={}&{1\over2}\sum_i\int
 \Delta B:(Y_i\otimes Y_i-\Gamma_{J,i})\,\dd x\\
&+\sum_i\int H_i^TB_+Y_i\,\dd x
 +{1\over2}\sum_i\int H_i^TB_+H_i\,\dd x.
\end{split}                                             \tag{3.2}
\]

\emph{Proof.}
Insert $Y_i+H_i$ and $B_+$ into (1.6), subtract the expression at $X$,
and expand the quadratic form.  Symmetry of $B_+$ combines the two mixed
terms.  The counterterm changes only by
$\operatorname{Tr}(\Delta B\Gamma_{J,i})$, because a deterministic
translation does not change the Gaussian covariance. \hfill$\square$

The final line of (3.2) is nonnegative.  The first two terms are signed and
must remain coupled.  Bounding them separately discards precisely the
centered covariance cancellation fixed in A7 and A9.

There is a second exact form.  Put
\[
 w_{A,i}=M_A(X)^TY_i,\qquad
 \Delta w_{A,i}=M_A(X+h)^T(Y_i+H_i)-w_{A,i},
 \qquad\Delta M_A=M_A(X+h)-M_A(X).                    \tag{3.3}
\]
Then
\[
\begin{split}
 \Delta V_J^{\rm ren}
 =\sum_{A,i}\int\{&w_{A,i}^TQ_{\rm II}\Delta w_{A,i}
 +\tfrac12\Delta w_{A,i}^TQ_{\rm II}\Delta w_{A,i}\\
 &-\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{\rm II}M_A^T\Gamma_{J,i}\Delta M_A)
 -\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}
 (Q_{\rm II}\Delta M_A^T\Gamma_{J,i}\Delta M_A)\}\dd x.
\end{split}                                             \tag{3.4}
\]
The $\Delta w$ square is positive, while the last counterterm square is
negative.  Formulae (3.2) and (3.4) are alternative decompositions of the
same quantity; their positive squares must not be counted twice.

\section*{4. Deterministic translations are positive in expectation}

For a common real-even regulator,
\[
 \E[X(x)\otimes\partial_iX(x)]=0.                     \tag{4.1}
\]
Joint Gaussianity therefore makes $X(x)$ and $Y_i(x)$ independent.  If $h$
is deterministic, $B(X+h)$ and $\Delta B$ depend only on $X$.  Conditioning
on $X$ in (3.2) gives
\[
 \E[\Delta B:(Y_i\otimes Y_i-\Gamma_{J,i})\mid X]=0,
 \qquad
 \E[H_i^TB_+Y_i\mid X]=0.                            \tag{4.2}
\]
Hence
\[
 \E\{V_J^{\rm ren}(X+h)-V_J^{\rm ren}(X)\}
 ={1\over2}\sum_i\int H_i^T\E[B(X+h)]H_i\,\dd x
 \ge0.                                                \tag{4.3}
\]
The executable checks (4.3) by exact covariance-reproducing sigma points and
an independent Gauss--Hermite rule.  Equation (4.3) does not apply to an
adapted random $h(X)$; that dependence is the remaining problem.

\section*{5. Cartan identity for the non-exact current}

For each Pauli generator define the target-space one-form
\[
 \omega_A=\dd m_A-q_A\dd\rho,\qquad K_A(X)=X^*\omega_A. \tag{5.1}
\]
It is not exact.  Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \dd\omega_A={1\over\rho+\epsilon_\rho}
 \dd\rho\wedge\dd m_A,\qquad
 \|\dd\omega_A\|_{\rm op}
 \le {4\rho\over\rho+\epsilon_\rho}\le4.            \tag{5.2}
\]
Let $X_t=X+th$.  Cartan's homotopy formula along this straight target-space
path gives the exact spatial-current translation
\[
\begin{split}
 K_A(X+h)-K_A(X)
={}&\dd_x\int_0^1\omega_A(X_t)[h]\,\dd t\\
 &+\int_0^1\dd\omega_A(X_t)
 [h,\dd_xX_t]\,\dd t.
\end{split}                                             \tag{5.3}
\]

\emph{Proof.}
Differentiate $X_t^*\omega_A$ in $t$.  In coordinates the result is
$D\omega_A(X_t)[h]\dd_xX_t+\omega_A(X_t)\dd_xh$.
Add and subtract $D\omega_A(X_t)[\dd_xX_t]h$; the exact derivative becomes
$\dd_x\{\omega_A(X_t)[h]\}$ and the remainder is
$\dd\omega_A(X_t)[h,\dd_xX_t]$.  Integrate from zero to one. \hfill$\square$

Formula (5.3) is stronger than a direct termwise Taylor estimate of $B$:
the rough dependence remains linear in $h\,\dd X$, and the nonzero Class-II
curl from v1.1 is retained with its signed antisymmetry.

\section*{6. Why a deterministic constant remainder is impossible}

Consider the stronger, pathwise proposal with a cutoff-independent constant
$C$,
\[
 \Delta V_J^{\rm ren}(X;h)
 \ge-\eta\|h\|_{\cH_C}^2
     -\epsilon_6\|X+h\|_6^6-C.                       \tag{6.1}
\]
At the Gaussian-null point $X=0$, choose a constant active-doublet
translation $h=t e_1$.  Its currents vanish, so
\[
 V_J^{\rm ren}(te_1)=-\cC_J(te_1).                   \tag{6.2}
\]
The A7 sharp-cube asymptotic is
\[
 {\cC_J(te_1)\over Jt^2L^3}
 \longrightarrow
 \delta_{\rm cube}(9a+12b+6c)
 =0.00124833439336115\ldots.                          \tag{6.3}
\]
The Cameron--Martin cost is only $O(t^2)$ with a $J$-independent
coefficient.  After that coefficient changes sign, minimising
$-A_Jt^2+\epsilon_6t^6$ yields a deficit proportional to
$A_J^{3/2}=\Theta(J^{3/2})$.  Thus (6.1) is false.

This is a deliberately narrow no-go.  The point $X=0$ has Gaussian measure
zero, and a random remainder $R_J(X)$ can be large there while satisfying
$\sup_J\E R_J(X)<\infty$.  The calculation does not refute the canonical
expected adapted-control inequality.

\section*{7. The exact translated-model gap}

For the production covariance $C(k)\asymp\langle k\rangle^{-4}$ in three
dimensions, take any
\[
 {1\over3}<\alpha<{1\over2},\qquad\kappa>0.           \tag{7.1}
\]
A7 supplies the particular contracted scalar
$B(X):(\partial X\otimes\partial X-\Gamma)$ in
$L^2(\Omega;H^{-1-\kappa})$.  It does not state the universal tensor or the
coefficient jets needed after translation.  The smallest non-circular next
theorem is
\[
 \texttt{A13-CLASSII-CAMERON-MARTIN-TRANSLATED-CURRENT-MODEL-LIFT}. \tag{7.2}
\]
It must construct, consistently across cutoffs and with all required finite
moments,
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{model object}&\text{target regularity}\\ \hline
X&C^\alpha\\
\mathbb X_i^{ab}=X^a\circ\partial_iX^b&C^{2\alpha-1}\\
Q_i^{ab}=: \partial_iX_a\partial_iX_b :&H^{-1-\kappa}\\
X^c\diamond Q_i^{ab}&H^{\alpha-1-\kappa}\\
:X^cX^d:\diamond Q_i^{ab}&H^{2\alpha-1-\kappa}.
\end{array}                                             \tag{7.3}
\]
The derivative-pair covariance $\Gamma_{J,i}$ is the only local subtraction
allowed.  Common evenness makes the same-point value--derivative contraction
zero.  Any new divergent interaction counterterm would be a new obstruction,
not an admissible silent repair.

The moment requirement is already visible in one deterministic Sobolev
calculation.  Testing $Q$ against $h^2$ gives
\[
 \|h^2\|_{H^{1+\kappa}}
 \lesssim
 \|h\|_{H^2}^{\,1+\kappa/2}
 \|h\|_6^{\,1-\kappa/2}.                              \tag{7.4}
\]
Three-factor Young therefore requires the model norm of $Q$ in
\[
 L^{\,6/(2-\kappa)}(\Omega),                          \tag{7.5}
\]
which tends to $L^3$ as $\kappa\downarrow0$.  A7's stated $L^2$ result does
not imply (7.5).

There is also a critical signed-current boundary.  Termwise absolute-value
estimates produce schematically
\[
 |\langle\mathbb J,h^2\nabla h\rangle|
 \lesssim\|\mathbb J\|_{H^{-1/2}}
 \|h\|_{H^2}^{3/2}\|h\|_6^{3/2}.                    \tag{7.6}
\]
Since
\[
 {3/2\over2}+{3/2\over6}=1,                           \tag{7.7}
\]
Young's inequality leaves no exponent with which to absorb an unbounded
random $\|\mathbb J\|$.  The positive current square, centered $Q$, and the
Cartan curvature term must therefore be estimated together.  Generic
termwise paraproduct absorption is not a valid successor architecture.

\section*{8. Conditional route to the umbrella one-use theorem}

For fixed $\eta,\epsilon_6>0$, define the strongest useful stochastic form
deficit
\[
 R_J^*(X)=\sup_h\left[
 -\Delta V_J^{\rm ren}(X;h)
 -\eta\|h\|_{\cH_C}^2
 -\epsilon_6\|X+h\|_6^6\right]_+.                    \tag{8.1}
\]
If the translated model lift and a signed reconstruction theorem prove
\[
 \sup_J\E R_J^*(X)<\infty,                            \tag{8.2}
\]
then substituting the adapted endpoint shift $h=h(v)$ and using
$\|h(v)\|_{\cH_C}^2\le\mathcal E(v)$ gives the required one-use estimate.
The identity $\E V_J^{\rm ren}(X)=0$ supplies the unshifted term.  Combining
that estimate with Lemma 2.1 yields the $q=10/9$ Nelson moment.

Equation (8.2) may be stronger than necessary.  If the all-$h$ supremum is
too strong, the honest fallback is the strict-past shell-progressive version
for triangular adapted shifts.  Merely renaming the original Nelson moment
as a Bellman value is circular and remains forbidden by v1.1.

\section*{Devil's-advocate}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{DISMISSED: the old $\gamma/12$ condition is necessary.}
Lemma 2.1 keeps the quartic absorption parameter independent of the one-use
field charge.  For every $\epsilon_6<\gamma/6$, a positive
$\delta<\gamma/6-\epsilon_6$ leaves a sextic margin and only a finite-volume
constant.

\item \textbf{VALID WITH MITIGATION: fitting the Ramer square below $0.15$
revives the one-shot map.}  It does not.  The immutable v1.1 finite-mode
fixture has a zero and sign change of $\det(I+(5/9)Db_J)$; this package
reclassifies the square comparison but preserves the determinant no-go.

\item \textbf{DISMISSED: the positive final term in (3.2) permits separate
absolute estimates of the first two terms.}  The frame identity (3.4) shows a
negative translated-counterterm square of the same order.  Separating the
terms loses the centered Gaussian cancellation and can manufacture a false
coercivity statement.

\item \textbf{VALID WITH MITIGATION: deterministic expectation positivity
proves the adapted case.}  It does not.  Formula (4.2) uses independence of
$h$ from $X$ and $Y$.  The remaining theorem is explicitly restricted to
random/adapted translations and requires the new model lift.

\item \textbf{DISMISSED: the homogeneous fixture refutes every random
remainder.}  It refutes only a cutoff-independent deterministic constant.
The Gaussian-null evaluation at $X=0$ supplies no lower bound on
$\E R_J(X)$.

\item \textbf{VALID WITH MITIGATION: A7's $L^2$ composite already contains
all tensors in (7.3).}  The A7 theorem states one coefficient-contracted
scalar.  Decontraction and translated coefficient jets are not consequences
of that statement.  The next claim must construct them and pin the absence of
new counterterms.

\item \textbf{UPHELD AGAINST TERMWISE METHODS: ordinary paraproduct Young
estimates close the last gate.}  The endpoint cubic moment in (7.5) and the
saturated exponents in (7.7) show that such an argument has no random-norm
absorption slot.  Only a coupled signed reconstruction remains viable.
\end{enumerate}
