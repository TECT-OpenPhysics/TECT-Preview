\section*{1. Purpose and scope}

This note executes the direct source-action successor selected after the
R-117 loop audit.  Its purpose is to decide whether a visit-count-free
operator-valued version of the R-068 centered-form estimate exists, and to
state exactly what a complete adapted owner must satisfy before that estimate
can be paid by one terminal sextic rather than by a visit square function.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\textwidth}{
\textbf{What is proved.}
For a finite visit space, a selfadjoint quadratic form descends to the endpoint
quotient if and only if it annihilates the synthesis kernel, equivalently if
and only if it has the form $L^*BL$.  The R-068 centered-form theorem extends
without a visit-count loss to spatially constant selfadjoint operator families
whose absolute spectral variation is bounded.  If those operators factor
through the endpoint synthesis with a uniformly bounded endpoint family, the
analytic estimate uses the endpoint field rather than the visit square
function.

Every scalar Gaussian polynomial has a canonical decomposition into its zero
chaos, first chaos, and one double divergence.  Thus a pure double-divergence
representation exists exactly when the zero and first chaoses vanish.  An
exact two-visit Hermite quotient has mean $-\epsilon^2$ and a unique
sign-changing scalar preimage, disproving a universal positive-semidefinite
random-$W$ factorisation.

\medskip
\textbf{What is not proved.}
R-104 and R-116 prove quotient descent for the recombined endpoint packet in
their fixed-environment scope; they do not prove that an isolated Hessian,
trace, current, or forest owner descends.  No current result verifies the
zero/first-chaos cancellation, vertical basicness, random-$W$ forest
compatibility, or spatial multiplier bound for the full adapted production
cluster.  The progressive/revisit extension, one-use aggregation,
$\mathrm{OVERLAP}_{\rm src}$, Nelson estimate, removals, interacting measure,
Sector-A closure, and tier promotion remain open.
}}
\end{center}

The analytic theorem is finite-cutoff and uses the fixed $L=16$ torus and the
sharp-cube maximal bounds already established in R-068/A12.  The Gaussian
range theorem is finite dimensional and polynomial.  The Hermite fixture is
an abstract contraction-connected output diagnostic, not an independently
embedded A1 production root.

\section*{2. The finite-visit quotient theorem}

Let $V$ and $H$ be finite-dimensional real Hilbert spaces, let
$L:V\to H$ be linear, and let $K=K^*:V\to V$.  Write

\[
 q_K(v)=\langle v,Kv\rangle .                            \tag{2.1}
\]

% evidence-anchor: finite-visit-quotient-theorem
\subsection*{Theorem 2.1 (quadratic quotient factorisation)}

The following statements are equivalent.

\begin{enumerate}
\item $q_K$ is constant on every fibre $v+\ker L$.
\item $\ker L\subseteq\ker K$.
\item There is a selfadjoint $B$ on $\Ran L$ such that
\[
                 K=L^*BL.                                \tag{2.2}
\]
\end{enumerate}

For the Moore--Penrose inverse $L^\dagger$, the canonical quotient operator is

\[
 B=(L^\dagger)^*K L^\dagger\quad\hbox{on }\Ran L.       \tag{2.3}
\]

Moreover, for $C\ge0$, the form bound

\[
 |\langle v,Kv\rangle|\le C\|Lv\|^2\quad(v\in V)       \tag{2.4}
\]

is equivalent to a factorisation (2.2) with $\|B\|\le C$, equivalently

\[
              -C L^*L\le K\le C L^*L.                   \tag{2.5}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
If $k\in\ker L$, fibre invariance applied to $v+tk$ says that

\[
 2t\langle v,Kk\rangle+t^2\langle k,Kk\rangle=0
 \quad\hbox{for every }v\in V,\ t\in\R .               \tag{2.6}
\]

The linear coefficient gives $Kk=0$.  Conversely, $Kk=0$ makes (2.6) zero.
When $K$ kills $\ker L$, the form

\[
 \langle Lv,B Lw\rangle:=\langle v,Kw\rangle            \tag{2.7}
\]

is well defined and selfadjoint on $\Ran L$, and gives (2.2).  Formula (2.3)
is the same construction in the orthogonal representative
$(\ker L)^\perp$.  Finally (2.4) defines a bounded selfadjoint form on
$\Ran L$ with norm at most $C$; the finite-dimensional representation theorem
gives $B$.  The converse and (2.5) are immediate. \qed

It is not enough to check only $q_K(k)=0$ on $\ker L$ for an indefinite form;
the mixed polarisation in (2.6) is load-bearing.

\section*{3. Fixed linear endpoints and the curvature boundary}

Let $\Phi:H\to\R$ be $C^2$, let $A_0\in H$, and suppose that the visit
dependence is genuinely through one fixed affine synthesis:

\[
                    F(v)=\Phi(A_0+Lv).                   \tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
 DF(v)=L^*D\Phi(A_0+Lv),\qquad
 D^2F(v)=L^*D^2\Phi(A_0+Lv)L.                            \tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the full endpoint Hessian annihilates every closed revisit cycle.  This
is the correct algebra behind the R-116 endpoint quotient when all objects
being differentiated are functions of the same fixed endpoint data.

The statement does not apply termwise to an adapted owner decomposition.  If
$E:V\to H$ is nonlinear, then

\[
 D^2(\Phi\circ E)
 =(DE)^*D^2\Phi(E)DE+D\Phi(E)\,D^2E.                    \tag{3.3}
\]

The curvature term contains the current Hessian, trace, and forest companions.
If coefficients also depend on a vertical variable, their first and second
vertical derivatives reappear as the $DW,D^2W$ forest.  Therefore an isolated
owner factors only after one proves its full vertical basicness, or after the
complete signed owner cancellation proves the same fact.  R-104/R-116 permit
mean and covariance mass to exchange between owners, so endpoint invariance of
their recombined scalar packet does not by itself establish termwise (2.2).

\section*{4. Operator-valued centered-form theorem}

Let $\cH$ be a real Hilbert space and let
$a\in H^2(\T^3;\cH)$.  Let $K_r=K_r^*$ be a finite family of spatially
constant operators satisfying the absolute spectral-variation bound

\[
                 \sum_r|K_r|\le K_0\Id_{\cH}.            \tag{4.1}
\]

With the R-068 sharp-cube projections, put

\[
 q_r(x)=\langle a(x),K_ra(x)\rangle_{\cH},\qquad
 \Pi_K(a)=\sum_r S_{r-1}q_r,                             \tag{4.2}
\]

\[
 X=\|a\|_{H^2(\T^3;\cH)}^2,\qquad
 Y=\||a|_{\cH}\|_{L^6}^6.                              \tag{4.3}
\]

% evidence-anchor: operator-valued-centered-form-theorem
\subsection*{Theorem 4.1 (visit-count-free operator R-068 extension)}

There are constants depending on the fixed torus and sharp-cube system, but
not on the number of operators, such that

\[
 \|\Pi_K(a)\|_2\le C_0K_0Y^{1/3},\qquad
 \|\Pi_K(a)\|_{H^2}\le C_2K_0X.                        \tag{4.4}
\]

For $0<\kappa<1$, with $\theta=(1+\kappa)/2$,

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\Pi_K(a)\|_{H^{1+\kappa}}
 \le C_\kappa K_0 X^{(1+\kappa)/2}Y^{(1-\kappa)/6}.}   \tag{4.5}
\]

Consequently, if $M=\|Q\|_{H^{-1-\kappa}}$, then for every
$\eta,\zeta>0$,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\langle Q,\Pi_K(a)\rangle|
 \le{}&\eta X+\zeta Y\\
 &+C_\kappa K_0^{3/(1-\kappa)}
 \eta^{-3(1+\kappa)/[2(1-\kappa)]}
 \zeta^{-1/2}M^{3/(1-\kappa)} .
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.6}
\]

At the production value $\kappa=1/10$, the powers of
$(X,Y,\hbox{Young gap})$ are $(11/20,3/20,3/10)$, and the random moment and
$K_0$ powers are $10/3$; the negative $\eta$ and $\zeta$ powers are $11/6$
and $1/2$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
For every $u,v\in\cH$, polar decomposition and Cauchy--Schwarz in the index
$r$ give

\[
 \sum_r|\langle u,K_rv\rangle|
 \le\left\langle u,\sum_r|K_r|u\right\rangle^{1/2}
     \left\langle v,\sum_r|K_r|v\right\rangle^{1/2}
 \le K_0\|u\|\|v\|.                                   \tag{4.7}
\]

For $g\in L^2$, selfadjointness of $S_r$, (4.7), the sharp-cube maximal
theorem at $3/2$, and finite volume give

\[
 |\langle\Pi_K(a),g\rangle|
 \le K_0\int |a|^2S_*|g|
 \le C K_0\||a|^2\|_3\|g\|_2,                         \tag{4.8}
\]

which proves the first bound in (4.4).  For $|\alpha|\le2$, differentiate
before dualising:

\[
 \partial^\alpha q_r
 =\sum_{\beta\le\alpha}c_{\alpha,\beta}
 \langle\partial^\beta a,
 K_r\partial^{\alpha-\beta}a\rangle.                   \tag{4.9}
\]

Equation (4.7), the $L^2$ sharp-cube maximal theorem, and the Hilbert-valued
three-dimensional $H^2$ algebra estimate prove the second bound in (4.4).
This differentiated duality is essential; the generally unjustified shortcut
$\sum_r\|q_r\|_{H^2}\lesssim X$ is not used.  Interpolation gives (4.5).
Three-factor weighted Young with gap $(1-\kappa)/3$ gives (4.6). \qed

Spatially varying $K_r(x)$ require a uniform multiplier bound at the same
Sobolev order.  Uniform operator norm alone is insufficient: a constant visit
multiplied by $e^{iMx}$ has fixed pointwise operator norm and an $H^2$ norm
growing like $M^2$.

\section*{5. Terminal ownership and the opposite-visit test}

Suppose $L:\cH_{\rm visit}\to\cH_{\rm end}$ is the endpoint synthesis and

\[
 K_r=L^*B_rL,\qquad \sum_r|B_r|\le B_0\Id.             \tag{5.1}
\]

Then $q_r(a)=\langle La,B_rLa\rangle$, so Theorem 4.1 applies directly to
the terminal aggregate $z=La$.  This is the sufficient bridge from the
analytic visit theorem to a terminal-sextic one-use payment.  It still requires
a cutoff-uniform source estimate for $\|z\|_{H^2}^2$ and the declared terminal
$L^6$ bound.

The condition is sharp at the quotient level.  With two visits,

\[
 L=(1,1),\qquad a=(f,-f),\qquad K_{\rm diag}=\Id_2,      \tag{5.2}
\]

one has

\[
 La=0,\qquad \langle a,K_{\rm diag}a\rangle=2|f|^2,
 \qquad Y_{\rm visit}=8\|f\|_6^6.                       \tag{5.3}
\]

Thus a diagonal visit square cannot be paid by the zero terminal sextic.  The
coherent endpoint matrix $L^*L$ does kill $(f,-f)$.  Likewise $m$ coherent
visits $f/\sqrt m$ have fixed visit source energy and terminal square
$m|f|^2$; a uniform source theorem therefore also needs a uniform synthesis
bound.  These fixtures do not refute Theorem 4.1, whose $Y$ is explicitly the
visit square-function sextic, or the complete signed action.

\section*{6. Canonical Gaussian double-divergence range}

Let $G\sim N(0,\Id_n)$ and let a scalar polynomial have Wiener-chaos expansion

\[
                         F=\sum_{k=0}^dF_k.              \tag{6.1}
\]

For a symmetric polynomial matrix $W$, use

\[
 \delta^2W
 =G^TWG-\Tr W-2G\cdot\Div W+\Div^2W.                  \tag{6.2}
\]

\subsection*{Theorem 6.1 (canonical second-divergence decomposition)}

Define

\[
 W_{\rm can}=2\sum_{k=2}^d{D^2F_k\over k(k-1)}.          \tag{6.3}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 F=\E F+\langle\E[GF],G\rangle+{1\over2}\delta^2W_{\rm can}.} \tag{6.4}
\]

Hence $F=\delta^2W/2$ for some polynomial symmetric $W$ if and only if

\[
                        \E F=0,\qquad \E[GF]=0.          \tag{6.5}
\]

The right inverse (6.3) is canonical; in dimension greater than one it need
not be the only tensor preimage.  In the scalar one-dimensional polynomial
case it is unique.

\paragraph{Proof.}
On the $k$th homogeneous chaos,

\[
             \delta^2D^2F_k=k(k-1)F_k.                  \tag{6.6}
\]

The range of $\delta^2$ contains no chaos of degree zero or one.  Summing
(6.6) proves (6.4)--(6.5).  In one dimension
$\delta^2H_k=H_{k+2}$, which is injective on polynomials. \qed

This theorem turns the proposed production identification into two explicit
low-chaos tests before any positivity or exponential estimate is attempted.

\section*{7. Exact score split and the two-visit mean debt}

Let $R:\R^n\to\R^m$ be polynomial, let $x_0=b+AG$, and set

\[
 F=\langle x_0,R\rangle+{1\over2}\|R\|^2
   -{1\over2}\delta\tau.                                \tag{7.1}
\]

For any symmetric polynomial $W$, define

\[
 \tau_W=\Tr W+2G\cdot\Div W-\Div^2W,\qquad
 N_W=\langle x_0,R\rangle+{1\over2}\|R\|^2
     -{1\over2}G^TWG.                                   \tag{7.2}
\]

Then the pointwise identity

\[
 \boxed{F={1\over2}\delta^2W+N_W
             +{1\over2}(\tau_W-\delta\tau)}             \tag{7.3}
\]

holds without an estimate.  Thus trace/forest compatibility
$\delta\tau=\tau_W$ and a favourable Schur remainder $N_W$ are distinct proof
obligations.  Neither follows from static positivity of $W$.

The smallest same-root diagnostic makes the obstruction exact.  Let $G$ now
be scalar standard Gaussian, let $H_k$ be probabilists' Hermite polynomials,
let $v$ be a unit output vector, and for $s=0,1/2,1$ set

\[
 x_s=\epsilon v\{aG+sH_2(G)\},\qquad
 t_s=\epsilon^2(a+2sG)^2.                                \tag{7.4}
\]

The two R-116 visit packets telescope to

\[
\begin{aligned}
 F_{2\rm v}
 &={1\over2}(\|x_1\|^2-\|x_0\|^2)
   -{1\over2}(t_1-t_0)\\
 &=\epsilon^2\left(aH_3+{1\over2}H_4-1\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \E F_{2\rm v}=-\epsilon^2,\qquad \E[GF_{2\rm v}]=0.   \tag{7.6}
\]

The centered part has the unique scalar polynomial preimage

\[
 W_{\rm can}=\epsilon^2(H_2+2aH_1)
 =\epsilon^2\{(G+a)^2-(a^2+1)\},                         \tag{7.7}
\]

\[
 F_{2\rm v}+\epsilon^2={1\over2}\delta^2W_{\rm can}.   \tag{7.8}
\]

The value of (7.7) at $G=-a$ is
$-\epsilon^2(a^2+1)$, while it is positive for sufficiently large $|G|$.
Thus the exact preimage is signed, and the remaining negative mean cannot be a
double divergence.  This is the exact authority for
\texttt{NG-2026-07-28-A13-UNIVERSAL-PSD-RANDOM-W-DOUBLE-DIVERGENCE} and
strengthens the mean-debt diagnosis behind
\texttt{NG-2026-07-28-A13-UNIVERSAL-NONLINEAR-TANGENT-SQUARE-FIRST-NORMALIZER}.
It does not produce a legal A1 counterexample.

\section*{8. Cartan homotopy does not remove the current defect}

R-102 gives, for the isolated complete-$L$ rational current one-form
$\omega$, the exact active/kernel fixture

\[
             \partial_y\omega_x-\partial_x\omega_y
             =-{40\over729}\ne0.                        \tag{8.1}
\]

On a star-shaped chart the exact homotopy split is

\[
 \omega=\dd\Phi+\kappa,\qquad
 \Phi(z)=\int_0^1\omega(tz)\cdot z\dd t,                \tag{8.2}
\]

\[
 \kappa_i(z)=\sum_jz_j\int_0^1t
  (\partial_j\omega_i-\partial_i\omega_j)(tz)\dd t.     \tag{8.3}
\]

Since $\dd\kappa=\dd\omega$, (8.1) implies
$\kappa\not\equiv0$.  The current subowner is therefore not itself a scalar
endpoint Hessian.  R-102's fixture omits the endpoint square, trace, forest,
low, and other rows, so it does not refute a factorisation of a future fully
recombined cluster.  It proves that such a result must use those companions,
not silently discard the Cartan remainder.

\section*{9. Exact production successor}

The new route is now a finite sequence of falsifiable checks on the smallest
legal contraction-closed root cluster.

\begin{enumerate}
\item After the frozen affine determinant absorbs its declared first chaos,
compute the zero and first Wiener chaoses of the complete adapted residual.
Failure of either equality in (6.5) identifies the exact mean or linear debt.
\item If both vanish, construct $W_{\rm can}$ by (6.3), rather than guessing a
positive covariance weight.
\item Match the actual heat, trace, mixed baseline, rational recovery, and
R-063 forest against $\tau_{W_{\rm can}}$ in (7.3), retaining every owner once.
\item Prove fibre invariance, equivalently vertical annihilation, for the
recombined coefficient.  Termwise endpoint descent is not assumed.
\item Prove the spectral-variation and spatial multiplier hypotheses of
Theorem 4.1 after endpoint factorisation, together with a uniform synthesis
bound from the R-093 source Hilbert space.
\item Only then apply (4.6) once over the directed source union and use the
centered determinant cost.  No shellwise or visitwise Young inequality is
permitted.
\end{enumerate}

This replaces the vague request for a positive complete matrix by three sharp
tests: low-chaos cancellation, quotient basicness, and operator variation.

\section*{12. Devil's-advocate}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Objection: R-116 endpoint descent already proves every owner
factors.}  \textbf{UPHELD as false.}  It proves the recombined fixed-environment
endpoint identity.  Mean, covariance, trace, and forest owners may exchange.

\item \textbf{Objection: kernel inclusion follows from checking
$q_K(k)=0$.}  \textbf{UPHELD as false for indefinite forms.}  The mixed
polarisation in (2.6) must vanish for every base point.

\item \textbf{Objection: the operator estimate pays the terminal sextic.}
\textbf{UPHELD as unsupported without (5.1).}  Theorem 4.1 initially sees the
visit square-function sextic.  Opposite visits separate the two quantities.

\item \textbf{Objection: the Young remainder remains linear in $K_0$.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  The three-factor Young step raises both $K_0$ and
$M$ to $3/(1-\kappa)$, equal to $10/3$ at $\kappa=1/10$.

\item \textbf{Objection: exact double-divergence centering makes the coefficient
PSD.}  \textbf{UPHELD as false.}  Formula (7.7) is the unique scalar preimage
and changes sign.

\item \textbf{Objection: the negative Hermite mean disproves the physical A1
cluster.}  \textbf{DISMISSED.}  It is an abstract connected diagnostic.  A
larger physical contraction-closed cluster may supply a cancelling mean owner.

\item \textbf{Objection: Cartan homotopy integrates away the curl.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  Equation (8.3) retains the curvature; its exterior
derivative is the original nonzero curl.

\item \textbf{Objection: a pointwise bounded random coefficient preserves the
$H^2$ estimate.}  \textbf{UPHELD as false.}  Spatial multiplier regularity and
Gaussian $DW,D^2W$ forest compatibility are independent obligations.
\end{enumerate}
