\section*{1. Purpose and scope}
\hypertarget{section-1-result}{ }

R-141 identifies the legal finite-chart projected force of the complete
source, covariance-normal endpoint, and terminal sixth-power action. R-146
then proves that A7 centering fixes the zero-control additive gauge, so the
direct endpoint identity is exactly relative and needs no separate absolute
anchor. R-163 nevertheless retained both an anchor and an origin-force bound
as hypotheses of its general reduced-action semiconvexity criterion.

This note closes the origin-force hypothesis on the direct zero-control
Gaussian chart. It also gives a force-only pullback corollary for uniformly
bounded origin-preserving causal graphs; the full T-050 composition extends
to such a graph only if its source metric and stabilizer decomposition are
separately transported without change.
The mechanism is not pointwise force regularity. It is the orthogonal
projection of a terminal scalar onto the predictable first Gaussian chaos.
For every finite temporally faithful chart,
\[
 \sum_b\E\|g_b(0)\|^2\leq\Var(F_J),                    \tag{1.1}
\]
where \(F_J\) is the complete zero-control endpoint scalar. A7 gives a
cutoff-uniform \(L^2\) bound for the renormalized Class-II energy, and the
A1/A11 point-covariance trace gives a uniform twelfth Gaussian moment for the
sixth-power stabilizer. Hence the right side of (1.1) is uniform in cutoff,
chart length, and independent-Gaussian linear/isometric refinement preserving
the same zero-control law.

Combining this with the exact R-146 zero-control anchor sharpens the R-163
criterion. If, for one \(\rho>0\), the complete direct owner satisfies
\[
 K_{\rm owner}\succeq-\left({10\over11}-\rho\right)G_{\rm src} \tag{1.2}
\]
on every radial segment, then
\[
 \E V_J^{\rm ren}(Z_h)
 \geq-\max\left\{0,{5\over11}-{\rho\over4}\right\}X(h)
       -{3\over20}Y_6(h)-{B_*\over\rho}.               \tag{1.3}
\]
Thus the direct route, corresponding to \(C_\chi=1\), no longer needs
separate anchor or origin-force assumptions. Equation (1.2), including
forward, legal reverse, balanced,
low, trace, current, future, forest, and sextic connections exactly once,
remains unproved in production scope. Therefore T-050, A13, Nelson,
removal, the interacting measure, every phase/PDE question, and Sector A
remain open.

The result is deliberately not asserted for stationary low elimination whose
base minimizer is not zero, for a graph which changes the zero-control law,
or for a nonzero adapted feedback base. In those cases the Malliavin
derivative contains feedback Jacobians and the A7 base variance does not
control the required fixed-law legal force.

\section*{2. Predictable first-Gaussian-chaos projection}
\hypertarget{section-2-projection}{ }

Let \(E_1,\ldots,E_B\) be finite-dimensional real Hilbert spaces and let
\(\xi_b\sim N(0,\Id_{E_b})\) be independent. Put
\[
 \cF_b=\sigma(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_b),\qquad \cF_0=\{\varnothing,\Omega\}.
                                                               \tag{2.1}
\]
For a smooth \(F\in L^2\) with square-integrable Gaussian derivatives, define
\[
 g_b=\E[\nabla_bF\mid\cF_{b-1}].                         \tag{2.2}
\]
The predictable source space is
\[
 \cH_{\rm pred}=\bigoplus_{b=1}^B
 L^2(\Omega,\cF_{b-1};E_b),\qquad
 \|k\|_{\rm pred}^2=\sum_b\E\|k_b\|^2.                \tag{2.3}
\]
Define its adapted first-chaos integral by
\[
 \delta k=\sum_b\ip{k_b}{\xi_b}.                         \tag{2.4}
\]

\subsection*{Theorem 2.1 (sharp predictable first-chaos bound)}

The map \(\delta:\cH_{\rm pred}\to L^2(\Omega)\) is an isometry and
\[
 \boxed{\sum_b\E\|g_b\|^2\leq\Var(F).}                 \tag{2.5}
\]
More precisely, \(\delta g\) is the orthogonal projection of \(F-\E F\)
onto the closed range of \(\delta\). The constant one is sharp.

\textit{Proof.}
For \(b<c\), conditioning on \(\cF_{c-1}\) and then on the fresh centered
block \(\xi_c\) makes the \(b,c\) cross term in
\(\E(\delta k)^2\) vanish. The diagonal terms give
\[
 \E(\delta k)^2=\sum_b\E\|k_b\|^2.                       \tag{2.6}
\]
For predictable \(k_b\), Gaussian integration by parts in the fresh block
\(\xi_b\) has no derivative of \(k_b\), and therefore
\[
 \E[(F-\E F)\delta k]
 =\sum_b\E\ip{\nabla_bF}{k_b}
 =\sum_b\E\ip{g_b}{k_b}
 =\E[(\delta g)(\delta k)].                              \tag{2.7}
\]
Thus \(F-\E F-\delta g\) is orthogonal to the range of \(\delta\).
Pythagoras and (2.6) prove (2.5). Taking \(F=\ip{u}{\xi_1}\) gives equality,
so the constant is sharp. \hfill\(\square\)

Equivalently, if \(M_b=\E[F\mid\cF_b]\), conditional Gaussian integration
by parts and Bessel's inequality in the current block give
\[
 g_b=\E[\xi_b(M_b-M_{b-1})\mid\cF_{b-1}],
\quad
 \|g_b\|^2\leq\E[(M_b-M_{b-1})^2\mid\cF_{b-1}].       \tag{2.8}
\]
Summation uses the orthogonality of the martingale increments. This second
proof makes clear why a discrete chart generally gives an inequality. In a
completed Brownian filtration with the predictable-representation property,
the full Clark--Ocone integral gives equality.

\section*{3. Identification with the R-141 origin force}
\hypertarget{section-3-force}{ }

Fix a finite cutoff \(J\) and a temporally faithful zero-control chart with
deterministic R-104 synthesis operators
\[
 Z_0=\sum_{b=1}^BS_b\xi_b\stackrel{\rm law}=X_J.          \tag{3.1}
\]
The R-104 terminal-stage identity is pathwise:
\(Z_{K,0}=Z_0=S_\pi\xi\). Define the terminal functional and its
zero-control random scalar by
\[
 \mathfrak G_J(\phi)=V_J^{\rm ren}(\phi)+{3\over20}\|\phi\|_6^6,
 \qquad F_J:=\mathfrak G_J(Z_0).                         \tag{3.2}
\]
At finite cutoff this is a smooth finite-dimensional scalar. The R-141
formula and the differentiated R-128 owner identity give
\[
 (g_{J,\pi}^{\rm prod}(0))_b
 =\E[S_b^*D\mathfrak G_J(Z_0)\mid\cF_{b-1}]
 =\E[\nabla_bF_J\mid\cF_{b-1}].                         \tag{3.3}
\]
The derivative of the explicit \(9\|h\|^2/20\) source cost is zero at the
origin. Theorem 2.1 therefore gives the chart-uniform statement
\[
 \boxed{
 \|g_{J,\pi}^{\rm prod}(0)\|_{{\rm src},*}^2
 \leq\Var(F_J).}                                        \tag{3.4}
\]
No field/current pointwise norm and no inverse synthesis occurs in (3.4).
The estimate is independent of the number of source blocks and survives every
independent-Gaussian linear/isometric source refinement that preserves the
zero-control terminal law, because each refined gradient is still an
orthogonal projection of the same centered endpoint scalar.

The force in (3.3) need not vanish. A7 centering states
\(\E V_J^{\rm ren}(X_J)=0\), which is a value normalization. R-153 gives the
strict-past first-variation formula and shows that the registered structure
does not force it to vanish. Thus neither
centering nor spatial stationarity may be substituted for (3.4).

\subsection*{Corollary 3.1 (origin-preserving causal graphs)}

Let \(\chi\) be an admissible reduced or graph chart with
\(\chi(0)=0\), the same zero-control terminal law, and a causal tangent
\(R_0=D\chi(0)\) satisfying
\[
 \|R_0\|_{\cH_{\rm red}\to\cH_{\rm pred}}\leq C_\chi.    \tag{3.5}
\]
Assume that the same constant \(C_\chi\) works uniformly over the declared
family of graphs. Then
\[
 \|D(\cA\circ\chi)(0)\|_{{\rm red},*}^2
 \leq C_\chi^2\Var(F_J).                                \tag{3.6}
\]
This corollary includes a prescribed nonlinear graph only at first order;
its Hessian still contains the registered projected-force connection
\(D\cA(0)D^2\chi(0)\). Changing the graph metric is not a free normalization:
it also changes the source cost and the owner threshold. Therefore (3.6)
alone does not transfer Theorem 5.1 to an arbitrary graph.

\section*{4. Cutoff-uniform endpoint variance and exact anchor}
\hypertarget{section-4-uniformity}{ }

A7 proves
\[
 \cV_J^{\rm ren}:=\int e_{{\rm II},J}^{\rm ren}(x)\dd x
 \longrightarrow\cV^{\rm ren}
 \quad\hbox{in }L^2(\Omega).                             \tag{4.1}
\]
Consequently
\[
 M_7:=\sup_J\|\cV_J^{\rm ren}\|_{L^2}<\infty.           \tag{4.2}
\]
The A1 symbol has order minus four in three dimensions, and A11 records the
corresponding point-covariance trace convergence. On the fixed side-16,
positive-floor, registered sharp-cube/dyadic cutoff sequence put
\[
 T_*:=\sup_{J,x}\E|Z_{K,0}(x)|^2<\infty.                 \tag{4.3}
\]
Here \(K\) is the registered terminal stage: the R-104 zero-control law gives
\(Z_{K,0}\stackrel{\rm law}=X_J\). Thus (4.3) is the same all-cutoff trace
ceiling, not an unproved contraction estimate.

For a centered finite-dimensional real Gaussian \(G\) with covariance
\(\Sigma\), weighted Jensen gives
\[
 \E|G|^{12}\leq11!!\,(\operatorname{Tr}\Sigma)^6.       \tag{4.4}
\]
If \(\operatorname{Tr}\Sigma=0\), then \(G=0\) almost surely. Otherwise,
write \(|G|^2=\sum_a\lambda_ag_a^2\) and apply convexity to the weights
\(\lambda_a/\operatorname{Tr}\Sigma\), followed by
\(\E g^{12}=11!!\). This is the safe sharp constant for the general real
Gaussian class; the circular-complex production subclass may be sharper.
With \(|\mathbb T_L^3|=L^3\), spatial
Cauchy--Schwarz therefore yields
\[
 \left\|\|Z_{K,0}\|_6^6\right\|_{L^2}
 \leq L^3\sqrt{11!!}\,T_*^3=:M_6.                       \tag{4.5}
\]
Minkowski and \(\Var F\leq\E|F|^2\) now give the explicit uniform certificate
\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{J,\pi}\|g_{J,\pi}^{\rm prod}(0)\|_{{\rm src},*}^2
 \leq B_*:=\left(M_7+{3\over20}M_6\right)^2<\infty.}     \tag{4.6}
\]
The theorem uses the analytic all-cutoff quantity \(T_*\) on that registered
cutoff sequence. A finite-ladder numerical trace ceiling may audit
(4.6), but is not substituted for (4.3).

The exact zero-control anchor is simultaneously favourable. A7 centering
and (3.2) give
\[
 \cA_{J,\pi}(0)
 =\E\cV_J^{\rm ren}+{3\over20}\E\|Z_{K,0}\|_6^6
 ={3\over20}\E\|Z_{K,0}\|_6^6\geq0.                    \tag{4.7}
\]
This is the direct-action version of R-146 Theorem 2.1. No separate
cutoff-uniform bound on the representation-dependent constants
\(a_{J,\pi}\) and \(\mathcal T_{J,\pi}(0)\) is inferred.

\section*{5. Anchor-free T-050 semiconvexity reduction}
\hypertarget{section-5-reduction}{ }

Let \(X(h)=\|h\|_{\rm src}^2\) and
\[
 \cA_{J,\pi}(h)
 =\E V_J^{\rm ren}(Z_h)+{9\over20}X(h)+{3\over20}Y_6(h)  \tag{5.1}
\]
be the direct complete action. Assume one uniform \(\rho>0\) and, on every
radial segment,
\[
 D^2\cA_{J,\pi}(th)[h,h]
 \geq\left(-{1\over110}+\rho\right)X(h),
 \qquad 0\leq t\leq1.                                   \tag{5.2}
\]
This is exactly what follows from the explicit \(9G_{\rm src}/10\) source
Hessian and the owner condition (1.2), where the notation is fixed by
\[
 D^2\cA={9\over10}G_{\rm src}+K_{\rm owner}.            \tag{5.2a}
\]
Thus the source Hessian is counted once, not included again inside
\(K_{\rm owner}\).

\subsection*{Theorem 5.1 (one remaining complete-owner gate)}

Under (3.1)--(4.3) and (5.2),
\[
 \boxed{
 \E V_J^{\rm ren}(Z_h)
 \geq-\epsilon_vX(h)-{3\over20}Y_6(h)-{B_*\over\rho},
 \quad
 \epsilon_v=\max\left\{0,{5\over11}-{\rho\over4}\right\}
 <{5\over11}.}                                           \tag{5.3}
\]
Since \(3/20<27/100\), (5.3) is the registered T-050 inequality on every
direct chart for which the complete owner hypothesis holds uniformly.

\textit{Proof.}
Taylor's formula, (4.7), and (3.4) give
\[
 \cA(h)\geq
 -\sqrt{B_*X(h)}
 +{1\over2}\left(-{1\over110}+\rho\right)X(h).          \tag{5.4}
\]
Use Young's inequality with \(\eta=\rho/4\):
\[
 -\sqrt{B_*X}
 \geq-{\rho\over4}X-{B_*\over\rho}.                   \tag{5.5}
\]
Subtracting the two stabilizers in (5.1) leaves the source coefficient
\[
 {9\over20}-{1\over2}\left(-{1\over110}+\rho\right)
 +{\rho\over4}
 ={5\over11}-{\rho\over4}.                              \tag{5.6}
\]
If it is negative, discard the favourable multiple of \(X\). This proves
(5.3). \hfill\(\square\)

Theorem 5.1 does not supply (5.2). It says that for the direct route, a
uniform complete-owner lower bound above
\(-(10/11)G_{\rm src}\) is now the sole new analytic inequality in the R-163
semiconvexity implication. Every block must still be assembled in one
self-adjoint owner: forward and legal reverse are one adjoint pair, while
balanced and low are other blocks, not new reserves.

For a graph, the same calculation gives constant \(C_\chi^2B_*/\rho\) only
after one separately proves that the pulled-back action still has the exact
form (5.1) in one fixed reduced source metric and that (5.2) holds in that
same metric. Corollary 3.1 alone is not that proof.

\section*{6. Scope firewalls and failed shortcuts}
\hypertarget{section-6-firewalls}{ }

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Centering does not make the force zero.}
R-146 subtracts a constant. It removes the absolute anchor but leaves the
first variation unchanged. R-153 records a predictable strict-past
first-variation formula that is not identically forced to vanish.

\item \textbf{Theorem 2.1 is an origin theorem.}
For nonzero feedback \(h\),
\[
 D_{\xi_b}Z_h=S_b+\sum_{c>b}S_cD_bh_c.                   \tag{6.1}
\]
Clark--Ocone then controls the feedback-corrected Malliavin force, not the
R-141 fixed-law legal control force. No uniform Malliavin derivative or
inverse feedback Jacobian is part of the Boue--Dupuis source budget.

\item \textbf{A stationary low base may move the origin.}
If independent-low elimination produces \(\ell_*(0)\neq0\), then the reduced
base endpoint is not the A7 zero-control Gaussian. Equations (3.4), (4.6),
and (4.7) do not automatically transfer. One must instead prove a uniform
shifted endpoint variance and the exact reduced tangent, or use the full
direct action.

\item \textbf{Scalar \(L^2\) convergence is used only at the base.}
A7 does not imply a pointwise or nonzero-feedback force norm. The predictable
projection theorem converts the zero-control scalar variance to the exact
origin dual norm; it makes no claim about
\(\E\ip{G_{\rm end}}{C_JG_{\rm end}}\) away from this projection.

\item \textbf{The result is phase-neutral.}
No BCC, uniform, modulated, disordered, or other vacuum is assumed or selected.
The theorem is a source-action reduction and remains valid if the present
candidate functional is later rejected.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{8. Devil's-advocate and code-discipline review}
\hypertarget{section-8-review}{ }

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Objection: Gaussian integration by parts differentiates the
adapted coefficient. DISMISSED.} In the \(b\)-th integration, \(k_b\) is
\(\cF_{b-1}\)-measurable and therefore independent of the fresh block
\(\xi_b\). Its \(\xi_b\) derivative is zero.

\item \textbf{Objection: the discrete first-chaos norm equals the whole
variance. UPHELD against equality.} It is an orthogonal projection and gives
only \(\leq\) in general. Equality is claimed only for the completed Brownian
predictable-representation setting or a terminal already in the adapted first
chaos.

\item \textbf{Objection: \(11!!\) assumes independent field components.
DISMISSED.} Diagonalize the covariance and use weighted Jensen. Correlated
and singular Gaussian components are included.

\item \textbf{Objection: a finite numerical trace ceiling proves the analytic
all-cutoff bound. UPHELD against that inference.} The theorem uses
\(T_*<\infty\) from trace convergence. Executable finite-ladder values are
regression checks only.

\item \textbf{Objection: (5.3) closes T-050. UPHELD against that claim.}
The complete production owner lower bound (1.2), its random/nonlinear/revisit
scope, and its pathwise/graph compatibility are not proved here.

\item \textbf{Objection: the same source square is spent twice. DISMISSED.}
The origin force is paid once by Young in (5.5); the owner threshold (5.2)
contains the explicit source Hessian once. Forward/legal-reverse/balanced/low
must still be evaluated as one Hessian and receive no second source reserve.
\end{enumerate}
