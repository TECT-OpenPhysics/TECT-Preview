\section*{8. Bookkeeping direct sums are not a $k$-Bessel theorem}

$G_{k\ell}=\langle T_k,T_\ell\rangle$.  A deterministic diagonal estimate
\[

 \le\sum_kq_k|c_k|^2\quad\hbox{for every }c            \tag{8.1}
\]
holds exactly when
\[
 \boxed{G\preceq\operatorname{diag}(q_1,\ldots,q_N).} \tag{8.2}
\]
This is the finite Gram/Loewner criterion.

For coherent unit columns $T_k=u$, $G$ is the all-ones matrix.  If every
$q_k>0$ and $D=\operatorname{diag}(q)$ majorizes $G$, then
$\sum_kq_k^{-1}\le1$.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \sum_kq_k\ge N^2,                                    \tag{8.3}
\]
with equality at $q_k=N$.  The sum of the individual energies is only $N$.
Thus bookkeeping cannot turn coherent physical columns into a constant-one
Bessel family.  With two opposite columns, the physical sum is zero while
the sum of separated squares is two, so unjustified splitting can also
destroy cancellation.
