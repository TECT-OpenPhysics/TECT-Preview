\[
 2^{-2s(m-k)},\qquad
 {1\over3}<\alpha<{1\over2},\quad
 {1\over2}<s<3\alpha-{1\over2}.
 \tag{1.1}

At the production value $\alpha=2/5$, the choice $s=7/12$ has strict squared

\section{The complete mixed Cartan input atom}

\[
 \Phi_{A,j}=P_{\Sigma_{j+1:J}}F_A,

 \tag{2.1}

Target heat convolution is a probability convolution and therefore
contracts derivative sup norms.  The positive floor gives

\[
 \sup_{j,\Sigma}\max_{1\le r\le5}

 \tag{2.2}

Condition on $\mathcal F_{j-1}$.  Write

\[
 w_j=U_{j-1}+g_j,\qquad
 u_{j,r}=w_j+rA^{(j)},\qquad
 A^{(j)}=A^{(\ell)}+\sum_{k=j_0}^ja_k,\qquad a_k=K_kh_k.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

All fields in (2.3) are supported in $Q_j$.  Linearity of the $a$ and
$Da$ slots in R-085 (3.1) gives the exact atomisation

\[
 \mathbb D_jH_{A,j,A^{(j)}}
 =\mathcal M_{A,j,\ell}+\sum_{k=j_0}^j\mathcal M_{A,j,k},
 \tag{2.4}

where the finite-low atom $\mathcal M_{A,j,\ell}$ stays under the accepted

\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal M_{A,j,k}[v]=\int_0^1\{&
 D^2\Phi_{A,j}(u_{j,r})[a_k,v]^TDu_{j,r}\\
 &+D\Phi_{A,j}(u_{j,r})[v]^TDa_k

\end{aligned}
 \tag{2.5}
\]

No channel in (2.5) is discarded.  Predictability says
$\mathbb D_ja_k=0$; it does not say $Da_k=0$.

\section{A reproduced order-two paralinearisation lemma}

Fix $\chi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$, equal to one on
$\{|\xi|_\infty\le3/4\}$ and supported in
$\{|\xi|_\infty\le4/3\}$.  Put

\[
 S_n=\chi(2^{-n}D),\qquad
 \Delta_n=S_n-S_{n-1},\qquad
 T_fg=\sum_nS_{n-2}f\,\Delta_ng.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

These smooth analytic blocks are distinct from, but compatible with, the
sharp physical filtration $Q_j$.  They are used only to decompose the exact
finite-cutoff function.

For a smooth vector- or tensor-valued map $G$, define

\[
 \mathcal P_1[G,u]=T_{DG(u)}u,
 \tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
 \mathcal P_2[G,u]=T_{DG(u)}u+{1\over2}
 \{T_{D^2G(u)}(u\otimes u)-2T_{D^2G(u)[u]}u\}.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

\subsection*{Lemma 3.1 (order-one and order-two remainders)}

If $0<\alpha<1$, $u\in\mathcal C^\alpha$, and the derivatives of $G$ needed
below are bounded, then, componentwise,

\[
 \|\Delta_n\{G(u)-\mathcal P_1[G,u]\}\|_\infty
 \le C_{G,\alpha}2^{-2\alpha n}
 (1+\|u\|_{\mathcal C^\alpha})^2,
 \tag{3.4}
\]

\[
 \|\Delta_n\{G(u)-\mathcal P_2[G,u]\}\|_\infty
 \le C_{G,\alpha}2^{-3\alpha n}
 (1+\|u\|_{\mathcal C^\alpha})^3.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

\emph{Proof.}
Let $K_n$ be the kernel of $\Delta_n$.  Its zero integral lets one write each
block of $G(u)$ as an integral of $G(u(y))-G(u(x))$.  Insert the first-order
Taylor formula around the low-frequency value $S_{n-2}u(x)$.  Subtracting
$S_{n-2}DG(u)\Delta_nu$ leaves two increments of $u$; each is bounded by
$C2^{-\alpha n}\|u\|_{\mathcal C^\alpha}$.  The uniformly integrable smooth
kernels give (3.4).

For the next order, expand once more.  The quadratic Taylor contribution is
exactly

\[
 {1\over2}\{T_{D^2G(u)}(u\otimes u)
              -2T_{D^2G(u)[u]}u\}.
\]

The two terms are both required: they remove the dependence on the chosen
low-frequency base point.  The integral remainder contains three increments
of $u$, hence $2^{-3\alpha n}$.  Summing the finitely overlapping smooth
kernels proves (3.5).  This is the compact Fourier version of the high-order
Taylor expansion in Bailleul--Bernicot, Forum of Mathematics, Sigma 7
(2019), e44, Section 2 and Appendix B. \hfill$\square$

Apply (3.4) to $G=D^2\Phi$ and (3.5) to $G=D\Phi$.  With

\[
 \mathcal R_2(u)=D^2\Phi(u)-\mathcal P_1[D^2\Phi,u],
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_3(u)=D\Phi(u)-\mathcal P_2[D\Phi,u],
 \tag{3.6}
\]

and $Z=1+\|u\|_{\mathcal C^\alpha}$, equations (2.2), (3.4), and (3.5)
give

\[
 \|\Delta_n\mathcal R_2(u)\|_\infty
 \le C_e2^{-2\alpha n}Z^2,\qquad
 \|\Delta_n\mathcal R_3(u)\|_\infty
 \le C_e2^{-3\alpha n}Z^3.
 \tag{3.7}
\]

\section{Exact principal support and the safe separation}

The explicit mask in (3.1) fixes the support constant, rather than hiding it
in $O(1)$.  A $Q_j$-supported input has no $\Delta_n$ block above $j+1$;
its square has no block above $j+2$.  The support of one paraproduct summand
is contained in $(5/3)Q_n$.  Therefore

\[
 \operatorname{supp}\mathcal P_1[D^2\Phi,u]
 \subset {10\over3}Q_j,\qquad
 \operatorname{supp}\mathcal P_2[D\Phi,u]
 \subset {20\over3}Q_j.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

After inserting the remaining factors in (2.5), the first principal channel
has radius at most $(10/3+3)2^j=19\,2^j/3$.  The other two have radius at
most $(20/3+2)2^j=26\,2^j/3$.  Hence every principal contribution lies in
$Q_{j+4}$.  Since $\Pi_m$ is supported where
$|\xi|_\infty>2^{m-1}$,

\[
 \boxed{\Pi_m\mathcal M^{\rm principal}_{A,j,k}=0
        \quad(m\ge j+5).}
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Thus $C_0=5$ is a safe explicit constant for the declared smooth analytic
partition.  The sharper value four belongs to a formal sharp paraproduct and
is not used, because sharp partial-sum kernels are not uniformly $L^1$.
Conditional OU averaging and target heat act in probability and target
variables, respectively, so both preserve (4.2).

There is no algebraic cancellation claim here.  On the two-dimensional slice
$\Phi(z)=q(z)z$, $q=(x^2-y^2)/(x^2+y^2+e)$, the coefficient one-form has

\[
 d(\Phi\cdot dz)={4xy\over x^2+y^2+e}\,dx\wedge dy.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

For $w=(1,1)$, $a=\varepsilon\sin(Kx)e_1$, and
$v=\cos(Jx)e_2$, the first-order mixed principal is

\[
 -{\varepsilon\over2+e}\{(K+J)\cos((J-K)x)
 +(K-J)\cos((J+K)x)\}.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

It is nonzero, but has only the sum and difference carriers and is therefore
killed in the far region (4.2).

\section{Hilbert--Schmidt decay of the complete remainder}

Let $(v_h)_h$ be an orthonormal fresh-root Cameron--Martin basis.  The
three-dimensional $|q|^{-4}$ shell covariance gives the exact scale powers

\[
 \sup_x\sum_h|v_h(x)|^2\le\kappa_0\,2^{-j},\qquad
 \sup_x\sum_h|Dv_h(x)|^2\le\kappa_1\,2^j.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Band limitation also gives

\[
 \|Du_{j,r}\|_\infty
 \le C_\alpha2^{(1-\alpha)j}Z_{j,r},\qquad
 Z_{j,r}=1+\|u_{j,r}\|_{\mathcal C^\alpha}.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

When $m\ge j+5$, Fourier support forces the output frequency into
$\mathcal R_2$ or $\mathcal R_3$.  Square (3.7), sum the fresh roots
pointwise using (5.1), and then integrate the control factor in $L^2$.  This
gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_h\|\Pi_m[\mathcal R_2(u)[a_k,v_h]^TDu]\|_2^2
 &\le C_e2^{-4\alpha m}2^{(1-2\alpha)j}
 Z^6\|a_k\|_2^2,\\
 \sum_h\|\Pi_m[\mathcal R_3(u)[v_h]^TDa_k]\|_2^2
 &\le C_e2^{-6\alpha m}2^{-j}
 Z^6\|Da_k\|_2^2,\\
 \sum_h\|\Pi_m[\mathcal R_3(u)[a_k]^TDv_h]\|_2^2
 &\le C_e2^{-6\alpha m}2^j
 Z^6\|a_k\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{5.3--5.5}
\]

There is no $2^{3k}$ Bernstein loss in (5.3): after the pointwise root sum,
$a_k$ remains in $L^2$.  Cross-channel orthogonality is not assumed; the
elementary three-term square inequality pays a fixed factor.

Put $\beta=6\alpha-1$ and $d=m-j$.  The two critical right-hand sides become

\[
 C_e2^{-\beta j}2^{-4\alpha d}Z^6\|a_k\|_2^2,\qquad
 C_e2^{-\beta j}2^{-6\alpha d}Z^6\|a_k\|_2^2.
 \tag{5.6}
\]

The middle channel is stronger.  Conditional OU contraction gives

\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-2\tau}
 \mathbb E\|P_\tau^{(j)}X\|^2d\tau
 \le {1\over2}\mathbb E\|X\|^2.
 \tag{5.7}
\]

Jensen in $r$ and (5.3)--(5.7) now prove the spatial atom theorem.

\subsection*{Theorem 5.1 (the spatial half of R-085 (4.10))}

Assume $1/3<\alpha<1/2$ and $1/2<s<3\alpha-1/2$.  The upper
restriction on $\alpha$ implies
$3\alpha-1/2<2\alpha$, so both decay conditions
$\beta-2s>0$ and $4\alpha-2s>0$ hold.  Define

\[
 W_k=\sup_{j\ge k}\sum_A\mathbb E\int_0^1
 Z_{j,r}^6(\|a_k\|_2^2+\|Da_k\|_2^2)\,dr,
 \tag{5.8}
\]

\[
 q_k^{\rm mod}=C_e2^{-(6\alpha-1)k}W_k.
 \tag{5.9}
\]

Then for $m\ge j+5$,

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_A\int_0^\infty e^{-2\tau}
 \mathbb E\|\Pi_mP_\tau^{(j)}
 \mathcal M_{A,j,k}\|_{\rm HS}^2d\tau
 \le2^{-2s(m-k)}q_k^{\rm mod}.}
 \tag{5.10}
\]

Indeed, after writing $j=k+r$, the excess powers are

\[
 (\beta-2s)r+(4\alpha-2s)d\ge0.
 \tag{5.11}
\]

At $\alpha=2/5$ and $s=7/12$,

\[
 \beta={7\over5},\qquad
 \beta-2s={7\over30},\qquad
 4\alpha-2s={13\over30},
 \tag{5.12}

so the R-085 nonorthogonal Schur threshold is crossed with strict room.

\section{The exact Cartan one-use obstruction}

The Cameron--Martin multiplier gives

\[
 \|a_k\|_2^2\lesssim2^{-4k}\|h_k\|_2^2,\qquad
 \|Da_k\|_2^2\lesssim2^{-2k}\|h_k\|_2^2.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

Using (6.1) in (5.9) and extracting the translated model norm would require

\[
 \sum_k2^{-(\beta+1)k}
 \sup_{j\ge k}\mathbb E[Z_j^6\|h_k\|_2^2]
 \lesssim1+\mathbb E[X^{1/2}Y^{1/2}].
 \tag{6.2}
\]

This implication is false as a general energy argument.  Let an event $E$
have probability $p=N^{-6}$ and take one predictable physical mode

\[
 h=N^3\mathbf1_Ee_N,\qquad a=K_Nh=N\mathbf1_Ee_N.
 \tag{6.3}
\]

Then $\mathbb EX\asymp\mathbb EY\asymp\mathbb E\sqrt{XY}\asymp1$, while
$Z\asymp N^{1+\alpha}$ on $E$.  Since $\beta+1=6\alpha$, the corresponding
extracted summand in (6.2) is of order $N^6=p^{-1}$.

This registers

\[
 \texttt{NG-2026-07-25-A13-PATHWISE-TRANSLATED-MODEL-NORM-EXTRACTION}.
 \tag{6.4}
\]

It is a proof-method no-go, not a counterexample to (5.10) or to the directly
averaged complete atom.  The remaining Cartan lemma must keep expectation
inside the three exact remainders and prove

\[
 \sum_k2^kq_k\lesssim1+\mathbb E[X^{1/2}Y^{1/2}]
 \tag{6.5}
\]

without extracting an unbounded translated model multiplier.  Thus R-085
(4.11), controlled Cartan CFAR, and REG remain open.

\section{Exact rational eta-completion and trace debt}

Use the R-086 notation

\[
 \mathcal P={1\over2}L:Q+G^TLc+{1\over2}c^TB_1c,\qquad
 Q=G\otimes G-\Gamma.
 \tag{7.1}
\]

For $\eta>0$, put

\[
 A_\eta=B_1+2\eta I,\qquad
 M_\eta=L-LA_\eta^{-1}L.
 \tag{7.2}
\]

Since $B_1\succeq0$, $A_\eta\succ0$ even on the endpoint kernel.  Completing
the $c$ square gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal P+\eta|c|^2={}&{1\over2}
 (c+A_\eta^{-1}LG)^TA_\eta(c+A_\eta^{-1}LG)\\
 &+{1\over2}M_\eta:Q
 -{1\over2}\operatorname{Tr}(LA_\eta^{-1}L\Gamma).
\end{aligned}}
 \tag{7.3}
\]

Expanding the square proves (7.3); the two $\Gamma$ contractions recombine
to $-L:\Gamma/2$.  For $\Gamma\succeq0$ the trace debt

\[
 \mathfrak D_\eta={1\over2}\operatorname{Tr}
 (LA_\eta^{-1}L\Gamma)
 \tag{7.4}
\]

is nonnegative, and congruence of
$A_\eta^{-1}\preceq(2\eta)^{-1}I$ gives

\[
 0\le\mathfrak D_\eta
 \le {1\over4\eta}\operatorname{Tr}(L^2\Gamma).
 \tag{7.5}
\]

This is a useful kernel-free normal form but not a form bound.  In general
$M_\eta$ is indefinite.  If $L=O(a^3)$ at fixed $\eta$, then

\[
 M_\eta=L+O_\eta(a^6),
 \tag{7.6}
\]

so the leading coefficient-dominant $L:Q$ resonance survives.  The old
$O(\sigma^{-2})$ inverse-heat loss has been traded for an $O(\eta^{-1})$
trace debt.  No cutoff-uniform bound on that debt or on the complete rational
packet is asserted.

\section{Fixed-cutoff bounded cylindrical variational core}

Fix a cutoff $J$.  Let $B$ be a finite-dimensional Brownian motion on its
completed canonical filtration, let $H_J$ be finite-dimensional, and let

\[
 X_J=\int_0^TK_J(t)\,dB_t,\qquad
 T_Jv=\int_0^TK_J(t)v_t\,dt,\qquad K_J\in L^2_t.
 \tag{8.1}
\]

Assume $G_J:H_J\to\mathbb R$ is continuous, bounded below, and, for every
$R<\infty$, obeys the local polynomial envelope

\[
 \sup_{\|y\|\le R}|G_J(x+y)|
 \le C_{J,R}(1+\|x\|^{r_J}).
 \tag{8.2}
\]

Let $\mathcal A$ be the finite-energy progressive controls,
$\mathcal A_b$ the bounded progressive controls, and $\mathcal A_{cs}$ the
bounded smooth cylindrical simple progressive controls.  Thus elements of
$\mathcal A_{cs}$ have the form

\[
 v_t=\sum_{\ell=1}^N
 \Psi_\ell(B_{r_{\ell,1}},\ldots,B_{r_{\ell,m_\ell}})
 \mathbf1_{(t_{\ell-1},t_\ell]}(t),\qquad
 r_{\ell,i}\le t_{\ell-1},
 \tag{8.3}
\]

with bounded smooth $\Psi_\ell$.

\subsection*{Theorem 8.1 (fixed-cutoff variational CORE)}

For every $q>0$,

\[
\boxed{
 -{1\over q}\log\mathbb E e^{-qG_J(X_J)}
 =\inf_{v\in\mathcal A}\mathbb E\left[
 G_J(X_J+T_Jv)+{1\over2q}\int_0^T|v_t|^2dt\right]}
 \tag{8.4}
\]

and the infimum is unchanged when $\mathcal A$ is replaced by
$\mathcal A_b$ or $\mathcal A_{cs}$.

\emph{Proof.}
Put $F=-qG_J(X_J)$.  If $m_J=\inf G_J$, then
$F\le-qm_J$ and $e^F$ is integrable.  The polynomial envelope and Gaussian
moments give $\mathbb EF^-<\infty$.  Hariya--Watanabe, Theorem 1.1 and
Corollary 2.1, therefore give the Boue--Dupuis identity and permit restriction
to bounded drifts.  This uses only the fixed-$J$ lower bound and local
polynomial envelope; no Nelson estimate is assumed.

For $v\in\mathcal A_b$, bounded simple progressive approximants $v_n$ can be
chosen with the same deterministic bound and
$v_n\to v$ in $L^2(\Omega\times[0,T])$.  Hence

\[
 \|T_J(v_n-v)\|_{L^2(\Omega;H_J)}
 \le\|K_J\|_{L^2_t}\|v_n-v\|_{L^2_{\Omega,t}},
 \tag{8.5}
\]

and the quadratic costs converge.  Also
$\|T_Jv_n\|\le M\int_0^T\|K_J(t)\|_{\rm op}dt$.  Continuity, convergence in
probability, and the common Gaussian-polynomial dominator from (8.2) imply
$G_J(X_J+T_Jv_n)\to G_J(X_J+T_Jv)$ in $L^1$.

Finally approximate each bounded past-measurable simple coefficient in
$L^2$ by bounded smooth functions of finitely many past Brownian evaluations.
Repeating (8.5) proves equality with the cylindrical-simple infimum.
Independent auxiliary randomisation cannot lower the value: condition on
the complete independent seed, apply the canonical lower bound sectionwise,
and integrate.  Correlated future information or auxiliary randomness in
the payoff is not covered. \hfill$\square$

At the A13 values

\[
 q={10\over9},\qquad {1\over2q}={9\over20},\qquad
 q-{11\over10}={1\over90},\qquad
 {1\over2(11/10)}-{9\over20}={1\over220}.
 \tag{8.6}
\]

Thus a cutoff-, partition-, and revisit-uniform OVERLAP estimate
$I_{J,10/9}(v)\ge-C$ for $v\in\mathcal A_{cs}$ would imply

\[
 \mathbb E e^{-(10/9)G_J}\le e^{(10/9)C}.
 \tag{8.7}
\]

The theorem bypasses a direct all-control \emph{packetwise} extension.  It
does not prove OVERLAP, termwise packet convergence, graph recovery for every
finite-energy control, or the Nelson lower bound itself.
