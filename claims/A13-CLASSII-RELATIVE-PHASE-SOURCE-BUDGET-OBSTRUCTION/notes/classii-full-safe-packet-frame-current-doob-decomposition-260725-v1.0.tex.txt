\section{Purpose and scope}

R-078 defined the remaining coefficient-dominant object without assigning
individual lower Wick chaoses to arbitrary Bony channels.  Its definition is
canonical: subtract the already paid nonresonant and payload-comparable
orientations from the complete translated endpoint, then apply the causal
projection once to the whole residual.  R-078 proved a Doob bracket identity
for factorised bilinear components, but deliberately left the exact
full-packet reconstruction open.

This note supplies that reconstruction.  The complete translated current is
placed in one Hilbert space carrying the spatial integral, generator sum,
direction sum, and the positive $Q_{\rm II}$ metric.  Martingale
orthogonality is then applied to the full terminal current, not term by term
to its Taylor forest.  A root-first insertion of the partial-control current
produces three exact blocks: a low conditional-current term, a current-control
injection term, and a future-feedback innovation term.  Each shell retains
its positive polarization square and its exact Wick covariance trace.  The
safe-packet identity then subtracts the two already paid orientations at
both endpoints.  Consequently both R-073 first variations, the terminal
square, coefficient curvature, the complete R-063 forest, the R-066/R-070
trace, and the complete low safe endpoint remain exactly once.

The reconstruction is an algebraic advance, not the desired lower bound.
Neither of the two shell blocks has a generic sign.  The low term contains a
conditional mean of the terminal future-controlled current and is not merely
the R-066 finite-low boundary.  The future innovation has both a coefficient
channel and a derivative-feedback channel.  A sharp one-root example remains
negative after the positive current square and Wick trace are put back.

Two useful positive facts do survive.  First, the controlled shells obey a
genuinely spatially weighted Cameron--Martin square-function estimate with
one use of the total control energy.  Second, the uncontrolled or predictable
translated base current has an exact same-point heat-semigroup projection,
because future field values and derivatives are independent under the common
even covariance.  These facts identify the production-specific near/far
continuation, but do not prove its far-tail estimate.

All exact identities are at finite cutoff, at a fixed positive production
floor, with deterministic $Q_{\rm II}$ and deterministic covariance
increments.  The controls are strict-past predictable, mutually orthogonal,
one-shot whole-shell controls.  This note does not prove the production
weighted packet lower bound, controlled-shell one-use for the energy, the
$q=10/9$ Nelson moment, a measure theorem, cutoff or floor removal, infinite
volume, or Sector-A closure.  The tier remains T4.

\section{The complete current endpoint}

Let $\ell=j_0-1$, $A^*=A^{(J)}$, and
$A^0=A^{(\ell)}=A_{<j_0}$.  For a partial control $H$, define the complete
$Q_{\rm II}$-weighted current
\[
 \cJ(H)_{\alpha i}(x)
 =Q_{\rm II}^{1/2}M_\alpha(U+H)^T
       \{D_iU+D_iH\}.                                      \tag{2.1}
\]
All generator and direction coordinates and the spatial $L^2$ integral are
part of one real Hilbert space $\mathcal H$.  Put
\[
 \cB(H)=B(U+H),                                             \tag{2.2}
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal W_J(H)
 ={1\over2}\|\cJ(H)\|_{\mathcal H}^2
 -{1\over2}\sum_i\int \cB(H):\Gamma_{J,i}\dd x.          \tag{2.3}
\]
The R-070 terminal identity gives, exactly,
\[
 \Delta V_J^{\rm ren}(U;H)=\mathcal W_J(H)-\mathcal W_J(0).
                                                                  \tag{2.4}
\]
Thus (2.3) is not a model replacement: it is the complete covariance-normal
translated current endpoint already reached by R-066, R-069, and R-070.

Write
\[
 \cJ_* =\cJ(A^*),\qquad \cJ_0=\cJ(A^0),\qquad
 \cJ_j=\cJ(A^{(j)}),                                       \tag{2.5}
\]
and use the same subscripts for $\cB$.  Let
\[
 P_jX=\E[X\mid\cF_j],\qquad d_j=P_j-P_{j-1}.               \tag{2.6}
\]
At finite cutoff $P_J$ is the identity on every terminal current.  Define
the root-first increments
\[
 f_j=d_j(\cJ_j-\cJ_0),\qquad
 i_j=d_j(\cJ_*-\cJ_j).                                     \tag{2.7}
\]
They obey
\[
 f_j+i_j=d_j(\cJ_*-\cJ_0),\qquad
 d_j\cJ_j=d_j\cJ_0+f_j.                                   \tag{2.8}
\]
The sequence $f_j$ is not the martingale-difference sequence of one fixed
terminal variable: its middle current $\cJ_j$ changes with $j$.  This point

\section{Exact root-first Doob decomposition}

Assume the deterministic covariance split
\[
 \Gamma_J=\Gamma_\ell+
       \sum_{j=j_0}^{J}\Delta\Gamma_j.                     \tag{3.1}
\]
Define the low conditional-current block
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathfrak L_\ell={}&{1\over2}\E\{
  \|P_\ell\cJ_*\|_{\mathcal H}^2
  -\|P_\ell\cJ_0\|_{\mathcal H}^2\}\;\\
 &-{1\over2}\E\sum_i\int
  (\cB_*-\cB_0):\Gamma_{\ell,i}\dd x .
 \end{split}                                               \tag{3.2}
\]
For every active root define the injected-current block
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathfrak F_j={}&\E\left\{
  \ip{d_j\cJ_0}{f_j}+{1\over2}\|f_j\|_{\mathcal H}^2
  \right\}\\
 &-{1\over2}\E\sum_i\int
  (\cB_j-\cB_0):\Delta\Gamma_{j,i}\dd x,
 \end{split}                                               \tag{3.3}
\]
and the future-feedback innovation block
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathfrak I_j={}&\E\left\{
  \ip{d_j\cJ_j}{i_j}+{1\over2}\|i_j\|_{\mathcal H}^2
  \right\}\\
 &-{1\over2}\E\sum_i\int
  (\cB_*-\cB_j):\Delta\Gamma_{j,i}\dd x.
 \end{split}                                               \tag{3.4}
\]

\paragraph{Theorem 3.1 (full-current identity).}
Under the hypotheses above,
\[
 \boxed{
 \E\{\mathcal W_J(A^*)-\mathcal W_J(A^0)\}
 =\mathfrak L_\ell+
  \sum_{j=j_0}^{J}(\mathfrak F_j+\mathfrak I_j).}          \tag{3.5}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Hilbert martingale orthogonality gives, for $H=*$ and $H=0$,
\[
 \E\|\cJ_H\|_{\mathcal H}^2
 =\E\|P_\ell\cJ_H\|_{\mathcal H}^2
  +\sum_{j=j_0}^{J}\E\|d_j\cJ_H\|_{\mathcal H}^2.       \tag{3.6}
\]
The difference of the $j$th squares is
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\ip{d_j\cJ_0}{f_j+i_j}+{1\over2}\|f_j+i_j\|^2\\
 &\quad=\ip{d_j\cJ_0}{f_j}+{1\over2}\|f_j\|^2
 +\ip{d_j\cJ_j}{i_j}+{1\over2}\|i_j\|^2.
 \end{split}                                               \tag{3.7}
\]
In particular, the easy-to-miss cross term $\ip{f_j}{i_j}$ is contained in
$\ip{d_j\cJ_j}{i_j}$.  The coefficient difference has the simultaneous
split
\[
 \cB_*-\cB_0=(\cB_j-\cB_0)+(\cB_*-\cB_j).                \tag{3.8}
\]
Insert (3.7) and (3.8) into (3.6), use (3.1), and compare with (2.3).
This proves (3.5).  No inequality or termwise forest assignment has entered.
\hfill$\square$

The future block also has the exact polarization
\[
 \ip{d_j\cJ_j}{i_j}+{1\over2}\|i_j\|^2
 ={1\over2}\{\|d_j\cJ_*\|^2-\|d_j\cJ_j\|^2\}.           \tag{3.9}
\]
Equation (3.9) is signless after the trace in (3.4) is restored.  In
particular, the trace is not generally the negative of the second square.

 \E\mathfrak B_{J,{\rm safe}}^>

\section{Two exact positive tools}

\subsection{Spatially weighted Cameron--Martin square function}

Write each one-shot control shell as $a_k=K_kh_k$, where $a_k$ is
$\cF_{k-1}$-measurable, supported in shell $k$, and
\[
 \|K_k\|_{\mathcal H_k\to L^2}\lesssim 2^{-2k}.           \tag{6.1}
\]
Then $d_ja_k=0$ for $j\geq k$.  Hilbert martingale orthogonality gives
\[
 \sum_{j<k}\E\|d_ja_k\|_2^2\leq\E\|a_k\|_2^2.          \tag{6.2}
\]
Multiplying by $2^{4k}$, using (6.1), and summing the mutually orthogonal
spatial shells proves
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_k\sum_{j<k}2^{4k}\E\|d_ja_k\|_2^2
 \lesssim\E\sum_k\|h_k\|_{\mathcal H_k}^2.}              \tag{6.3}
\]
Equivalently, with the standard shell norm,
\[
 \sum_j\E\|d_jA\|_{H^2}^2\lesssim \E X.                 \tag{6.4}
\]
This is a genuine weighted one-use result for the control coordinate.  It is
not yet a bound for the current bracket in (3.3)--(3.4), because the other
factor has no proved compensating negative spatial regularity.

\subsection{Predictable base-current heat projection}

Let $U=U_j+V_{>j}$ and $DU=DU_j+DV_{>j}$.  For a common real-even
covariance,
\[
 {\rm Cov}\{V_{>j}(x),D_iV_{>j}(x)\}=0.                  \tag{6.5}
\]
Joint Gaussianity makes the two variables independent at each $x$.  If
\[
 \overline M_j=P_{\Sigma_{j+1:J}}M,                       \tag{6.6}
\]
then the base current satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 P_j\{M(U)^TD_iU\}
 =\overline M_j(U_j)^TD_iU_j.}                            \tag{6.7}
\]
The same formula holds after an $\cF_{j-1}$-predictable translation, with
$U_j,DU_j$ translated by that control.  Subtracting the $j-1$ formula gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 d_j\cJ_i={}&\overline M_j(U_j)^Td_{j,i}\\
 &+\{\overline M_j(U_j)-\overline M_j(U_{j-1})\}^TD_iU_{j-1}\\
 &+\{\overline M_j-\overline M_{j-1}\}(U_{j-1})^TD_iU_{j-1}.
 \end{split}                                               \tag{6.8}
\]
These are respectively the derivative injection, centered value innovation,
and heat compensator.  Formula (6.7) does not apply to $\cJ_*$ when the
terminal future feedback itself depends on root $j$.

\section{Why the generic weighted shortcuts fail}

\subsection{Square-function Cauchy is exponent-supercritical}

R-078 proves, for a raw-current order $s$,
\[
 \|A^2DA\|_{B^s_{1,1}}
 \lesssim X^{(1+s)/4}Y^{(5-s)/12}.                        \tag{7.1}
\]
Spending (6.4) by Cauchy contributes $X^{1/2}$.  The resulting powers are
\[
 a={3+s\over4},\qquad b={5-s\over12},\qquad
 c=1-a-b=-{1+s\over6}.                                   \tag{7.2}
\]
At $s=3/5$ this is
\[
 X^{9/10}Y^{11/30},\qquad c=-{4\over15}.                  \tag{7.3}
\]
There is no positive Young slack and hence no finite random Young remainder.
The exact square, trace, forest, and paid subtraction must be used before
Cauchy; (6.3) alone cannot close the packet.

\subsection{Expected budgets do not imply predictable Carleson control}

Fix a shell frequency $N$ and an event $E\in\cF_j$ of probability
$p=N^{-6}$.  On one normalized carrier set
\[
 h_{j+1}=p^{-1/2}{\bf1}_E e_N,\qquad
 a_{j+1}=N^{-2}h_{j+1}.                                   \tag{7.4}
\]
Then, up to the fixed carrier normalization,
\[
 \E\|h_{j+1}\|^2=1,\qquad
 \E\|a_{j+1}\|_6^6
 =p\{N^{-2}p^{-1/2}\}^6=1.                               \tag{7.5}
\]
But on $E$ the conditional future energy is
\[
 \E[\|h_{j+1}\|^2\mid\cF_j]=p^{-1}=N^6.                \tag{7.6}
\]
Thus the two expected Bou\'e--Dupuis budgets do not control an essential-sup
martingale BMO or predictable Carleson norm.

\subsection{An unweighted bracket has no generic spatial gain}

On a one-step symmetric tree let $\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}$ be the centered
Rademacher root, $\E\varepsilon=0$, and take
\[
 H_N=\varepsilon\cos(Nx),\qquad
 Y_N=-\varepsilon N^s\cos(Nx).                            \tag{7.7}
\]
The $L^2$ square function of $H_N$ and the homogeneous $H^{-s}$ norm of $Y_N$ stay
bounded, whereas
\[
 \E\int H_NY_N\dd x=-{1\over2}\varepsilon^2N^s.         \tag{7.8}
\]
Therefore the unweighted R-078 Doob identity cannot imply the missing
spatially weighted bracket estimate by an abstract embedding.

\section{Adapted Wick--carr\'e-du-champ no-go}

The positive innovation square also has no universal sign once its exact
trace is restored.  Let $\xi\sim N(0,1)$,
$H_2(\xi)=\xi^2-1$, $Q=\Gamma=1$, and use the scalar frame $m(a)=1+a$.
Allow a later value control $A=A(\xi)$ and set its spatial derivative control
to zero.  After subtracting the base term and all first variations, the exact
remainder is
\[
 \cR(A)={1\over2}A^2H_2
 ={1\over2}(A\xi)^2-{1\over2}A^2.                         \tag{8.1}
\]
The two terms on the right are exactly the terminal current square and Wick
trace.

For $A_\alpha=\alpha(\xi^2-4)=\alpha(H_2-3)$, probabilists' Hermites give
\[
 A_\alpha^2=\alpha^2(H_4-2H_2+11),                       \tag{8.2}
\]
\[
 A_\alpha^2H_2
 =\alpha^2(H_6+6H_4+15H_2-4).                            \tag{8.3}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \E\cR(A_\alpha)=-2\alpha^2,                            \tag{8.4}
\]
while
\[
 {1\over2}\E(A_\alpha\xi)^2={7\over2}\alpha^2,\qquad
 -{1\over2}\E A_\alpha^2=-{11\over2}\alpha^2.          \tag{8.5}
\]
Moreover
\[
 dA_\alpha=A_\alpha-\E A_\alpha=\alpha H_2,\qquad
 \E|dA_\alpha|^2=2\alpha^2,                             \tag{8.6}
\]
so the natural coefficient carr\'e-du-champ occurs with exactly the wrong
sign:
\[
 \boxed{\E\cR(A_\alpha)=-\E|dA_\alpha|^2.}               \tag{8.7}
\]
This is not an omitted-chaos artefact: (8.3) displays the complete finite
forest including its negative zero chaos.

There is also a bounded positive-frame fixture.  For $a>0$ take
$A=a\exp(-\xi^2)$, so $m(A)\geq1$.  Direct Gaussian integration gives
\[
 {1\over2}\E(A\xi)^2={a^2\over10\sqrt5},\qquad
 -{1\over2}\E A^2=-{a^2\over2\sqrt5},                   \tag{8.8}
\]
and hence
\[
 \E\cR(A)=-{2a^2\over5\sqrt5}<0.                        \tag{8.9}
\]
A diagonal two-component matrix lift leaves the first channel unchanged.
Thus positive $Q$, a matrix-square coefficient, a bounded smooth adapted
control, and the positive current square do not yield generic positivity.

This is an abstract adapted root counterexample, not a production
$\mathfrak B^>$ counterexample.  It omits the production frequency geometry,
harmonic enhancement, exact paid subtraction, and the remaining signed
companions.  It forbids only a universal rootwise positivity or nonnegative
carr\'e-du-champ proof.

\section{Conditional positive-gain ledger and successor}

The failed routes identify a quantitative target.  Suppose that, after the
exact square--trace--forest reassembly, the remaining production adapted
coefficient obeys for some $\gamma>0$
\[
 \|\mathbb K_\gamma(A)\|_{H^{-3/5+\gamma}}
 \leq R_\gamma\|A\|_{H^{2/5}},\qquad
 R_\gamma\in\bigcap_{p<\infty}L^p.                        \tag{9.1}
\]
This is an assumption, not a theorem of R-063 for adapted controls.  The
standard interpolation gives
\[
 \|A\|_{H^{2/5}}\lesssim X^{1/10}Y^{2/15}.                \tag{9.2}
\]
Apply the R-078 payload estimate at
$\beta=3/5-\gamma$.  The resulting term has powers
\[
 R_\gamma X^{1/2-\gamma/4}Y^{1/2+\gamma/12}.              \tag{9.3}
\]
The exact Young ledger is
\[
 a={1\over2}-{\gamma\over4},\qquad
 b={1\over2}+{\gamma\over12},\qquad
 c={\gamma\over6},\qquad p_R={6\over\gamma}.             \tag{9.4}
\]
For the R-063-inspired target $\gamma=3/10$,
\[
 (a,b,c)=\left({17\over40},{21\over40},{1\over20}\right),
 \qquad p_R=20,                                           \tag{9.5}
\]
with Young losses $\eta^{-17/2}\zeta^{-21/2}$.  At
$\gamma=0$ the route is exactly critical.  Thus any successful absolute
near-block theorem must produce a genuine positive gain, or instead prove a
direct signed production Schur inequality.

The safest continuation is now precise.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Split probability root $j$ from spatial output shell $m$ only after
the exact identity (4.4).
\item In the far region $m\geq j+C$, prove a production current-tail estimate
from the heat formula (6.7)--(6.8), and combine it with (6.3).  No such tail
is claimed here.
\item In the near region $|m-j|\leq C$, retain
$\mathfrak F_j+\mathfrak I_j$ with both positive squares, both traces, the
value-innovation and heat compensator, the complete R-063 forest, and the
paid subtraction.  Prove either (9.1) or a direct signed Schur bound.
\item Bound the conditional low current $\mathfrak L_\ell$ separately and
then add the complete low endpoint (4.6).  Neither may be renamed as the
other.
\end{enumerate}
