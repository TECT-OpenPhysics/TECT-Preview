\section*{1. Purpose and scope: result and boundary}
\hypertarget{section-1-purpose}{ }

R-150 installed the actual A1 antipodal field and current synthesis but left
the relative final secant and earlier-root feedback open.  The subsequent
matrix route tried to manufacture separate forest, balanced, and low blocks.
That is unnecessary and unsafe: after the terminal scalar owner is fixed,
those objects are alternate expansions or estimates of the same owner, not
new orthogonal reserves.  The correct finite-cylinder matrix is obtained by
differentiating the complete A7 endpoint once.

This note carries out that direct differentiation for the full six-real,
both-sign, linear adapted chart
\[
 h_2=tH\xi_1,\qquad H\in\mathbb R^{12\times12},       \tag{1.1}
\]
where $\xi_1$ is the simultaneous $\{\pm p\}$ root and $h_2$ shifts the
simultaneous $\{\pm2p\}$ root.  Every sign-to-sign and internal-component
coupling is retained, so the Hessian has 144 real coordinates.  Here $p$ is
an admissible nonzero dual-lattice momentum and the finite cutoff retains all
four modes $\pm p,\pm2p$.

The exact endpoint Hessian is reduced to one covariance response tensor.  The
global R-130 pointwise coefficient-Hessian envelope then proves, for every
such retained pair and every stationary common-even Gaussian background,
\[
 \cR''(0)[H,H]>-{4\over5}\|H\|_{\HS}^2,\qquad H\ne0. \tag{1.2}
\]
The Cameron--Martin term $(9/20)\E\|h_2\|^2$ has Hessian
$9\|H\|_{\HS}^2/10$, while the terminal sixth-power Hessian is nonnegative.
Consequently the augmented local Hessian has the strict certified gap
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \cA''(0)[H,H]>{1\over10}\|H\|_{\HS}^2,\qquad H\ne0.
 \quad}                                               \tag{1.3}
\]

The radial estimate is uniform for every $x=|p|^2\ge0$; the torus theorem
applies only to admissible nonzero dual-lattice $p$ for which both roots are
retained.  It is not the nonlinear, nonzero-past, multi-root aggregation
required by T-050.  It neither identifies the historical complete-low object
nor selects a spatial phase or a PDE.

\section*{3. One scalar endpoint and its exact Hessian}
\hypertarget{section-3-endpoint}{ }

Let $B(w)=C_6(w)^*C_6(w)\succeq0$ be the exact six-row A7 coefficient
matrix and let $Q_0$ be the primitive derivative covariance used in the
normal ordering.  The baseline-subtracted endpoint is
\[
 \cR(t)={1\over2}\int
 \E\left\{\sum_iV_{t,i}^*B(W_t)V_{t,i}
              -\Tr[B(W_t)Q_0]\right\}\,dx.          \tag{3.1}
\]
For a centered joint Gaussian pair, two Gaussian integrations by parts give
the exact identity
\[
\begin{split}
2\cR(t)=\int\{&\Tr[(Q_t-Q_0)\,\overline B(C_t)]\\
 &+\sum_i(K_{t,i})_{am}(K_{t,i})_{bn}\,
       \overline T_{abmn}(C_t)\}\,dx,
\end{split}                                        \tag{3.2}
\]
where
\[
 \overline B(C)=\E_C B(W),\qquad
 \overline T_{abmn}(C)=\E_C\partial_{ab}B_{mn}(W).  \tag{3.3}
\]
Equivalently, when $C$ is invertible,
\[
 \overline T_{abmn}
 =\E\left[
 \{(C^{-1}W)_a(C^{-1}W)_b-(C^{-1})_{ab}\}B_{mn}(W)
 \right].                                           \tag{3.4}
\]
This derivative-free score formula is useful for numerical audit but is not
needed for the rigorous bound below.

Since $K_0=0$, differentiating (3.2) gives
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \cR''(0)[H,H]=\int\{&\Tr(Q_2\overline B)
 +{1\over2}(Q_1)_{mn}(C_1)_{ab}\overline T_{abmn}\\
 &+\sum_i(K_{1,i})_{am}(K_{1,i})_{bn}\overline T_{abmn}\}\,dx .
\end{aligned}}                                      \tag{3.5}
\]
Thus the relative baseline cross, earlier-root feedback, trace, returned
mean, forest/future recoding, and balanced spatial crosses are present once
inside one self-adjoint quadratic form.  Adding them again as separate
functionals would double count the endpoint.

After the Gaussian covariance reduction (3.2), the first derivative contains
$Q_1$, whose spatial frequencies are
$\pm p$ and $\pm3p$.  Stationarity makes $\overline B(C_0)$ independent of
$x$, hence Fourier orthogonality gives
\[
                         \cR'(0)=0.                  \tag{3.6}
\]

\section*{4. Uniform owner bound}
\hypertarget{section-4-bound}{ }

R-130 proves for every frozen real symmetric $Q$ that the physical coefficient
energy obeys
\[
 \left\|{1\over2}D^2(B:Q)\right\|_{\op}
 \le H_6\|Q\|_{\op},
 \qquad H_6={7083\over500P},\quad P=4+10^{-12}.      \tag{4.1}
\]
Set
\[
 a=\|S_1\|_{\op},\quad b=\|S_2\|_{\op},\quad
 d=|p|a,\qquad e=2|p|b.                            \tag{4.2}
\]
For $\|H\|_{\HS}=1$, (2.8) gives
\[
 \|C_1\|_{\op}\le2ab,\qquad
 \|Q_1\|_{\op}\le2de,\qquad
 \|K_1\|_{\HS}:=\left(\sum_i\|K_{1,i}\|_{\HS}^2\right)^{1/2}
 \le bd+ae.                                        \tag{4.3}
\]
The first term in (3.5) is nonnegative.  Since the matrices are six-real,
$\|C_1\|_*\le6\|C_1\|_{\op}$.  Equation (4.1) therefore bounds the mixed
covariance term by
\[
 6H_6(2ab)(2de)=24H_6abde.                          \tag{4.4}
\]
For the last term take a singular-value decomposition of $K_1$, polarize
(4.1), and use $\|K_1\|_*^2\le6\|K_1\|_{\HS}^2$.
This gives
\[
 12H_6(bd+ae)^2.                                    \tag{4.5}
\]
Substitution of $d=|p|a$, $e=2|p|b$ yields the exact factor
\[
 24H_6abde+12H_6(bd+ae)^2
 =156H_6|p|^2a^2b^2.                                \tag{4.6}
\]

If $\lambda_1=\lambda_{\min}A(p)$ and
$\lambda_2=\lambda_{\min}A(2p)$, the two antipodal signs give
\[
 a^2\le{2\over V\lambda_1},\qquad
 b^2\le{2\over V\lambda_2}.                       \tag{4.7}
\]
After spatial integration, (4.6) becomes
\[
 \cR''(0)[H,H]
 \ge-{624H_6|p|^2\over V\lambda_1\lambda_2}
       \|H\|_{\HS}^2.                              \tag{4.8}
\]

\section*{5. Exact radial certificate}
\hypertarget{section-5-sturm}{ }

The exact Sylvester minors of $M-(7/250)\Id$ are
\[
 {9\over125},\qquad {1211\over250000},\qquad
 {89\over31250000},                                \tag{5.1}
\]
so $M\succ(7/250)\Id$.  Put $x=|p|^2$ and
\[
 f(x)=x^2+Zx+r+{7\over250}.                         \tag{5.2}
\]
The exact registered decimals give a negative discriminant, hence $f>0$.
Also $H_6<7083/2000$.  It remains to prove
\[
 {624(7083/2000)x\over V f(x)f(4x)}<{4\over5}.      \tag{5.3}
\]

For $V=4096$, clear the positive denominator in (5.3).  The resulting
quartic is
\[
\begin{split}
P(x)={}&{262144\over5}x^4
-{592176264064\over9765625}x^3\\
&+{74744439754677505969\over1907348632812500}x^2\\
&-{29970036890042018799\over3051757812500000}x\\
&+{25203778302134079729\over30517578125000000}.
\end{split}                                        \tag{5.4}
\]
Its exact Sturm chain has degrees $(4,3,2,1,0)$.  The signs at zero are
$(+,-,-,+,+)$ and the leading signs at positive infinity are
$(+,+,-,-,+)$.  Both have two variations.  Thus $P$ has no positive root;
since $P(0)>0$, (5.3) holds for every $x\ge0$.  Combining (4.8) and (5.3)
proves (1.2).

The source cost in this normalized root chart is
\[
 {9\over20}\E\|tH\xi_1\|^2
 ={9\over20}t^2\|H\|_{\HS}^2,                     \tag{5.5}
\]
and therefore has Hessian $9\|H\|_{\HS}^2/10$.
Finally, for $R(t)=|w+tz|^2$,
\[
 {d^2\over dt^2}R(t)^3
 =24R(t)\{(w+tz)\cdot z\}^2+6R(t)^2|z|^2\ge0.     \tag{5.6}
\]
Equations (1.2), (5.5), and (5.6) prove (1.3).
