\section*{1. Purpose and scope}
\hypertarget{section-1-result}{ }

The objective of this note is not to manufacture a preferred spatial phase.
It asks what follows from the registered finite-cutoff action and covariance,
independently of whether a later physical minimizer is BCC, another modulated
state, uniform, disordered, or absent. No phase-selection statement is used
or obtained.

R-145 leaves three apparent burdens on the direct T-050 trace-excess route:
an absolute chart anchor, temporal transport of the anisotropic terminal
covariance, and the scalar-principal signed owner. Two can be removed without
changing the action.

First, A7 fixes the zero-control additive normalization and R-104 identifies
the zero-control terminal law. Therefore the R-145 identity is exactly
relative and no separate absolute chart-anchor estimate is needed. Second,
one must telescope the complete owner before splitting the endpoint
covariance. The anisotropic term is then bounded by
\[
 {1\over100}Y_6(h)+24                                      \tag{1.1}
\]
with zero source loss. This is not a blockwise temporal transfer.

Third, R-087 permits a distinct fixed-cutoff Boue--Dupuis problem built from
the constant Brownian factor of the same endpoint covariance. Its temporal
covariance increments are proportional and hence admit an exact positive
scalar-principal/anisotropic split. The variational value is unchanged, but
the old chart's visit, low, trace, and forest owners are not transported.
They must be reconstructed in the new chart.

Finally, strict-past scalar common-noise rows have an exact nonpositive
conditional defect. A bounded same-root coefficient supplies an exact
positive counterfixture, so canonical covariance alone does not close the
scalar sea. The remaining analytic target is the complete contemporaneous
scalar signed relative owner with future variance, forest, and returned low
retained once.

Thus R-146 is a genuine reduction, not T-050 closure. A13, Nelson, removal,
the interacting measure, every phase question, and Sector A remain open.

\section*{2. Zero-control relative-anchor theorem}
\hypertarget{section-2-relative-anchor}{ }
% audit-id: AUDIT-2026-08-02-A13-ZERO-CONTROL-RELATIVE-ANCHOR

Fix a cutoff \(J\) and a temporally faithful chart \(\pi\). R-145 gives the
complete identity
\[
 \E V_J^{\rm ren}(Z_h)=a_{J,\pi}-\cT_{J,\pi}(h),
 \qquad
 \cT_{J,\pi}(h)
 ={1\over2}\{\|\cU_\pi(h)\|^2-\|\Phi_\pi(h)\|^2\},          \tag{2.1}
\]
where \(a_{J,\pi}\) is independent of \(h\). R-104 supplies
\[
 Z_\pi(0)\stackrel{\rm law}=X_J,                            \tag{2.2}
\]
and the A7 covariance-normal definition supplies the external centering
\[
 \E V_J^{\rm ren}(X_J)=0.                                  \tag{2.3}
\]

% evidence-anchor: theorem-2.1-relative-anchor
\textbf{Theorem 2.1 (exact relative anchor).}
Under (2.1)--(2.3),
\[
 \boxed{
 a_{J,\pi}=\cT_{J,\pi}(0),\qquad
 \E V_J^{\rm ren}(Z_h)
 =-[\cT_{J,\pi}(h)-\cT_{J,\pi}(0)].
 }                                                           \tag{2.4}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
Set \(h=0\) in (2.1), use (2.2) to replace the terminal law by \(X_J\),
and then use (2.3). This gives \(0=a_{J,\pi}-\cT_{J,\pi}(0)\).
Substitution into (2.1) proves (2.4). \hfill\(\square\)

Consequently a uniform relative estimate
\[
 \cT_{J,\pi}(h)-\cT_{J,\pi}(0)
 \leq \eta X(h)+\zeta Y_6(h)+B,                             \tag{2.5}
\]
with
\[
 \eta<{5\over11},\qquad \zeta<{27\over100},                \tag{2.6}
\]
implies the direct T-050 lower bound with constant \(B\). No additional
assumption \(a_{J,\pi}\geq-C_0\) is required on this relative route.

This does not refute
NG-2026-07-30-A13-NORMALIZED-GIBBS-DOOB-ABSOLUTE-ANCHOR.
Normalized laws are invariant under \(V\mapsto V+c\) and cannot discover
the constant \(c\). Here the external A7 covariance-normal convention has
already fixed that gauge through (2.3). Also, (2.4) does not prove that
\(\cT_{J,\pi}(0)\) or \(a_{J,\pi}\) is separately bounded uniformly in
cutoff or chart; the subtraction occurs before the limiting estimate.

\section*{4. Arbitrary blockwise anisotropic transport is unavailable}
\hypertarget{section-4-temporal-nogo}{ }

R-129 already shows that total Fourier diagonality does not make arbitrary
temporal increments commute with a shell projector. A stronger obstruction
shows that even a positive suballocation of a fixed anisotropic remainder
need not exist.

Put
\[
 P_+={1\over2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 P_-={1\over2}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad P_++P_-=\Id_2,                                      \tag{4.1}
\]
and request a decomposition of
\[
 R=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}                    \tag{4.2}
\]
as \(R=R_++R_-\) with \(0\preceq R_\pm\preceq P_\pm\).
The elementary dominance lemma is: if \(0\preceq A\preceq P\) and \(P\)
is an orthogonal projection, then for \(x\in\Ker P\),
\(\ip{x}{Ax}=0\); hence \(A^{1/2}x=0\), so
\(\operatorname{Ran}A\subseteq\operatorname{Ran}P\).
Since \(P_\pm\) have rank one, this support inclusion forces
\[
 R_\pm=a_\pm P_\pm,\qquad 0\leq a_\pm\leq1.                 \tag{4.3}
\]
But both diagonal entries of \(a_+P_++a_-P_-\) equal
\((a_++a_-)/2\), whereas the diagonal entries of \(R\) are one and zero.
No such decomposition exists.

This proves
NG-2026-08-02-A13-ARBITRARY-TEMPORAL-ANISOTROPIC-POSITIVE-SUBALLOCATION.
It refutes the method, not the full action bound. The legal repair is
Theorem 3.1: telescope first, split once, and use the favourable zero-control
term.

\section*{5. Canonical covariance as an exact alternative chart}
\hypertarget{section-5-canonical-covariance}{ }

Fix \(J\) and write an arbitrary endpoint factorization as
\[
 X_J=\int_0^T K_J(t)\,dB_t,\qquad
 C_J=\int_0^T K_J(t)K_J(t)^*\,dt.                            \tag{5.1}
\]
Define on a fresh canonical Brownian space
\[
 K_{\rm can}(t)=T^{-1/2}C_J^{1/2},\qquad
 \widetilde X_J=\int_0^T K_{\rm can}(t)\,d\widetilde B_t.   \tag{5.2}
\]
Then
\[
 \widetilde X_J\stackrel{\rm law}=X_J\sim N(0,C_J).         \tag{5.3}
\]

% evidence-anchor: theorem-5.1-canonical-bd
\textbf{Theorem 5.1 (fixed-cutoff canonical covariance normal form).}
Let \(G_J\) obey the fixed-cutoff continuity, lower-bound, and local
polynomial-envelope hypotheses of R-087 Theorem 8.1. For every \(q>0\),
\[
 -{1\over q}\log\E e^{-qG_J(X_J)}
 =\inf_{v\in\cA_{cs}}\E\left[
 G_J\left(\widetilde X_J+
 {C_J^{1/2}\over\sqrt T}\int_0^Tv_t\,dt\right)
 +{1\over2q}\int_0^T\|v_t\|^2dt\right].                    \tag{5.4}
\]
At the Nelson exponent \(q=10/9\), the cost coefficient is \(9/20\).

\textit{Proof.}
Equation (5.3) makes the left side equal to the original endpoint value.
Apply R-087 Theorem 8.1 to the deterministic factor \(K_{\rm can}\).
Its restriction to bounded smooth cylindrical-simple controls is exact.
This proves (5.4). \hfill\(\square\)

For a deterministic partition \(I_b=(t_{b-1},t_b]\), put
\(\tau_b=|I_b|/T\). The field increment covariance is exactly
\[
 \Delta C_b=\tau_bC_J.                                      \tag{5.5}
\]
If \(C_J=C_{0,J}+R_J\) with \(C_{0,J},R_J\succeq0\), an expanded Brownian
space gives the rectangular factor
\[
 K_{\rm rect}=T^{-1/2}
 [\,C_{0,J}^{1/2}\quad R_J^{1/2}\,],                        \tag{5.6}
\]
and hence the exact positive split
\[
 \Delta C_b=\tau_bC_{0,J}+\tau_bR_J.                        \tag{5.7}
\]
No false identity
\(C_J^{1/2}=C_{0,J}^{1/2}+R_J^{1/2}\) is used. In the A1
translation-invariant cutoff, these covariance operators and Fourier shell
projections are simultaneous multipliers, so
\[
 [\Delta C_b,\Pi_m]=0,\qquad
 \Delta C_{b,m}=\tau_b\Pi_mC_J\Pi_m.                        \tag{5.8}
\]

For a blockwise constant control \(v_t=u_b\) on \(I_b\), put
\(h_b=\sqrt{\tau_bT}\,u_b\). Then
\[
 \int_0^T\|v_t\|^2dt=\sum_b\|h_b\|^2,\qquad
 \int_0^TK_{\rm can}v_tdt
 =C_J^{1/2}\sum_b\sqrt{\tau_b}\,h_b.                        \tag{5.9}
\]
Thus every tail \(S\) obeys
\[
 \left\|C_J^{1/2}\sum_{b\in S}\sqrt{\tau_b}h_b\right\|^2
 \leq\|C_J\|_{\rm op}
 \left(\sum_{b\in S}\tau_b\right)
 \sum_{b\in S}\|h_b\|^2.                                   \tag{5.10}
\]
The primary and independent certificates verify this normalization on
decimal and rational fixtures. A source in \(\Ker C_J\) costs energy but
produces no endpoint displacement, so it is zero at the infimum and may be
removed by the range quotient.

Theorem 5.1 preserves the complete endpoint variational value. It does not
identify a control from the old chart with one in the new chart, and does not
preserve individual reveal, trace, forest, or returned-low terms. The
original arbitrary-chart T-050 theorem is therefore not proved. A new uniform
lower bound established directly in (5.4), however, would imply the
fixed-cutoff Nelson estimate by the exact R-087 variational equality.

\section*{6. Scalar common-noise sign and its exact boundary}
\hypertarget{section-6-scalar}{ }

Let \(\cF_{\rm past}\) be a strict-past sigma-field. Conditionally on it,
let \(A\) and \(u\) be fixed, let \(g\sim N(0,\Gamma)\), and let
\(\zeta\sim N(0,\Id)\) be independent. Define
\[
 U=A\Gamma^{1/2}\zeta,\qquad \Phi=A(g+u).                    \tag{6.1}
\]

% evidence-anchor: lemma-6.1-predictable-scalar
\textbf{Lemma 6.1 (strict-past scalar common-noise sign).}
\[
 \boxed{
 \E[\|U\|^2-\|\Phi\|^2\mid\cF_{\rm past}]
 =-\|Au\|^2\leq0.
 }                                                           \tag{6.2}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
Both Gaussian squares have the same conditional covariance trace
\(\Tr(A\Gamma A^*)\). The shifted current square has the additional term
\(\|Au\|^2\), and its mixed Gaussian term has conditional mean zero.
\hfill\(\square\)

For two endpoints using the same Gaussian and probe, the corresponding
polarized identity is
\[
 \E\ip{U_q}{U_p}-\E\ip{\Phi_q}{\Phi_p}
 =-\ip{A_qu_q}{A_pu_p}.                                     \tag{6.3}
\]
Equation (6.3) is exact, but a mixed inner product has no sign by itself.
Only a legally recombined diagonal or an established adjoint owner may use
it as a sign statement.

The strict-past hypothesis is load-bearing. Let independent
\(g,\zeta\sim N(0,1)\), and for \(t>0\) define the bounded smooth coefficient
\[
 A_t(g)=g e^{-tg^2/2},\qquad U=A_t(g)\zeta,\qquad
 \Phi=A_t(g)g.                                              \tag{6.4}
\]
Gaussian differentiation gives
\[
 \E\|U\|^2=(1+2t)^{-3/2},\qquad
 \E\|\Phi\|^2=3(1+2t)^{-5/2},                              \tag{6.5}
\]
and therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 \E(\|U\|^2-\|\Phi\|^2)
 ={2(t-1)\over(1+2t)^{5/2}}>0\quad(t>1).
 }                                                           \tag{6.6}
\]
Relative to the trace square, the defect is
\[
 {2(t-1)\over1+2t}\longrightarrow1.                        \tag{6.7}
\]
At \(t=2\) this ratio is exactly \(2/5\). This proves
NG-2026-08-02-A13-CANONICAL-COVARIANCE-AUTOMATIC-SCALAR-CANCELLATION.
It is a counterexample to the inference from covariance equality, not a
counterexample to the complete production action.

Canonical proportional covariance also does not manufacture a common
terminal martingale. With independent centered roots \(\xi_1,\xi_2\),
\[
 \Phi_1=\xi_1,\qquad \Phi_2=2\xi_1+\xi_2,\qquad
 \E[\Phi_2\mid\cF_1]=2\xi_1\ne\Phi_1.                      \tag{6.8}
\]
Thus the R-125 common-terminal hypothesis remains a separate theorem.

\section*{9. Devil's-advocate and code-discipline review}
\hypertarget{section-9-adversarial}{ }

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Objection: Theorem 2.1 contradicts the absolute-anchor no-go.
DISMISSED.}
The no-go concerns normalized data without an external additive convention.
A7 supplies that convention. No separate uniform zero-control trace bound
is inferred.

\item \textbf{Objection: The endpoint-first proof secretly transfers the
anisotropic covariance through time. DISMISSED.}
All temporal owners are recombined first. Only the terminal covariance trace
is split, and the zero-control term is discarded in the favourable direction.

\item \textbf{Objection: Every positive temporal increment admits a positive
anisotropic subincrement. UPHELD against the shortcut.}
Equations (4.1)--(4.3) give an exact rank-one counterexample.

\item \textbf{Objection: Equal endpoint covariance preserves every old
owner. UPHELD against the shortcut.}
It preserves the Gaussian endpoint law and variational value only.
Filtration-dependent visit, forest, future-variance, and low terms must be
rebuilt.

\item \textbf{Objection: Proportional increments imply a common-terminal
Doob family. UPHELD against the shortcut.}
Equation (6.8) separates covariance normalization from the tower property.

\item \textbf{Objection: Canonical covariance makes the complete scalar sea
nonpositive. UPHELD against the shortcut.}
The strict-past lemma is valid, while (6.4)--(6.7) show a positive same-root
defect. The forest/low recombination is indispensable.

\item \textbf{Objection: The diagnostic coefficient and ceiling in (7.5)
are a production bound. DISMISSED.}
They are explicitly non-production diagnostics. The registered endpoint
theorem has source coefficient zero and integer constant 24.

\item \textbf{Objection: A factor \(1/2\), realification multiplicity, or

scripts separately derive the R-145 constants, \(h_b=\sqrt{\tau_bT}u_b\),
the rational tail inequality, and exact thresholds. Numbers are computed
from A1/R-145 authorities, not pasted as proof outputs.

\item \textbf{Objection: The argument assumes invertible covariance.
DISMISSED.}
The positive square root exists for singular covariance. Kernel controls
produce no shift, retain positive cost, and are removed at the infimum.

\item \textbf{Objection: This result favors BCC or any other phase.
DISMISSED.}
No phase ansatz or energy ranking enters any proof. The result is entirely
phase-neutral.

\item \textbf{Objection: These reductions close T-050. DISMISSED.}
The complete contemporaneous scalar signed owner and its future/forest/low
coordinates remain open. T-050, Nelson, and Sector A therefore remain open.
\end{enumerate}
