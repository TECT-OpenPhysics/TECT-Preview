\section*{Purpose and scope}

This note continues
\texttt{A13-CLASSII-NPC-CONE-MARTINGALE-INJECTION-BALANCE} inside the
existing A13 claim.  It does not create a peer-level claim and does not close
the controlled-shell one-use theorem.  Its purpose is to separate three
parts of the remaining problem which can now be proved from the one genuinely
geometric part which cannot yet be proved.

First, the amount of raw current energy which one shell can causally remove
is written as an exact nonlinear Tikhonov harvest.  Its linearisation is the
retained A11 resolvent, not a coefficient-blind source norm.  Second, the
complete conservative score, including coefficient differentiation, obeys a
cutoff-uniform A12-compatible Carleson theorem.  At the uncontrolled Gaussian
base its two pieces improve further to dyadic rates $N_j^{-1}$ and $N_j^{-2}$.
Third, CAT(0) strong convexity is extended through the aggregate cone tip, and
the centered $H^{-1-\kappa}$ quadratic remainder is paid globally with one
Cameron--Martin/sextic allocation.

Two tempting shortcuts are also retired.  Integrating the absolute full-score
bound along a finite physical shell path produces superquadratic control
powers and cannot meet arbitrary one-use allocations.  More seriously, the
often suggested full nonlinear remainder estimate
$\|\delta_j-L_jh_j\|\lesssim N_j^{-3/2}\|h_j\|^2$ omits a coefficient-curvature
term and is false.  The exact remaining theorem is a tip-safe production
Schur--Jacobi inequality which keeps that term inside the grouped current
square.

\section{The exact nonlinear harvest}

Put every current component, spatial direction, heat dummy, and generator in
the Hilbert direct sum with the production $Q_{\rm II}$ metric.  At shell $j$
let $w_j$ be the past current and let $\delta_j(h)$ be the exact current
increment reachable by the physical shell control $h$.  Then the raw secant
is simply
\[
 \cR_j(h)={1\over2}\|w_j+\delta_j(h)\|^2
                    -{1\over2}\|w_j\|^2.                 \tag{1.1}
\]
For $\eta>0$ define
\[
 \boxed{
 \cH_{\eta,j}(w)
 ={1\over2}\|w\|^2-
   \inf_h\left\{{1\over2}\|w+\delta_j(h)\|^2
                         +\eta\|h\|_{\cH_j}^2\right\}.} \tag{1.2}
\]
Thus, pathwise and without a Taylor expansion,
\[
 \cR_j(h)+\eta\|h\|_{\cH_j}^2\ge-\cH_{\eta,j}(w_j).      \tag{1.3}
\]
This is the energy which the declared control can actually harvest, rather
than the total Gaussian energy injected at the scale.  A sufficient global
successor estimate is
\[
 \E\sum_{j\ge j_0}\cH_{\eta,j}(w_j)
 \le \zeta\E\|Z_J\|_6^6+C_{\zeta,\eta,j_0}               \tag{1.4}
\]
uniformly in $J$ and along every regular controlled trajectory.

If $\delta_j(h)=L_jh$ is linear and $s_j=L_j^*w_j$, completing the Hilbert
square gives the exact retained resolvent
\[
 \boxed{
 \cH_{\eta,j}^{\rm lin}(w)
 ={1\over2}\left\langle s_j,
   (L_j^*L_j+2\eta\Id)^{-1}s_j\right\rangle.}             \tag{1.5}
\]
The executable solves the normal equation independently and checks (1.5).
This is the same structural object as the A11 determinant resolvent.  Replacing
it by $\|s_j\|^2/(4\eta)$ deletes the retained operator and returns to the
already failed coefficient-blind budget.

\section{The full conservative-score Carleson theorem}

Let
\[
 \cE(u)={1\over2}\sum_i\int (D_i u)^TB(u)D_i u\dd x,
 \qquad u_j=S_{j-1}\phi,\qquad a_j=K_jh.                  \tag{2.1}
\]
For a vector $q(u,Du)\in\R^6$ define it by duality,
\[
 \xi\cdot q(u,Du)=\sum_i
 DB(u)[\xi]:(D_i u\otimes D_i u).                         \tag{2.2}
\]
Differentiating before any completion gives
\[
 D\cE(u_j)[K_jh]=\langle\sigma_j(u_j),h\rangle,
 \quad
 \boxed{\sigma_j
 =G_j^*[B(u_j)Du_j]+{1\over2}K_j^*q(u_j,Du_j).}            \tag{2.3}
\]
The factor $1/2$ in the second term is compulsory.

Set
\[
 \beta_0=\sup_{u\ne0}{\|B(u)\|_{\op}\over|u|^2},
 \qquad
 \beta_1=\sup_{u\ne0,\,|\xi|=1}
 {\|DB(u)[\xi]\|_{\op}\over|u|}.                       \tag{2.4}
\]
The fixed floor and the frame estimates
\[
 \|M_A(u)\|_F\le\sqrt{20}|u|,
 \qquad \|DM_A(u)[\xi]\|_F\le\sqrt{68}|\xi|           \tag{2.5}
\]
give the safe production constants
\[
 \beta_0=4(a+2b+c)={339\over2000P},
 \qquad
 \beta_1\le6\sqrt{1360}\,\|Q_{\rm II}\|_{\op},
 \quad P=4+10^{-12}.                                     \tag{2.6}
\]
In particular $|q(u,Du)|\le\beta_1|u||Du|^2$.

Let $A_0=M_R^2/c_{\rm sym}$ and retain the A12 sharp-cube constants $M_6,Q_6$.
The derivative source obeys the A12 estimate.  For the coefficient source use
\[
 \|K_j^*F\|_2^2\le {A_0\over(1+\kappa_j^2)^2}\|F\|_2^2 \tag{2.7}
\]
and
\[
 \sum_j{|Du_j|^4\over(1+\kappa_j^2)^2}
 \le\left(\sum_j{|Du_j|^2\over1+\kappa_j^2}\right)^2.   \tag{2.8}
\]
Hölder and the A12 maximal/square-function theorem then prove
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_j\|\sigma_j(u_j)\|_{\cH_j}^2
 \le A_0\left(\beta_0M_6^2Q_6
       +{\beta_1\over2}M_6Q_6^2\right)^2\|\phi\|_6^6.} \tag{2.9}
\]
This is a cutoff-uniform theorem for the complete linearised energy score,
not only the old derivative source.  It is qualitative: its fixed coefficient
does not become an arbitrary $\zeta$, so (2.9) alone does not close A13.

\section{The uncontrolled Gaussian score is dyadically summable}

There is extra decay at the uncontrolled Gaussian base which deterministic
$L^6$ envelopes cannot see.  Let $U_j=X_{<j}+r_{j:J}$ be the point value,
including the spatially frozen terminal heat dummy, and let $Y_j=DX_{<j}$.
Evenness of the common covariance makes $U_j$ independent of $Y_j$ at each
spatial point.  Define
\[
 S_*:=\sup_{j,J}\Tr\operatorname{Cov}(U_j),
 \qquad D_j:=\Tr\operatorname{Cov}(Y_j).                  \tag{3.1}
\]
Grouping lattice points by $\|n\|_\infty=m$ gives
\[
 S_*\le {6L^{-3}M_R^2\over c_{\rm sym}}
 \left[1+\sum_{m\ge1}{24m^2+2\over(1+\alpha_L^2m^2)^2}\right],             \tag{3.2}
\]
\[
 D_j\le {6L^{-3}M_R^2\over c_{\rm sym}}\alpha_L^{-2}
 \left(24N+{\pi^2\over3}\right),
 \quad N=N_{j-1},\quad \alpha_L={2\pi\over L}.           \tag{3.3}
\]
For a centered Gaussian vector with covariance trace $S$,
$\E|U|^4\le3S^2$; similarly $\E|Y|^4\le3D^2$.  Jensen in the heat dummy,
(2.7), and value--derivative independence yield
\[
 \E\|\ell_j\|^2
 \le {3A_0\beta_0^2L^3S_*^2D_j\over1+\kappa_j^2}
 \le {C_\ell\over N},                                   \tag{3.4}
\]
\[
 \E\|m_j\|^2
 \le {3A_0\beta_1^2L^3S_*D_j^2\over4(1+\kappa_j^2)^2}
 \le {C_m\over N^2}.                                    \tag{3.5}
\]
Here $\ell_j=G_j^*[B(U_j)Y_j]$ and
$m_j=K_j^*q(U_j,Y_j)/2$.  Safe explicit constants are obtained from
\[
 C_D={6L^{-3}M_R^2\over c_{\rm sym}}\alpha_L^{-2}
       \left(24+{\pi^2\over3}\right),                   \tag{3.6}
\]
\[
 C_\ell=3A_0\beta_0^2L^3S_*^2C_D\alpha_L^{-2},
 \qquad
 C_m={3\over4}A_0\beta_1^2L^3S_*C_D^2\alpha_L^{-4}.     \tag{3.7}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \E\sum_{j\ge j_0}\|\ell_j+m_j\|^2
 \le {4C_\ell\over N_{j_0-1}}
      +{8C_m\over3N_{j_0-1}^2}.                          \tag{3.8}
\]
For every predictable infinitesimal feedback,
\[
 \E\sum_{j\ge j_0}
 \{\langle\ell_j+m_j,h_j\rangle+\eta\|h_j\|^2\}
 \ge-{1\over4\eta}\E\sum_{j\ge j_0}\|\ell_j+m_j\|^2.\tag{3.9}
\]
Thus no linear-response strategy around the base Gaussian can create a
nonsummable ultraviolet loss.  Equations (3.4)--(3.9) may not be transplanted
to a controlled base: that would reuse the Gaussian independence after the
control has destroyed it.

\section{CAT(0) strong secants through the cone tip}

Let $Y=C(B)$ be the complete aggregate production cone already identified in
the preceding A13 result.  For $U_0,U_1\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb T_L^3;Y)$ let
$U_t$ be the pointwise constant-speed geodesic and put
$d(x)=d_Y(U_0(x),U_1(x))$.  The CAT(0) quadrilateral inequality applied at
$x$ and $x+he_i$, divided by $h^2$ and passed to the Korevaar--Schoen limit,
proves
\[
 \boxed{
 \cE(U_t)+{t(1-t)\over2}\|\nabla d\|_2^2
 \le(1-t)\cE(U_0)+t\cE(U_1).}                            \tag{4.1}
\]
This statement includes $R=0$ and requires no smooth-stratum approximation.
It also proves $d\in H^1$ and lower semicontinuity of the cone energy.

The function
\[
 t\longmapsto\cE(U_t)-{t^2\over2}\|\nabla d\|_2^2       \tag{4.2}
\]
is convex.  Hence the right slope $\cF_+$ exists in the extended real line,
and
\[
 \cE(U_1)-\cE(U_0)
 \ge\cF_+(U_0,U_1)+{1\over2}\|\nabla d\|_2^2.           \tag{4.3}
\]
On the smooth $R>0$ stratum this is the preceding Cartan--Jacobi formula.
For a general tip-touching finite-energy pair, however, finiteness of
$\cF_+$ and convergence of smooth first variations are not yet established.
The safe tip theorem is therefore the whole finite secant (4.1), not a
separately estimated logarithm.

There is also a global physical endpoint comparison.  Write $Z$ for the
active doublet of $X$.  The production pullback satisfies
\[
 |D\Phi_e(X)H|_g^2=H^TB_e(X)H\le\beta_0|Z|^2|H|^2.       \tag{4.4}
\]
The affine physical path $X_t=X+ta$ then gives, even if it crosses the tip,
\[
 \boxed{
 d_g(\Phi_e(X),\Phi_e(X+a))
 \le\sqrt{\beta_0}\left(|Z|+{|a_Z|\over2}\right)|a|
 \le\sqrt{\beta_0}(|X|+|a|)|a|.}                        \tag{4.5}
\]
The constant is floor-uniform and equals
$\sqrt{339/[2000(4+10^{-12})]}$.  In particular $L^4$ convergence of physical
fields implies $L^2(Y)$ convergence of their cone images, so the energy lower
semicontinuity in (4.1) applies directly at the tip.  This controls endpoint
distance; it does not control the spatial derivative of a cone logarithm.

\section{A global centered-form one-use lemma}

The balanced coefficient-jet result supplies every finite probability moment
of the centered derivative-square model in $H^{-1-\kappa}$, with
$0<\kappa<1$; the production audit uses $\kappa=1/10$.  Let
$Q_{j-1}=S_{j-1}Q$ and let $a_j$ be mutually orthogonal sharp-shell physical
controls.  Put
\[
 \Pi(a)=\sum_jS_{j-1}(a_j\otimes a_j),\quad
 X=\sum_j\|a_j\|_{H^2}^2,\quad
 A=\left(\sum_j|a_j|^2\right)^{1/2},\quad
 Y=\|A\|_6^6.                                             \tag{5.1}
\]
All Sobolev spaces here are inhomogeneous; this is load-bearing for zero
modes.  By self-adjointness of $S_{j-1}$, duality, and the sharp-cube maximal
theorem at exponent $3/2$,
\[
 \|\Pi(a)\|_2\le C_LY^{1/3}.                              \tag{5.2}
\]
The $p=3/2$ maximal estimate follows from the same de la Vallee Poussin plus
cube-annular product-Marcinkiewicz proof used in A12.  At the high endpoint,
the three-dimensional $H^2$ algebra and $L^2$ boundedness of each sharp
projection give
\[
 \|\Pi(a)\|_{H^2}\le C_HX.                               \tag{5.3}
\]
Interpolating (5.2)--(5.3), with
$\vartheta=(1+\kappa)/2$, yields
\[
 \boxed{
 \|\Pi(a)\|_{H^{1+\kappa}}
 \le C X^\vartheta Y^{(1-\vartheta)/3}.}                  \tag{5.4}
\]

Let $M=\|Q\|_{H^{-1-\kappa}}$.  Duality and three-factor weighted Young now
give, for every $\eta,\zeta>0$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \left|\sum_j\langle Q_{j-1},a_j\otimes a_j\rangle\right|
 \le\eta X+\zeta Y
 +C_\kappa\eta^{-{3(1+\kappa)\over2(1-\kappa)}}
  \zeta^{-1/2}M^{3/(1-\kappa)}.}                          \tag{5.5}
\]
At $\kappa=1/10$, the model moment is $10/3$ and the negative $\eta$ exponent
is $11/6$.  Both are available from the balanced-jet theorem.

A1 ellipticity makes $X\lesssim\sum_j\|h_j\|_{\cH_j}^2$.  Moreover
$a_j=P_j(Z_J-X_J)$, and the sharp-cube Littlewood--Paley theorem gives
\[
 \E Y\le C\E\|Z_J\|_6^6+C\E\|X_J\|_6^6
 \le C\E\|Z_J\|_6^6+C.                                 \tag{5.6}
\]
Thus any centered quadratic remainder of the form on the left of (5.5) is
globally one-use: it spends one control allocation, one terminal sextic
allocation, and one integrable random constant.  Applying Young shell by
shell would create one constant per shell and is forbidden.

\section{Why absolute score integration still fails}

The full-score theorem does extend to current-shell inserted states
\[
 v_j=S_{j-1}\phi+t_jK_jh_j,\qquad 0\le t_j\le1.          \tag{6.1}
\]
For $x=\|\phi\|_6$, $y=(\sum_j\|h_j\|^2)^{1/2}$ and
$\delta=N_{j_0}^{-1}$, shell Bernstein and A1 smoothing give
\[
 \left\|\sup_j|v_j|\right\|_6\le M_6x+C_a\delta y,
\quad
 \left\|\left(\sum_j{|Dv_j|^2\over1+\kappa_j^2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_6
 \le Q_6x+C_d\delta y.                                  \tag{6.2}
\]
Substitution in (2.9) and integration over $t$ control the absolute secant
only by a constant times
\[
 x^3y+\delta x^2y^2+\delta^2xy^3+\delta^3y^4.             \tag{6.3}
\]
For any fixed $\delta>0$, the last term cannot be bounded by
$\eta y^2+\zeta x^6+C$ for all $x,y$.  Even the first term has a fixed Young
tradeoff between the two requested arbitrary allocations.  Therefore direct
absolute-score paralinearisation is a valid estimate but an invalid closure
architecture.  The grouped positive current/Jacobi square must be retained.

There is an even simpler obstruction to an initial-score-only comparison.
In the flat scalar target $m(u)=u^2$, take
\[
 h=-u+\sqrt{u^2+c}.                                       \tag{6.4}
\]
Then the finite target displacement is the constant $c$.  Its distance
gradient, energy secant, and Jacobi term all vanish, while the initial affine
target displacement $2uh$ generally has nonzero gradient.  Hence no universal
finite-geodesic inequality may replace the endpoint displacement by the
initial physical score with only $\cJ$ as an error.

\section{The exact scalar good/bad Schur prototype}

The correct local structure is visible for
\[
 f(u,p)=u^2p^2.                                            \tag{7.1}
\]
When $u+a\ne0$, completing the endpoint derivative $b$ gives exactly
\[
\begin{split}
 &f(u+a,p+b)-f(u,p)-Df(u,p)[a,b]\\
 &=(u+a)^2\left[b+{ap(2u+a)\over(u+a)^2}\right]^2
 -{a^2p^2u(3u+2a)\over(u+a)^2}.
\end{split}                                                \tag{7.2}
\]
If $|a|\le\theta|u|$ with $0\le\theta<1$, the sharp uniform bound is
\[
 f(u+a,p+b)-f(u,p)-Df(u,p)[a,b]
 \ge-{3-2\theta\over(1-\theta)^2}a^2p^2,                 \tag{7.3}
\]
uniformly in $b$.  On the bad region $|u|\le C|a|$, discard the tangent and
use the whole secant:
\[
 f(u+a,p+b)-f(u,p)\ge-u^2p^2\ge-C^2a^2p^2.               \tag{7.4}
\]
Keeping the tangent globally is impossible.  At $u=1,a=-1$ its remainder is
$p^2-2pb$, unbounded below as $b$ varies.  This is precisely why the cone
geodesic and its tip behavior are load-bearing rather than cosmetic.

The production successor must prove a frame-valued analogue of
(7.3)--(7.4), uniformly in the derivative displacement, while converting the
$a_j^2p_j^2$ remainder into the centered form of Section 5 plus the covariance
trace.  The latter is already harmless:
\[
 \langle\Gamma_{j-1},a_j^2\rangle
 \lesssim N_j\|a_j\|_2^2
 \lesssim N_j^{-3}\|h_j\|_{\cH_j}^2.                    \tag{7.5}
\]

\section{A production-scaled gauge-null stress test}

The initial physical score can vanish even when a finite secant is negative.
On a normalized circle take one real active component
\[
 u=C+A\cos(qx),\qquad
 h=if,\qquad f=B(\cos(nx)-\cos(mx)),\quad q=m-n.           \tag{8.1}
\]
Assume $m\ne2n,3n$, so no unintended resonance meets $q$ or $2q$.  The affine
moment-map and density variations vanish because $h$ is a phase tangent, but
the affine endpoint changes the density by $f^2$.  With
$I=\langle(u u_x)^2\rangle$, Fourier orthogonality gives
\[
 \Delta I=-{1\over2}ACq^2B^2
       +{3\over8}(n^2+m^2)B^4.                            \tag{8.2}
\]
The production $J$-sector secant, including its full positive square, is
\[
 \Delta E_J=-aACq^2B^2+{3a\over4}(n^2+m^2)B^4.           \tag{8.3}
\]
If the CM symbol lower bound is $\kappa_4|k|^4$, optimization in $B^2$ yields
\[
 \inf_B\{\Delta E_J+\eta\|h\|_{\cH}^2\}
 =-{L_+^2\over3a(n^2+m^2)},                              \tag{8.4}
\]
\[
 L=aACq^2-{\eta\kappa_4\over2}(n^4+m^4).                \tag{8.5}
\]
For $n=N+1,m=2N,q=N-1$, $N\ge2$, negativity requires
$AC\gtrsim\eta N^2$.  A production Gaussian $q$-mode has standard deviation
$O(N^{-2})$.  With bounded $C$ this is an $N^4$-standard-deviation event; if
$C$ is also Gaussian, the optimized joint tail is still $\exp(-cN^4)$.
Alternatively, finite moments give after $N^3$ multiplicity the bound
$N^{9-4p}$ for any $p>9/4$.  The mechanism is therefore summable.  It refutes
initial-score-only reasoning but is not a production counterexample.

\section{The corrected nonlinear remainder and exact successor}

For
\[
 \delta=M(z+a)^T(y+b)-M(z)^Ty                              \tag{9.1}
\]
the true linearisation is
\[
 Lh=DM(z)[a]^Ty+M(z)^Tb.                                  \tag{9.2}
\]
The exact nonlinear remainder has two terms:
\[
\boxed{
 \delta-Lh=
 [M(z+a)-M(z)-DM(z)a]^Ty
 +[M(z+a)-M(z)]^Tb.}                                      \tag{9.3}
\]
Only the second term obeys the attractive shell bound
\[
 \|[M(z+a)-M(z)]^Tb\|
 \lesssim N_j^{-3/2}\|h_j\|^2.                           \tag{9.4}
\]
The first is the frame-curvature/Cartan term.  A naive fixed-floor Taylor
bound at Gaussian derivative size is only of order
$N_j^{-1/2}\|h_j\|^2$, and cone coordinates become singular near the tip.
Thus any proof which assigns (9.4) to the whole remainder is invalid.  The
term must remain inside the grouped current/Jacobi square and be reduced to
the centered form (5.5) by a good/bad Schur argument.

The remaining finite-cutoff lemma is now precise.  It must combine:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a smooth good-region tangent where the active radius dominates the
control displacement;
\item a tip-safe bad-region whole secant, with no forbidden $b$-linear
tangent;
\item a Schur complement uniform in $b_j$ which leaves only the centered
$Q_{j-1}$ form and the summable trace (7.5); and
\item a causal grouping in which pure-control current creation telescopes,
while only Gaussian-rooted pieces use the $N_j^{-1}$/$N_j^{-2}$ tail.
\end{enumerate}
Once this grouped production Schur--Jacobi lemma is proved, Sections 3--5
provide the required arbitrary $\eta,\zeta$ allocations for regular one-shot
controls.  Localization and lower semicontinuity must then extend the result
to the declared finite-energy class before one-use or Nelson can be invoked.

\section{Devil's-advocate review}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{``The full-score Carleson theorem closes A13.''}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  Its coefficient is fixed, and finite path
integration creates (6.3).  It proves the complete linear score, not the
required grouped finite secant.

\item \textbf{``The Gaussian score tail applies after adapted control.''}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  Equations (3.4)--(3.9) use same-point Gaussian
value--derivative independence.  The production successor must first isolate
Gaussian-rooted terms without differentiating the control.

\item \textbf{``The cone tip was already harmless because the floor is
positive.''}  \textbf{DISMISSED in the stated form.}  The density floor does
not prevent the active doublet radius from vanishing.  The whole-secants
inequality (4.1), not the floor, supplies the tip-safe result.  A finite
separate first variation at the tip remains open.

\item \textbf{``The centered form can be paid shell by shell.''}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  That creates one Young constant per shell.
Equations (5.1)--(5.6) sum first and pay the random model norm once.

\item \textbf{``The full nonlinear remainder has shell power $-3/2$.''}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  Equation (9.3) displays the omitted coefficient-
curvature term.  Only the product remainder has that power.

\item \textbf{``The gauge-null beat is a production counterexample.''}
\textbf{DISMISSED.}  Its CM threshold is $AC\gtrsim\eta N^2$, and every
base-Gaussian tail estimate is summable after shell multiplicity.  A
control-created carrier also pays its creation energy in the global telescope.

\item \textbf{``CAT(0) strong convexity alone proves one-use.''}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  The previous flat reset no-go remains valid.
Production Fourier/CM decay and the grouped Schur--Jacobi comparison are
essential.
\end{enumerate}
