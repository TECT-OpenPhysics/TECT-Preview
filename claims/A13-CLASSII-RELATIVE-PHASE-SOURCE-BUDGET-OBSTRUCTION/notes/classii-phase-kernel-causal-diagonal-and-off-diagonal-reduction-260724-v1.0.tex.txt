\section{Purpose and scope}

R-071 closes the complete fixed-floor linear production frame and proves that
the nonlinear terminal current square has a genuine kernel leakage.  This note
identifies that kernel exactly, proves the strict-past one-use estimate for the
matched same-shell part of the leakage, and expands the full terminal leakage
into that diagonal plus one explicit off-diagonal remainder.

The diagonal estimate is cutoff-uniform and uses one Cameron--Martin
allocation, one terminal sextic allocation, and one integrable random
constant.  It does not close the full nonlinear subgate: the off-diagonal
remainder is nonzero and can dominate the diagonal.  The next theorem must
triangularly group that remainder together with the range-visible terminal
square.  No finite-energy extension, controlled-shell one-use theorem, or
Nelson estimate is asserted here.

All statements use the hash-pinned A1 production coefficients, the R-050/A7
common real-even scalar regulator, the R-066 strict sharp-cube filtration, a
fixed positive density floor, and regular whole-shell predictable controls.
The finite-low boundary is retained.

\section{The stacked production frame has an exact gauge kernel}

Write a field value as $x=(u,s)\in\mathbb C^2\oplus\mathbb C$.  For the
three embedded Pauli generators put
\[
 p_r(x)=2(\sigma_ru,0),\qquad
 v_r(x)=p_r(x)-2q_r(x)x,\qquad
 M_r(x)=[p_r(x),v_r(x)],\qquad\text{(2.1)}
\]
and define the stacked current map
\[
 T_xb=(M_r(x)^Tb)_{r=1}^3.\qquad\text{(2.2)}
\]
The production matrix $Q_{\rm II}$ is positive definite, so weighting (2.2)
does not change its kernel.

Let $J_D(u,s)=(iu,0)$ and $J_S(u,s)=(0,is)$.  Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \ker T_x=
 \begin{cases}
 \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{J_Dx,J_Sx\},
     &u\ne0,\ s\ne0,\\
 \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{J_Dx\}\oplus
     (\{0\}\times\mathbb C),&u\ne0,\ s=0,\\
 \mathbb R^6,&u=0.
 \end{cases}}\qquad\text{(2.3)}
\]

Indeed, the three vectors $\sigma_ru$ are mutually orthogonal in the real
pairing and have common squared norm $|u|^2$.  Their real orthogonal
complement is therefore $\mathbb R,iu$.  The Pauli/Hopf identity
\[
 \sum_{r=1}^3m_r\sigma_ru=|u|^2u,
 \qquad m_r=u^\dagger\sigma_ru.\qquad\text{(2.4)}
\]
shows that the $v_r$ conditions additionally impose
$\operatorname{Re}(\bar t s)=0$ on a singlet displacement $t$.  This proves
(2.3), including both degenerate strata.  It also proves that a globally
smooth inverse or fixed-rank pseudoinverse cannot be part of the argument.

The realified Pauli matrices commute with both $J_D$ and $J_S$.  Hence
\[
 M_r(x)^TJ_\alpha x=0,\qquad \alpha\in\{D,S\}.\qquad\text{(2.5)}
\]
Differentiating (2.5) at $z$ along $a$ gives
\[
 DM_r(z)[a]^TJ_\alpha z+M_r(z)^TJ_\alpha a=0.\qquad\text{(2.6)}
\]
For
\[
 E_r=M_r(z+a)-M_r(z)-DM_r(z)[a].\qquad\text{(2.7)}
\]
one obtains the exact nonlinear gauge identity
\[
 \boxed{E_r^TJ_\alpha(z+a)=-DM_r(z)[a]^TJ_\alpha a.}\qquad\text{(2.8)}
\]

The R-071 fixture is precisely this mechanism.  Its recorded vector
$n=(-1,0,0,1,-1,0)$ equals $J_D(z+a)$, not an accidental exceptional
nullvector.  Equation (2.8) reproduces the positive leakage slope
\[
 n\cdot\sum_rE_rQ_{\rm II}M_r(z)^Ty
 ={27(6e^2+22e+27)\over400(e+3)^3}>0.\qquad\text{(2.9)}
\]

\section{Inverse-free local completion}

Let $T=T_x$, $H=T^*Q_{\rm II}T\succeq0$, $d\in\mathbb R^6$ denote stacked
terminal-current data in its natural target coordinates, and let
$k\in\mathbb R^6$ be a derivative-displacement coefficient.  For every
$0<\vartheta<1$ and $\tau>0$, completion with the strictly positive matrix
$\vartheta H+\tau I$ gives
\[
\begin{split}
 {1\over2}|d+T b|_Q^2+k\cdot b\ge{}&
 {1-\vartheta\over2}|d+Tb|_Q^2-{\tau\over2}|b|^2
 +{\vartheta\over2}|d|_Q^2\\
 &-{1\over2}(\vartheta T^*Qd+k)^T
 (\vartheta H+\tau I)^{-1}(\vartheta T^*Qd+k).\qquad\text{(3.1)}
\end{split}
\]
No singular frame inverse occurs, and a fraction $1-\vartheta$ of the terminal
square remains.  On an exact gauge direction, however, the last term can grow
like $|k_{\ker}|^2/\tau$ as $\tau\downarrow0$.  Thus $\tau$ must be supplied
by Cameron--Martin energy; (3.1) is an architecture, not by itself a closure.

For the actual frame set
\[
 w_r=M_r(z)^Ty,\qquad k(z,a,y)=\sum_{r=1}^3E_rQ_{\rm II}w_r.\qquad\text{(3.2)}
\]
The R-069 good/bad split gives the inverse-free global estimate
\[
 \boxed{|k(z,a,y)|\le C_*|a|^2|y|.}\qquad\text{(3.3)}
\]
\[
 C_*=24\|Q_{\rm II}\|_{\rm op}(\sqrt{34}+4\sqrt2)
 =1.5397534378598672.\qquad\text{(3.4)}
\]
For completeness, on $|a|\le\theta s_e(z)$ Taylor's formula gives the safe
constant $48\sqrt8\|Q\|/(1-\theta)$.  On the complement, global frame
Lipschitz continuity and $|z|<|a|/\theta$ give
$24\sqrt{34}\|Q\|/\theta$.  They agree at
\[
 \theta={\sqrt{34}\over\sqrt{34}+4\sqrt2}.\qquad\text{(3.5)}
\]
which proves (3.3)--(3.4) uniformly in the positive floor.

\section{Strict-past diagonal one-use theorem}

Fix a high block $j_0\le j\le J$, $N_j=2^j$, and write
\[
 a_j=K_jh_j,\qquad b_{j,i}=D_i a_j,\qquad
 z_{<j}=U_J+A_{<j}.\qquad\text{(4.1)}
\]
Here $h_j$, $a_j$, and $b_j$ are $\mathcal F_{j-1}$-measurable; $U_J$ is the
coherent terminal heat-value variable and is not spatially differentiated.
Put
\[
 E_{r,j}=M_r(z_{<j}+a_j)-M_r(z_{<j})-DM_r(z_{<j})[a_j].\qquad\text{(4.2)}
\]
and, with $G_{j,i}=D_iX_{<j}$,
\[
 \Lambda_j=\sum_{r,i}\int
 \langle M_r(z_{<j})^TG_{j,i},Q_{\rm II}E_{r,j}^Tb_{j,i}\rangle
 \dd x.\qquad\text{(4.3)}
\]

Use full-gradient constants
\[
 \|Da_j\|_2\le C_\nabla N_j^{-1}\|h_j\|_{\cH_j},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Cov}G_j(x)\le C_DN_j.\qquad\text{(4.4)}
\]
The second trace is for the full eighteen-real-component vector: six target
components times three spatial directions.  If R-066's $C_G$ is used one
direction at a time, $C_\nabla=\sqrt3C_G$; the corresponding directional
trace bounds are summed into $C_D$.

Equation (3.3), spatial Holder, and (4.4) give
\[
 |\Lambda_j|\le m_jx_j^{1/2}y_j^{1/3}.\qquad\text{(4.5)}
\]
\[
 x_j=\|h_j\|_{\cH_j}^2,\qquad y_j=\|a_j\|_6^6,\qquad
 m_j=C_*C_\nabla N_j^{-1}\|G_j\|_6.\qquad\text{(4.6)}
\]
Apply sequence Holder once, with exponents $(2,3,6)$:
\[
 \sum_j|\Lambda_j|
 \le X^{1/2}Y_d^{1/3}R_\infty^{1/6}.\qquad\text{(4.7)}
\]
\[
 X=\sum_jx_j,\qquad Y_d=\sum_jy_j,\qquad
 R_\infty=\sum_{j\ge j_0}m_j^6.\qquad\text{(4.8)}
\]
One weighted AM--GM inequality then gives, pathwise,
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{j=j_0}^J|\Lambda_j|
 \le\eta X+\zeta_dY_d+{R_\infty\over432\eta^3\zeta_d^2}.}\qquad\text{(4.9)}
\]
This is not shellwise Young with an arbitrary constant at every scale.  It is
one inequality with one accumulated random constant.

For a centered Gaussian vector $g$ with covariance $\Sigma$,
\[
 \E|g|^6=(\operatorname{Tr}\Sigma)^3
 +6\operatorname{Tr}\Sigma\operatorname{Tr}(\Sigma^2)
 +8\operatorname{Tr}(\Sigma^3)
 \le15(\operatorname{Tr}\Sigma)^3.\qquad\text{(4.10)}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 \E R_\infty\le15L^3(C_*C_\nabla)^6C_D^3
 {8\over7}N_{j_0}^{-3}.}\qquad\text{(4.11)}
\]
Before conversion to the terminal sextic, the deterministic expected
remainder in (4.9) is
\[
 {5\over126}L^3(C_*C_\nabla)^6C_D^3
 \eta^{-3}\zeta_d^{-2}N_{j_0}^{-3}.\qquad\text{(4.12)}
\]
Only a sixth Gaussian moment is used.

\subsection{Exactly one terminal sextic and one cutoff-independent constant}

Pointwise,

 Y_d\le\left\|\left(\sum_j|a_j|^2\right)^{1/2}\right\|_6^6.\qquad\text{(4.13)}

\[
 Y_d\le C_{\rm LP}^6\|A^{\rm hi}\|_6^6.\qquad\text{(4.14)}
\]
For every $\alpha>1$ there is $C_\alpha<\infty$ such that
\[
 |Z-U|^6\le\alpha|Z|^6+C_\alpha|U|^6.\qquad\text{(4.15)}
\]
Choose $\zeta_d=\zeta/(\alpha C_{\rm LP}^6)$.  If
\[
 U_*:=\int\sup_J|U_J(x)|^6\dd x.\qquad\text{(4.16)}
\]
then the vector martingale Doob inequality gives
\[
 \E U_*\le(6/5)^6\E\|U_\infty\|_6^6<\infty.\qquad\text{(4.17)}
\]
Consequently (4.9) spends precisely $\zeta\|Z_J\|_6^6$ and leaves the single
cutoff-independent integrable random constant
\[
 \boxed{
 C_{\eta,\zeta}(\omega)=
 {\alpha^2C_{\rm LP}^{12}\over432\eta^3\zeta^2}R_\infty
 +{\zeta C_\alpha\over\alpha}U_*.}\qquad\text{(4.18)}
\]
No Nelson exponential moment and no Malliavin norm is used.

\subsection{Causalization is expectation-only}

The terminal root in the algebra is $G=D X_J$, not $G_j$.  At each spatial
point condition on $\mathcal F_{j-1}$ enlarged by all future same-point value
variables.  Real-even covariance makes every fresh/future derivative
increment centered and independent of that enlarged value sigma-field.
Thus spatial Fubini gives exactly
\[
 \E\Lambda_j(G)=\E\Lambda_j(G_j).\qquad\text{(4.19)}
\]
Equation (4.19) is not a pathwise replacement and not global field
value--derivative independence.  It fails for progressive within-shell
feedback.

\section{Exact off-diagonal remainder}

The diagonal theorem is not the terminal theorem.  To state the exact gap
without dropping a finite-low boundary, start the high expansion at
\[
 z_0=U_J+A_{<j_0},\qquad
 A^{\rm hi}=\sum_{j=j_0}^Ja_j,\qquad
 C^{\rm hi}=\sum_{\ell=j_0}^Jb_\ell.\qquad\text{(5.1)}
\]
The R-066 finite-low estimate retains the difference between $U_J$ and $z_0$.
For $z_{<j}=z_0+\sum_{j_0\le k<j}a_k$, set
\[
 E_j=M(z_{<j}+a_j)-M(z_{<j})-DM(z_{<j})[a_j].\qquad\text{(5.2)}
\]
\[
 F_{k,j}=[DM(z_{\le k})-DM(z_{<k})][a_j],\qquad k<j.\qquad\text{(5.3)}
\]
Two telescopes give
\[
 \boxed{
 E_{\rm tot}:=M(z_0+A^{\rm hi})-M(z_0)-DM(z_0)[A^{\rm hi}]
 =\sum_jE_j+\sum_{k<j}F_{k,j}.}\qquad\text{(5.4)}
\]

Suppress generator and spatial-direction sums, put
$w_0=M(z_0)^TG$ and $w_{<j}=M(z_{<j})^TG$, and define
\[
\begin{split}
 \cO={}&\sum_j\langle w_0-w_{<j},QE_j^Tb_j\rangle\\
 &+\sum_j\sum_{\ell\ne j}\langle w_0,QE_j^Tb_\ell\rangle\\
 &+\sum_{k<j}\sum_\ell\langle w_0,QF_{k,j}^Tb_\ell\rangle.\qquad\text{(5.5)}
\end{split}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \langle w_0,QE_{\rm tot}^TC^{\rm hi}\rangle
 =\sum_j\langle w_{<j},QE_j^Tb_j\rangle+\cO.}\qquad\text{(5.6)}
\]
The first sum is the matched diagonal covered by Section 4 after (4.19).
The remainder $\cO$ is exact and load-bearing.  In the independent production
fixture,
\[
 \mathrm{LHS}=0.0583409867702,\qquad
 \mathrm{diagonal}=1.42693028427\times10^{-5},\qquad
 \cO=0.0583267174674.\qquad\text{(5.7)}
\]
so $|\cO|/|\mathrm{diagonal}|>4087$.  Calling it a small bookkeeping error is
therefore false.

Terms in (5.5) with $b_\ell$ from a later shell can depend on intervening
derivative innovations.  Applying (4.19) to them before triangular expansion
would require Malliavin derivatives of future controls.  R-069 instead shows
that accumulated past control currents and their mixed currents telescope
when kept together with the terminal square.  The missing compatibility
lemma must prove, without double counting the R-071 linear payment, that
(5.5) either returns to that telescope or satisfies a two-parameter
largest-shell Carleson estimate with the range square retained.

\section{Quantitative discriminator for the next root model}

Let $X=\|A\|_{H^2}^2$ and $Y=\|A\|_6^6$.  For $0\le\sigma\le1$, endpoint
product estimates followed by Sobolev interpolation give
\[
 \boxed{
 \|A^2DA\|_{H^\sigma}
 \le C X^{(1+\sigma)/2}Y^{(2-\sigma)/6}.}\qquad\text{(6.1)}
\]
At $\sigma=0$, use
$\|A^2DA\|_2\le\|A\|_6^2\|DA\|_6$.  At $\sigma=1$, use
$\|A\|_\infty^2\lesssim\|A\|_{H^2}\|A\|_6$ and the
Leibniz rule.  Interpolation proves (6.1).

If a root model has norm $M$ in $H^{-\sigma}$, termwise duality leaves room
for one random constant exactly when $\sigma<1/2$.  Weighted Young then needs
\[
 M^{6/(1-2\sigma)},\qquad
 \eta^{-3(1+\sigma)/(1-2\sigma)},\qquad
 \zeta^{-(2-\sigma)/(1-2\sigma)}.\qquad\text{(6.2)}
\]
At $\sigma=1/2$, (6.1) is the saturated product
$X^{3/4}Y^{1/4}$ and leaves no exponent for an unbounded random norm.
Therefore a raw $H^{-1/2-\delta}$ kernel-root model must gain more than
$\delta$ derivatives from causal structure.  If the gain is $\gamma$, its
required moment is
\[
 \boxed{p={3\over\gamma-\delta},\qquad\gamma>\delta.}\qquad\text{(6.3)}
\]
This is a discriminator for an absolute Sobolev fallback, not a no-go against
signed range-square cancellation.

\section{Why the local fixture is not an integrated counterexample}

On a normalized circle lift the R-071 jet by
\[
 U_N=z+N^{-1}\sin(Ns)y,\qquad
 A_{N,t}=a+tN^{-1}\sin(Ns)n.\qquad\text{(7.1)}
\]
Then $DU_N=\cos(Ns)y$, $DA_{N,t}=t\cos(Ns)n$, and the mean local leakage
retains the affine slope from (2.9).  However
\[
 \|A_{N,t}\|_{H^2}^2
 =6+{3t^2\over2}(N^2+1+N^{-2}).\qquad\text{(7.2)}
\]
while the terminal sextic tends to $|z+a|^6=27$.  The Cameron--Martin cost
therefore grows like $N^2$.  This route does not disprove the integrated
theorem; it confirms that derivative energy is the necessary missing input.
