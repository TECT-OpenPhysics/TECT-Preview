\subsection*{Theorem 2.1 (finite normalized Jacobian)}

In the fixed six-real convention,
\[
 \boxed{
 J_S(x):=DF_S(x)=q_xI+2P_x(S-q_xI)
 =q_xI+2P_xS-2q_xP_x.}                                 \tag{2.3}
\]
Consequently $J_S$ is a polynomial of degrees two and four in the bounded
variable $n_x$, and
\[
 \boxed{J_S(x)^T-J_S(x)=2(SP_x-P_xS).}                 \tag{2.4}
\]
In particular, the Cartan antisymmetric part is the quadratic commutator
$SP_x-P_xS$.

\emph{Proof.}
Since
\[
 Dq_x[h]={2\over\sqrt{d_x}}(S-q_xI)n_x\cdot h,
\]
one has
$DF_S(x)[h]=q_xh+2n_xn_x^T(S-q_xI)h$, which is (2.3).
Taking the transpose cancels both scalar terms and gives (2.4).
The accepted production variance identity also gives
$\|(S-q_xI)n_x\|\le\|n_x\|$, hence $\|J_S(x)\|\le3$.
\hfill$\square$

For $x_-=x$, $x_+=x+a$, write
\[
 n_\pm=n_e(x_\pm),\qquad \delta n=n_+-n_-,\qquad
 \Delta q=q_+-q_-,\qquad \Delta P=P_+-P_-.             \tag{2.5}
\]
Then
\[
 \Delta q=(n_++n_-)^TS\delta n,\qquad
 \Delta P=\delta n\,n_+^T+n_-\delta n^T,               \tag{2.6}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta J
 =\Delta q I+2\Delta P S-2\Delta q P_+-2q_-\Delta P.} \tag{2.7}
\]
Every term in (2.7) contains exactly one $\delta n$ and at most three other
bounded normalized factors.  Thus
\[
 \boxed{\|\Delta J\|\le14|\delta n|.}                 \tag{2.8}
\]
The antisymmetric increment is even simpler:
\[
 {\Delta(J^T-J)\over2}=S\Delta P-\Delta P S.            \tag{2.9}
\]
