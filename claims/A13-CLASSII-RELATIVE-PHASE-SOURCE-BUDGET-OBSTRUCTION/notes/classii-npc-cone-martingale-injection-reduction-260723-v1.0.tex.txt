\section{The retained square is a Jacobi form}

Let $U_0,U_1$ be two smooth target maps into the $R>0$ stratum and let $U_t$
be their pointwise ambient-cone geodesic.  Write $V_t=\partial_tU_t$.  For the
Dirichlet energy
\[
 \cE(U)={1\over2}\sum_i\int|\partial_iU|_g^2\dd x,       \tag{3.1}
\]
the standard second variation is
\[
 {\dd^2\over\dd t^2}\cE(U_t)
 =\sum_i\int\left\{|\nabla_iV_t|_g^2
 -\langle R^g(V_t,\partial_iU_t)\partial_iU_t,V_t\rangle_g
 \right\}\dd x.                                          \tag{3.2}
\]
Equation (2.6) makes the integrand nonnegative.  Taylor's formula with
integral remainder therefore gives the exact secant decomposition
\[
 \cE(U_1)-\cE(U_0)=\mathfrak F(U_0,U_1)
                                  +\mathfrak J(U_0,U_1),   \tag{3.3}
\]
\[
 \mathfrak F=\sum_i\int
       \langle\partial_iU_0,\nabla_iV_0\rangle_g\dd x,     \tag{3.4}
\]
\[
\mathfrak J=\sum_i\int_0^1(1-t)\int
\left\{|\nabla_iV_t|_g^2
-\langle R^g(V_t,\partial_iU_t)\partial_iU_t,V_t\rangle_g
\right\}\dd x\dd t\ge0.                                  \tag{3.5}
\]
In fact the remainder is quantitatively stronger.  The pointwise geodesic is
constant speed, so $|V_t|_g=d_g(U_0,U_1)$ is independent of $t$.  Kato's
inequality and the curvature sign give
\[
 |\nabla_iV_t|_g^2
 -\langle R^g(V_t,\partial_iU_t)\partial_iU_t,V_t\rangle_g
 \ge|\partial_i d_g(U_0,U_1)|^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak J(U_0,U_1)
 \ge {1\over2}\|\nabla d_g(U_0,U_1)\|_2^2.}                \tag{3.6}
\]

Apply this pointwise for every target-space heat dummy $\xi$ in the previous
backward-heat representation.  Wherever the smooth formula is legitimate,
the remaining raw secant is
\[
\boxed{\cR_j=\mathfrak F_j+\mathfrak J_j,\qquad
        \mathfrak J_j\ge0.}                               \tag{3.7}
\]
This identifies the positive Gauss--Newton square and the signed Cartan term
as one geometric Jacobi remainder.  It also explains why deleting the square
was fatal.  CAT(0) convexity supplies a metric-space extension of the energy
inequality, but the exact stochastic theorem still needs an approximation at
$R=0$ and a control-displacement comparison.  Neither is hidden in (3.6).

Most importantly, (3.7) does not permit one to discard $\mathfrak J_j$ and
estimate $\mathfrak F_j$ shell by shell.  The latter is the same critical
relative-phase source channel isolated earlier.  The favorable curvature
must remain coupled to the signed first variation in the global sum.

\section{Restricted adapted resonances are summable}

 \ge-{9a^2C^2X^4\over64\eta\kappa}.                       \tag{6.3}

Since $\E X^4=3v_N^2$, the expected loss is $O(N^{-8})$ per
