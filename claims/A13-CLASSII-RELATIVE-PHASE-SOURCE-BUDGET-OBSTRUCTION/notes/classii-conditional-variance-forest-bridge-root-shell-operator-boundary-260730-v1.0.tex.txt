\section*{1. Purpose and scope}

R-124 isolated one coefficient-level gate: match the R-121 covariance-normal
endpoint, the R-123 primitive trace, and the R-063 forest exactly once before
attempting a correlated root-shell estimate.  Three independent proof routes
were run against that gate.  Their common conclusion corrects the proposed
three-object equality: conditional future averaging creates a load-bearing
variance rebate.  Omitting it fails on a deterministic production fixture.

The corrected identity is exact at finite cutoff.  It also exposes the exact
owner-resolved stationary-baseline residual and a sufficient common-terminal
Doob criterion under which the complete low-plus-root aggregate is
nonpositive.  A root-only upper bound additionally needs a nonnegative
complete-low stationary atom.  The production middle-current family is root
dependent, so neither extra hypothesis nor the required stationary-baseline
upper bound is proved here.  Individual stationary atoms need not vanish or
have one sign.

A separate Hilbert-matrix lemma gives a sharp quantitative acceptance test for
the remaining correlated root-shell operator.  It uses all root and shell
sums inside expectation and requires no separated fifth moment, no
$10/3$ moment, and no BMO estimate.  The production total-symbol
factorisation and its decay are not proved.

\section*{3. Exact conditional-variance/covariance-normal bridge}

\subsection*{Theorem 3.1 (primitive trace and future-variance rebate)}

Under (2.1)--(2.8), at each endpoint
\[
 \sum_\cC\Theta_{u,\cC}
 =\E_f\sum_i\int (P_\Sigma B)(U_u):
   (\Gamma_{\rm own})_i\,\dd x,                         \tag{3.1}
\]
and
\[
 \E_f\sum_i\int (P_\Sigma B)(U_u):V_{u,i}^{\otimes2}\,\dd x
 =\sum_\cC\|\Phi_{u,\cC}\|^2+\cV_u.                  \tag{3.2}
\]
Consequently the covariance-normal endpoint $\cE_u^{\rm CN}$ satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \E_f\cE_u^{\rm CN}
 =\frac12\sum_\cC
   (\|\Phi_{u,\cC}\|^2-\Theta_{u,\cC})
  +\frac12\cV_u.}                                      \tag{3.3}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
The orthogonal resolution (2.3), Parseval, and (2.8) give
\[
 \sum_\cC\E_{f,G_{\rm own}'}
 \|P_\cC C_6(W)\partial G_{\rm own}'\|^2
 =\E_f\Tr(C_6(W)\Gamma_{\rm own}C_6(W)^*)
 =\E_f B(W):\Gamma_{\rm own}.
\]
Heat integration and the spatial derivative sum give (3.1).  Conditional
Pythagoras gives
\[
 \E_f\|P_\cC J_u\|^2
 =\|\Phi_{u,\cC}\|^2
  +\E_f\|P_\cC J_u-\Phi_{u,\cC}\|^2.
\]
Summing proves (3.2), and subtracting (3.1) from (3.2) proves (3.3).
\hfill$\square$

For the moving-endpoint secant define
\[
 \Psi_\cC=\Delta\Theta_\cC
 -\operatorname{Re}\langle
 \Phi_{h,\cC}+\Phi_{0,\cC},\Delta\Phi_\cC\rangle.     \tag{3.4}
\]
For the fixed root owner of Sections 2--4, R-121 gives the complete
moving-endpoint visit telescope.  Taking the difference of (3.3) therefore
yields
\[
 \boxed{
 \E_f\sum_v(\mathcal R_{Q,v}+\mathcal M_{U,v}+\mathcal K_{R,v})
 ={1\over2}\left(\Delta\cV-\sum_\cC\Psi_\cC\right).}  \tag{3.5}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_\cC\Psi_\cC
 =\Delta\cV
 -2\E_f\sum_v(\mathcal R_{Q,v}+\mathcal M_{U,v}+\mathcal K_{R,v}).}
                                                               \tag{3.6}
\]
Every intermediate visit endpoint cancels.  Thus (3.6) has no visit-count
factor and descends through every legal representation-preserving same-root
subdivision.

If (2.3) is not exhaustive, (3.5) gains $\Delta\mathcal L/2$, where
\[
 \mathcal L_u=
 \E_f\langle J_u,(I-\sum P_\cC)J_u\rangle
 -\E_{f,G_{\rm own}'}
  \langle T_u,(I-\sum P_\cC)T_u\rangle.               \tag{3.7}
\]
Contraction closure alone does not make (3.7) vanish.

\section*{4. Finite-cutoff adapted partial-Wick coordinate}

Let $Y_\lambda$ be fresh-root linear coordinates and put
\[
 Q_{\lambda\mu}=Y_\lambda Y_\mu-\Gamma_{\lambda\mu}.  \tag{4.1}
\]
For a smooth cylindrical symmetric coefficient $F_{\lambda\mu}$, directional

\[
 \boxed{
 F:Q=\sum_{\lambda,\mu}\left[
 F_{\lambda\mu}\diamond_jQ_{\lambda\mu}
 +Y_\lambda D_\mu F_{\lambda\mu}
 +Y_\mu D_\lambda F_{\lambda\mu}
 -D_\lambda D_\mu F_{\lambda\mu}\right].}           \tag{4.2}
\]
Indeed, apply $F\diamond_jY=FY-D_YF$ twice and use Wick associativity.

For visit $v$, take $F=B_v-B_{v-1}$ and define
\[
 \Forest_{RQ,v}^{\rm ad}
 =(B_v-B_{v-1}):Q_{v-1}=2\mathcal R_{Q,v}.             \tag{4.3}

Then (3.6) becomes the fully owner-resolved finite-cutoff formula
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_\cC\Psi_\cC
 =\Delta\cV
 -\E_f\sum_v\Forest_{RQ,v}^{\rm ad}
 -2\E_f\sum_v(\mathcal M_{U,v}+\mathcal K_{R,v}).}    \tag{4.4}

Writing the fixed-root complete covariance-normal endpoint without its outer

 \boxed{\Psi=\Delta\cV-\Delta\Forest_{063}^{\rm ad}.}       \tag{4.5}

For $F=B(Z_*)$, chain rule gives

\section*{6. Owner-resolved stationary-baseline residual}

After every representation-preserving same-root visit has been recombined,
the stationary current is a root family $J_{0,k}$, not in general the
projections of one common terminal current.  Root $k$ uses its once-owned
covariance increment $\Delta\Gamma_k$, while the complete-low covariance
$\Gamma_\ell$ remains a distinct owner:
\[
 \Gamma_\ell+\sum_k\Delta\Gamma_k=\Gamma_{\rm target}.          \tag{6.1}
\]
A same-root subdivision creates no new covariance owner: it keeps
$\Delta\Gamma_k$ fixed and sums only moving-endpoint differences.  Repeating
$\Gamma_{\rm target}$ at each root, or adding $\Delta\Gamma_k$ as a fresh
primitive trace at each same-root subvisit, duplicates the trace.  Let
\[
 \Phi_{0,k,\cC}=\E_f[P_\cC J_{0,k}],\qquad
 \cV_{0,k,\cC}=\E_f\|P_\cC J_{0,k}-\Phi_{0,k,\cC}\|^2,          \tag{6.2}
\]
and let $\Theta_{0,k,\cC}$ use an independent derivative with covariance
$\Delta\Gamma_k$.  Define the actual stationary atom by
\[
 S_{0,k,\cC}=\Theta_{0,k,\cC}-\|\Phi_{0,k,\cC}\|^2.             \tag{6.3}
\]

\subsection*{Proposition 6.1 (exact production residual)}

For an exhaustive orthogonal output partition, Theorem 3.1 applied root by
root gives
\[
 \boxed{
 S_{0,k,\cC}
 =\cV_{0,k,\cC}-2\E_f\cE^{\rm CN}_{0,k,\cC}.}                  \tag{6.4}
\]
Define the full, visit-recombined, no-outer-half root reconstruction by
\[
 \Forest^{\rm ad}_{063,0,k}:=2\E_f\cE^{\rm CN}_{0,k}.             \tag{6.4a}
\]
Under the exhaustive orthogonal partition this equals
$2\sum_\cC\E_f\cE^{\rm CN}_{0,k,\cC}$.  The complete stationary quantity is
therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 B_{J,\pi}:=\sum_{k,\cC}\E S_{0,k,\cC}
 =\sum_{k,\cC}\E\cV_{0,k,\cC}
  -\sum_k\E\Forest^{\rm ad}_{063,0,k}.}                    \tag{6.5}
\]
Here $J_{0,k}$ is visit recombined and $\cE^{\rm CN}_{0,k}$ uses the same
once-owned $\Delta\Gamma_k$ as $\Theta_{0,k}$.  For a nonexhaustive partition,
the exact formula is
\[
 B_{J,\pi}=\sum_{k,\cC}\E\cV_{0,k,\cC}
 -\sum_k\E\Forest^{\rm ad}_{063,0,k}
 +\sum_k\E\mathcal L_{0,k}.                            \tag{6.5a}
\]
Formula (6.5) or (6.5a) identifies the precise residual; it does not determine
its sign or give a cutoff-uniform upper bound.

\subsection*{Lemma 6.2 (conditional common-terminal low-plus-root diagnostic)}

Assume additionally that there is one stationary terminal current $J_0$ such
that the complete low output and all centered root/cluster outputs form an
orthogonal exhaustive Doob decomposition, and that the once-owned low and
root traces form the matching covariance partition.  Put
\[
 b_{0,k,\cC}=\E[\Phi_{0,k,\cC}\mid\cF_{k-1}],\qquad
 Y_{0,k,\cC}=\Phi_{0,k,\cC}-b_{0,k,\cC}.              \tag{6.6}
\]
The two additional energy identities are
\[
 \E\|J_0\|^2=\sum_\cC\E\|Y_{0,\ell,\cC}\|^2
 +\sum_{k,\cC}\E\|Y_{0,k,\cC}\|^2,                 \tag{6.7}
\]
\[
 \E\|J_0\|^2=\sum_\cC\E\Theta_{0,\ell,\cC}
 +\sum_{k,\cC}\E\Theta_{0,k,\cC}.                   \tag{6.8}
\]
Then, with $b_{0,\ell,\cC}=0$,
\[
 \sum_\cC\E D_{0,\ell,\cC}+
 \sum_{k,\cC}\E D_{0,k,\cC}=0,                     \tag{6.9}
\]
and
\[
 B^{\rm full}_{J,\pi}:=\sum_\cC\E S_{0,\ell,\cC}+B_{J,\pi}
 =-\sum_{k,\cC}\E\|b_{0,k,\cC}\|^2\le0.           \tag{6.10}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 B_{J,\pi}=-\sum_{k,\cC}\E\|b_{0,k,\cC}\|^2
 -\sum_\cC\E S_{0,\ell,\cC}.                         \tag{6.11}
\]
Thus the displayed common-terminal hypotheses alone do not imply the
root-only R-124 bound $B_{J,\pi}\le0$ or $C_0=0$.  That corollary additionally
requires $\sum_\cC\E S_{0,\ell,\cC}\ge0$, including the special case of
equality.  Neither the common-terminal identification nor this complete-low
sign is proved for production, so R-124 equation (7.3) remains open.

Under the additional hypotheses of Lemma 6.2, neither atom need vanish or
have one sign.  The following common-terminal fixture has
$S_{0,\ell}=0$ and is diagnostic only; it is not the production
stationary-baseline theorem.  Let $u_k,v_k$ be
independent $N(0,1/N)$ variables,
$U=\sum u_k$, $V=\sum v_k$, $J=UV$, and reveal the first $k$ pairs.  With
$\Theta_k=1/N$,
\[
 \E\|\Phi_k\|^2={k^2\over N^2},\quad
 \E\|b_k\|^2={(k-1)^2\over N^2},\quad
 \E\|Y_k\|^2={2k-1\over N^2}.                        \tag{6.12}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^N D_{0,k}=0,qquad
 \sum_{k=1}^N S_{0,k}
 =-{(N-1)(2N-1)\over6N}\le0.                         \tag{6.13}
\]
At $N=2$, the two $S_0$ atoms are $1/4$ and $-1/2$; only their diagnostic
aggregate is $-1/4$.  Positive-part or absolute-value summation destroys this
conditional cancellation and therefore cannot prove the production bound.

\section*{8. Correlated root-shell operator criterion}

\subsection*{Theorem 8.1 (far-tail Hilbert operator budget)}

Suppose that only after complete cluster, owner, variance, and forest
recombination, the adverse far term has the form
\[
 T_{\rm far}=\E\operatorname{Re}
 \sum_{j\ge j_0}\sum_{k>j}\langle x_k,A_{kj}y_j\rangle, \tag{8.1}
\]
where, pathwise,
\[
 \sum_k\|x_k\|^2\le X,\qquad
 \sum_j\|y_j\|^2\le Y,                                \tag{8.2}
\]
and
\[
 \|A_{kj}\|_{\rm op}\le C_*2^{j-4k}.                 \tag{8.3}
\]
Then
\[
 \|A\|_{\rm op}\le K_{\rm far}
 :={8C_*\over\sqrt{16065}}2^{-3j_0},                  \tag{8.4}
\]
and, for every $\eta_f>0$,
\[
 \boxed{
 T_{\rm far}\le2\eta_f\E X
 +{K_{\rm far}^2\over8\eta_f}\E Y.}                 \tag{8.5}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
The scalar dominating matrix has
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|A\|_{\rm op}^2
 &\le\sum_{j\ge j_0}\sum_{k>j}C_*^2 2^{2j-8k}\\
 &={64C_*^2\over16065}2^{-6j_0}.
 \end{split}                                           \tag{8.6}
\]
Equations (8.1)--(8.2), Cauchy in the combined root-shell Hilbert space, and
one scalar Young inequality prove (8.5). \hfill$\square$

For an actual total operator, the sharp mixed-form criterion is
\[
                    \|A\|_{\rm op}\le4\sqrt{\eta\zeta}.          \tag{8.7}
\]
Since (8.4) is a Hilbert--Schmidt upper bound, the check
$K_{\rm far}<4\sqrt{\eta\zeta}$ is sufficient, not necessary.  The full
R-124 trace budgets give
\[
 4\sqrt{{9\over20}{3\over20}}={3\sqrt3\over5}.        \tag{8.8}
\]
At action normalization the corresponding coefficient is
$3\sqrt3/10$.  Retaining the disjoint R-103 debts and the R-124 legal-row
source allocation gives the stricter trace threshold
\[
 4\sqrt{\left({197\over440}-{3\over125P}\right){3\over25}}
 =0.92093233\ldots.                                    \tag{8.9}
\]

The decay (8.3) is not a theorem for the production symbol.  R-093's
$2^{j-4k}$ law is diagnostic, and R-074 forbids importing it into an
unsubtracted coefficient.  The missing analytic theorem must establish
(8.2)--(8.3) for the complete signed symbol
\[
                 \Delta\cV-\Delta\Forest_{063}^{\rm ad}     \tag{8.10}
\]
and treat the balanced coefficient-dominant band separately.
