\section*{2. Exact mixed-endpoint innovation compression}
\hypertarget{section-2-innovation-compression}{}

Fix one completely recombined owner and one common future conditional
projection $P_f=\E[\cdot\mid\cF_f]$.  At every endpoint $q$, put
\[
 \Phi_q=P_fJ_q,\qquad R_q=(\Id-P_f)J_q.              \tag{2.1}
\]
For arbitrary endpoints $q,p$, orthogonality to the same conditional
subspace gives
\[
 \E_f\ip{J_q}{J_p}
 =\ip{\Phi_q}{\Phi_p}+\E_f\ip{R_q}{R_p}.             \tag{2.2}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \E_f\{-\ip{J_q}{J_p}+\ip{R_q}{R_p}\}
 =-\ip{\Phi_q}{\Phi_p}. \;}                         \tag{2.3}
\]
Thus the R-141 signature
\[
 (U,J,R),\qquad S_3=\operatorname{diag}(+\Id,-\Id,+\Id)
\]
has the exact compressed mixed kernel
\[
 K(q,p)=\E_f\ip{U_q}{U_p}-\ip{\Phi_q}{\Phi_p}
       =\E_f\ip{F_q}{S_2F_p},                       \tag{2.4}
\]
where
\[
 F_q=(U_q,\Phi_q),\qquad S_2=\operatorname{diag}(+\Id,-\Id).
\]
This is a recombination, not a deletion.  In particular, the innovation
product identified in R-102 becomes part of the derivatives of $\Phi$.
Using different projections at $q$ and $p$, or applying (2.3) pointwise
before future expectation, is invalid.
