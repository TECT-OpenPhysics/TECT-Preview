cells to one complete signed terminal-minus-prefix trace-excess secant.  This
removes the R-138 reveal-to-insertion reanchoring factor, but it does not by
itself estimate the resulting production response.  The present checkpoint

The first result is a predictable triangular coanalysis--Hardy theorem.  It

obstruction.  The missing production input is an owner-complete conditional

\section*{3. The predictable triangular one-use theorem}

\textbf{Theorem 3.1.}  Assume (2.3) and
\[
 \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{r,\omega}\|A_r^C(\omega)\|
       \le\Lambda_C.                                \tag{3.1}
\]
Then
\[
\begin{split}
 &\sum_r\sum_{m\ge r+C}2^{2\gamma(m-r-C)}
 \E\left\|\sum_{k>r}T_{r,m,k}d_rh_k\right\|^2\\
 &\hspace{18mm}\le \Lambda_C^2
   \sum_r\sum_{k>r}\E\|d_rh_k\|^2\\
 &\hspace{18mm}=\Lambda_C^2\sum_k
   \E\|h_k-P_\ell h_k\|^2
 \le\Lambda_C^2 X_{\rm src}.
\end{split}                                           \tag{3.2}
\]

\textbf{Proof.}  Apply (3.1) pointwise to $x_r$, integrate, and sum over
$r$.  Exchange the two finite source sums.  Since $h_k$ is
$\F_{k-1}$-measurable, the increments $d_rh_k$, $\ell<r<k$, are orthogonal;
therefore
\[
 \sum_{\ell<r<k}\E\|d_rh_k\|^2
       =\E\|h_k-P_\ell h_k\|^2.                    \tag{3.3}
\]
This is the only Doob-square use.  No insertionwise positive square and no
factor $2^{2\gamma(k-r)}$ is introduced. \hfill$\square$

The same proof applies directly to the full signed trace-excess when it has
an owner-complete quadratic representation
\[
 S_C=\sum_r\E\ip{x_r}{Q_r^Cx_r},\qquad
             Q_r^C\preceq\Lambda_C^2\Id.            \tag{3.4}
\]
Then $S_C\le\Lambda_C^2X_{\rm src}$ and R-139 gives
\[
 \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{fut}}^C\ge-{\Lambda_C^2\over2}X_{\rm src}.
                                                               \tag{3.5}
\]
Thus $\Lambda_C^2$ is an adverse source-Hessian debt, not a new positive
reserve.  The radial Taylor action receives its one-half, not a second copy.

\section*{7. Exact source-metric graph--Feshbach theorem}

Let $\cS,\cY,\cL$ be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces.  Let $E,F$ be
self-adjoint, $D\succeq0$, and let
$A_0:\cY\to\cS$, $B:\cL\to\cS$, $C_0:\cL\to\cY$.  Write
\[
 M_2=\begin{pmatrix}E&-A_0/2\\-A_0^*/2&F\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad K=\binom{B}{C_0}.                              \tag{7.1}
\]
For production tangents $V:\cS\to\cY$ and
$\Lambda:\cS\to\cL$, put
\[
 Zu=(u,Vu),\qquad \widehat Gu=(u,Vu,\Lambda u).        \tag{7.2}
\]
Let $P_0$ project onto $\Ker D$, $P_+=\Id-P_0$, and let $W$ be the reduced
solution
\[
 D^{1/2}W=P_+K^*Z.                                    \tag{7.3}
\]
Define
\[
 N=P_0K^*Z,\qquad \Delta=D^{1/2}\Lambda-W,
 \qquad \Lambda_0=P_0\Lambda.                        \tag{7.4}
\]

\textbf{Theorem 7.1.}  For
\[
 \mathcal M(T)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 E&-(A_0+T)/2&-B\\
 -(A_0+T)^*/2&F&-C_0\\
 -B^*&-C_0^*&D
 \end{pmatrix},                                      \tag{7.5}
\]
one has the exact zero-tail identity
\[
\begin{split}
 \ip{\widehat Gu}{\mathcal M(0)\widehat Gu}
 ={}&\ip{Zu}{M_2Zu}-\|Wu\|^2+\|\Delta u\|^2\\
 &-2\Rea\ip{Nu}{\Lambda_0u}.
\end{split}                                           \tag{7.6}
\]
Consequently, for every $T:\cY\to\cS$ with $\|T\|\le\tau$,
\[
 \ip{\widehat Gu}{\mathcal M(T)\widehat Gu}
       \ge\mu\|u\|^2                               \tag{7.7}
\]
for every $u$ if and only if the right side of (7.6) satisfies
\[
 \ip{Zu}{M_2Zu}-\|Wu\|^2+\|\Delta u\|^2
 -2\Rea\ip{Nu}{\Lambda_0u}
 \ge\tau\|u\|\|Vu\|+\mu\|u\|^2.                 \tag{7.8}
\]
The product term is sharp, attained pointwise by an aligned rank-one tail.

\textbf{Proof.}  Expand the low cross as
$-2\Rea\ip{K^*Zu}{\Lambda u}+\|D^{1/2}\Lambda u\|^2$.
Complete the square on $P_+\cL$ and retain the $P_0\cL$ cross.  This gives
(7.6).  The tail contribution is $-\Rea\ip{u}{TVu}$; optimizing it over the
operator-norm ball gives exactly $-\tau\|u\|\|Vu\|$. \hfill$\square$

If the graph-Douglas compatibility
\[
 K^*Z(\cS)\subseteq\Ran D^{1/2}                       \tag{7.9}
\]
holds, then $N=0$.  Without (7.9), deleting the last term of (7.6) is false.
The executable includes a semidefinite fixture in which the true value is
$-15$ while the formula with the kernel cross omitted gives $15$.

\section*{8. Sharp scalar source-gap corollary}

Assume now
\[
 D\succeq d\Id,\quad E\succeq e\Id,\quad F\succeq f\Id,
 \quad\|K\|\le k,\quad\|A_0\|\le a_0,
 \quad \sigma={k^2\over d}.                          \tag{8.1}
\]
If $f>\sigma$, then (7.8) is implied by
\[
 \boxed{\mu_{\rm src}
 =e-\sigma-{(a_0+\tau)^2\over4(f-\sigma)}>0.}        \tag{8.2}
\]
Indeed, discard $\|\Delta u\|^2$ and use
\[
 (e-\sigma)\|u\|^2+(f-\sigma)\|Vu\|^2
       -(a_0+\tau)\|u\|\|Vu\|.                    \tag{8.3}
\]
Minimization in $\|Vu\|/\|u\|$ gives (8.2).  Equivalently,
\[
 a_0+\tau<2\sqrt{(e-\sigma)(f-\sigma)}.              \tag{8.4}
\]
For a prescribed source gap $\mu$, only the source diagonal is shifted:
\[
 a_0+\tau\le2\sqrt{(e-\sigma-\mu)(f-\sigma)}.       \tag{8.5}
\]
Shifting $F$ or $D$ by $\mu$ would demand the stronger ambient gap and is
not equivalent to (7.7).  The elementary identity
\[
 4x^2+9y^2-6xy=3x^2+(3y-x)^2                       \tag{8.6}
\]
has source gap $3$ but fails after subtracting $3$ from both diagonals.

Within the norm-data class, (8.2) is sharp.  Take scalar aligned $A_0,T$,
choose $K$ and $D$ so the Schur loss is $\sigma$, put
\[
 Vu={a_0+\tau\over2(f-\sigma)}u,                    \tag{8.7}
\]
and choose $\Lambda u$ as the low Schur minimizer.  Equality is attained;
at $\mu_{\rm src}=0$ there is a kernel.  This is a sharpness fixture, not a
counterexample to a production estimate with stronger structural data.
