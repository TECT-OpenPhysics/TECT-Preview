\section{Exact support floors}

The phase minimum is attained at $\cos\phi=-1$.  R-113 proved the two
identities
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_-={}&(\rho+2\sigma)^2-\left(1+{b\over2}\right)
 (\rho+2\sigma)+(5b+1)\sigma\\
&+6\rho\left(\sqrt\sigma-{\sqrt b\over2}\right)^2-{b\over2},
\end{aligned}                                         \tag{2.1}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_-={}&\rho^2+(b/10-1)\rho+4\sigma^2+(4b-1)\sigma\\
&+10\rho\left(\sqrt\sigma-{3\sqrt b\over10}\right)^2-{b\over2}.
\end{aligned}                                         \tag{2.2}
\]
Completing the remaining one-variable quadratics yields the valid floors
\[
 F_b\ge-\beta_6(b),\qquad
 \beta_6={b^2+12b+4\over16},                          \tag{2.3}
\]
and
\[
 F_b\ge-\beta_{10}(b),\qquad
 \beta_{10}={b\over2}+{(1-b/10)_+^2\over4}
                         +{(1-4b)_+^2\over16}.        \tag{2.4}
\]
The polynomial branches used below are
\[
 \beta_{10,a}={401b^2-20b+125\over400}
       \quad(0\le b\le1/4),                           \tag{2.5}
\]
\[
 \beta_{10,b}={b^2+180b+100\over400}
       \quad(1/4\le b<10).                            \tag{2.6}
\]
They agree at $b=1/4$.  All selected floors are positive, so every division
below is legal.  At the transition to the new floor,
\[
 \beta_{10,b}(3/2)={1489\over1600}.                   \tag{2.7}
\]

There is a sharper floor on the large-$b$ side.  Put $y=\sqrt\sigma$.  A
direct expansion of the phase minimum gives
\[
 F_-+{b\over2}=\rho^2+a_b(y)\rho
       +y^2(4y^2+4b-1),                              \tag{2.8}
\]
\[
 a_b(y)=10y^2-6\sqrt b\,y+b-1.                       \tag{2.9}
\]
For $b\ge6/5$, define
\[
 q_b(y)=a_b(y)+2y\sqrt{4b-1}.                        \tag{2.10}
\]
Its discriminant as a quadratic in $y$ is
\[
 12\{b+3-2\sqrt{b(4b-1)}\}.                          \tag{2.11}
\]
This is strictly negative on $b\ge6/5$: all quantities being squared are
positive, and
\[
 4b(4b-1)-(b+3)^2
 =15b^2-10b-9
 ={3\over5}+{(5b-6)(15b+8)\over5}>0.                \tag{2.12}
\]
Thus $q_b(y)>0$.  If $a_b(y)\ge0$, every term in (2.8) is nonnegative.  If
$a_b(y)<0$, minimization over $\rho\ge0$ gives
\[
 y^2(4y^2+4b-1)-{a_b(y)^2\over4}
 ={(2\sqrt d-a_b(y))(2\sqrt d+a_b(y))\over4}>0,
 \quad d=y^2(4y^2+4b-1),                             \tag{2.13}
\]
because $2\sqrt d+a_b(y)\ge q_b(y)>0$.  At $y=0$ the right side of
(2.8) is $\rho(\rho+b-1)$.  Consequently
\[
 \boxed{F_b\ge-{b\over2}\quad(b\ge6/5)},            \tag{2.14}
\]
with equality only at $\rho=\sigma=0$.  The floor is therefore sharp (an
attained support minimum), not merely a convenient estimate.  The rational
cut $6/5$ is a safe proof cutoff; it is not asserted to be the optimal onset
of the floor.  Below we use
\[
 \beta_\sharp(b)={b\over2}                            \tag{2.15}
\]
on the two new slabs.

\section{The support--two-moment majorant}

\subsection{Hermite reduction}

\textbf{Lemma 3.1.}  Let $Y$ be centered, $Y\ge-\beta$ almost surely,
$\beta>0$, and $V=\E Y^2>0$.  For $t\ge0$, define
\[
 r={\beta^2+V\over\beta},\qquad
 p={\beta^2\over\beta^2+V}.                          \tag{3.1}
\]
Then
\[
 \E e^{-tY}\le e^{t\beta}\{1-p+pe^{-tr}\}.          \tag{3.2}
\]

\emph{Proof.}  Set $Z=Y+\beta\ge0$.  Let $H$ be the quadratic Hermite
interpolant of $f(z)=e^{-tz}$ at the nodes $0,r,r$.  For every $z\ge0$,
the Hermite remainder is
\[
 f(z)-H(z)={f'''(\xi)\over3!}\,z(z-r)^2\le0,          \tag{3.3}
\]
because $f'''<0$.  The two-point law with mass $p$ at $r$ and mass $1-p$
at zero has the same first two moments as $Z$:
\[
 pr=\beta,\qquad pr^2=\beta^2+V.                     \tag{3.4}
\]
Since the expectation of a quadratic depends only on these moments,
$\E H(Z)=1-p+pe^{-tr}$.  Multiplication by $e^{t\beta}$ proves (3.2).
\hfill$\square$

Put
\[
 \Phi(t)=t\beta+\log(1-p+pe^{-tr}),\qquad
 p_t={pe^{-rt}\over1-p+pe^{-rt}}.                    \tag{3.5}
\]
Then $\Phi(0)=\Phi'(0)=0$ and
\[
 \Phi''(t)=r^2p_t(1-p_t).                             \tag{3.6}
\]
If $\beta^2\le V$, then $p\le1/2$ and
$0\le p_t\le p\le1/2$.  Hence
\[
 \Phi''(t)\le r^2p(1-p)=V,\qquad
 \Phi(t)\le {Vt^2\over2}.                            \tag{3.7}
\]

\subsection{A self-contained centered-Bernoulli bound}

For completeness, we prove the only non-polynomial estimate used in the
other case.  Define the centered Bernoulli log moment-generating function by
\[
 g(\lambda)=\log\{(1-p)e^{p\lambda}+p e^{-(1-p)\lambda}\}.
\]
It satisfies
\[
 g(\lambda)\le
 {1-2p\over4\log((1-p)/p)}\lambda^2                  \tag{3.8}
\]
for $0<p<1$, with the continuous value $1/8$ at $p=1/2$.

By exchanging the atoms and reversing $\lambda$, assume $p<1/2$.  Put
$m=1-2p$, $a=\atanh m$, and $u=-\lambda/2$.  Direct algebra gives
\[
 g(\lambda)=mu+\log\cosh(u-a)-\log\cosh a.           \tag{3.9}
\]
Thus (3.8) is equivalent to $h(u)\ge0$, where
\[
 h(u)={mu^2\over2a}-mu-\log\cosh(u-a)+\log\cosh a.  \tag{3.10}
\]
Writing $v=(u-a)/a$ gives
\[
 h'(u)=mv-\tanh(av).                                  \tag{3.11}
\]
The function $\tanh z/z$ decreases for $z>0$, because
$z\operatorname{sech}^2z-\tanh z<0$.  Consequently $h'$ has signs
$-,+,-,+$ on the four intervals cut by $v=-1,0,1$.  The two global minima
are $u=0$ and $u=2a$, and direct substitution gives $h=0$ at both.  The
case $p=1/2$ follows by the limit, equivalently $\log\cosh u\le u^2/2$.

Return now to (3.5).  If $\beta^2\ge V$, set
\[
 y={\beta^2-V\over\beta^2+V}\in[0,1).                \tag{3.12}
\]
Equation (3.8), after rescaling by $r$, gives
\[
 \Phi(t)\le {r^2y\over8\atanh y}\,t^2.              \tag{3.13}
\]
Since
\[
 \atanh y\ge y+{y^3\over3},                          \tag{3.14}
\]
it is sufficient that
\[
 r^2\le2K\left(1+{y^2\over3}\right).                \tag{3.15}
\]
The derivative of the difference in (3.14) is
$y^4/(1-y^2)\ge0$, so no external inequality is being imported.

\section{Polynomial certificate and cone theorem}

For any selected floor $\beta=\beta(b)$, define
\[
 Q_\beta=V-\beta^2,                                   \tag{4.1}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
 S_\beta={}&6K\beta^2(\beta^2+V)^2
 +2K\beta^2(\beta^2-V)^2\\
 &-3(\beta^2+V)^4.
\end{aligned}                                         \tag{4.2}
\]
Clearing the positive denominator in (3.15) gives the exact identity
\[
 S_\beta=3\beta^2(\beta^2+V)^2
 \left\{2K\left(1+{y^2\over3}\right)-r^2\right\}.  \tag{4.3}
\]
At a point with $Q_\beta\ge0$, (3.7) applies.  At a point with
$Q_\beta<0$, condition $S_\beta>0$ implies (3.15).  It is therefore enough
to cover each rectangle by a high-$c$ certificate $Q_\beta>0$ and a low-$c$
certificate $S_\beta>0$.  The low-$c$ region need not have
$\beta^2\ge V$ everywhere: where it does not, (3.7) already applies.

After affine transport to $[0,1]^2$, every polynomial is written in the
tensor Bernstein basis.  If
\[
 P(u,v)=\sum_{m,n}a_{mn}u^mv^n
\]
has bidegree $(d,e)$, its Bernstein coefficients are
\[
 \widehat P_{ij}=\sum_{m\le i,\,n\le j}
 a_{mn}{\binom{i}{m}\over\binom{d}{m}}
       {\binom{j}{n}\over\binom{e}{n}}.              \tag{4.4}
\]
Strict positivity of all coefficients proves strict positivity on the whole
closed rectangle.

The exact cover is as follows.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\text{slab}&b\text{-interval}&\beta& S_\beta\text{ region}
 &Q_\beta\text{ region}\\ \hline
I_1&[0,1/10]&\beta_6&c\in[0,1/2]&c\in[1/2,1]\\
I_2&[1/10,1/4]&\beta_{10,a}&c\in[0,1/2]&c\in[1/2,1]\\

For $I_1,I_2,I_3$, each $S$ rectangle has $289$ bidegree-$(16,16)$

For $I_4$, the low-$c$ interval is divided into the eight closed cells

The $Q$ and $S$ bidegrees are respectively $(2,4)$ and $(8,16)$, so $I_6$

\textbf{Theorem 4.1 (closed physical cone).}  For every
$c\in[0,1]$, $\tau>0$, and $0\le x\le643\tau/200$,
\[
 \boxed{G(c,x,\tau)>0.}                              \tag{4.11}
\]
At $(x,\tau)=(0,0)$, $G=0$.

\emph{Proof.}  Set $b=x/\tau$.  The six slabs cover

In the other case the relevant Bernstein coefficient minimum is strictly

\section{A reusable symmetric Bessel majorant}

Although Theorem 4.1 uses the original packet directly, the proof search
also produced a stable future classifier:
\[
 \boxed{I_0(z)\le {1+\cosh z\over2}.}                 \tag{5.1}
\]
Indeed the coefficient of $z^{2n}$ on the two sides is respectively
\[
 {1\over4^n(n!)^2},\qquad {1\over2(2n)!}.             \tag{5.2}
\]
Writing $r_n=\binom{2n}{n}/4^n$, one has $r_1=1/2$ and
\[
 {r_{n+1}\over r_n}={2n+1\over2n+2}<1.              \tag{5.3}
\]
Thus (5.1) is coefficientwise, with first defect $z^4/192$.  In fact
\[
 I_0(z)\le {1+\cosh z\over2}-{z^4\over192},           \tag{5.4}
\]
because every remaining defect coefficient is nonnegative.

The uncorrected right side is the moment-generating function of the
three-point phase law with weights $1/4,1/2,1/4$ at $-1,0,1$.  It preserves
the physical-ray mean and variance and creates no linear origin debt.
However, it changes the fixed-$x$ projective second coefficient; one must not
import the R-112 $D_2$ certificate into that bare surrogate.  The correction
in (5.4) matches through the $z^4$ term and is the appropriate future
one-dimensional $\operatorname{erfcx}$ classifier.  Neither surrogate is
needed for, nor extends the scope of, Theorem 4.1 here.
