\section*{3. Exact affine common-heat Gaussian score theorem}

 T=\E_g\|P_{\cC}C_6(W_{f,r})\partial_iG'_{k,g}\|^2.  \tag{3.3}

 H^{(1)}_{a,b}(\xi)

 H^{(2)}_{a,b}(\xi,\xi')

\paragraph{Theorem 3.1 (affine mixed-replica score identity).}

source coordinate realizes all owner tuples and that $a,b$ are its common

\[
 \boxed{

 H^{(2)}_{a,b}K-H^{(1)}_{a,b}T\right].}              \tag{3.7}

\paragraph{Proof.}

$h=ta+ub$, the product Gaussian density ratio is

density ratio has second score (3.5).  Apply these two identities to
$\frac12\E_{f,f'}K-\frac12\E_fT$, keeping $r$ fixed, and then integrate
the common heat once.  This is exactly (2.1). \hfill$\square$

If different owner tuples use different affine source coordinates, the

\section*{4. Exact covariance cost}

Let a physical affine Gaussian have covariance $\Gamma$.  For
$z,w\in\Ran\Gamma^{1/2}$ define
\[
 \kappa(z,w)=\ip{z}{\Gamma^\dagger w},
 \qquad
 D_\Gamma(z,w)
 =\kappa(z,z)\kappa(w,w)+\kappa(z,w)^2.              \tag{4.1}
\]
In physical variables the scores are
\[
 s_z=\ip{\Gamma^\dagger z}{W-\overline W},           \tag{4.2}
\]
\[
 H^{(1)}_{z,w}=s_zs_w-\kappa(z,w),                   \tag{4.3}
\]
\[
 H^{(2)}_{z,w}=(s_z+s'_z)(s_w+s'_w)-2\kappa(z,w).    \tag{4.4}
\]
Wick's formula gives the exact costs
\[
 \boxed{
 \|H^{(1)}_{z,w}\|_2^2=D_\Gamma(z,w),
 \qquad
 \|H^{(2)}_{z,w}\|_2^2=4D_\Gamma(z,w).}             \tag{4.5}
\]
Consequently, in the common-coordinate setting of Theorem 3.1,
\[
 \boxed{
 |q_{\rm comp}(z,w)|
 \le D_\Gamma(z,w)^{1/2}
 \sum_{k,\cC,i}\E_r
 \left(\|K\|_2+{1\over2}\|T\|_2\right).}          \tag{4.6}
\]
For owner-dependent affine coordinates the precise replacement is the
ownerwise sum
\[
 \sum_{k,\cC,i}\E_r\left[
 D_{\Gamma_{k\cC i,r}}(z,w)^{1/2}
 \left(\|K\|_2+{1\over2}\|T\|_2\right)\right],    \tag{4.6a}
\]
with the physical directions and covariance interpreted in that owner's
coordinate.  No common covariance may be pulled outside this sum unless it
is actually shared by all owners.

The range condition is load-bearing.  Outside
$\Ran\Gamma^{1/2}$ the translated and original Gaussian laws are singular.
Even inside the range, for

 \qquad z=e_2,                                       \tag{4.7}

\section*{5. Common and relative replica directions}

The affine theorem has the same direction on both replicas.  General
feedback does not.  The following identity locates the difference exactly.
Let $A\sim N(0,\Sigma)$ be common heat and let
$\Phi(A+x,A+y)$ be smooth.  For two replica directions $p,q$, write
\[
 c={p+q\over2},\qquad d={p-q\over2},
 \qquad
 \nabla_-:=\nabla_x-\nabla_y.                        \tag{5.1}
\]
Then
\[
 p\cdot\nabla_x+q\cdot\nabla_y
 =c\cdot\nabla_A+d\cdot\nabla_-.                    \tag{5.2}
\]
For two direction pairs $(p_z,q_z),(p_w,q_w)$, Gaussian integration by
parts gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \E_A L_zL_w\Phi={}&
 \E_A[H_\Sigma(c_z,c_w;A)\Phi]\\
 &+\E_A[S_\Sigma(c_z;A)d_w\cdot\nabla_-\Phi]\\
 &+\E_A[S_\Sigma(c_w;A)d_z\cdot\nabla_-\Phi]\\
 &+\E_A[(d_z\cdot\nabla_-)(d_w\cdot\nabla_-)\Phi]
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{5.3}
\]
where
\[
 S_\Sigma(c;A)=\ip{\Sigma^\dagger c}{A},             \tag{5.4}
\]
\[
 H_\Sigma(c,w;A)=S_\Sigma(c;A)S_\Sigma(w;A)
 -\ip{c}{\Sigma^\dagger w}.                          \tag{5.5}
\]

For iid conditional replica directions $p,p'$ in any Hilbert metric,
\[
 \E\left\|{p+p'\over2}\right\|^2
 =\|\E p\|^2+{1\over2}\Var(p),                      \tag{5.6}
\]
\[
 \E\left\|{p-p'\over2}\right\|^2
 ={1\over2}\Var(p),
 \qquad
 \E\ip{p+p'}{p-p'}=0.                               \tag{5.7}
\]
Thus common heat transfers the synchronous direction.  The unsmoothed
relative derivative is localized to one half of the conditional variation
variance.  In the affine mean-shift theorem $p=p'$ and it vanishes.  In a
predictable feedback chart it is an additional owner, not a free
cancellation.

\section*{6. Exact predictable-feedback divergence theorem}

On a smooth finite triangular source chart write
\[
 Z_h(\xi)=L_\pi\{\xi+h(\xi)\},
 \qquad J_h=I+D_\xi h.                               \tag{6.1}
\]
For legal fixed-law control directions $u,v$, put
\[
 a=J_h^{-1}u,
 \qquad b=J_h^{-1}v,
 \qquad d=J_h^{-1}D_\xi^2h[a,b].                    \tag{6.2}
\]
For standard Gaussian divergence define
\[
 \delta(a)=\xi\cdot a-\operatorname{div}a,           \tag{6.3}
\]
and for a matrix field $M$ define
\[
 \delta^2(M)=\xi^TM\xi-\Tr M
 -2\xi\cdot\operatorname{div}M
 +\operatorname{div}^2M.                             \tag{6.4}
\]

\paragraph{Theorem 6.1 (feedback-corrected score).}
Assume that $J_h$ is pointwise invertible and that $h,u,v,F$, together with
the fields in (6.2)--(6.4), lie in a Gaussian--Sobolev/integrability core on
which both adjunctions below are valid.  Then, for every such smooth
cylindrical scalar owner $F$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \E D^2F(Z_h)[L_\pi u,L_\pi v]
 =\E F(Z_h)\left[
 \delta^2(\operatorname{sym}(a\otimes b))-\delta(d)\right].}   \tag{6.5}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Since $J_ha=u$ and $J_hb=v$,
\[
 D_\xi^2(F\circ Z_h)[a,b]
 =D^2F(Z_h)[L_\pi u,L_\pi v]
 +DF(Z_h)[L_\pi D^2h[a,b]].                         \tag{6.6}
\]
The definition of $d$ gives
\[
 D_\xi(F\circ Z_h)[d]
 =DF(Z_h)[L_\pi D^2h[a,b]].                          \tag{6.7}
\]
Apply Gaussian adjunction twice to (6.6) and once to (6.7). \hfill$\square$

For the replica kernel, apply (6.5) on the product Gaussian space.  For the
trace use one copy.  Thus (3.7) remains exact after replacing $H^{(2)}$ and
$H^{(1)}$ by the corresponding product- and single-space scores
\[
 \mathfrak S_{u,v}=\delta^2(\operatorname{sym}(a\otimes b))-\delta(d). \tag{6.8}
\]
The coefficient derivatives disappear, but the price is $J_h^{-1}$,
derivatives of $a,b$ inside $\delta^2$, and the feedback connection $d$.
After expansion, up to third Gaussian derivatives of $h$ and second
Gaussian derivatives of $u,v$ can occur.
These are not among the declared finite-energy graph coordinates.

The connection term is not cosmetic.  On the exact triangular chart
\[

 \qquad u=v=e_1,                                      \tag{6.9}
\]
the owner $F(z)=z_2^2/2$ has physical Hessian zero.  The naked double
divergence contributes $2\lambda^2$, and $-\delta(d)$ cancels exactly the
same amount.  The primary and independent executables reconstruct this
identity by separate exact polynomial algebras.

\paragraph{Theorem 10.1 (collar headroom criterion).}
The strict low-coupled condition $k^2<dm_2(a)$ is equivalent to
\[
 \boxed{
 a<a_*:=2\sqrt{(e-\sigma)(f-\sigma)}.}               \tag{10.3}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
The condition is $m_2(a)>\sigma$.  Since
$\sigma<\min(e,f)$, both sides remain positive while squaring.  The exact
factorization is
\[
 (e+f-2\sigma)^2-(e-f)^2
 =4(e-\sigma)(f-\sigma).                             \tag{10.4}
\]
This proves (10.3). \hfill$\square$
