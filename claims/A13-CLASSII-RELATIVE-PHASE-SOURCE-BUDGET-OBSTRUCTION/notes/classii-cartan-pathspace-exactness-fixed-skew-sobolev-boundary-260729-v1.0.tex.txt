\section*{1. Purpose and scope}

R-119 and R-120 left a rational first-order Cartan term and proposed a
checksum: the isolated R-102 current has repository curl $-40/729$, so the
unwritten remainder of the complete terminal owner was said to be required
to provide $+40/729$.  The present note audits that implication before
spending more proof effort on an arithmetic companion search.

The audit finds a category error.  Exactness of the differential of a scalar
functional on path or jet space does not imply that a target-space one-form
appearing as one local coefficient is closed.  The complete rational owner
does telescope exactly, but its recombined local current is itself not
closed.  There is therefore no structurally mandatory $+40/729$ local
companion.  The observed $-40/729$ and its chain-primitive no-go remain
valid.

The correction unlocks a different analytic route.  A fixed-skew Cartan form
paired with an $H^{-s}$ coefficient has positive deterministic Young slack
for every $0\le s<1$.  At $s=3/5$ it requires only a fifth coefficient
moment, improving the older R-071 Cartan payment on any branch already
reduced to its unshifted stationary one-form current.  A sharp high-frequency
fixture proves that the R-120 zeroth-order $H^{-11/10}$ coefficient class
cannot be reused for this first-order form.

\subsection*{Theorem 2.1 (exact two-visit owner telescope)}

Pathwise at every finite cutoff,
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{k=1}^2(\cR_{Q,k}+\cM_{U,k}+\cK_{R,k})
 =\frac12B_2:Q_2-\frac12B_0:Q_0.}                  \tag{2.7}

Equivalently,
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac12(G_2^TB_2G_2-G_0^TB_0G_0)
 -\frac12(B_2-B_0):\Gamma_i.}                      \tag{2.8}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Since $G_k=G_{k-1}+d_k$,
\[
 Q_k=Q_{k-1}+G_{k-1}\otimes d_k+d_k\otimes G_{k-1}
       +d_k\otimes d_k.                            \tag{2.9}
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{split}
 \frac12B_k:Q_k-\frac12B_{k-1}:Q_{k-1}
 ={}&\frac12(B_k-B_{k-1}):Q_{k-1}\\
 &+G_{k-1}^TB_kd_k+\frac12d_k^TB_kd_k.
\end{split}                                         \tag{2.10}
\]
Using $B_k=T_k+L_k$, the right side is exactly (2.4)--(2.6).
Summing (2.10) cancels $B_1:Q_1/2$ and proves (2.7).  No independence or
conditioning is used.  Thus adapted dependence of $a_2$ does not affect the
finite-cutoff algebra; it matters only when conditional expectation, Wiener
projection, or continuum reconstruction is subsequently taken.

\section*{4. Path-space exactness does not imply target-space closure}

Let $\theta$ be a smooth one-form on a finite-dimensional target and let $u$
be a smooth loop.  Define the scalar first-order functional
\[
 I[u]=\int_{\T}\theta_{u(t)}(\partial_tu(t))\dd t.                              \tag{4.1}
\]

\subsection*{Theorem 4.1 (path-space exactness lemma)}

The differential $DI$ is an exact one-form on loop space by construction,
even when $d\theta\ne0$ on the target.  For fixed variations $z,w$,
\[
 DI[u]z=\int_{\T}(d\theta)_u(z,\partial_tu)\dd t,                              \tag{4.2}
\]
\[
 D^2I[u](z,w)=\int_{\T}
 \left[(D_wd\theta)_u(z,\partial_tu)+(d\theta)_u(z,\partial_tw)\right]\dd t. \tag{4.3}
\]
Although (4.3) is not manifestly symmetric, the difference under
$z\leftrightarrow w$ vanishes by $d(d\theta)=0$ and integration by parts on
the torus.  Hence symmetry of the scalar Hessian does not force
$d\theta=0$.

\paragraph{Explicit falsifying example.}
Take
\[
 \theta=-\frac y2\dd x+\frac x2\dd y,
 \qquad d\theta=\dd x\wedge\dd y,                  \tag{4.4}
\]
and $u(t)=(\cos t,\sin t)$.  Then $I[u]=\pi$.  For
$z=(\cos t,0)$ and $w=(0,\sin t)$, direct integration gives
\[
 D^2I[u](z,w)=D^2I[u](w,z)=\pi.                    \tag{4.5}
\]
Thus a nonclosed target one-form produces a perfectly exact path-space
differential and a symmetric scalar Hessian.

There is also a production-specific version.  Let $z_0=(1,1)$,
$\omega=Le_1$, use normalized Haar measure on the loop parameter, and set
\[
 u_{\varepsilon,\delta}(t)=z_0+\varepsilon e_1\cos t
                         +\delta e_2\sin t.         \tag{4.6}
\]
For $I(\varepsilon,\delta)=\langle\omega(u)\cdot\partial_tu\rangle$, the
affine jet of $\omega$ at $z_0$ gives exactly
\[
 I_{\rm aff}(\varepsilon,\delta)
 =\frac12\left(\partial_x\omega_y-\partial_y\omega_x\right)
   \varepsilon\delta
 =\frac{20}{729}\varepsilon\delta.                \tag{4.7}
\]
Hence both mixed derivative orders equal $20/729$, while the repository curl
is $-40/729$.  This directly falsifies the old implication on the actual
R-102 jet, not merely on the illustrative form (4.4).

Apply this to the endpoint scalar
\[
 \cE(U,G)=\frac12B(U):(G^{\otimes2}-\Gamma).        \tag{4.8}
\]
The complete owner is the scalar difference
$\cE(U_2,G_2)-\cE(U_0,G_0)$.  Extracting one coefficient linear in $G$ and
then taking the target exterior derivative is not a chain map from the full
$(U,G)$ differential.  Equations (3.6)--(3.8) are an exact production-specific
witness.  This proves the R-121 correction.

\section*{5. Fixed-skew first-order Sobolev theorem}

Let $A_\nu^*=-A_\nu$ be a fixed finite family of real matrices.  Put
\[
 \mathbf F(z)_{\nu i}=z^TA_\nu\partial_i z,
 \qquad
 \cC_Q(z)=\sum_{\nu,i}\langle Q_{\nu i},
                                  \mathbf F(z)_{\nu i}\rangle.                \tag{5.1}
\]
The coefficient norm is the vector-valued norm
\[
 M_s=\|\mathbf Q\|_{H^{-s}(\ell^2)},\qquad 0\le s<1.                          \tag{5.2}
\]
Let $C_{\rm sk}$ denote the fixed-family constant in the vector-valued
pointwise skew estimate.  For the canonical six-real basis
\[
 A_{pq}=\frac12(E_{pq}-E_{qp}),\qquad 1\le p<q\le6,                            \tag{5.3}
\]
the exact wedge identity is
\[
 \sum_{p<q}|z^TA_{pq}v|^2
 =\frac14\left(|z|^2|v|^2-(z\cdot v)^2\right),                                \tag{5.4}
\]
so one may take the algebraic $\ell^2$ constant $C_{\rm sk}=1/2$ before the
universal Sobolev product constant.  Separately,
\[
 \sum_{p<q}|A_{pq}|=\frac52\Id_6.                                             \tag{5.5}
\]
Equation (5.5) is an absolute-operator inventory, not the coefficient-index
$\ell^2$ constant in (5.4); conflating them would produce a norm-convention
error.

Write
\[
 X=\|z\|_{H^2}^2,\qquad Y=\|z\|_6^6.                                        \tag{5.6}
\]

\subsection*{Lemma 5.1 (two endpoint product estimates)}

In three dimensions,
\[
 \|\mathbf F(z)\|_{L^2(\ell^2)}
 \le C C_{\rm sk}\|z\|_6\|\nabla z\|_3
 \le C C_{\rm sk}X^{1/4}Y^{1/4},                                             \tag{5.7}
\]
and
\[
 \|\mathbf F(z)\|_{H^1(\ell^2)}
 \le C C_{\rm sk}X.                                                          \tag{5.8}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Equation (5.7) uses (5.4), Holder, and the three-dimensional
Gagliardo--Nirenberg inequality
$\|\nabla z\|_3\le C\|D^2z\|_2^{1/2}\|z\|_6^{1/2}$, with harmless lower
Fourier modes absorbed in $H^2$.  Differentiating (5.1) gives products of
$\nabla z$ with itself and of $z$ with $D^2z$.  The embeddings
$H^2\hookrightarrow L^\infty$ and $H^2\hookrightarrow W^{1,4}$ prove (5.8).
\hfill$\square$

\subsection*{Theorem 5.2 (sharp fixed-skew Sobolev one-use boundary)}

Complex interpolation of (5.7)--(5.8) gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \|\mathbf F(z)\|_{H^s(\ell^2)}
 \le C_sC_{\rm sk}
 X^{(1+3s)/4}Y^{(1-s)/4},\qquad0\le s<1.}                                     \tag{5.9}
\]
The two deterministic exponents sum to $(1+s)/2$, leaving Young slack
$(1-s)/2$.  Vector-valued duality and weighted Young therefore give, for
every $\eta,\zeta>0$,
\[
\boxed{
 |\cC_Q(z)|\le\eta X+\zeta Y
 +C_s C_{\rm sk}^{2/(1-s)}
 \eta^{-(1+3s)/(2(1-s))}\zeta^{-1/2}
 M_s^{2/(1-s)}.}                                                             \tag{5.10}
\]

At $s=3/5$,
\[
 \boxed{
 |\cC_Q(z)|\le\eta X+\zeta Y
 +C C_{\rm sk}^{5}\eta^{-7/2}\zeta^{-1/2}M_{3/5}^{5}.}                       \tag{5.11}
\]
The exact ledger is
\[
 (X\hbox{-power},Y\hbox{-power},\hbox{slack})
 =\left(\frac7{10},\frac1{10},\frac15\right),                               \tag{5.12}
\]
and the coefficient moment is five.

\subsection*{Corollary 5.3 (covariance-horizontal form)}

Let
\[
 A_0=\frac{M_R^2}{c_{\rm sym}},\quad
 S=\|h\|_{\cS}^2,\quad T=\|Z_h\|_6^6,
 \quad B=\|X_J\|_6^6.                                                        \tag{5.13}
\]
R-120 gives $X\le A_0S$ and $Y\le32(T+B)$.  Choosing
$\eta=\varepsilon/A_0$ and $\zeta=\varepsilon_6/32$ in (5.11) yields
\[
\boxed{
 |\cC_Q(z)|\le\varepsilon S+\varepsilon_6(T+B)
 +C\sqrt{32}\,C_{\rm sk}^{5}A_0^{7/2}
 \varepsilon^{-7/2}\varepsilon_6^{-1/2}M_{3/5}^{5}.}                         \tag{5.14}
\]
Thus a cutoff-, chart-, visit-, and control-uniform fifth moment of the
correct reconstructed $H^{-3/5}$ Cartan coefficient would close this

\section*{7. Sharp failure of the rough first-order reuse route}

Work on a normalized one-dimensional torus embedded in $\T^3$, let
\[
 J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 z_N=(1,N^{-2}\sin Nx),\qquad Q_N=N^s\cos Nx.       \tag{7.1}
\]
Then $\|z_N\|_{H^2}$ and $\|z_N\|_6$ are bounded uniformly in $N$, while
\[
 \|Q_N\|_{H^{-s}}^2
 =\frac{N^{2s}}{2(1+N^2)^s}\le\frac12.             \tag{7.2}
\]
But
\[
 \langle Q_N,z_N^TJ\partial_xz_N\rangle
 =-\frac12N^{s-1}.                                  \tag{7.3}
\]
For $s=11/10$, (7.3) diverges as $N^{1/10}$.  Hence an absolute first-order
Cartan bound from only $H^2$, $L^6$, and $H^{-11/10}$ norms is impossible.
At $s=1$ the positive Young slack vanishes; for $s<1$ it is exactly
$(1-s)/2$.  This proves that the R-120 zeroth-order coefficient class and the
present first-order class have different sharp Sobolev boundaries.
