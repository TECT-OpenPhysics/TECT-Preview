R-073 reduced the unresolved nonlinear part of A13 to adapted terminal
phase-root coercivity.  It left two possible proof mechanisms: a signed
strict-past cancellation or a positive regularity gain for the value-high
Taylor branches.  This note decides three parts of that fork.

First, the unsubtracted nonlinear secant remainder has a nonzero
high--high-to-low production resonance in its mismatched $E_x$--$b_\ell$

Therefore no coefficient-blind theorem can place that bare root in
$H^{-1/2+\rho}$ with $\rho>0$, and R-063's balanced jet gain may
not be imported before the resonance and all lower chaoses are identified.

Second, strict-past dependence and phase-root algebra do not automatically
center the terminal Wick coefficient.  A smooth production-frame diagnostic
has a strictly negative expectation.  It is not a coercivity counterexample:
it rotates a value while freezing its derivative and has no ultraviolet
replication defeating Cameron--Martin cost.

Third, a genuine integrable local phase orbit is much better.  The full raw
current is exactly invariant.  The only Wick change is the phase-noncommuting
part of the A1 derivative covariance.  A resolvent identity makes that part
cutoff-uniform with an $O(\Lambda^{-3})$ tail, so the anomaly is paid by one
arbitrarily small terminal sextic allocation.  Thus the pure phase-orbit
subchannel closes; the remaining gate is the adapted gauge-quotient,
horizontal Taylor one-form.

The note also proves the deterministic Besov product estimate which would
pay that horizontal root once at the raw R-050 order.  It needs a sixth
moment, not a positive regularity gain.  Constructing the corresponding

\section{Pure phase-orbit Wick theorem}

Let $J_D,J_S$ be the commuting doublet and singlet phase generators and
\[
 R(x)=\exp\{\theta_D(x)J_D+\theta_S(x)J_S\}.             \tag{5.1}
\]
Both generators commute with every $S_r$.  Hence
\[
 q_r(Rz)=q_r(z),\qquad M_r(Rz)=RM_r(z),                  \tag{5.2}
\]
and the phase roots obey
\[
 M_r(z)^TJ_\alpha z=0,qquad\alpha\in\{D,S\}.           \tag{5.3}
\]
Differentiating the actual rotated field gives
\[
 D_i(Rz)=R\left(D_iz+\sum_\alpha
                   (\partial_i\theta_\alpha)J_\alpha z\right).
                                                                    \tag{5.4}
\]
Combining (5.2)--(5.4) proves the pathwise identity
\[
 \boxed{M_r(Rz)^TD_i(Rz)=M_r(z)^TD_iz.}                 \tag{5.5}
\]
Thus the complete raw terminal current square is exactly invariant under any
regular local phase orbit.  Randomness of the angles is irrelevant to this
pointwise identity; no conditioning, projector, pseudoinverse, or Malliavin
derivative is used.  An additive displacement $Rz-z$ need not itself be an
admissible strict-past control, so (5.5) closes a geometric subchannel rather
than the whole control class.

For the Wick term, average a covariance over the compact phase group:
\[
 \Gamma_{\Lambda,i}^{\parallel}
 =\int_{\mathbb T^2}R_\vartheta^T\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}
                       R_\vartheta\,d\vartheta,
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{\Lambda,i}^{\perp}
 =\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}-\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}^{\parallel}.   \tag{5.6}
\]
The parallel part commutes with every phase.  Using
$B(Rz)=RB(z)R^T$ and (5.5),
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal W_\Lambda(Rz)-\mathcal W_\Lambda(z)
 =-{1\over2}\sum_i\int
 \operatorname{Tr}\{[RB(z)R^T-B(z)]
                       \Gamma_{\Lambda,i}^{\perp}\}\,dx.}    \tag{5.7}
\]

The A1 Fourier symbol is
\[
 A(k)=K(k)I+H,\qquad
 K(k)=r+Z|k|^2+Y|k|^4.                                  \tag{5.8}
\]
The scalar resolvent is phase commuting, and
\[
 (K I+H)^{-1}-K^{-1}I=-K^{-1}(K I+H)^{-1}H.             \tag{5.9}
\]
For sufficiently large $|k|$, (5.9) gives
\[
 \|\mathrm P_\perp A(k)^{-1}\|\lesssim |k|^{-8}.       \tag{5.10}
\]
The derivative covariance contributes a $k_i^2$ weight.  Therefore
\[
 k_i^2\|\mathrm P_\perp A(k)^{-1}\|\lesssim |k|^{-6},
 \qquad
 \sum_{|k|>\Lambda}|k|^{-6}\lesssim\Lambda^{-3}.       \tag{5.11}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_\Lambda\|\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}^{\perp}\|<\infty,
 \qquad
 \|\Gamma_{\infty,i}^{\perp}
       -\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}^{\perp}\|\lesssim\Lambda^{-3}.} \tag{5.12}
\]
All covariance norms in (5.12)--(5.13) are operator norms.
Equations (2.6), (5.7), and (5.12) yield
\[
 |\mathcal W_\Lambda(Rz)-\mathcal W_\Lambda(z)|
 \le72\lambda_Q\max_i\|\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}^{\perp}\|
       \|z\|_2^2
 \le\zeta\|z\|_6^6+C_\zeta.                         \tag{5.13}
\]
The terminal sextic is spent once.  The common phase $J_D+J_S$ commutes with
the entire complex A1 covariance, so its anomaly is exactly zero.  Only
relative doublet--singlet phase sees the finite defect.  In the R-070
telescope the finite-low boundary $-\mathcal W_{j_0}$ remains intact; (5.13)
does not reset or discard it.

\section{Besov one-use discriminator}

The resonance should not be estimated by the R-073 termwise shell norms.
After the exact endpoint grouping, its deterministic payload is cubic in a
total control and has the form $A\otimes A\otimes DA$.  The correct duality is
the endpoint Besov pairing, not a fictitious positive regularity gain.

\begin{lemma}[cubic control payload]
For $0<\kappa<1/2$ and $A\in H^2(\mathbb T^3)\cap L^6(\mathbb T^3)$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \|A\otimes A\otimes DA\|_{B^{1/2+\kappa}_{1,1}}
 \le C_\kappa\|A\|_{H^2}\|A\|_6^2.}                 \tag{7.1}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use an inhomogeneous Littlewood--Paley partition and expand the three factors
by largest input frequency.  If the differentiated factor has largest scale
$2^j$, put it in $L^2$ and the other two factors in $L^4$; the weighted sum is
bounded by
\[
 \|A\|_6^2\sum_j2^{(3/2+\kappa)j}\|\Delta_jA\|_2.
\]
If an undifferentiated factor has the largest scale, put that factor in
$L^2$, the other value factor in $L^6$, and use
$\|D S_jA\|_3\lesssim2^j\|A\|_6$ for the lower factor.  Comparable-largest
terms are assigned to either largest factor and obey the same bound by the
vector-valued square-function inequality.  Finally Cauchy--Schwarz against
$\sum_j2^{4j}\|\Delta_jA\|_2^2$ converges because
$3/2+\kappa<2$.  The finite low block is controlled by the inhomogeneous
norm.  Summing the finitely many Bony orientations proves (7.1).
\end{proof}

Let $\mathfrak J$ be a reconstructed adapted Taylor one-form at the honest
R-050 order
\[
 \mathfrak J\in C^{-1/2-\kappa}
 =B^{-1/2-\kappa}_{\infty,\infty}.                       \tag{7.2}
\]
Besov duality and (7.1), with
$X=\|A\|_{H^2}^2$ and $Y=\|A\|_6^6$, give
\[
 |\langle\mathfrak J,A^2DA\rangle|
 \le C\|\mathfrak J\|X^{1/2}Y^{1/3}.                  \tag{7.3}
\]
The exponents $2,3,6$ are conjugate.  Weighted Young therefore yields
\[
 \boxed{
 |\langle\mathfrak J,A^2DA\rangle|
 \le\eta X+\zeta Y
 +C\eta^{-3}\zeta^{-2}\|\mathfrak J\|^6.}            \tag{7.4}
\]
There is positive probability slack $1/6$.  R-050 supplies every finite
moment for its unshifted current, so the required sixth moment is compatible
with the existing enhancement.  What remains unproved is that the complete
R-073 horizontal Taylor root, including the resonance and all R-063 lower
chaoses, reconstructs as such an adapted $\mathfrak J$ with a cutoff-uniform
sixth moment and with the terminal square and finite-low boundary retained.

\section{Finite-energy recovery is not automatic}

Once a uniform regular one-shot control inequality is proved, stopping and
smooth cylindrical approximation are natural candidates for extending it.
Plain lower semicontinuity or Fatou, however, has the wrong direction for a
lower bound if approximants carry excess positive sextic or raw-current
energy.  Predictability also forbids a terminal-event truncation.

The nontrivial finite-energy successor must construct predictable recovery
sequences converging simultaneously in the Cameron--Martin norm, terminal
$L^6$, and the positive raw-current graph functional (or prove a biting and
uniform-integrability substitute).  At each fixed cutoff the negative part
has the useful bound
\[
 \mathcal W_J(z)^-\le C_J(1+\|z\|_2^2),                 \tag{8.1}
\]
but (8.1) alone does not control excess positive energy.  Accordingly this
note records the density shortcut as unresolved rather than claiming the
finite-energy extension.
