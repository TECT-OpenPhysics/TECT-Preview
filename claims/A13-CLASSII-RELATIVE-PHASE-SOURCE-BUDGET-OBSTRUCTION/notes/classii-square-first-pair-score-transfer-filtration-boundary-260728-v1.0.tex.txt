\section{Purpose and scope}

R-108 reduced the active A13 theorem to a cutoff-, chart-, control-, and
subdivision-uniform signed complete-cluster lower bound, or to a legal
square-before-average conditional normalizer which implies it.  On the
smallest contraction-closed Fourier pair, R-108 established only a small-
amplitude expansion and one finite viability check.  The present package
turns that viability check into an all-amplitude theorem and then audits
exactly which parts survive same-root feedback.

The result has six parts.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A conditional one-sided Bennett lemma converts a centered lower floor
      and a variance-to-floor ratio into a global quadratic log-Laplace
      cost.  No smallness assumption remains.
\item The complete one-pair cluster $\{0,+2,-2\}$ satisfies that lemma for
      every predictable scale, every nonnegative weight, and every $q\ge0$.
      Its exact cost is bounded by one quarter of the conditional expectation
      of the squared realized whole-cluster covariance.  Legal fresh pairs
      therefore iterate to a supermartingale.
\item The R-108 target schema is repaired at the filtration level.  A
      covariance revealed by the current root cannot appear raw on the
      right of an $\mathcal F_{j-1}$ conditional normalizer.  The legal cost
      is its conditional expectation, or a proved predictable envelope.
\item The pure quartic pair has a selector-independent pathwise floor whose
      production diagonal sum is cutoff-uniform.  The complete pair's
      baseline floor nevertheless diverges; its determinant conversion is
      indispensable, and physical cross-mode resonances prevent pairwise
      tensorization.
\item A complete signed second jet with one past-measurable covariance
      operator admits exact Gaussian score transfer and a source/sextic
      form bound.  This proves uniform control of the signed oscillatory
      companion left open by R-108, but only for that fixed-operator owner.
\item Gaussian score transfer is expectation-only.  An exact oscillatory
      family has a derivative-representative log-Laplace cost growing
      linearly while the raw Wick coordinate tends to zero exponentially in
      the relevant Orlicz sense.  Thus the derivative representative may
      not be substituted inside a conditional exponential normalizer.
\end{enumerate}

These are genuine analytic advances and one target correction.  They do not
identify the full adapted production forest with one predictable fixed-
operator second jet, control cross-mode same-root feedback, prove the uniform
complete-cluster bound, establish $\mathrm{OVERLAP}_{\rm src}$ or Nelson,
remove any cutoff or floor, construct an interacting measure, close Sector A,
or change the tier from T4.

\section{A conditional one-sided normalizer lemma}

% evidence-anchor: theorem-2.1-conditional-floor-variance-normalizer
\subsection*{Theorem 2.1 (conditional floor--variance normalizer)}

Let $\mathcal F$ be a sigma algebra.  Suppose $Z$ is real, $\beta\ge0$ is
$\mathcal F$-measurable, and conditionally on $\mathcal F$,
\[
 \E[Z\mid\mathcal F]=0,\qquad Z\le\beta,\qquad
 \E[Z^2\mid\mathcal F]\le8\beta^2.                 \tag{2.1}
\]
Then for every $q\ge0$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \log\E[e^{qZ}\mid\mathcal F]\le5q^2\beta^2.}     \tag{2.2}
\]

\emph{Proof.}
The event $\{\beta=0\}$ is immediate.  Else set
$X=Z/\beta\le1$ and $a=q\beta$.  For every $x\le1$,
\[
 e^{ax}\le1+ax+(e^a-1-a)x^2.                        \tag{2.3}
\]
For $0\le x\le1$, (2.3) follows term by term from the exponential series.
For $x<0$, use $e^{ax}\le1+ax+a^2x^2/2$ and
$a^2/2\le e^a-1-a$.  Taking conditional expectations gives
\[
 \log\E e^{aX}\le8(e^a-1-a).                        \tag{2.4}
\]
If $0\le a\le1/5$, then $n!\ge2\,3^{n-2}$ for $n\ge2$, so
\[
 e^a-1-a\le {a^2\over2(1-a/3)},
 \qquad
 8(e^a-1-a)\le {30\over7}a^2<5a^2.                 \tag{2.5}
\]
If $a\ge1/5$, the pointwise bound $X\le1$ gives
\[
 \log\E e^{aX}\le a\le5a^2.                       \tag{2.6}
\]
The two intervals cover $[0,\infty)$. \hfill$\square$

The constants are deliberately compatible with the realized covariance of
the production one-pair fixture.  The infinitesimal coefficient in that
fixture is $4$, so a universal coefficient below $4$ is impossible.  The
coefficient $5$ has one unit, or twenty percent, of leading room.

\section{The all-amplitude complete one-pair theorem}

Let $\mathcal F_{j-1}$ be the strict past.  Assume $\lambda,\sigma\ge0$ are
$\mathcal F_{j-1}$-measurable and, conditionally on the past,
$\xi,\eta$ are independent standard Gaussians.  Put
\[
 z={\sigma\over2}(\xi-i\eta),\qquad
 R=|z|^2={\sigma^2\over2}T,\qquad T\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1), \tag{3.1}
\]
and retain the complete output cluster $\{0,+2,-2\}$.  Its diagonal packet is
\[
 P_{\lambda,\sigma}
 =\lambda(R^2-\sigma^2R)
 ={\lambda\sigma^4\over4}(T^2-2T).                  \tag{3.2}
\]

% evidence-anchor: theorem-3.1-all-amplitude-one-pair-square-first
\subsection*{Theorem 3.1 (all-amplitude square-before-average normalizer)}

Let $S_z^\lambda$ be the R-107/R-108 realized independent-copy covariance of
the whole cluster after the current is scaled by $\sqrt\lambda$.  Then for
every $q\ge0$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \log\E[e^{-qP_{\lambda,\sigma}}\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}]
 \le {q^2\over4}
 \E[\|S_z^\lambda\|_{\mathfrak S_2}^2
       \mid\mathcal F_{j-1}].}                       \tag{3.3}
\]

\emph{Proof.}
Set
\[
 \beta={\lambda\sigma^4\over4},\qquad
 U=2T-T^2=1-(T-1)^2.                                 \tag{3.4}
\]
Then $-P=\beta U$, $U\le1$, and the exact exponential moments
$\E T^n=n!$ give
\[
 \E U=0,\qquad \E U^2=8.                            \tag{3.5}
\]
Theorem 2.1 yields
\[
 \log\E e^{-qP}\le5q^2\beta^2.                     \tag{3.6}
\]
The two nonzero eigenvalues of $S_z^\lambda$ are
\[
 {3\over2}\lambda\sigma^2R,\qquad
 {1\over2}\lambda\sigma^2R.                        \tag{3.7}
\]
Since $\E R^2=\sigma^4/2$,
\[
 \E\|S_z^\lambda\|_{\mathfrak S_2}^2
 ={5\over4}\lambda^2\sigma^8,                      \tag{3.8}
\]
and the right side of (3.3) is exactly
\[
 {5q^2\lambda^2\sigma^8\over16}=5q^2\beta^2.
\]
\hfill$\square$

This theorem upgrades the R-108 small-$\sigma$ expansion and
$(q,\sigma)=(1,1/2)$ check to every amplitude.  Indeed
\[
 \log\E e^{-qP}=4a^2-40a^3+O(a^4),
 \qquad a={q\lambda\sigma^4\over4},                 \tag{3.9}
\]
while the square-first cost is $5a^2$.

% evidence-anchor: corollary-3.2-fresh-pair-supermartingale
\subsection*{Corollary 3.2 (legal sequential fresh-pair product)}

Suppose $P_j$ has the form (3.2) conditionally on $\mathcal F_{j-1}$ and
define the predictable cost
\[
 C_j={q^2\over4}
 \E[\|S_j^{\rm real}\|_{\mathfrak S_2}^2
       \mid\mathcal F_{j-1}].                        \tag{3.10}
\]
Then
\[
 M_n=\exp\bigl(-q\sum_{j\le n}P_j-\sum_{j\le n}C_j\bigr) \tag{3.11}
\]
is a nonnegative supermartingale.  This is the legal triangular iteration
which the global frozen future-row determinant of R-107 cannot replace.

The hypotheses are exact: the pair is fresh; its scale and nonnegative weight
are strict-past measurable; every contraction-connected output remains in
one cluster; and no same-root selector, cross-mode resonance, or rational
coefficient variation is hidden in $P_j$.  No injectivity of the rectangular
root-to-output row is required.  The identity operator in the frozen
determinant makes singular covariance harmless.

\subsection*{Audit 4.1 (square first, then condition)}

R-108 (8.3) is a target schema, not an asserted determinant.  Its right-hand
cost must be measurable at the conditioning step.  If
$S_{j,\mathcal C}$ is the covariance of a strict-past affine row, then
$\|S_{j,\mathcal C}\|_{\mathfrak S_2}^2$ is already
$\mathcal F_{j-1}$-measurable and the displayed R-108 form is legal.

If instead $S_{j,\mathcal C}^{\rm real}$ depends on the root being integrated,
the legal square-before-average target is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\log\E\left[
 e^{-q(\mathfrak P_{j,\mathcal C}+\text{payments})}
 \mid\mathcal F_{j-1}\right]\\
 &\qquad\le {q^2\over4}
 \E[\|S_{j,\mathcal C}^{\rm real}\|_{\mathfrak S_2}^2
       \mid\mathcal F_{j-1}]
 +r_{j,\mathcal C}^{\rm pred},
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{4.1}
\]
where $r^{\rm pred}\ge0$ is $\mathcal F_{j-1}$-measurable, or the conditional
cost is replaced by an explicitly proved predictable envelope.  Equation
(4.1) keeps the square inside and the averaging after, as R-108 Proposition
6.1 requires.  It remains a target for a general production cluster.

There is a subtle but essential two-stage boundary.  After revealing the
root, an auxiliary independent copy gives the frozen R-107 determinant
\[
 \log\E_w e^{-q(\|A_zw\|^2-\Tr S_z)/2}
 \le {q^2\over4}\|S_z\|_{\mathfrak S_2}^2.          \tag{4.2}
\]
Returning to $\mathcal F_{j-1}$ generally produces
\[
 \log\E\left[
  \exp\left\{{q^2\over4}\|S_z\|_{\mathfrak S_2}^2+r_z\right\}
  \mid\mathcal F_{j-1}\right],                      \tag{4.3}
\]
not the cheaper conditional mean in (4.1).  Moreover, (4.2) normalizes the
auxiliary decoupled packet, not the original diagonal current.  As
$R\downarrow0$, the original negative exponent is linear in $R$, whereas the
determinant cost in (4.2) is quadratic in $R$; no pointwise comparison exists.
Theorem 3.1 is the separate diagonal-cluster lemma which justifies (4.1) in
the one-pair case.

This audit does not withdraw any exact R-108 endpoint, covariance, quotient,
or order-of-operations result.  It makes the target's admissible
measurability interpretation explicit.

\section{Selector-independent quartic floor and its exact limitation}

% evidence-anchor: proposition-5.1-arbitrary-selector-quartic-floor
\subsection*{Proposition 5.1 (arbitrary same-root selector floor)}

For every $R\ge0$, $\sigma\ge0$, and nonnegative weight $\lambda$, without
any Gaussianity, independence, differentiability, or filtration assumption,
\[
 \boxed{
 \lambda(R^2-\sigma^2R)
 =\lambda\left(R-{\sigma^2\over2}\right)^2
  -{\lambda\sigma^4\over4}
 \ge-{\lambda\sigma^4\over4}.}                      \tag{5.1}
\]
Thus any sequential family satisfies
\[
 \sum_{j\le J}\lambda_jP_j
 \ge-{1\over4}\sum_{j\le J}\lambda_j\sigma_j^4.  \tag{5.2}
\]
If the right side is deterministically bounded, (5.2) gives a uniform direct
and exponential bound.  If it is random, only
$\log\E\exp(q\sum\lambda_j\sigma_j^4/4)$ is justified; its expectation is
not enough.

For the artificial R-105 diagonal pair, put
\[
 R={\sigma_k^2\over4}S_h,\qquad
 \mu_k={\beta_0k^2\sigma_k^4\over16}.                \tag{5.3}
\]
Then
\[
 \mu_k(S_h^2-4S_h)=\beta_0k^2(R^2-\sigma_k^2R)       \tag{5.4}
\]
has floor $-4\mu_k$.  Since
$\sigma_k^2\lesssim\langle k\rangle^{-4}$ in three dimensions,
\[
 \sum_k4\mu_k
 \lesssim\sum_k\langle k\rangle^{-6}<\infty.       \tag{5.5}
\]
The shell decay is $2^{-3j}$.  Hence the pure centered quartic diagonal
submodel is cutoff-uniform even for an arbitrary same-root selector.

% evidence-anchor: proposition-5.2-full-pair-floor-boundary
\subsection*{Proposition 5.2 (the full direct pair floor still diverges)}

The complete artificial pair of R-105 is
\[
 V(S)=\alpha(S-2)+\mu(S^2-4S),\qquad S\ge0.          \tag{5.6}
\]
For $\mu>0$ its exact minimum is
\[
 \inf_{S\ge0}V(S)=
 \begin{cases}
  -2\alpha, & \alpha\ge4\mu,\\[2mm]
  -4\mu-\alpha^2/(4\mu), & 0\le\alpha<4\mu.
 \end{cases}                                         \tag{5.7}
\]
For $\mu=0$ the first line remains.  In R-105,
\[
 {\alpha_k\over\mu_k}={4A^2\over\sigma_k^2}\longrightarrow\infty
 \quad(A\ne0),                                      \tag{5.8}
\]
so the high modes are forced onto the $-2\alpha_k$ branch, and
\[
 \sum_k\alpha_k\asymp
 A^2\sum_k\langle k\rangle^{-2}=\infty.             \tag{5.9}
\]
The determinant changes this nonsummable linear loss to
$t-\log(1+t)=O(t^2)$, whose shell cost is $2^{-j}$ and is summable.

The physical action cannot be closed by multiplying these pair estimates.
R-105 (8.10)--(8.11) gives a sign-indefinite $k{:}2k$ resonance, while R-107
requires contraction-connected outputs, covariance blocks, mixed baseline,
trace, and forest owners to remain in one atom.  Equations (5.5) and (5.7)
are therefore a positive diagonal submodel theorem and an exact limitation,
not a production cluster decomposition.

\section{Signed second-jet score transfer}

Let $\mathcal F_{\rm past}$ be fixed.  Conditionally on it, let
$\xi\sim N(0,\Id_d)$ be fresh, let $W=W^*\succeq0$ be finite-rank and
$\mathcal F_{\rm past}$-measurable, and let
$h=h(\mathcal F_{\rm past},\xi)$ be a twice differentiable cylindrical map
into a real Hilbert space.  Assume the displayed terms are integrable and
define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak F_W(h)=\E_\xi\big[&
 \|D_\xi h\,W^{1/2}\|_{\mathfrak S_2}^2\\
 &+\langle h,\Tr_{\R^d}(W D_\xi^2h)\rangle
 \mid\mathcal F_{\rm past}\big].
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{6.1}
\]

% evidence-anchor: theorem-6.1-complete-second-jet-score-transfer
\subsection*{Theorem 6.1 (exact score transfer and form bound)}

One has the exact identity
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak F_W(h)
 ={1\over2}\E_\xi\left[
 (\xi^TW\xi-\Tr W)\|h\|^2
 \mid\mathcal F_{\rm past}\right].}                \tag{6.2}
\]
Writing
\[
 X=\E_\xi[\|h\|^2\mid\mathcal F_{\rm past}],
 \qquad
 Y=\E_\xi[\|h\|^6\mid\mathcal F_{\rm past}],     \tag{6.3}
\]
gives
\[
 \boxed{
 |\mathfrak F_W(h)|
 \le {\|W\|_{\mathfrak S_2}\over\sqrt2}
       X^{1/4}Y^{1/4}.}                              \tag{6.4}
\]
Consequently, for every $q,\eta,\zeta>0$,
\[
 \boxed{
 -{q\over2}\mathfrak F_W(h)
 \ge-\eta X-\zeta Y
 -{q^2\|W\|_{\mathfrak S_2}^2
    \over64\sqrt{\eta\zeta}}.}                    \tag{6.5}
\]

\emph{Proof.}
The Hilbert chain rule gives
\[
 {1\over2}D^2\|h\|^2
 =(Dh)^*Dh+\langle h,D^2h\rangle.                   \tag{6.6}
\]
Contract (6.6) with $W$ and apply Gaussian integration by parts twice.  This
proves (6.2).  Also
\[
 \E_\xi(\xi^TW\xi-\Tr W)^2=2\|W\|_{\mathfrak S_2}^2. \tag{6.7}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and interpolation
$\E\|h\|^4\le X^{1/2}Y^{1/2}$ give (6.4).  Finally
\[
 A x^{1/4}y^{1/4}
 \le\eta x+\zeta y+{A^2\over8\sqrt{\eta\zeta}}     \tag{6.8}
\]
is sharp at
\[
 x={A^2\over16\eta^{3/2}\zeta^{1/2}},
 \qquad
 y={A^2\over16\eta^{1/2}\zeta^{3/2}}.              \tag{6.9}
\]
Use $A=q\|W\|_{\mathfrak S_2}/(2\sqrt2)$. \hfill$\square$

At $q=10/9$, the coefficient $q^2/64$ in (6.5) is exactly $25/1296$.
For an R-063 derivative-root covariance shell,
\[
 W_{i,j}(k)=\mathbf1_{\{|k|\sim2^j\}}
 k_i^2r_\Lambda(k)^2\widehat C(k),                  \tag{6.10}
\]
the A1 bound $\|\widehat C(k)\|_{\rm op}\lesssim
\langle k\rangle^{-4}$ gives
\[
 \|W_{i,j}\|_{\mathfrak S_2}^2
 \lesssim\sum_{|k|\sim2^j}\langle k\rangle^{-4}
 \lesssim2^{-j}.                                    \tag{6.11}
\]
Thus a genuinely fixed, past-measurable $W$ has cutoff-uniform HS cost after
complete aggregation.

The missing production identification is load-bearing.  R-063 proves the
deterministic-shift reconstruction of the $P_3/P_1/P_4/P_2/\Sigma Q/P_0$
forest.  R-107 and R-108 do not prove for arbitrary same-root future feedback
that the whole contraction-closed trace defect equals one
$\mathfrak F_{W_{\mathcal C}}(h_{\mathcal C})$ with $W_{\mathcal C}$ past
measurable, nor that its $X,Y$ use the canonical source energy and terminal
sextic without visit multiplicity.  If $W=W(\xi)$, integration by parts also
differentiates $W$ and creates the unmapped $DW,D^2W$ forest terms.

\section{Score transfer may not be exponentiated}

% evidence-anchor: proposition-7.1-stein-second-jet-exponentiation-nogo
\subsection*{Proposition 7.1 (expectation cancellation is not exponential cancellation)}

Let $G\sim N(0,1)$ and scale
\[
 h_M(G)=M^{-1/2}\sin(MG).                            \tag{7.1}
\]
The derivative representative is
\[
 K_M=(h_M')^2+h_Mh_M''=M\cos(2MG),
 \qquad \E K_M=Me^{-2M^2}.                          \tag{7.2}
\]
For every fixed $\theta\ne0$, the Fourier--Bessel expansion gives
\[
 \E e^{\theta M\cos(2MG)}
 =I_0(\theta M)+2\sum_{n\ge1}I_n(\theta M)e^{-2n^2M^2}. \tag{7.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 \log\E e^{\theta(K_M-\E K_M)}
 =|\theta|M-{1\over2}\log(2\pi|\theta|M)+o(1).}    \tag{7.4}
\]
The centered derivative-representative log-Laplace cost diverges linearly.

The raw Wick coordinate on the other side of the Stein identity is
\[
 W_M=h_M(G)^2(G^2-1),
 \qquad \E W_M=2\E K_M.                             \tag{7.5}
\]
But
\[
 |W_M|\le {G^2+1\over M},                            \tag{7.6}
\]
and for $M>2|\theta|$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \log\E e^{|\theta||W_M|}
 \le {|\theta|\over M}
 -{1\over2}\log\left(1-{2|\theta|\over M}\right)
 \longrightarrow0.}                                 \tag{7.7}
\]

Equations (7.4)--(7.7) prove that the expectation identity (6.2) cannot be
used as a pathwise substitution inside a conditional exponential estimate.
A determinant or log-Laplace proof must remain in the raw Wick and complete-
endpoint coordinate until after the exponential step, unless a separate
pathwise full-cluster identity is proved.  This is not a counterexample to
the direct expectation bound (6.5), Theorem 3.1, the raw complete packet, or
the Nelson objective.
