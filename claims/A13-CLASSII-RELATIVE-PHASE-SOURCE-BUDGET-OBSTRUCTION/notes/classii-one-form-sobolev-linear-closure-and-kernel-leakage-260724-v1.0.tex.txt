\section{Purpose and scope}

R-070 reduced the remaining regular finite-cutoff problem to a weighted
linear production frame and a coupled nonlinear shifted residual.  It proposed
to put the raw non-$pp$ symmetric coefficient in $H^{-1-\kappa}$ at
$\kappa=1/10$, pay it by R-068, and then use the terminal current square on
the nonlinear remainder.

Both proposed steps require correction.  The raw symmetric coefficient has a
nonzero strict low--high principal term and is only in
$H^{-3/2-\delta}$ after taking its spatial divergence.  The same issue affects
the R-070 regularity attribution of the pure-$pp$ and Cartan currents.
Covariance centering does not remove this nonzero-frequency term.  This note
proves the correct fixed-floor matrix one-form lift in
$H^{-1/2-\delta}$, extends the R-068 payment to every $1<s<2$, and thereby
closes the complete symmetric and Cartan linear frame with larger but finite
probability moments.

The nonlinear terminal square also has a genuine frame kernel.  An exact
production fixture makes the remaining nonlinear coefficient term affine and
unbounded along a derivative-displacement direction invisible to that square.
Thus the next theorem must be an integrated Cameron--Martin/strict-past
estimate for the kernel leakage, not a pointwise Schur absorption.

All statements use the hash-pinned A1 covariance on the fixed torus, the A7
covariance-normal convention, fixed $e>0$, and common scalar real-even
spectral approximations.  The linear-frame completion is a continuum model
statement.
The kernel fixture is finite-dimensional and exact for every $e>0$.  No
finite-energy one-use or Nelson conclusion is asserted.

\section{Exact weighted one-form and its polynomial core}

Use the R-070 notation
\[
 \rho=|U|^2,\quad m_r=U^TS_rU,\quad q_r={m_r\over\rho+e},
 \quad p_r=2S_rU,\quad v_r=2(S_r-q_rI)U,
\]
\[
 P_{r,i}=p_r\cdot\partial_iU,\qquad
 V_{r,i}=v_r\cdot\partial_iU,
\]
and
\[
 K_{r,p}=2S_r,\qquad
 K_{r,v}=2(S_r-q_rI)-{v_rU^T+Uv_r^T\over\rho+e}.
\]
The complete symmetric current preceding the R-070 divergence is
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal J_{r,i}^{\rm lin}={}&q_{11}P_{r,i}K_{r,p}
 +q_{12}(P_{r,i}K_{r,v}+V_{r,i}K_{r,p})\\
 &+q_{22}V_{r,i}K_{r,v},
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{2.1}
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb Q_r^{\rm lin}=-{1\over2}\sum_i\partial_i
 \mathcal J_{r,i}^{\rm lin}.                                 \tag{2.2}
\]

Put $R_i=\partial_i\rho$ and $H_r=K_{r,v}-K_{r,p}$.  Directly,
\[
 V_{r,i}=P_{r,i}-q_rR_i,                                     \tag{2.3}
\]
\[
 \begin{split}
 P_{r,i}K_{r,v}&=P_{r,i}K_{r,p}+P_{r,i}H_r,\\
 V_{r,i}K_{r,p}&=P_{r,i}K_{r,p}-q_rR_iK_{r,p},\\
 V_{r,i}K_{r,v}&=P_{r,i}K_{r,p}+P_{r,i}H_r
                 -q_rR_iK_{r,p}-q_rR_iH_r.
 \end{split}                                                  \tag{2.4}
\]
Consequently the full polynomial core of (2.1), not merely its visibly
labelled $pp$ summand, is
\[
 (q_{11}+2q_{12}+q_{22})P_{r,i}K_{r,p}.                       \tag{2.5}
\]
At the production point
\[
 2q_{12}+q_{22}=0.00609374999999848\ne0,
 \qquad q_{11}+2q_{12}+q_{22}=0.0105937499999974.             \tag{2.6}
\]
Thus the nominal non-$pp$ block contains a nonzero copy of the same
polynomial current.  Equation (2.5) is useful bookkeeping, but extracting it
does not improve the generic regularity of the remaining rational one-form.

Entrywise there is a smooth matrix one-form $\Theta_r$ such that
\[
 (\mathcal J_{r,i}^{\rm lin})_{\mu\nu}
 =\Theta_{r,\mu\nu,a}(U)\,\partial_iU^a.                    \tag{2.7}
\]
The positive floor makes all derivatives of $\Theta_r$ smooth with at most
polynomial growth.  This is the correct model class.

\section{The raw one-tenth attribution is false}

Take a nonzero constant low-frequency background $x$ and a high-frequency
Gaussian perturbation $z_n$.  Near the origin at fixed floor,
\[
 q_r(x)=O(|x|^2/e),\quad v_r(x)=p_r(x)+O(|x|^3/e),\quad
 K_{r,v}(x)=K_{r,p}+O(|x|^2/e).
\]
Hence the nominal non-$pp$ one-form coefficient has the principal part
\[
 \Theta_r^{\rm np}(x)
 =(2q_{12}+q_{22})p_r(x)K_{r,p}+O(|x|^3/e),                  \tag{3.1}
\]
which is nonzero for a suitable $r,x$ by (2.6).  The strict low--high
contribution to (2.2) then contains
\[
 -{1\over2}\Theta_r^{\rm np}(x)\Delta z_n.                  \tag{3.2}
\]
Since the production covariance is comparable to $\langle n\rangle^{-4}$,
the Fourier variance of (3.2) is bounded below and above by nonzero constants
on a fixed cone.  Therefore
\[
 \E\|\mathbb Q_{r,\Lambda}^{\rm np}\|_{H^{-1-\kappa}}^2
 \gtrsim\sum_{|n|\le\Lambda}\langle n\rangle^{-2-2\kappa}
 \asymp\Lambda^{1-2\kappa}.                                \tag{3.3}
\]
The raw attribution fails for $\kappa\le1/2$; at $\kappa=1/10$ the growth is
$\Lambda^{4/5}$.  Subtracting a same-point covariance changes only the
zero-frequency contraction channel and cannot cancel (3.2).

The pure polynomial term has the same low--high principal symbol, so the
R-070 statement that the raw pure-$pp$ Laplacian is an
$H^{-1-1/10}$ centered derivative-square model is also withdrawn.  Likewise,
the Gaussian coefficient in the Cartan term is a first-derivative one-form
current with generic regularity $H^{-1/2-\delta}$, not $H^{-1/10}$.

R-063 does not contradict this conclusion.  Its improved jets explicitly
remove the low-value/high-root region and retain the omitted lower chaoses in
the exact grouped reconstruction.  They cannot be relabelled as the literal
full product in (2.7).

\section{Fixed-floor matrix one-form Sobolev lift}

\subsection{Statement}

Let $U$ be a centered stationary finite-dimensional Gaussian field on
$\mathbb T_L^3$ with even matrix covariance
\[
 c\langle k\rangle^{-4}I\le\widehat C(k)
 \le C\langle k\rangle^{-4}I.                              \tag{4.1}
\]
Let $U_\Lambda=R_\Lambda U$, where $R_\Lambda$ is any admitted common
real-even scalar approximation from R-050.  Let
$\Theta:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^{m\times d}$ be smooth, with every
derivative of polynomial growth.  Define smooth currents
\[
 \mathcal J_{\Theta,i}^{(\Lambda)}
 =\Theta(U_\Lambda)\partial_iU_\Lambda.                    \tag{4.2}
\]

\noindent\textbf{Matrix one-form Sobolev lift.}
For every finite $p$ and every $\delta>0$, (4.2) converges to one regulator-
independent canonical geometric current in
\[
 L^p(\Omega;H^{-1/2-\delta}).                              \tag{4.3}
\]
The current is a continuous reconstruction from the R-050 enhanced field
$(U,\mathbb X)$, where
\[
 \mathbb X_i^{ab}=U^a\circ\partial_iU^b.                   \tag{4.4}
\]
No first-derivative current counterterm occurs in the admitted regulator
class.

\subsection{Proof}

Fix $\alpha\in(1/3,1/2)$.  R-050 gives, in every finite probability moment,
\[
 U\in\mathcal C^\alpha\cap H^{1/2-\epsilon},\qquad
 \partial_iU\in H^{-1/2-\epsilon},                        \tag{4.5}
\]
and the Fourier--Wick estimate
\[
 \E|\widehat{\mathbb X_i^{ab}}(n)|^2
 \lesssim\langle n\rangle^{-3}.                          \tag{4.6}
\]
Thus $\mathbb X_i^{ab}\in H^{-\epsilon}$ in every finite probability
moment, and the coupled-cutoff tails converge there.  Gaussian
hypercontractivity upgrades the second-moment estimate to every finite $p$.

Bony decomposition first puts the strict low--high term in the target space:
\[
 T_{\Theta(U)}\partial_iU\in H^{-1/2-\epsilon},            \tag{4.7}
\]
because $\Theta(U)$ is bounded pathwise and its bound has every finite
moment.  For the only nonclassical resonant term, first-order
paralinearisation gives
\[
 \Theta_a(U)=T_{\partial_b\Theta_a(U)}U^b+R_{\Theta,a},
 \qquad R_{\Theta,a}\in\mathcal C^{2\alpha}.             \tag{4.8}
\]
The leading resonance is reconstructed as
\[
 \partial_b\Theta_a(U)\,\mathbb X_i^{ba}                 \tag{4.9}
\]
plus the standard paraproduct commutator.  Multiplication in (4.9) is
classical after choosing $\epsilon<\alpha$.  The remainder resonance has
regularity $3\alpha-1>0$.  The high--low paraproduct is no rougher than
(4.7).  The same $L^2$ Littlewood--Paley estimates apply to matrix entries;
only finitely many entries are present.

Equations (4.7)--(4.9) define a locally Lipschitz map from
\[
 (U,\mathbb X)\longmapsto\mathcal J_\Theta
 \quad\hbox{in }H^{-1/2-\delta}.                          \tag{4.10}
\]
Polynomial-growth coefficient bounds are integrable because the Gaussian
$\mathcal C^\alpha$ norm has every finite moment.  Convergence and scheme
independence of the R-050 enhancement therefore imply (4.3).  Finally,
Gaussian integration by parts gives
\[
 \E[\Theta_a(U_\Lambda)\partial_iU_\Lambda^a]
 =\E[\partial_b\Theta_a(U_\Lambda)]
   \operatorname{Cov}(U_\Lambda^b,\partial_iU_\Lambda^a)=0,\tag{4.11}
\]
because the common covariance is even.  This proves the no-current-
counterterm assertion.

The corresponding covariance-normal divergence identity is worth recording.
At every finite cutoff,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \partial_i[\Theta_a(U)\partial_iU^a]
 ={}&\partial_b\Theta_a(U)
   (\partial_iU^b\partial_iU^a-\Gamma_i^{ba})\\
 &+\Theta_a(U)\diamond\partial_i^2U^a,
 \end{split}                                               \tag{4.12}
\]
where
\[
 \Theta_a(U)\diamond\partial_i^2U^a
 :=\Theta_a(U)\partial_i^2U^a
   +\Gamma_i^{ba}\partial_b\Theta_a(U).                  \tag{4.13}
\]
The two covariance terms in (4.12) cancel exactly.  Keeping only the first
line or citing a balanced derivative-square jet while deleting (4.13) would
break the finite-cutoff identity.

\section{Complete linear payment}

Apply the theorem entrywise to (2.7).  For every finite $p$ and $\delta>0$,
\[
 \mathcal J_r^{\rm lin}\in L^p(\Omega;H^{-1/2-\delta}),
 \qquad
 \mathbb Q_r^{\rm lin}\in L^p(\Omega;H^{-3/2-\delta}).    \tag{5.1}
\]

The R-068 deterministic payment extends without change of architecture.
For $1<s<2$, its low and high endpoints give
\[
 \|\Pi(A)\|_{H^s}
 \le C X^{s/2}Y^{(2-s)/6},                               \tag{5.2}
\]
where
\[
 X=\sum_j\|A_j\|_{H^2}^2,\qquad
 Y=\left\|\left(\sum_j|A_j|^2\right)^{1/2}\right\|_6^6.
\]
If $M_s=\|Q\|_{H^{-s}}$, three-factor weighted Young gives
\[
 \boxed{
 |\langle Q,\Pi(A)\rangle|
 \le\eta X+\zeta Y
 +C_s\eta^{-{3s\over2(2-s)}}\zeta^{-1/2}
 M_s^{3/(2-s)}.}                                         \tag{5.3}
\]
Set $s=3/2+\delta$, $0<\delta<1/2$.  Then (5.1)--(5.3) pay the
entire symmetric linear frame, including the polynomial and rational,
$pp$ and non-$pp$ pieces, in one global allocation.  At $\delta=1/10$,
\[
 |\mathcal L_J^{\rm sym}|
 \le\eta X+\zeta Y+C\eta^{-6}\zeta^{-1/2}
 \|\mathcal J^{\rm lin}\|_{H^{-3/5}}^{15/2}.             \tag{5.4}
\]
Here the displayed norm is that of the undifferentiated current; its
divergence is tested in $H^{-8/5}$.  Every required moment is supplied by
(4.3).

The R-070 Cartan coefficient is also a finite smooth matrix one-form in $U$.
The same theorem gives a model $\mathbb J^{\rm Car}$ in
$H^{-1/2-\delta}$.  The deterministic interpolation used in R-070, now at
$\kappa=1/2+\delta$, yields
\[
 \|A\wedge DA\|_{H^{1/2+\delta}}
 \le C X^{(5+2\delta)/6}Y^{(1-2\delta)/18}.              \tag{5.5}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 |\langle\mathbb J^{\rm Car},A\wedge DA\rangle|
 \le\eta X+\zeta Y
 +C\eta^{-{3(5+2\delta)\over2(1-2\delta)}}\zeta^{-1/2}
 \|\mathbb J^{\rm Car}\|_{H^{-1/2-\delta}}^{9/(1-2\delta)}.}
                                                                    \tag{5.6}
\]
At $\delta=1/10$, the negative $\eta$ exponent is $39/4$ and the model
moment is $45/4$.  These are finite.  Equations (5.4) and (5.6) replace the
false $\kappa=1/10$ attributions in R-070 and close its full weighted linear
subgate.

\section{The nonlinear terminal square has kernel leakage}

For one generator and direction suppress indices and put
\[
 z=U,\quad a=A,\quad y=G_i,\quad b=C_i,
\]
\[
 M_0=M(z),\quad M_1=M(z+a),\quad
 E=M_1-M_0-DM(z)[a],\quad w=M_0^Ty,                       \tag{6.1}
\]
\[
 \delta=M_1^T(y+b)-M_0^Ty.                               \tag{6.2}
\]
Combining the $G\otimes G$ part of the coefficient remainder with
$\mathcal N_J$, the cross, and the terminal square gives the exact local
identity
\[
 \sum_r\left\{\langle w_r,Q_{\rm II}E_r^T(y+b)\rangle
       +{1\over2}|\delta_r|_{Q_{\rm II}}^2\right\}.       \tag{6.3}
\]
The remaining covariance part is
\[
 -{1\over2}[B(z+a)-B(z)-DB(z)[a]]:\Gamma_i,              \tag{6.4}
\]
which is independent of $b$.

The following six-real production fixture is exact:
\[
 \begin{split}
 z&=(-1,-1,0,0,-1,0),\\
 x=z+a&=(1,-1,0,1,0,0),\\
 a&=(2,0,0,1,1,0),\qquad y=(1,0,0,0,0,0),\\
 n&=(-1,0,0,1,-1,0),\qquad b=tn.
 \end{split}                                               \tag{6.5}
\]
For every $e>0$ and every $r=1,2,3$,
\[
 M_r(x)^Tn=0.                                               \tag{6.6}
\]
Indeed $n\cdot x=n\cdot S_rx=0$.  Hence the terminal current square in
(6.3) is independent of $t$, and its $c=M_1^Tb$ cross and square pieces
vanish.  Direct production-frame algebra gives
\[
 n\cdot\sum_r E_rQ_{\rm II}w_r
 ={27(6e^2+22e+27)\over400(e+3)^3}>0.                    \tag{6.7}
\]
At $e=10^{-12}$ this coefficient is
$0.06749999999997061$.  Consequently
\[
 \mathcal R_{\rm shift}(z,a,y,tn)=C(z,a,y,\Gamma)+t,c_e,
 \qquad c_e>0,                                             \tag{6.8}
\]
and it is unbounded below as $t\to-\infty$.

This is not a counterexample to an integrated estimate: a spatial field with
large $b=DA$ also spends Cameron--Martin energy.  It does prove that the
terminal square alone cannot absorb $\mathcal N_J$, and that no pointwise or
derivative-energy-free version of the R-070 successor can hold.  Even adding
back the separated linear frame does not remove the need for an energy or
expectation mechanism; the old-frame first variation carries the compensating
derivative displacement in the exact untranslated identity.

matrix one-form lift (4.3).
\item \textbf{Retired:} literal raw

\item \textbf{Closed:} extend R-068 from $s=1+\kappa$ to every $1<s<2$.

\item \textbf{Corrected and closed:} the Cartan current is paid at

\item \textbf{Retired:} pointwise absorption of the nonlinear coefficient

\item \textbf{Still open:} an integrated strict-past kernel-leakage estimate.

bad/tip region as a whole secant.  It may not invoke Malliavin derivatives of

The smallest honest next statement is therefore a range/kernel grouped form:
decompose the nonlinear derivative-displacement coefficient relative to the

after spatial integration, with one arbitrary Cameron--Martin allocation,
one terminal sextic allocation, and one integrable random constant.  The
decomposition must be implemented without a singular inverse at the cone tip;
the R-068 good/bad whole-secant architecture remains load-bearing.
