\section{Complete-owner gauge collapse}

Fix a conditional fibre $\cF$.  Let $B=B^T\succeq0$ be a random Gram
coefficient, let $G$ be the derivative carrier, and let $c,R,\Gamma$ be
$\cF$-measurable with $R=R^T\succ0$.  Put
\[
 K=\E_{\cF}B,\qquad q=\E_{\cF}(BG),\qquad
 A=K+2R,                                                   \tag{2.1}
\]
and use the R-097--R-099 coordinates
\[
 S_R={1\over2}\|c+A^{-1}q\|_A^2,\qquad P_R=c^TRc,         \tag{2.2}
\]
\[
 \mathfrak C_{\rm post}
 =\E_{\cF}[B:(G^{\otimes2}-\Gamma)]-q^TA^{-1}q.           \tag{2.3}
\]

% evidence-anchor: theorem-2.1-complete-owner-gauge-collapse
\subsection*{Theorem 2.1 (complete-owner gauge identity)}

On every such fibre,
\[
\boxed{
 S_R+{1\over2}\mathfrak C_{\rm post}-P_R
 ={1\over2}\E_{\cF}
 \left[B:\bigl((G+c)^{\otimes2}-\Gamma\bigr)\right].}    \tag{2.4}
\]
Consequently the complete owner after the once-only baseline subtraction is
\[
\boxed{
 \mathcal O_{\cF}(B,R)
 :=S_R+{1\over2}\mathfrak C_{\rm post}-P_R-W_0
 ={1\over2}\E_{\cF}
 \left[B:\bigl((G+c)^{\otimes2}-\Gamma\bigr)\right]-W_0.} \tag{2.5}
\]
In particular, the right side is independent of $R$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Expanding the Schur square gives
\[
 S_R={1\over2}c^TAc+c^Tq+{1\over2}q^TA^{-1}q.             \tag{2.6}
\]
The last term cancels the rebate in half of (2.3).  Since
$A=K+2R$, subtracting $P_R$ leaves $c^TKc/2+c^Tq$.
Adding the raw Wick term in (2.3) produces exactly the expansion of the
right side of (2.4).  Subtract $W_0$ to obtain (2.5). \qed

This theorem sharpens the interpretation of the R-099 payment gauge.  A new
choice of ridge can improve an intermediate Schur coordinate, but the
matching square and actual payment change by the opposite amount.  Payment
tuning cannot improve the sign of the physical owner.

\section{Rows and revelations exchange, but do not create mass}

Let $B=\sum_aB_a$, $R=\sum_aR_a$, and $W_0=\sum_aW_{0,a}$ be matching
splits.  Define
\[
 A_a=\E_{\cF}B_a+2R_a,\qquad q_a=\E_{\cF}(B_aG),         \tag{3.1}
\]
\[
 D_{\rm row}=\sum_aq_a^TA_a^{-1}q_a-q^TA^{-1}q\ge0.      \tag{3.2}
\]
The nonnegativity is the R-098 posterior superadditivity theorem.

% evidence-anchor: theorem-3.1-exact-complete-owner-row-additivity
\subsection*{Theorem 3.1 (exact complete-owner row additivity)}

The bracket and square gaps are
\[
 \mathfrak C_{\rm post}(B;R)
 -\sum_a\mathfrak C_{\rm post}(B_a;R_a)=D_{\rm row},      \tag{3.3}
\]
\[
 S_R(B)-\sum_aS_{R_a}(B_a)=-{1\over2}D_{\rm row}.         \tag{3.4}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
 \mathcal O_{\cF}(B,R)=\sum_a\mathcal O_{\cF}(B_a,R_a).} \tag{3.5}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
The raw Wick part of (2.3), $K$, the $c^TKc$ term, $c^Tq$, the payment, and
the baseline are linear under the matching split.  The only nonlinear
quantity is the inverse-matrix rebate.  Comparing it gives (3.3), while the
same comparison in (2.6) gives (3.4).  Their half-weighted sum vanishes.
Equivalently, apply (2.5), whose right side is visibly row-linear. \qed

Thus the cross-row Schur compensation is available for the stronger
bracket-only target, but not as an additional reserve in the complete owner.
Counting it after retaining the full square duplicates ownership.  This is
registered as
\texttt{NG-2026-07-27-A13-COMPLETE-OWNER-CROSS-ROW-SCHUR-RESERVE}.

There is an analogous conditioning exchange.  Let
$\cH_0\subset\cH_1$ both contain $\cF\vee\sigma(B)$ and define
\[
 \mu_r=\E[G\mid\cH_r],\qquad V_r=\Cov(G\mid\cH_r),
 \qquad r=0,1.                                           \tag{3.6}
\]
Conditional Pythagoras gives
\[
 D_{\rm rev}
 =\E_{\cF}\bigl[(\mu_1-\mu_0)^TB(\mu_1-\mu_0)\bigr]

\[
 \E_{\cF}[B:V_1]-\E_{\cF}[B:V_0]=-D_{\rm rev},          \tag{3.8}

\[
 \E_{\cF}[(\mu_1+c)^TB(\mu_1+c)]

Finer revelation transfers exactly the same mass from covariance to mean.
It creates neither a sign nor free Gaussian entropy; the information-cost

\section{Posterior covariance-debt normal form}

Let $\cH\supset\cF\vee\sigma(B)$, and write
\[
 \mu_{\cH}=\E[G\mid\cH],\qquad
 V_{\cH}=\Cov(G\mid\cH).                                \tag{4.1}
\]

% evidence-anchor: theorem-4.1-posterior-covariance-debt-normal-form
\subsection*{Theorem 4.1 (posterior covariance-debt normal form)}

The complete pre-baseline owner equals
\[
\boxed{
 S_R+{1\over2}\mathfrak C_{\rm post}-P_R
 ={1\over2}\E_{\cF}\left[
 B:(V_{\cH}-\Gamma)
 +(\mu_{\cH}+c)^TB(\mu_{\cH}+c)
 \right].}                                               \tag{4.2}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Because $B$ is $\cH$-measurable,
\[
 \E[G^{\otimes2}\mid\cH]
 =V_{\cH}+\mu_{\cH}^{\otimes2}.                        \tag{4.3}
\]
Insert (4.3) into the right side of (2.4) and expand the shifted mean. \qed

After summing spatially and over derivative directions, define
\[
 \mathcal M_{\cH}
 =\E\sum_i\int(\mu_{\cH,i}+c_i)^TB
                (\mu_{\cH,i}+c_i)\dd x,                \tag{4.4}
\]
\[
 \mathcal D_{\cH}
 =\E\sum_i\int B:(\Gamma_i-V_{\cH,i})\dd x
  +2\E W_0.                                              \tag{4.5}
\]
Then the target is exactly
\[
\boxed{\mathcal T={1\over2}
       (\mathcal M_{\cH}-\mathcal D_{\cH}).}             \tag{4.6}
\]
Hence the missing coefficient-unconditioned theorem is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{
 \mathcal D_{\cH}\le
 \mathcal M_{\cH}+2\eta\E X+2\zeta\E Y+C_{\eta,\zeta}.} \tag{4.7}

A stronger useful version would place $(1-\delta)\mathcal M_{\cH}$ on the
right, leaving a positive posterior-mean reserve.  Equation (4.7), rather
than positivity of $\mathfrak C_{\rm post}$, is the exact frontier.

For the production frame
\[

\]
the two sides have the geometric forms
\[

 =\E\sum_{\alpha,i}\int
 \left\|Q_{\rm II}^{1/2}M_\alpha(Z)^T
       (\mu_{\cH,i}+c_i)\right\|^2\dd x,               \tag{4.9}
\]
\[
 \mathcal D_{\cH}-2\E W_0
 =\E\sum_{\alpha,i}\int

\section{Heat-centred full-Wick reveal residual}

\[

For one derivative direction put
\[

\]
and let $d_j=Dg_j$ be the fresh derivative root.  Its full Wick increment is
\[
 \Delta Q_j=Q_j-Q_{j-1}
 =2G_{j-1}\mathbin\odot d_j

% evidence-anchor: theorem-5.1-heat-centered-full-wick-residual
\subsection*{Theorem 5.1 (heat-centred full-Wick residual)}

At finite cutoff,
\[
\boxed{
 \E\langle H_j-H_{j-1},\Delta Q_j\rangle
 =\E\langle B(Z_*)-B(Z_j),\Delta Q_j\rangle.}           \tag{5.8}
\]
Moreover, uniformly in the terminal cutoff,
\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{j=j_0}^J\left|
 \E\int[B(Z_*)-B(Z_j)]:\Delta Q_j\dd x\right|
 \le C_{\rm full}2^{-j_0}X^{1/2}(1+Y)^{1/6}.}           \tag{5.9}
\]
Consequently, for every $\eta,\zeta\in(0,1]$,
\[
\boxed{
 \sum_j\left|
 \E\int[B(Z_*)-B(Z_j)]:\Delta Q_j\dd x\right|
 \le\eta X+\zeta Y+C_{\eta,\zeta},}                  \tag{5.10}
\]
with the same Young-loss type as R-094,
\[
 C_{\eta,\zeta}\le
 C\{1+\eta^{-1}+\eta^{-3/2}\zeta^{-1/2}\}.            \tag{5.11}
\]

\paragraph{Proof of the identity.}
The increment $\Delta Q_j$ is $\cF_j$-measurable and satisfies
$\E_{j-1}\Delta Q_j=0$.  Hence the $H_{j-1}$ term vanishes, and the tower
property gives
\[
 \E\langle H_j,\Delta Q_j\rangle
 =\E\langle B(Z_*)-B(Z_j),\Delta Q_j\rangle.            \tag{5.12}
\]

\paragraph{Proof of the estimate.}
R-094 assumes
\[
 \|d_j\|_6\le\kappa_1 2^{j/2},\qquad
 \|d_j^{\otimes2}-\Delta\Gamma_j\|_3
 \le\kappa_Q2^j.                                       \tag{5.13}
\]
For a centred Gaussian vector of covariance $C$,
\[
 \E|G|^6=(\operatorname{tr}C)^3
 +6(\operatorname{tr}C)(\operatorname{tr}C^2)
 +8\operatorname{tr}C^3
 \le15(\operatorname{tr}C)^3.                          \tag{5.14}
\]
The cumulative derivative covariance therefore gives, after absorbing the
fixed low block,
\[
 \|G_{j-1}\|_6\le C_G2^{j/2},\qquad
 C_G=15^{1/6}\kappa_\Gamma^{1/2}.                       \tag{5.15}
\]
Equations (5.4), (5.13), and (5.15) imply
\[
 \|\Delta Q_j\|_3
 \le(2C_G\kappa_1+\kappa_Q)2^j.                        \tag{5.16}
\]
Apply the R-094 Gram secant
\[
 |B(z+a)-B(z)|
 \le L_B\{(1+|z|)|a|+|a|^2\}                           \tag{5.17}
\]
with $a=A_{>j}$.  The proof of R-094 (4.5)--(4.9) now applies verbatim with
the constant in (5.16): the linear tail uses the strict-past $L^2$ control
decay, and the quadratic tail uses the weighted Hardy constant
$3+2\sqrt2$, product-space $L^2$--$L^6$ interpolation, and the accepted
Littlewood--Paley sixth-moment sum.  This proves (5.9).  The mixed exponents
$1/2$ and $1/6$ leave Young slack $1/3$, proving (5.10)--(5.11). \qed

The theorem closes the nonlinear future-control reveal/Jensen residual as a
