\section{The exact frozen-shell convention}

Let
\[
 x=\phi_{j-1},\qquad \xi_j=\phi_j-\phi_{j-1},\qquad
 z=\phi_j,                                               \tag{2.1}
\]
and let
\[
 \Gamma_{j,i}=\E[D_i\xi_jD_i\xi_j^T],\qquad
 \Gamma_{\le j,i}=\sum_{\ell\le j}\Gamma_{\ell,i}.    \tag{2.2}
\]
The A6/A7 convention is load-bearing here.  If
$D_i=\E[\partial_i\Psi\,\partial_i\Psi^\dagger]$, then
$\E[\Psi_k\Psi_k^\dagger]=2A(k)^{-1}$ and
$\Gamma_i=\tfrac12\operatorname{realify}(D_i)$.  Hence, after summing over
directions,
\[
 \Gamma_{\le j}=\operatorname{realify}\!\left(
 {1\over V}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\le j}}|k|^2A(k)^{-1}\right),
 \qquad \Lambda_{\le j}:=\{k_n:\max_a|n_a|\le N_j\}.  \tag{2.2a}
\]
There is no further factor one half in (2.2a).
For a matrix field $b$ define
\[
 q_b(w)={1\over2}\sum_i\int (D_iw)^Tb(D_iw),\qquad
 t_\Gamma(b)={1\over2}\sum_i\int\operatorname{Tr}(\Gamma_i b).
                                                                  \tag{2.3}
\]
The noncentral determinant in A9 integrates only the independent innovation
$\xi_j$.  Its exact covariance-normal variable is therefore
\[
 \boxed{Q_j^{\fr}=q_{B(x)}(z)-t_{\Gamma_j}(B(x)).}       \tag{2.4}
\]
The trace in (2.4) is $\Gamma_j$, not $\Gamma_{\le j}$.  The coefficient
increment is
\[
 \boxed{\cC_j=q_{B(z)-B(x)}(z)
       -t_{\Gamma_{\le j}}(B(z)-B(x)).}                 \tag{2.5}
\]
Consequently, for $0\le\theta\le1$,
\[
\begin{split}
 R_j^\theta:=\theta Q_j^{\fr}+\cC_j
 ={}&q_{B(z)}(z)-(1-\theta)q_{B(x)}(z)
      -t_{\Gamma_{\le j}}(B(z))\\
    &+t_{\Gamma_{\le j-1}}(B(x))
      +(1-\theta)t_{\Gamma_j}(B(x)).
\end{split}
\tag{2.6}
\]
This identity is bookkeeping, but it is load-bearing.  Using a cumulative
trace in (2.4) would define a different random variable and would invalidate
the A9 determinant factor.

\section{Raw retained energy and its sharp dyadic threshold}

Write
\[
 q_x=q_{B(x)}(z),\qquad q_z=q_{B(z)}(z).                 \tag{4.1}
\]
The raw part of (2.5) is $\cC_j^{\rm raw}=q_z-q_x$, and
\[
 \boxed{\theta Q_{j,\rm raw}^{\fr}+\cC_j^{\rm raw}
       =q_z-(1-\theta)q_x.}                              \tag{4.2}
\]
Thus $\cC_j^{\rm raw}\ge-q_x$, and $q_x=0$ implies
$\cC_j^{\rm raw}=q_z\ge0$.  The choice $\theta=1$ leaves $q_z\ge0$.
We now prove that no smaller fixed $\theta$ is a cost-free all-field choice,
even when $x$ and $z$ must arise from a genuine dyadic Fourier split.

\subsection{Strict-dyadic Blaschke lemma}

Fix $0<a_0<1$ and the inner function
\[
 F(w)={w-a_0\over1-a_0w},\qquad |F(e^{it})|=1.           \tag{4.3}
\]
Its analytic Fourier partial sum is
\[
 F_N=-a_0+(1-a_0^2)\sum_{n=1}^N a_0^{n-1}e^{int}.       \tag{4.4}
\]
Set
\[
 x_m=F_me_1,\qquad z_m=F_{2m}e_1.                       \tag{4.5}
\]
Then $x_m=P_{\le m}z_m$ and the innovation contains exactly the modes
$m<n\le2m$.  On this physical scalar ray the Class-II density with a base
$f$ and derivative carrier $d$ is
\[
 e_B(f;d)={1\over2}\left[a+2bs_f+cs_f^2\right]
             \left(2\operatorname{Re}(\overline f,d)\right)^2,
 \qquad s_f={\rho_{\rm floor}\over |f|^2+\rho_{\rm floor}}.     \tag{4.6}
\]
The fields $F_N$ converge uniformly to the unit-modulus $F$, so the bracket
in (4.6) stays between two positive constants.

An exact autocorrelation calculation gives
\[
 |F_N|^2=1-(1-a_0^2)a_0^{2N}
 -2(1-a_0^2)\sum_{k=1}^N a_0^{2N-k}\cos(kt),             \tag{4.7}
\]
hence
\[
 \|\partial_t|F_{2m}|^2\|_2^2
 =2(1-a_0^2)^2\sum_{k=1}^{2m}k^2a_0^{8m-2k}
 =O_{a_0}(m^2a_0^{4m}).                                 \tag{4.8}
\]
This bounds $q_z=O_{a_0}(m^2a_0^{4m})$.  On the other hand, the sine mode
$m+1$ of
\[
 J_{\fr,m}=2\operatorname{Re}
        (\overline{F_m}\,\partial_tF_{2m})               \tag{4.9}
\]
has coefficient
\[
 -2\sum_{p=0}^{m-1}(p+m+1)c_pc_{p+m+1}
 =-2a_0^{m+1}(1+o(1)),                                  \tag{4.10}
\]
where $c_0=-a_0$ and $c_n=(1-a_0^2)a_0^{n-1}$.  Parseval
therefore yields $q_x\ge c_{a_0}a_0^{2m}$ for all large $m$.  Combining
(4.8)--(4.10),
\[
 {q_z\over q_x}=O_{a_0}(m^2a_0^{2m})\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad {-\cC_j^{\rm raw}\over q_x}\longrightarrow1.  \tag{4.11}
\]
For every $\theta<1$, (4.2) is negative along (4.5) for all sufficiently
large $m$.  Thus
\[
 \boxed{\theta_{\rm raw,cost-free}=1}                   \tag{4.12}
\]
is sharp under strict dyadic geometry.  The executable at $a_0=1/2$ reaches
$0.999899249067$ by $m=10$ and separately verifies (4.7)--(4.8); this number
illustrates the theorem and is not its proof.

\section{Why a positive entropy budget is necessary}

Consider a proposed uniform relative estimate
\[
 \E_\nu\sum_{j\le J}R_j^\theta
 \ge-\alpha_c\cH(\nu\mid\gamma_J)
     -\epsilon_6\E_\nu\|\phi_J\|_6^6
     -K_\theta\E_\nu\|\phi_J\|_4^4-C_\theta.         \tag{6.1}
\]
If $\alpha_c=0$, take the plane-wave mean in (5.1) at the top shell and the
finite-dimensional absolutely continuous law
\[
 \nu_{N,\sigma}=N(h_N,\sigma^2C_N).                     \tag{6.2}
\]
At each fixed $N$, let $\sigma\downarrow0$.  The fixed positive floor makes
all displayed finite-dimensional functions continuous with polynomial
growth.  Equation (5.4) sends the left side to $-dNA^2$, while the quartic
and sextic terms are $O(A^4)$ and $O(A^6)$ independently of $N$.  No uniform
$C_\theta$ can repair (6.1).  Thus
\[
 \boxed{\alpha_c>0\ \hbox{is necessary}.}              \tag{6.3}
\]

This defect is not a counterexample when entropy is present.  If
$\sum_i\|\Gamma_{\le j,i}\|_F\le C_\Gamma K$ and the production symbol
obeys
\[
 A(k)\ge c_{\rm sym}(1+|k|^2)^2I,
 \qquad c_{\rm sym}=0.10836453058711468,                 \tag{6.4}
\]
then its trace loss is $O(KA^2)$, whereas the Cameron--Martin entropy of the
same shift is at least $O(c_{\rm sym}K^4A^2)$.  Their ratio is $O(K^{-3})$.
This proves entropy mitigation only for the identified plane-wave family; it
does not prove the relative form bound for arbitrary adapted laws.

\section{The registered triad budget is necessary, not global}

For the A9 triad

For $x=Ae_1$, $z=A(1+\epsilon g_K)e_1$, and $A=tK$, define

 c_F=4a\epsilon^2,\quad

 \boxed{\alpha_c\epsilon_6\ge

The no-extra-budget threshold on this family is

 \theta\ge\epsilon-{5\over4}\epsilon^2.                \tag{7.5}

It is $3/16$ at $\epsilon=0.3$ and has supremum $7/36$ over

\section{Exact action mismatch and the two remaining gates}

Let the actual final-cutoff A7 energy at scale $j$ be
\[
 V_j=q_{B(\phi_j)}(D\phi_j)-t_{\Gamma_{\le j}}(B(\phi_j)).
                                                                  \tag{8.1}
\]
The definitions (2.4)--(2.5), with $x=\phi_{j-1}$ and $z=\phi_j$, give the
exact identity
\[
 \boxed{Q_j^{\fr}+\cC_j
 =V_j-V_{j-1}+q_{B(\phi_{j-1})}(D\phi_{j-1}).}          \tag{8.2}
\]
Indeed $Q_j^{\fr}+\cC_j=V_j+t_{\Gamma_{\le j-1}}(B(x))$ and
$t_{\Gamma_{\le j-1}}(B(x))=q_{B(x)}(Dx)-V_{j-1}$.
Consequently, when $V_0=0$,
\[
 \sum_{j\le J}(Q_j^{\fr}+\cC_j)=V_J+E_J,\qquad
 E_J:=\sum_{j\le J}q_{B(\phi_{j-1})}(D\phi_{j-1})\ge0. \tag{8.3}
\]
This is an obstruction, not a favourable remainder: reconstructing $V_J$
requires subtracting $E_J$.  A lower bound for the larger shell expression
does not lower-bound the actual A7 action.  The gate
\texttt{A10-CLASSII-MULTISCALE-ACTION-DECOMPOSITION} may be closed either by
a true-increment determinant theorem or by a cutoff-uniform upper form bound
\[
 \E_\nu E_J\le\alpha_d\cH(\nu\mid\gamma_J)
 +\epsilon_d\E_\nu\|\phi_J\|_6^6
 +K_d\E_\nu\|\phi_J\|_4^4+C_d.                       \tag{8.4}
\]
No such estimate is proved here.

For $\alpha_c>0$ put
\[
 G_J=\sum_{j\le J}R_j^\theta
       +\epsilon_6\|\phi_J\|_6^6+K_\theta\|\phi_J\|_4^4.   \tag{8.5}
\]
At finite cutoff, the Gibbs variational formula makes
\[
 \E_\nu G_J\ge-\alpha_c\cH(\nu\mid\gamma_J)-C_\theta
 \quad(\nu\ll\gamma_J)                                \tag{8.6}
\]
equivalent to
\[
 \boxed{\log\E_{\gamma_J}\exp(-G_J/\alpha_c)
       \le C_\theta/\alpha_c.}                          \tag{8.7}
\]
The uniform version is the separate gate
\texttt{A10-CLASSII-STABILISED-}\allowbreak
\texttt{RELATIVE-LOG-LAPLACE}.  It controls the shell expression in (8.5),
but (8.3) proves that it does not recover the actual A7 action by itself.

The A9 determinant alone cannot prove (8.7) by discarding endpoint terms.
For every $\theta<1$, $B(z)-(1-\theta)B(x)$ may be indefinite and the past
$x$ is unbounded.  A successful proof must retain the self-coupled endpoint
square or allocate the final sextic through a trace-safe mechanism.

\section{Closed cube filtration and conditional composition}

The filtration prerequisite can be closed for one concrete route.  Put
$N_j=2^j$ and
\[
 P_{\le j}=\mathbf1_{\{\max_a|n_a|\le N_j\}},
 \qquad \xi_j=(P_{\le j}-P_{\le j-1})\phi.             \tag{9.1}
\]
The production Gaussian is diagonal in Fourier momentum, so disjoint blocks
$\xi_j$ are independent.  In one dimension the symmetric partial-sum
operator satisfies
\[
 S_Nf=e^{-iNx}P_+(e^{iNx}f)
      -e^{i(N+1)x}P_+(e^{-i(N+1)x}f).                  \tag{9.2}
\]
The M.~Riesz theorem bounds $P_+$ on $L^4$ uniformly; Fubini and tensorisation
therefore give
$\|P_{\le j}\|_{L^4(\T^3)\to L^4(\T^3)}\le C_4^3$.
Thus $\|\phi_{j-1}\|_4\le C_4^3\|\phi_J\|_4$.  Cube shells also have
$O(2^{3j})$ modes of size $|k|\asymp2^j$, so the A9 counting gives
\[
 \|T_{j,B(\phi_{j-1})}\|_{\mathfrak S_2}^2
 \le C_{\rm sh}(L)M_R^4c_{\rm sym}^{-2}w_j
       \|B(\phi_{j-1})\|_2^2,\qquad \sum_jw_j<\infty. \tag{9.3}
\]
This proves the scoped subgate
\texttt{A10-CLASSII-DYADIC-FILTRATION-REALISATION}.  It does not extend to
overlapping smooth Littlewood--Paley increments without another proof.

Write the actual production action as $S_J=U_J+V_J$, with the non-Class-II
part satisfying
\[
 U_J\ge\int\left({\lambda\over4}\rho^2
                         +{\gamma\over6}\rho^3\right). \tag{9.3a}

Let $s=1-\theta$.  The A9 noncentral determinant and conditional entropy
chain rule yield
\[
 \E_\nu\sum_j sQ_j^{\fr}
 \ge-\alpha_f\cH(\nu\mid\gamma_J)
       -K_f\E_\nu\|\phi_J\|_4^4,                       \tag{9.4}

where
\[
 C_{\fr}=C_{\rm sh}(L)M_R^4c_{\rm sym}^{-2}
     \beta_B^2C_4^{12}S_{\rm dy},\qquad
 K_f={s^2C_{\fr}\over4\alpha_f}.                       \tag{9.5}

Assume both open estimates (8.4) and (8.6).  With the production lower bound

 \alpha=\alpha_f+\alpha_c+\alpha_d,\quad
 B_6={\gamma\over6}-\epsilon_6-\epsilon_d,\quad

Equations (8.3), (8.4), (8.6), and (9.4) then give

If $B_6>0$ and some $p>1$ satisfies $p\alpha<1$, the Gibbs variational

 \boxed{\log\E_{\gamma_J}e^{-pS_J}
 \le p(C_\theta+C_d)+{4pL^3A_4^3\over27B_6^2}.}        \tag{9.8}

This is the A7 Nelson estimate conditional on both named open gates.

At the production point, the proof-friendly target
