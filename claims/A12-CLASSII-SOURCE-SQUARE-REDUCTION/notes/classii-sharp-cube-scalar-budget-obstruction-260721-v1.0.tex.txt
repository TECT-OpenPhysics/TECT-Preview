The setting is the fixed torus $\T_L^3$ with $L=16$,
$\alpha_L=2\pi/L$, dyadic integers $N_j=2^j$, and
\[
 S_j=\mathbf1_{\{\max_a|n_a|\le N_j\}}.

\section{The separated constants and the production target}

A12 defined
\[
 M_6=\sup_{f\ne0}
 {\|\sup_j|S_jf|\|_6\over\|f\|_6}
\]
and
\[
 Q_6=\sup_{f\ne0}
 {\left\|\left(\sum_j{ |DS_{j-1}f|^2\over
 1+\alpha_L^2(N_{j-1}+1)^2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_6
 \over\|f\|_6}.
\]
Its valid qualitative reduction is
\[
 \sum_j\|G_j^*B(S_{j-1}\phi)DS_{j-1}\phi\|_2^2
 \le {\beta_{\op}^2\over c_{\rm sym}}M_R^2H_6\|\phi\|_6^6.
 \tag{2.1}
\]
At $p=1.1$, $M_R=1$, and production $\gamma=1.62$, source-only
absorption would require
\[
 H_6<29.62571266025876.                              \tag{2.2}
\]
Equation (2.2) is a proposed threshold, not an established estimate.

\section{Dyadic boundary modulation}

Let $P_{\le0}$ be the one-dimensional Fourier projection onto frequencies
$n\le0$.  Hollenbeck and Verbitsky proved the exact Riesz projection norm
\[
 \|P_{\le0}\|_{L^6(\T)\to L^6(\T)}
 =\csc(\pi/6)=2.                                      \tag{3.1}
\]
Choose a finite trigonometric polynomial $g$ and let
$F(x)=g(x_1)g(x_2)g(x_3)$.  For a dyadic $N$ larger than the Fourier degree
of $g$, set
\[
 U_NF(x)=e^{i\alpha_LN(x_1+x_2+x_3)}F(x).
\]
For every relative frequency in the support of $F$,
\[
 -N\le N+n_a\le N\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n_a\le0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 S_NU_NF=U_N(P_{\le0}^{\otimes3}F).                  \tag{3.2}
\]
The input fits below the next terminal cube $2N$, so (3.2) is an admissible
finite-cutoff witness.  Finite trigonometric polynomials are dense in $L^6$;
using product near-extremisers for (3.1) gives
\[
 M_6\ge\|P_{\le0}^{\otimes3}\|_{6\to6}\ge2^3=8.   \tag{3.3}
\]

Use the single square-function term whose previous cutoff is $N$.  If
$h=P_{\le0}^{\otimes3}F$, then
\[
 D(U_Nh)=U_N\{i\alpha_LN(1,1,1)h+Dh\}.
\]
After division by $\sqrt{1+\alpha_L^2(N+1)^2}$ and passage to large dyadic
$N$, the norm ratio tends
\[
 \sqrt3,{\|h\|_6\over\|F\|_6}.
\]
Taking the same product near-extremisers yields
\[
 Q_6\ge8\sqrt3.                                      \tag{3.4}
\]
Combining (3.3)--(3.4),
\[
 \boxed{H_6\ge8^4(8\sqrt3)^2=3\,8^6=786432.}        \tag{3.5}
\]
This is more than $2.65\times10^4$ times the target (2.2).

\section{The coefficient-blind six-linear route also fails}

Define the scalar-envelope constant
\[
 \cP_6:=\sup_{f\ne0,J}
 {\displaystyle\sum_{j\le J}{1\over1+\kappa_j^2}
 \int_{\T_L^3}|S_{j-1}f|^4|DS_{j-1}f|^2
 \over\|f\|_6^6}.                                   \tag{4.1}
\]
The single boundary term of the preceding witness converges to
\[
 3{\|P_{\le0}^{\otimes3}F\|_6^6\over\|F\|_6^6}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{\cP_6\ge3\,8^6=786432.}                     \tag{4.2}
\]
Even a one-axis witness gives $\cP_6\ge2^6=64$, already above the production
target.  Thus replacing $M_6^4Q_6^2$ by a direct six-linear theorem after the
pointwise estimate
\[
 |B(u)Du|\le\beta_{\op}|u|^2|Du|
\]
cannot repair the budget.  The pointwise multiplier bound in A12 controls
symbol size and qualitative Marcinkiewicz variation; it does not state that
the multilinear $L^6$ operator norm is one.

\section{Independent finite rational countercertificate}

The manifest contains a Gaussian-integer polynomial supported on
$[-10,10]$.  Its coefficients share denominator $10^{10}$, so Parseval gives
the sixth power of the $L^6$ norm exactly: if $c*c*c$ is the triple discrete
convolution, then
\[
 \left\|\sum_nc_nz^n\right\|_6^6
 =\sum_k|(c*c*c)_k|^2.                               \tag{5.1}
\]
For the stored polynomial, the exact ratio obeys
\[
 {\|P_{\le0}g\|_6^6\over\|g\|_6^6}
 =3.9475110593975523284\ldots .                      \tag{5.2}
\]
Tensorisation and the carrier derivative give the elementary certified lower
bound
\[
 3\left({\|P_{\le0}g\|_6^6\over\|g\|_6^6}\right)^3
 =184.5403419180373503\ldots>29.62571266025876.      \tag{5.3}
\]
This finite exact countercertificate fires the gate without relying on a
numerical approximation to the sharp norm (3.1).  Equation (3.5) is the
stronger asymptotic theorem.

\section{Why the actual Class-II source is not refuted}

Let $X\in\R^6$ be the realification of the complex field and let
\[
 \cJ=\begin{pmatrix}0&-I_3\\ I_3&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
be the global phase generator.  For each embedded Pauli generator, write
\[
 p_A=2S_AX,\qquad v_A=2(S_A-q_AI)X.
\]
The real symmetric matrix $S_A$ commutes with $\cJ$.  Hence $S_A\cJ$ is
skew-symmetric and
\[
 p_A^T\cJ X=0,\qquad v_A^T\cJ X=0.                  \tag{6.1}
\]
Since
\[
 B(X)=\sum_A\{a,p_Ap_A^T+b(p_Av_A^T+v_Ap_A^T)
                  +c,v_Av_A^T\},
\]
equation (6.1) gives the exact fixed-floor identity
\[
 \boxed{B(X)\cJ X=0.}                                \tag{6.2}
\]
The large derivative in the boundary-modulation witness is precisely the
common phase direction $\cJ X$.  The actual matrix removes it, whereas the
coefficient-blind envelope (4.1) retains it.

Two further structures were also discarded in passing to (2.1): the actual
source contains the output shell projection of
$B(S_{j-1}\phi)DS_{j-1}\phi$, and the noncentral determinant contains a
positive resolvent.  A viable successor must retain at least (6.2) and the
output shell localisation, and should test whether the resolvent gives an
additional gain.

For completeness, positivity of the production Class-II quadratic form is
explicit rather than inferred from $b>0$:
\[
 a=0.004499999999998875>0,\quad
 c=0.002343749999999414>0,
\]
\[
 ac-b^2=7.031249999996483\times10^{-6}>0.             \tag{6.3}
\]

\section{Devil's-advocate review}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{DISMISSED -- the modulation is not dyadic.}  The carrier is
chosen as a power of two.  Equation (3.2) is exact on every fixed finite
polynomial once the dyadic carrier exceeds its degree.

\item \textbf{DISMISSED -- the witness falls outside the terminal cutoff.}
Its largest frequency is $N+m<2N$ for $N>m$, so the next dyadic terminal cube
contains the full input while the previous cube supplies the boundary term.

\item \textbf{DISMISSED -- complex tests are inadmissible for a six-real
field.}  A complex scalar is embedded as two real components.  Fourier
projection, Euclidean norm, and derivatives agree under this embedding.

\item \textbf{DISMISSED -- separate lower bounds for $M_6$ and $Q_6$ cannot
be multiplied.}  Both are lower bounds on fixed universal constants, so their
product is valid.  Moreover the same witness directly proves (4.2).

\item \textbf{VALID WITH MITIGATION -- the exact Riesz norm is an external
theorem.}  It is cited precisely.  The independent rational witness (5.3)
already exceeds the target using finite integer arithmetic.

\item \textbf{UPHELD -- this disproves the actual source-square estimate.}
It does not.  Equation (6.2), the output shell, and the determinant resolvent
were discarded by the scalar route.  The exact-B problem remains open.

\item \textbf{UPHELD -- a generic six-linear paraproduct bound is enough.}
It is not.  Equation (4.2) refutes any coefficient-blind bound with the
required production constant.

\item \textbf{UPHELD as an overclaim -- this closes A11 or promotes A12.}
It closes only the failed separated/scalar branch.  A12 remains T4 and the
coefficient-aware shell-localised source bound is open.
\end{enumerate}
