\section*{1. Purpose and scope}

This v1.2 note closes only \texttt{A2-FULL-ENERGY-CONTINUATION-AUDIT}. The scope is the canonical P1 functional on the fixed three-torus, in six real coordinates, with positive regularisers and $\eta_{\rm shell}=0$. The v1.1 note already establishes the local $H^2\to L^2$ Lipschitz property of the full lower-order map. Here the task is the exact energy identity, the Fourier-Galerkin compactness passage, and global continuation.

The historical backend remains a non-variational proxy and is excluded. This note does not settle positive-time smoothing or continuous $H^2$ dependence. The claim consequently remains T4, not T6.

\section*{2. Functional derivative and finite Galerkin identity}

Let $H=L^2(\mathbb T^3;\mathbb R^6)$ and let $L$ be the positive self-adjoint fourth-order operator fixed by P1. Write
\[
 \mathcal F(u)={1\over2}\langle u,Lu\rangle_H+\Phi(u),
 \qquad R(u)=Lu+N(u).                                      \tag{2.1}
\]
Here the family and lock matrices are included in $L$, while $\Phi$ contains
the quartic, sextic, and Class-II terms. The v1.1 real-coordinate calculation gives
\[
 N:H^2\longrightarrow H\ \hbox{ locally Lipschitz},
 \qquad D\Phi(u)[h]=\langle N(u),h\rangle_H\quad(u,h\in H^2).
                                                               \tag{2.2}
\]

Let $P_n$ be the real $L^2$-orthogonal Fourier projection, $V_n=P_nH$, and $u_{0,n}=P_nu_0$. The finite-dimensional ODE is
\[
 \partial_tu_n=-P_nR(u_n),\qquad u_n(0)=u_{0,n}.             \tag{2.3}
\]
For $v\in V_n$, $D(\mathcal F|_{V_n})(u_n)[v]=\langle P_nR(u_n),v\rangle_H$. Thus (2.3) is the exact real gradient flow of the restricted functional, and the ordinary finite-dimensional chain rule gives
\[
 {d\over dt}\mathcal F(u_n(t))
 =\langle R(u_n),-P_nR(u_n)\rangle_H
 =-\|P_nR(u_n)\|_H^2=-\|\partial_tu_n\|_H^2.              \tag{2.4}
\]
The projection in (2.3) is essential: an unprojected residual would not in general remain in $V_n$.

\section*{3. Uniform bounds and compactness}

The production coercivity audit gives
\[
 \mathcal F(u)\ge {c_{H^2}\over2}\|u\|_{H^2}^2
 +{\gamma\over12}\|u\|_{L^6}^6-C_Y|\mathbb T^3|.          \tag{3.1}
\]
Here $c_{H^2}>0$ and $\gamma>0$. As $P_nu_0\to u_0$ in $H^2$ and $\mathcal F$ is continuous on $H^2$, the Galerkin initial energies are uniformly bounded. Equations (2.4)--(3.1) imply, on every finite interval,
\[
 \sup_n\|u_n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;H^2)}<\infty,
 \qquad\sup_n\|\partial_tu_n\|_{L^2(0,T;H)}<\infty.       \tag{3.2}
\]

By (2.2), $N(u_n)$ is bounded in $L^\infty(0,T;H)$. Since $P_n$ commutes with the Fourier multiplier $L$, (2.3) gives
\[
 Lu_n=-\partial_tu_n-P_nN(u_n),
 \qquad\sup_n\|u_n\|_{L^2(0,T;H^4)}<\infty.                \tag{3.3}
\]
The graph norm of $L$ is equivalent to the $H^4$ norm by the positive fourth-order coefficient and bounded internal matrices. Apply Aubin--Lions to $H^4\Subset H^2\hookrightarrow H$. After a subsequence,
\[
 u_n\to u\ \hbox{ strongly in }L^2(0,T;H^2),\qquad
 u_n\rightharpoonup u\ \hbox{ in }L^2(0,T;H^4).            \tag{3.4}
\]
The local Lipschitz property makes $N(u_n)\to N(u)$ strongly in $L^2(0,T;H)$. Passing through (2.3) yields
\[
 u\in L^2(0,T;H^4)\cap H^1(0,T;H),
 \qquad\partial_tu=-Lu-N(u).                               \tag{3.5}
\]
The Hilbert interpolation result for (3.5) gives $u\in C([0,T];H^2)$.

\section*{4. Infinite-dimensional Class-II chain rule}

Passing only a lower-semicontinuous inequality would not suffice. The identity follows from a direct lemma. If $u\in C([0,T];H^2)\cap H^1(0,T;H)$ and $\Phi,N$ satisfy (2.2), then
\[
 \Phi(u(t))-\Phi(u(s))=\int_s^t\langle N(u(\tau)),\partial_\tau u(\tau)\rangle_Hd\tau. \tag{4.1}
\]
Indeed, apply the fundamental theorem of calculus on the $H^2$ segment from $u(t)$ to $u(t+h)$:
\[
 \Phi(u(t+h))-\Phi(u(t))=\int_0^1\langle N(u(t)+\theta\delta_hu(t)),\delta_hu(t)\rangle_Hd\theta.
\]
After integration in time, continuity in $H^2$, local Lipschitz continuity of $N$, and $L^2(0,T;H)$ convergence of difference quotients prove (4.1). The v1.1 Class-II expansion supplies (2.2), so this is a true real-gradient chain rule rather than formal complex differentiation.

For $u$ in (3.5), the spectral quadratic-form chain rule gives
\[
 {d\over dt}{1\over2}\langle u,Lu\rangle_H
 =\langle Lu,\partial_tu\rangle_H\quad\hbox{a.e.}         \tag{4.2}
\]
Combining (4.1), (4.2), and (3.5) proves
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal F(u(t))+\int_s^t\|\partial_\tau u(\tau)\|_H^2d\tau
 =\mathcal F(u(s)),\qquad 0\le s\le t\le T.}              \tag{4.3}
\]

\section*{5. Global continuation}

Taking $s=0$ in (4.3), the coercive inequality (3.1) supplies a uniform $H^2$ bound on every finite interval from $\mathcal F(u_0)$. Hence the local continuation alternative cannot occur in finite time. As the construction works for arbitrary $T$, the canonical solution extends globally in $H^2$. Only the hash-pinned canonical functional, zero shell bias, positive regularisers, and the earlier lower-bound/mapping audits are used here.
