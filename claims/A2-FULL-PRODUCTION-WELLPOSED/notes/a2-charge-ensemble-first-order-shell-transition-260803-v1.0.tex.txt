\section*{1. Purpose and scope: result and boundary}

R-157 proved that the hash-pinned P1 functional has no nonzero equilibrium in
its unconstrained field space.  That result did not decide a fixed-charge
sector or a chemical-potential deformation.  This note solves those two
different variational problems exactly.  Put
\[
 Q(\Psi)={1\over2}\|\Psi\|_{L^2}^2,
 \qquad
 \cO_\mu(\Psi)=\cF_{\rm P1}(\Psi)-\mu Q(\Psi).       \tag{1.1}
\]
On the fixed cubic torus of side $L=16$, define $\lambda_0$ to be the exact
spectral bottom of the P1 quadratic operator and set
\[
 \rho_*={43\over216},\qquad
 c_*={1849\over86400},\qquad
 \mu_t=\lambda_0-c_*.                              \tag{1.2}
\]
Then the exact identity
\[
 \boxed{
 \cF_{\rm P1}(\Psi)=\mu_t Q(\Psi)
 +{1\over2}\langle\Psi,(\mathcal L-\lambda_0)\Psi\rangle
 +\cF_{\rm II}(\Psi)
 +{\gamma\over6}\int\rho(\rho-\rho_*)^2,dx }
                                                               \tag{1.3}
\]
holds, where $\rho=\Psi^\dagger\Psi$.  Every term after $\mu_tQ$ is
nonnegative.

Consequently a finite-wave-number plane wave is an exact constrained global
minimizer at $Q=Q_*:=11008/27$.  In the grand-canonical problem, zero is the
unique global minimizer below $\mu_t$, zero coexists with nonzero shell
minimizers at $\mu_t$, and a nonzero global minimizer wins above $\mu_t$.
The charge jumps from zero to $Q_*$ before the zero field loses linear
stability, so this is an exact first-order transition.

This result is deliberately not called a physical-vacuum theorem.  Neither a
conserved physical charge nor an external reservoir producing $\mu$ has been
derived.  At the coexistence point
\[
 \cF_{\rm P1}(\Psi_*)=\mu_tQ_*>0=\cF_{\rm P1}(0),   \tag{1.4}
\]
so the state does not beat the neutral zero reference in the original
functional.  Its gauge-invariant density and all registered internal
bilinears are uniform.  The finite wave number is a common-phase winding, not
a proof of BCC, a density crystal, or any unique morphology.

\[
 {\lambda_0\over2}\rho+f(\rho)

Adding the nonnegative spectral remainder and the positive-semidefinite

\section*{4. Fixed-charge theorem}

For any prescribed $Q$, put $\bar\rho=2Q/16^3$.  The exact Bregman identity
is
\[
 f(\rho)-f(\bar\rho)-f'(\bar\rho)(\rho-\bar\rho)
 ={(\rho-\bar\rho)^2\over12}
 \bigl(4\gamma\bar\rho+2\gamma\rho+3\lambda\bigr). \tag{4.1}
\]
If $\bar\rho\ge\rho_*$, the bracket is nonnegative.  On the affine constraint
$\int\rho=2Q$, the linear Bregman term integrates to zero.  Therefore the
difference from a ground-shell plane wave of constant density $\bar\rho$ is
the sum of the nonnegative spectral remainder, $\cF_{\rm II}$, and the
nonnegative integral (4.1).  Such a plane wave is an exact constrained global
minimizer for every
\[
 {2Q\over16^3}\ge\rho_*.                            \tag{4.2}
\]
At equality, this proves the $Q_*$ assertion.  The form of the constrained
global minimizer below $Q_*$ is not decided here.

The weak Euler--Lagrange equation for a constrained critical point is
\[
 D\cF_{\rm P1}(\Psi)[v]=\mu\langle\Psi,v\rangle_{\mathbb R}
 \quad\hbox{for all }v\in H^2.                     \tag{4.3}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 Y\Delta^2\Psi-Z\Delta\Psi+(r\Id+M)\Psi
 +\lambda\rho\Psi+\gamma\rho^2\Psi+R_{\rm II}(\Psi)
 =\mu\Psi.                                         \tag{4.4}
\]
For (3.3), $R_{\rm II}=0$ and $\mu=\mu_t$.  Thus R-157 is not contradicted:
$D\cF_{\rm P1}(\Psi_*)=\mu_t\Psi_*\ne0$, so this is not an unconstrained
critical point.

\section*{5. Grand-canonical transition and exact ordering}

Subtracting $\mu Q$ from (1.3) gives
\[
 \cO_\mu(\Psi)=(\mu_t-\mu)Q
 +{1\over2}\langle\Psi,(\mathcal L-\lambda_0)\Psi\rangle
 +\cF_{\rm II}(\Psi)
 +{\gamma\over6}\int\rho(\rho-\rho_*)^2.          \tag{5.1}
\]
Hence:
\begin{itemize}
\item if $\mu<\mu_t$, zero is the unique global minimizer;
\item if $\mu=\mu_t$, zero and nonzero ground-shell states of density
  $\rho_*$ coexist;
\item if $\mu>\mu_t$, (3.3) has negative grand potential, while sextic
  coercivity supplies a global minimizer, which must be nonzero.
\end{itemize}

The zero-field Hessian is $\mathcal L-\mu\Id$, so its linear spinodal is
\[
 \mu_{\rm lin}=\lambda_0.                          \tag{5.2}
\]
On the single ground-plane-wave amplitude family, stationary densities obey
\[
 \gamma\rho^2+\lambda\rho+(\lambda_0-\mu)=0.       \tag{5.3}
\]
The amplitude branch appears at
\[
 \mu_{\rm sn}=\lambda_0-{\lambda^2\over4\gamma}
 =\lambda_0-{1849\over64800},                      \tag{5.4}
\]
whereas coexistence occurs at
\[
 \mu_t=\lambda_0-{1849\over86400}.                 \tag{5.5}
\]
Thus
\[
 \boxed{\mu_{\rm sn}<\mu_t<\mu_{\rm lin}}.         \tag{5.6}
\]
At coexistence the two stationary densities are
\[
 \rho_+={43\over216},\qquad \rho_-={43\over648}.   \tag{5.7}
\]
The global charge changes discontinuously from zero to $Q_*$ while zero is
still a strict local minimum.  This closes the first-order verdict.  Equation
(5.4) is only an amplitude-family saddle-node statement; this note does not
claim full-space stability for every off-coexistence plane-wave branch.

\section*{6. Devil's-advocate audit and physical interpretation}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Objection: fixed charge proves a spontaneous TECT vacuum.}
\textbf{UPHELD against that inference.}  The charge sector removes zero from
the comparison class.  No microscopic conservation law, superselection rule,
or cosmological initial condition selecting $Q\ne0$ has been derived.

\item \textbf{Objection: the chemical potential is already a parameter of
P1.} \textbf{UPHELD as false.}  The term $-\mu Q$ changes the variational
problem.  A reservoir or a derived effective ensemble is a new hypothesis.

\item \textbf{Objection: a nonzero torus wave vector establishes a spatial
crystal.} \textbf{UPHELD as false.}  For (3.3), $\rho$ and every $m_A$ are
constant.  The theorem selects a common-phase winding in this representation,
not a gauge-invariant density modulation, BCC star, or unique lattice.

\item \textbf{Objection: local gauge completion may remove the phase wave.}
\textbf{UPHELD as an open physical gate.}  P1 has a global phase symmetry but
no dynamical gauge field.  A local gauge model could gauge away or screen this
configuration and must be analysed as a distinct candidate.

\item \textbf{Objection: the canonical A2 gradient flow realizes the
fixed-charge transition.} \textbf{UPHELD as false.}  That dissipative flow
does not generally conserve $Q$.  Projected relaxation or Hamiltonian
dynamics would require separate well-posedness and stability theorems.

\item \textbf{Objection: the Class-II cross term spoils saturation.}
\textbf{DISMISSED for the states (3.3).}  Both registered currents vanish
pointwise, while the complete coefficient matrix is positive semidefinite.

\item \textbf{Objection: the body-diagonal shell is regulator independent.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  The integer shell follows from the side-$16$ torus.
Robustness under $L$, cutoff, and infinite-volume limits is not proved.

\item \textbf{Objection: exact algebra can hide a sign, factor, or convention
error.} \textbf{VALID WITH MITIGATION.}  A SymPy/Sturm implementation and an
independent standard-library rational implementation reconstruct all inputs
separately.  External review is invited for the real-pairing factor $1/2$,
Fourier normalization, charge normalization, matrix eigenvalue enclosure,
quartic--sextic signs, Bregman factor, units, finite-volume dependence,
hardcode masking, and the $Q\to0$ and $\mu\to-\infty$ limits.
\end{enumerate}

The truth-first candidate verdict is therefore two-sided.  The P1 polynomial
and torus spectrum do contain a mathematically exact finite-shell first-order
mechanism once a charge ensemble is imposed.  The same calculation also shows
why it is not yet the sought physical explanation: the ordered state is above
the neutral reference, its ensemble provenance is absent, and its registered
local observables are uniform.
