\section*{1. Purpose and scope}

This v1.3 note closes only \texttt{A2-FULL-SMOOTHING-AUDIT}. The scope is the canonical P1 functional on the fixed three-torus, in six real coordinates, with positive regularisers and $\eta_{\rm shell}=0$. Versions v1.0--v1.2 already establish: a positive self-adjoint fourth-order linear operator $L$ with $D(L)=H^4$ and $D(L^{1/2})=H^2$; a full lower-order map $N:H^2\to L^2$ that is locally Lipschitz; a unique local mild solution; the exact energy identity; and a coercive global $H^2$ bound.

Write $H=L^2(\mathbb T^3;\mathbb R^6)$, $X_\alpha=D(L^\alpha)$, and $S(t)=e^{-tL}$. Positivity and ellipticity give
\[
 X_\alpha=H^{4\alpha}\quad(0\leq\alpha\leq1),
 \qquad \|L^\alpha S(t)\|_{H\to H}\leq C_\alpha t^{-\alpha}.       \tag{1.1}
\]
All Sobolev spaces are periodic. Norm equivalence constants depend only on the pinned production coefficients and torus.

\section*{2. Continuous dependence in $H^2$}

Let $u,v$ be two canonical solutions on $[0,T]$ and set $w=u-v$. On a common $H^2$ ball of radius $R$, the v1.1 estimate gives
\[
 \|N(u)-N(v)\|_H\leq K_R\|u-v\|_{H^2}.                    \tag{2.1}
\]
The energy estimate provides such a common ball on $[0,T]$ for initial data in any bounded neighbourhood of a fixed datum. The mild difference equation is
\[
 w(t)=S(t)w(0)-\int_0^tS(t-s)[N(u(s))-N(v(s))],ds.        \tag{2.2}
\]
Apply $L^{1/2}$, use commutation on $X_{1/2}$ and (1.1), and use the equivalence $X_{1/2}=H^2$:
\[
 \|w(t)\|_{H^2}\leq C\|w(0)\|_{H^2}
 +C K_R\int_0^t(t-s)^{-1/2}\|w(s)\|_{H^2},ds.           \tag{2.3}
\]
The kernel is integrable. The weakly singular Gronwall lemma (equivalently, iteration of the convolution followed by the Mittag--Leffler bound) yields
\[
 \sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{H^2}
 \leq C(T,R)\|u(0)-v(0)\|_{H^2}.                         \tag{2.4}
\]
This proves uniqueness again and continuous dependence on every finite time interval.

\section*{3. Fractional positive-time gain}

For $1/2\leq\alpha<1$, the mild formula and (1.1) give, for $t>0$,
\[
 \|u(t)\|_{X_\alpha}
 \leq C t^{-(\alpha-1/2)}\|u_0\|_{X_{1/2}}
 +C\int_0^t(t-s)^{-\alpha}\|N(u(s))\|_H,ds.             \tag{3.1}
\]
The second integral is finite precisely because $\alpha<1$ and $N(u)$ is bounded in $H$ on energy-bounded $H^2$ balls. Hence
\[
 u\in C((0,T];X_\alpha)\quad\hbox{for every }\alpha<1,   \tag{3.2}
\]
with uniform bounds on $[\delta,T]$ for every $\delta>0$.

Choose one $\alpha\in(1/2,1)$. On a shifted interval, the semigroup difference estimate and the short Duhamel integral imply
\[
 \|u(t+h)-u(t)\|_{X_{1/2}}
 \leq C_\delta\bigl(h^{\alpha-1/2}+h^{1/2}\bigr).        \tag{3.3}
\]
Thus $u$ is Holder continuous in $H^2$ with some exponent $\theta>0$ away from $t=0$. By (2.1), $f(t)=N(u(t))$ is Holder continuous into $H$ with the same positive exponent.

\section*{4. The first $H^4$ gain}

The estimate with $\alpha<1$ alone does not reach $D(L)$. Fix $0<a<t$ and use the shifted mild formula. For the nonlinear convolution, add and subtract $f(t)$:
\[
 L\int_a^tS(t-s)f(s),ds
 =(I-S(t-a))f(t)
 +\int_a^tLS(t-s)[f(s)-f(t)],ds.                         \tag{4.1}
\]
The first term belongs to $H$. In the second,
\[
 \|LS(t-s)[f(s)-f(t)]\|_H
 \leq C(t-s)^{-1}(t-s)^\theta=C(t-s)^{\theta-1},          \tag{4.2}
\]
which is integrable because $\theta>0$. The linear term $LS(t-a)u(a)$ is also in $H$. Therefore $Lu(t)\in H$ and
\[
 u(t)\in D(L)=H^4\qquad\hbox{for every }t>0,              \tag{4.3}
\]
uniformly on compact positive-time intervals. Formula (4.1), rather than an invalid direct integration of the non-integrable $(t-s)^{-1}$ bound, is the essential endpoint step.

\section*{5. Bootstrap to smoothness}

The positive floors $\rho_{\rm floor}>0$ and $M_X^2+\varepsilon_M>0$ make every Class-II coefficient a smooth function of the six real field components. The v1.1 local expression contains at most two spatial derivatives. Periodic Moser product and composition estimates consequently give, for every integer $m\geq2$,
\[
 N:H^m\longrightarrow H^{m-2}\quad\hbox{locally Lipschitz}.              \tag{5.1}
\]
After (4.3), repeat Sections 3--4 on the base space $H^{m-2}$. The fourth-order operator has domain $H^{m+2}$ there, while $H^m$ is its half-power domain. Each shifted repetition therefore gives
\[
 H^m\longrightarrow H^{m+2}\quad\hbox{for positive time}.               \tag{5.2}
\]
Starting at $m=4$ and iterating proves $u(t)\in H^m$ for every $m$ and every $t>0$. Sobolev embedding gives spatial $C^\infty$ regularity. The equation $u_t=-Lu-N(u)$ and differentiation on intervals $[\delta,T]$ then give all time derivatives, so the solution is classical and $C^\infty$ in space-time for $t>0$.
