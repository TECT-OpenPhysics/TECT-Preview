\section*{2. Canonical functional and real Hilbert structure}

Let
\[
  \mathcal H=L^2(\mathbb T^3;\mathbb C^3),\qquad
  \langle U,V\rangle_{\mathbb R}=\operatorname{Re}
  \int_{\mathbb T^3}U^\dagger V\,dx .
\]
Write $\rho=\Psi^\dagger\Psi$, and let $T_A$ be the three Hermitian generators
fixed by P1.  Define
\[
 m_A=\Psi^\dagger T_A\Psi,\quad J_A=\nabla m_A,\quad
 q_A={m_A\over\rho+\epsilon_\rho},\quad
 K_A=J_A-q_A\nabla\rho,
\]
where $\epsilon_\rho=10^{-12}$.  The canonical functional is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal F[\Psi]={}&{1\over2}\langle\Psi,L\Psi\rangle_{\mathbb R}
 +\int_{\mathbb T^3}\left({\lambda\over4}\rho^2
 +{\gamma\over6}\rho^3\right)dx \\
 &+\sum_A\int_{\mathbb T^3}
 \left({a\over2}|J_A|^2+b\operatorname{Re}(J_A^\dagger K_A)
 +{c\over2}|K_A|^2\right)dx .
\end{align}
Here
\[
 L=\bigl(r+Z(-\Delta)+Y(-\Delta)^2\bigr)I_3
   +M_{\rm fam}+k_{\rm lock}(I_3-P_0),
\]
$M_{\rm fam}=\operatorname{diag}(0,0.03,0.07)$,
$P_0=z_0z_0^\dagger/(z_0^\dagger z_0)$, and $k_{\rm lock}=0.15$.  The
Class-II denominator is $M_X^2+\epsilon_M$ with $\epsilon_M=10^{-12}$, and
\[
 a={c_{JJ}\alpha_X^2\over M_X^2+\epsilon_M},\quad
 b={c_{JK}\alpha_X\beta_X\over M_X^2+\epsilon_M},\quad
 c={c_{KK}\beta_X^2\over M_X^2+\epsilon_M}.
\]
The PDE is the real gradient flow
\[
                 \partial_t\Psi=-\nabla_{\mathbb R}\mathcal F[\Psi].
\]

\section*{5. Local existence and uniqueness: proof candidate}

Identify $\mathbb C^3$ with $\mathbb R^6$ and write the field as $u$.  The
quantities $m_A$ and $\rho$ are quadratic in $u$.  Hence
\[
 J_A=Dm_A(u)\nabla u,\qquad
 K_A=\bigl(Dm_A(u)-q_A(u)D\rho(u)\bigr)\nabla u.
\]
Since $\rho+\epsilon_\rho>0$, every coefficient is a smooth function of $u$.
The Class-II density therefore has the real-coordinate form
\[
            G_{II}(u,\nabla u)={1\over2}
            B^{j\ell}_{\alpha\beta}(u)
            \partial_j u_\alpha\partial_\ell u_\beta,
\]
with smooth $B$.  Its Euler--Lagrange map is a sum of terms of the two forms
\[
             B(u)\nabla^2u,qquad DB(u)[\nabla u,\nabla u].       \tag{5.1}
\]
On an $H^2$ ball in three dimensions, $u$ is bounded in $L^\infty$ and
$\nabla u$ is bounded in $L^6$.  Thus the first term of (5.1) lies in $L^2$;
the second lies in $L^3$, hence also in $L^2$ on the fixed finite torus.
Applying the same product estimates to a difference gives the candidate bound
\[
 \|N_{II}(u)-N_{II}(v)\|_{L^2}
 \le C_R\|u-v\|_{H^2},\qquad
 \|u\|_{H^2},\|v\|_{H^2}\le R.                              \tag{5.2}
\]
The quartic and sextic Euler--Lagrange terms satisfy the same local-Lipschitz
bound because $H^2$ is an algebra.  Consequently the full lower-order map
$N:H^2\to L^2$ is locally Lipschitz, provided the coordinate reduction leading
to (5.1) is confirmed term by term.

The positive self-adjoint fourth-order operator $L$ generates an analytic
semigroup and $D(L^{1/2})=H^2$ with equivalent norms.  Since
\[
             \|L^{1/2}e^{-tL}\|_{L^2\to L^2}\le Ct^{-1/2},
\]
the singularity is integrable.  The standard contraction argument for
\[
 u(t)=e^{-tL}u_0-\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)L}N(u(s))\,ds
\]
then gives a unique local mild solution in $C([0,T];H^2)$ and the continuation
alternative that a finite maximal time forces the $H^2$ norm to diverge.
Equation (5.2) and its explicit coordinate expansion are the subject of
\texttt{A2-FULL-NONLINEAR-MAPPING-AUDIT}; no theorem-tier claim is made before
that audit closes.

\section*{6. Energy decrease and global continuation: proof candidate}

Let $P_n$ be the Fourier projection onto finitely many modes.  The projected
equation is a finite-dimensional real gradient flow for $\mathcal F(P_nu)$, so
\[
 {d\over dt}\mathcal F(u_n(t))
 =-\|\nabla_{\mathbb R}\mathcal F(u_n(t))\|_{L^2}^2\le0.       \tag{6.1}
\]
The coercive estimate (4.1) bounds $u_n$ uniformly in $H^2$ on every interval
on which the initial energy is fixed.  The functional is continuous on $H^2$:
the linear part is its quadratic norm, the local potential is continuous by
$H^2\hookrightarrow L^\infty$, and the Class-II density is quadratic in
$\nabla u$ with smooth coefficients of $u$.

The candidate limiting argument uses the uniform $H^2$ bound, the projected
equation, compact Sobolev embedding on the torus, and lower semicontinuity to
pass (6.1) to the local solution.  It yields
\[
 \mathcal F(u(t))+\int_0^t
 \|\nabla_{\mathbb R}\mathcal F(u(s))\|_{L^2}^2ds
 =\mathcal F(u_0).                                           \tag{6.2}
\]
Then (4.1) and (6.2) give a uniform $H^2$ bound.  The local continuation
alternative cannot fire, so the solution extends globally.  The exact
Class-II chain rule and the compactness passage in (6.2) remain the named
\texttt{A2-FULL-ENERGY-CONTINUATION-AUDIT}.

\section*{7. Continuous dependence and smoothing: proof candidate}

the first $H^4$ gain and the induction are retained as
