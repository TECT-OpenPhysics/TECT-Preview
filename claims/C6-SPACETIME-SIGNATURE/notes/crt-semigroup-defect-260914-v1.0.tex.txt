\section*{3. Full finite Markov contraction and the time-error estimate}
At each finite $r$ the original off-diagonal rates are nonnegative and finite,
and the row sum is zero. Let $M_r=1+\max_x\sum_{y\ne x}q_r(x,y)$.
The matrix $P_r=1+L_r/M_r$ is stochastic. The exact matrix exponential is
\[
 S_r(t)=e^{-M_rt}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(M_rt)^n}{n!}P_r^n.
\]
The coefficients sum to one. Triangle inequalities in each row therefore give
$\|S_r(t)v\|_\infty\le\|v\|_\infty$ for complex $v$ and all $t\ge0$.
$M_r$ need not be uniform: it is an algebraic proof device, not a changed
rate or time scale. This proves contraction for all original roots at once.

Differentiate $S_r(t-u)e^{\lambda_ru}f_r$ for $0\le u\le t$ in the finite
function space. Using (4), its derivative is
$-e^{\lambda_ru}S_r(t-u)e_r$. Integrating gives
\[
 u_r(t):=S_r(t)f_r-e^{\lambda_rt}f_r
       =\int_0^t e^{\lambda_ru}S_r(t-u)e_r\,du,
 \qquad \|u_r(t)\|_\infty\le\frac{256t}{K_r}.                \tag{5}
\]
The sign and absence of any factor $1/2$ follow from the displayed original
generator. The last inequality uses $\lambda_r<0$, contraction, and (4).
It is uniform in the full finite state and every $t$ in a fixed compact
interval. No common Hilbert realization, Gibbs projectivity, or limiting
generator-domain theorem was used.

Independently, $u_r'=L_ru_r+e^{\lambda_rt}e_r$ and $u_r(0)=0$.
For any fixed angle $\theta$, put $v_\theta=\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}u_r)$.
At a maximum over the finite state space, $L_rv_\theta\le0$; the forcing
is at most $256/K_r$. The finite comparison principle, or its upper-right
Dini-derivative version at a change of maximizer, gives
$v_\theta(x,t)\le256t/K_r$. Taking the supremum over all $\theta$ gives
(5), without a spurious $\sqrt2$ loss. This is an alternative full-operator
derivation, not a numerical enumeration or an exact-eigenfunction assumption.

\section*{4. One fixed positive time and arbitrarily late pairs}

\[

                +u_s(t)-I_{r,s}u_r(t),
\]
\[
 |D_{r,s}(t)(x)|\ge |e^{\lambda_st}-e^{\lambda_rt}|
                  -256t(K_s^{-1}+K_r^{-1}).                 \tag{6}
\]

\]

When $K_s\ge K_r\ge768$, the error in (6) is at most $1/15$.
Thus every such flipping pair satisfies $|D_{r,s}(t_*)(x)|\ge1/5$ everywhere.

The required pairs occur beyond every threshold by the following elementary
argument, reproduced from R-573. For $B\ge11$, let
$P_B=\prod_{p\le B,\,p\ne5}p$, and choose $j\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ with
$jP_B\equiv1\pmod5$. Then $1+jP_B$ is two modulo five and one modulo every
