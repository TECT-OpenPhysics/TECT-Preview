\section*{2. One fixed admitted character}
Let $\Phi(x)=\sum_{e\in\partial p}\sigma_e u_e$ be the signed integer
link sum around the sole face, and put
\[
 f_0(x)=\exp(2\pi i\Phi(x)/5),\quad
 a_r=(K_r/K_2)^{-1}\pmod 5,\quad
 f_r(x)=\exp(2\pi i a_r\Phi(x)/5).                            \tag{3}
\]
Since $K_2=30$ and $5\mid K_r$, these are well-defined functions of the
original modular link coordinates. The signed sum of each gauge coboundary
vanishes around the closed face; wrap corrections are multiples of $K_r$,
also invisible modulo five. Thus the functions are gauge invariant.
The full anchor-preserving cell group of this anchored square is trivial:
fixing the adjacent labelled vertices fixes their two other neighbours.
No symmetry subgroup or physical projection is substituted.

The admitted cutoff projection multiplies links by $P^{-1}$ modulo $K_r$,
where $P=p_{r+2}$. Its reduction modulo five gives exactly
$a_{r+1}=a_rP^{-1}\pmod5$ and (3). Composition gives every nonadjacent
pullback. Thus $f_0$, not a cutoff-dependent selection, is fixed once and for
all, and $|f_r|=1$ on every full counting state.

PH, TR and AP do not change any link coordinate, so their observable
increments in (3) vanish individually. Their rates are not set to zero and
their incidences are not removed. Each of the eight signed LK incidences is
valid, including modular wrap. A unit-rate algebraic leading part therefore is
\[
 \lambda(a) =4\{2\cos(2\pi a/5)-2\},\qquad
 L_r f_r=\lambda(a_r)f_r+e_r.                                \tag{4}
\]
Equation (4) is a decomposition of the original generator, not a replacement
by a unit-rate model. The remainder $e_r$ is bounded next using the actual rates.

\section*{3. Uniform original-rate remainder on all states}
Write $A_v=2^{-r}\ell_v$. Then $A_v\ge0$ and $\sum_v A_v=1$ on every
source state. At (2), every aperture is one, every $J_e,J_p$ is one, and all
mobilities are one. Under one LK incidence the only changing functional terms
are its matter edge and the one plaquette containing it. With
$\delta=2\pi/K_r$ and $|e^{i\delta}-1|\le|\delta|$, the matter change has
absolute value at most $A_vA_w\delta\le\delta$, and the plaquette change
has absolute value at most $\delta$. Onsite and all other terms are unchanged.
Hence, uniformly in every occupation, phase, aperture and link label,
\[
 |F_r(bx)-F_r(x)|\le4\pi/K_r\le16/K_r.                       \tag{5}
\]
The original rate is $c_b=e^{-\Delta F_b/2}$. For $K_r\ge8$, set
$u=8/K_r\le1$. Convexity gives
$e^u-1\le(e-1)u\le2u$ on $[0,1]$; thus
\[
 |c_b-1|\le16/K_r,\qquad
 |e_r(x)|\le 8\cdot2\cdot16/K_r=256/K_r.                    \tag{6}
\]
The factor eight counts both signs on all four source edges; the factor two
bounds a unit character difference. There is no generator half factor.
The half in $B$ belongs only to the Dirichlet form convention.
For $s=r+1$, exact comparison of (4) gives
\[
 \Delta_{r,s}f_r=(\lambda(a_s)-\lambda(a_r))f_s
                  +e_s-e_r\circ p_{s,r},\qquad
 |e_s-e_r\circ p_{s,r}|\le512/K_r.                           \tag{7}
\]
No Gibbs projectivity or norm comparison between different states was used.

\section*{4. Arbitrarily late CRT flips and the Gibbs lower bound}
The nonzero residues modulo five split into $\{1,4\}$ and $\{2,3\}$.
Multiplication by the inverse of a residue in $\{2,3\}$ swaps these two sets.
The elementary fifth-root cosine values give
\[
 \lambda(a)\in\{-10+2\sqrt5,-10-2\sqrt5\}.
\]
Whenever $p_{r+2}\equiv2$ or $3\pmod5$,
\[
 |\lambda(a_{r+1})-\lambda(a_r)|=4\sqrt5>8.                  \tag{8}
\]
An elementary Euclid construction supplies such primes beyond every bound;
no asymptotic prime-distribution theorem is imported. Given any prime bound
$B\ge11$, put $P_B=\prod_{p\le B,\,p\ne5}p$ and choose
$t\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ with $tP_B\equiv1\pmod5$. The integer
$n_*=1+tP_B$ is $2$ modulo five and $1$ modulo every prime at most $B$
other than five. All its prime factors exceed $B$. If each factor were
$1$ or $4$ modulo five, so would their product be, a contradiction.
Consequently some prime factor is $2$ or $3$ modulo five.

For any tail threshold $R$, choose $B=p_{\max(R,4)+1}$ and such a prime
$q=p_k>B$. Put $r=k-2$ and $s=r+1$. Then $r\ge\max(R,4)$ and the flip
condition holds. Moreover $K_r\ge K_4=2310>512$. Equations (7)--(8) imply,
for \emph{every} original fine state $x$,
\[
 |\Delta_{r,r+1}f_r(x)|\ge8-512/K_r\ge7.                    \tag{9}
\]
The actual Gibbs weights are positive and sum to one, so
\[
 \sum_x\pi_{r+1}(x)|\Delta_{r,r+1}f_r(x)|^2\ge49.           \tag{10}
\]
This proves the exact fixed-data negation of (1), with $\eta_0=7$ and
arbitrarily late adjacent pairs. Adjacent pairs are used as witnesses to a
universal all-pair claim, not as sufficient evidence of Cauchy convergence.
In particular this obstruction does not live only on a Gibbs-negligible set.
All constants in (5)--(10) are derived conservative bounds, not fitted values.

\section*{5. State consistency and nontriviality, separately}
State projectivity, a common limiting pairing and semigroup convergence are
not proved or disproved by (10); the argument does not require them.
For nontriviality only, let $\gamma_r$ denote normalized uniform counting on
the same original finite set. This auxiliary measure is used in a domination
inequality, never substituted for $\pi_r$ in (1) or (10).
The displayed functional at (2) is nonnegative and satisfies
\[
 0\le F_r\le\frac16+\frac12+\frac42+2=\frac{14}{3}.         \tag{11}
\]
Indeed $\sum A_v^6\le1$, $\sum A_v^2\le1$, each matter edge contributes
at most $(A_v+A_w)^2/2\le1/2$, and the plaquette contributes at most two.
Thus $\pi_r(x)\ge e^{-14/3}\gamma_r(x)$ uniformly. Independent uniform
link counting makes $\Phi\bmod5$ uniform, so
$\mathbb E_{\gamma_r}f_r=0$ and
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\pi_r}(f_r)
 =\inf_{z\in\mathbb C}\mathbb E_{\pi_r}|f_r-z|^2
 \ge e^{-14/3}>0.                                           \tag{12}
\]
For $K_r\ge8$, (5) also gives $c_b\ge e^{-1}$ on every LK incidence.
With the original root half convention,
\[
 \mathcal E_r(f_r,f_r)
 =\frac12\sum_{x,b}\pi_r(x)c_b(x)|f_r(bx)-f_r(x)|^2
 \ge4e^{-1}>0,                                               \tag{13}
\]
since $2(1-\cos(2\pi a_r/5))>1$ and there are eight LK incidences.
The fixed character therefore does not collapse to a constant or a null-energy
observable along this sequence. This is not a construction of a limiting
physical state or nontrivial limiting semigroup.
