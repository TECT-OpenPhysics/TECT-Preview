\section*{Finite ensemble and stationarity}
For each nonempty grade define
\[
 Z_Q=\sum_{q(x)=Q}e^{-F_R(x)},\qquad
 Z=\sum_QZ_Q,\qquad w_Q=\frac{Z_Q}{Z}.
\]
All sums are finite and strictly positive. The mixture of the original
component Gibbs laws satisfies
\[
 \mu(x)=w_Q\pi_Q(x)=\frac{e^{-F_R(x)}}{Z},\qquad
 \mu(\,\cdot\mid Q)=\pi_Q,\qquad \mu(1)=1.
\]
This choice of weights is a new ensemble hypothesis, not a uniquely
derived or physically selected state.

For every allowed root $r$ and $y=rx$, inverse pairing and symmetric
mobility give the exact identity
\[
 \mu(x)c_r(x)=\frac{m_r(x)}Z e^{-(F_R(x)+F_R(y))/2}
             =\mu(y)c_{r^{-1}}(y).
\]
Summing against $f(y)-f(x)$ proves $\mu(Lf)=0$ on the entire finite
function domain. The two labelled phase/link channels at $K=2$ are retained.
Gauge transformations preserve the squared covariant differences and the
Wilson characters, hence the energy, counting law and finite projection.
This does not prove limiting stationarity or an infinite-volume semigroup.

\section*{Exact cutoff estimate}
Write $z=(j,k,u)$ and let $F_0(z)=F_R(z,0)$. Erasing occupancy for this
comparison is a summation device, not a changed transition or projection.
The original energy decomposes as
\begin{align*}
 F_R(z,\ell)-F_0(z)
 &=Q\left(\frac{R^4}{4}+\frac{R^6}{6}\right)
   +\frac{R^2}{2}\sum_vs_v^2\ell_v\\
 &\quad+\frac{R^2}{2}\sum_{e=(v,w)}J_e(s)
    \bigl(\ell_w(-1)^{k_w}-U_e\ell_v(-1)^{k_v}\bigr)^2.
\end{align*}
Since $s_v\geq1/2$, binary occupancy and nonnegative squares imply
\[
 F_R(z,\ell)\geq F_0(z)+Qc(R),\qquad
 c(R)=\frac{R^2}{8}+\frac{R^4}{4}+\frac{R^6}{6}.
\]
All aperture and Wilson boundary terms remain in $F_0$. Summing over the
same $z$ gives $\sum_z e^{-F_R(z,\ell)}\leq e^{-Qc(R)}Z_0$.
Let $t=e^{-c(R)}\in(0,1]$. For any fixed vertex $v$, $Z\geq Z_0$ gives
\[
 0\leq\mu_{n,R}(\ell_v^2)
 \leq\sum_{\ell:\ell_v=1}t^{\sum\ell}
 =t(1+t)^{N-1}\leq 2^{N-1}e^{-c(R)}.
\]
The binomial sum counts every occupation pattern; phase multiplicities
already occur identically in $Z_0$. Independently, summing entire charge
sectors gives
\[
 \mu_{n,R}(Q>0)\leq(1+t)^N-1\leq(2^N-1)t,
 \qquad \ell_v^2\leq\mathbf1_{Q>0}.
\]
This provides a second bound with the same conclusion.

For an explicit modulus, $c(R)\geq R/6$ for $R\geq1$ and
$e^c\geq1+c$. Therefore
\[
 \mu_{n,R}(\ell_v^2)\leq\frac{6\,2^{N-1}}R.
\]
For any $\delta>0$, $R\geq\lfloor6\,2^{N-1}/\delta\rfloor+1$ makes
the right side smaller than $\delta$. The modulus depends on $n$;
no uniform-volume bound or interchange of limits is asserted.

\section*{Devil's-advocate review}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Sign/factor: reversed Gibbs sign and missing sextic or aperture
denominators are rejected by exact coefficient controls.
\item Multiplicity: identifying zero-radius phases changes the counting
measure. Both lanes retain the coordinate labels and count all grades.
\item Domain: charge erasure is used only for a summation bound. All
generator roots still conserve charge and retain their inverse channels.
\item Limit: finite positivity is insufficient. The displayed modulus
proves the inner limits; it does not justify reversing the order.
\item Scope: a vanishing radial witness rejects this nondegeneracy target
only. The holonomy witnesses remain nonzero. No other ensemble is tested.
\end{enumerate}
These objections are dismissed within the explicitly retained restrictions.
