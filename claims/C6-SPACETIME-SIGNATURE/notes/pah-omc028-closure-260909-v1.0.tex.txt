\section*{The statement under inherited hypotheses}
Let $H$ be the real invariant state-$L^2$ space, $D$ the exact bounded
gauge-invariant, globally amplitude-$\ell^1$-Lipschitz finite-prefix
cylinders, and
\[
 E(f,g)=\frac12\sum_r\nu[c_r\Delta_r f\Delta_r g].
\]
R-511 supplies the dense domain, the nonnegative conductances and this
identity. R-512 supplies the minimal closure $(\overline E,V)$, with
$\|u\|_V^2=\|u\|_H^2+\overline E(u,u)$ and an injective continuous
inclusion into $H$. These are inherited analytic hypotheses.

For every scalar $\eta$ with $\eta(0)=0$ and
$|\eta(a)-\eta(b)|\leq |a-b|$, and every $u\in V$,
\[
 \eta(u)\in V,\qquad
 \overline E(\eta(u),\eta(u))\leq\overline E(u,u). \tag{1}
\]
The existing spectral semigroup $T_{\min}(t)$ is then positivity preserving,
$L^\infty$ contractive and satisfies $T_{\min}(t)1=1$ for $t\geq0$.

\section*{The precise flaw and its exact diagnostic}
The R-530 closure paragraph passes from $E(\eta f)\leq E(f)$ directly
to form-Cauchy behavior of $\eta(f_k)$. The implied difference estimate
$E(\eta f-\eta g)\leq E(f-g)$ is false. To test that universal
inequality, use probability weights $(1/2,1/2)$, both directed rates two,
and the directed-half convention. Then $E(h)=(h_0-h_1)^2$.
With $f=(1,2)$, $g=(0,1)$, and $\eta(x)=(0\vee x)\wedge1$,
\[
 E(f-g)=0,\quad E(\eta f-\eta g)=1,\qquad
 \|f-g\|_V^2=1,\quad\|\eta f-\eta g\|_V^2=\frac32.
\]
Each single-vector inequality remains true. This diagnostic rejects the
paired inequality; it does not refute (1), establish a non-Cauchy image
sequence, or substitute a finite model for PAH. The original R-530 bytes
are preserved, with this proof superseding only the flawed inference.

\section*{Core stability}
Scalar composition preserves finite-prefix dependence and gauge invariance.
If $|f|\leq M$, then $|\eta f|\leq M$, and its amplitude Lipschitz
constant does not increase. Thus $\eta(D)\subset D$. The scalar
Lipschitz bound integrates to
\[
 \|\eta f-\eta g\|_H\leq\|f-g\|_H.
\]
This also proves quotient well-definedness and continuity on $H$.
On $D$, the rootwise squared bound, weighted by the unchanged nonnegative
conductances, proves $E(\eta f)\leq E(f)$.

\section*{Primary proof: closed energy epigraph}
Put $q(u)=\overline E(u,u)$ for $u\in V$. The set
\[
 C=\{(u,a)\in H\times\mathbb R:u\in V,\ q(u)\leq a\}
\]
is closed. Indeed, for $(u_k,a_k)\to(u,a)$ in $H\times\mathbb R$,
the energies and $H$ norms are bounded. Choose an energy-liminf subsequence;
Hilbert weak compactness supplies a weakly $V$-convergent further subsequence.
Its weak $H$ limit is the strong
$H$ limit $u$, so $u\in V$. Weak lower semicontinuity gives
$q(u)\leq\liminf q(u_k)\leq a$. This lower semicontinuity follows
because the energy balls are convex and norm-closed in $V$, hence weakly
closed by separation. Domain membership is part of the argument.

Given any $u\in V$, take $f_k\in D$ with $f_k\to u$ in $V$.
The energy seminorm triangle inequality gives $E(f_k)\to q(u)$.
Core contraction gives $(\eta f_k,E(f_k))\in C$, while $H$ continuity
gives $\eta f_k\to\eta u$. Closedness of $C$ proves (1).
No form-Cauchy hypothesis on $\eta f_k$ is inserted. Growing supports
are allowed, and no root formula on all of $V$ is assumed.

\section*{Independent proof: convex averages}
The same $w_k=\eta f_k$ is bounded in $V$. A weakly convergent
subsequence has limit $v=\eta u$ by strong $H$ convergence. Mazur's
lemma yields finite convex combinations $z_m$ of successive tails with
$\|z_m-v\|_V<1/m$. They belong to $D$. Convexity implies
\[
 E(z_m)\leq\sup_{\ell\geq m}E(w_{k_\ell})
 \leq\sup_{\ell\geq m}E(f_{k_\ell})\longrightarrow q(u).
\]
Their strong $V$ limit gives $q(v)\leq q(u)$. The convex combinations
are approximants in the proof, with no averaging of rates or states.

\section*{Semigroup and conservation}
For the self-adjoint $K\geq0$ associated with $q$, the resolvent
$J_\lambda=(I+\lambda K)^{-1}$ uniquely minimizes
$q(v)+\|v-h\|_H^2/\lambda$. If $0\leq h\leq1$, clipping to
$[0,1]$ decreases both terms. Uniqueness forces $0\leq J_\lambda h\leq1$.
The spectral limit $(J_{t/m})^m\to e^{-tK}$ in $H$ preserves that
closed order interval. Positivity extends by bounded positive truncation.

The original $D$ contains $1$, the state is a probability, and all root
differences of $1$ vanish. Thus $q(1)=0$ and form Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$\overline E(1,v)=0$ for every $v\in V$. The associated-operator
definition implies $1\in\operatorname{Dom}K$ and $K1=0$.
Consequently $e^{-tK}1=1$. Positivity and linearity now give the
$L^\infty$ contraction. Preservation of $[0,1]$ alone would not prove
conservation.
