$C(\tau)=e^{-i(1+q)\tau}C_0$, with $\Psi=0$ and
$\mu_R=(1+q)/2$. In the weighted frame generated by $H-\mu_R Q$,
\[
 i\dot\psi=(L-\mu_R)\psi-2\epsilon C_0\bar\psi,\qquad
 \dot D=-2i\,\Re\langle C_0,D\rangle_F\,C_0.
\]
\textbf{The Frobenius contraction is pointwise in space}, not an integrated
$L^2$ inner product. The scalar replaces $C_0\bar\psi$ by $r\bar\psi$ and

Derivation: the reservoir gradient is $(1+\|C\|_F^2)C$ in the original
real-gradient convention; subtract $2\mu_R C$ and differentiate at $C_0$.

order--reservoir Hessian vanishes at $\Psi=0$. P1 local quartic/sextic terms
have no quadratic contribution there. Positive-floor Class-II currents are
at least quadratic in $\Psi$, so their energy is at least quartic and its
Hessian vanishes. The pinned shell coefficient is zero. The remaining
quadratic operator is exactly $L$ from P1. Constant-coefficient linearized
multiplication and the original Fourier projections preserve every retained
mode. Thus no collocation aliasing or nonlinear invariant-subspace assumption
enters. A real orthogonal eigenbasis of $M$ is a coordinate change at this

\section{Universal reservoir shear and a uniform finite-time bound}
Choose a retained nonzero mode $k=(2\pi/16)(1,0,0)$ and
$D_0(x)=C_0\cos(k\cdot x)$. This is tangent to the exact fixed-charge curve
\[
 C_\delta(x)=\frac{C_0[1+\delta\cos(k\cdot x)]}{\sqrt{1+\delta^2/2}},
 \qquad \Psi_\delta=0.
\]
Its charge is $Vq$ for every small real $\delta$, using mean cosine zero
and mean square cosine $1/2$. The curve is admitted at cutoff $2K$.
The exact linearized solution is
\[
 D(\tau,x)=(1-2iq\tau)D_0(x),\qquad
 \frac{\|D(\tau)\|_2^2}{\|D_0\|_2^2}=1+4q^2\tau^2.
\]
The zero-mean growing direction is orthogonal to the \emph{global} phase
tangent $iC_0$; removing that one phase does not remove the shear. Removing
arbitrary local phases would invent a different quotient. Thus full linear
evolution is not uniformly bounded for all $\tau$, even on the fixed-$Q$
tangent modulo the global phase. This holds for every constant symmetric
$C_0$ of the stated norm, and also for the scalar reservoir. It is a nonzero
nilpotent/Jordan block, not a positive exponent or a nonlinear theorem.

There is nevertheless a useful finite-time estimate for the low alignment.
Exact rational detuning bounds yield $|d|\ge\Delta>0$ for all paired modes,
$c<1/25$ and $15\Delta>17/25$. The paired block conserves the definite
quadratic form with weights $|d-c|,|d+c|$, up to an overall sign. Therefore
its Euclidean operator norm is at most
\[
 \sqrt{\frac{|d|+c}{|d|-c}}<4.
\]
Unpaired rotations have norm one. For the pointwise reservoir operator $N$,
$N^2=0$ and $\|N\|=2q$, so $e^{\tau N}=I+\tau N$ has norm at most
$1+2q|\tau|$. Orthogonal mode decomposition and Parseval give, on the full
real $L^2$/Frobenius space and hence its constrained tangent,
\[
 \|U_{K,\rm low}(\tau)\|\le4+2q|\tau|
 \quad\text{for every separately finite }K\ge1.
\]
The constant is cutoff independent, but the estimate is only for the
linearized fixed-volume system. It is not nonlinear compactness, a common
core for full dynamics, or construction of a regulator limit.
