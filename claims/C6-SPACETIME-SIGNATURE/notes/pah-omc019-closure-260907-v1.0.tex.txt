\section*{Applicability and quotient check}
The standard nonnegative symmetric-operator form construction is used; it is
not claimed as a new theorem. The primary reference is G.~Teschl,
\emph{Mathematical Methods in Quantum Mechanics}, second edition, Section 2.3,
printed pp.~77--81, equations (2.47)--(2.49), Theorem 2.13; author-hosted text
consulted 2026-09-07. Theorem 2.14 assumes closedness and is not used circularly.
Density, symmetry, nonnegativity and $Sg\in H$ match. Self-adjointness,
$S(D)\subset D$, bounded global exit rate and a uniform operator norm are not
required. The quantum interpretation of the reference does not apply.

First let $x\in D$ be zero in $H$. For every $y\in D$,
\[
 \langle y,Sx\rangle=E(y,x)=E(x,y)=\langle x,Sy\rangle=0.
\]
Density implies $Sx=0$. Thus both $S$ and $E$ descend to the state quotient;
the operator theorem is not applied to an ill-defined representative map.

\section*{Two closability proofs}
Positivity of $E(x+ty,x+ty)$ for all real $t$ gives form Cauchy--Schwarz.
Let $K$ be the Hilbert completion of $D/\ker E$ in the energy norm and let
$j:D\to K$ be its dense natural map. The exact pairing is
\[
 \langle jf,jg\rangle_K=E(f,g)=\langle f,Sg\rangle_H. \tag{1}
\]
For any $f_n\to0$ in $H$ which is energy-Cauchy, completeness gives
$jf_n\to v$ in $K$. For each \emph{fixed} $g\in D$, (1) gives
$\langle v,jg\rangle=\lim_n\langle f_n,Sg\rangle=0$. Density implies
$v=0$, hence $E(f_n,f_n)\to0$. Supports may grow arbitrarily: neither a
common support nor a bound on $\|Sf_n\|$ was used.

Independently, the form seminorms of an energy-Cauchy sequence are bounded
by $M$. For $\varepsilon>0$ choose $N$ with
$\sqrt{E(f_n-f_m)}<\varepsilon$ for $m,n\geq N$, and fix $m\geq N$. Then
\[
 E(f_n,f_n)\leq M\varepsilon+|E(f_n,f_m)|
 =M\varepsilon+|\langle f_n,Sf_m\rangle|.
\]
The last term tends to zero against the fixed $Sf_m\in H$. Arbitrary
$\varepsilon$ proves the same criterion. Taking $m=n$ would be an invalid
shortcut; the hostile audit explicitly tests that error.

\section*{Minimal closure and the entire radial subspace}
Complete $D$ in $\|f\|_1^2=\|f\|_H^2+E(f,f)$ to $V$. Its contractive
map $i:V\to H$ is injective: a completion vector over zero is represented
by an $H$-null energy-Cauchy sequence, so closability makes its form norm
tend to zero. On $\overline D=i(V)$ define
\[
 \overline E(iu,iv)=\langle u,v\rangle_V-\langle iu,iv\rangle_H.
\]
It is nonnegative, agrees with $E$, and its form norm is the complete $V$
norm. Any other closed nonnegative extension agreeing on $D$ contains the
limits of all these form-Cauchy sequences and agrees on $\overline D$.
This proves minimality, not equality with a maximal jump-form domain or
uniqueness of all self-adjoint extensions.

For every $h\in H_{\rm rad}$ choose $a_n\in D_{\rm rad}$ with $a_n\to h$
in $H$. Every difference $a_n-a_m$ has zero energy, so it is form-Cauchy.
Consequently $h\in\overline D$ and $\overline E(h,h)=0$. Form
Cauchy--Schwarz gives zero cross-energy against all of $\overline D$.
The form norm on $H_{\rm rad}$ equals its $H$ norm. Finite-prefix bounded
Lipschitz functions are dense in the spatial amplitude $L^2$ space by
compact approximation and the cylinder monotone-class argument. Thus this
is the whole amplitude-only $L^2$ subspace, not a finite list of witnesses.
No conditional averaging of the generator is introduced.

Since the source aperture cylinder $s_v$ belongs to the original domain,
$\overline E(s_v,s_v)=E(s_v,s_v)>0$. Positive radial variance and zero
radial energy coexist with nonzero label activity at the unchanged time scale.
