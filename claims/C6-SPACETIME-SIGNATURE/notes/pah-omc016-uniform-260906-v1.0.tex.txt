\section*{Exact energy and fixed-graph cutoff limit}
Write $s_v\in\{1/2,1\}$ and let $\sigma_e\in\{-1,1\}$ be the
product of the endpoint phase and link signs. The source formula is exactly
\[
 F(r,z)=F_0(z)+\sum_v\left(\frac{r_v^6}{6}+\frac{r_v^4}{4}
                  +\frac{s_v^2r_v^2}{2}\right)
       +\sum_{e=\{v,w\}}\frac{J_e(s)}2(r_v-\sigma_e r_w)^2,
 \qquad 1\leq J_e=\frac2{s_v+s_w}\leq2.
\]
Here $F_0$ is the unchanged nonnegative aperture/Wilson energy. Thus
$F\geq\sum_v r_v^6/6$ with no term discarded from the model.

For $x\in\mathbb R_+^N$ define $a_j(x)_v=h_j\lfloor x_v/h_j\rfloor$,
$D_j=[0,R_j+h_j)^N$ and
$q_j(x,z)=\mathbf1_{D_j}(x)e^{-F(a_j(x),z)}$. The upper endpoint
$\ell=M_j$ has its own half-open cell. Consequently
\[
 \sum_z\int q_j(x,z)\,dx=h_j^N Z_{n,j}
\]
exactly. This common factor cancels from expectations; it does not change
the finite reference measure. Pointwise $a_j(x)\to x$ and $D_j$ exhausts
the orthant. Since $h_j\leq1$,
\[
 q_j(x,z)\leq W(x):=\prod_v w(x_v),\qquad
 w(t)=\exp\left(-\frac{\max(t-1,0)^6}{6}\right).
\]
For $t\geq4$, $(t-1)^6/6\geq t$, so
$\int_0^\infty w(t)\,dt\leq4+e^{-4}\leq5$.
Every polynomial moment of $W$ is finite.
Dominated convergence gives the finite, positive normalization
\[
 Z_n^{\mathrm{rad}}=\sum_z\int_{\mathbb R_+^N}e^{-F(r,z)}\,dr,
 \qquad d\nu_n=(Z_n^{\mathrm{rad}})^{-1}e^{-F(r,z)}\,dr
\]
with labelled counting in $z$. Applying the same argument to
$g(a_j(x),z)q_j(x,z)$ proves weak convergence for every bounded continuous
$g$, including $b_v^2$.

An explicit all-$j$ tightness bound uses the unit cube, where
\[

The vertex contribution is $1/8+1/6+1/4+1/2$, the edge contribution is

Every $D_j$ contains this cube, so the lifted partition is at least

\section*{Boundary-valid identity and a uniform local moment}
All polynomial moments under $\nu_n$ are finite. For fixed other
coordinates and labels, $r_v e^{-F}$ vanishes at $0$ and at infinity.
Its derivative, and its integral over the other coordinates, are absolutely
integrable by polynomial-times-sextic domination. Integration by parts
and Fubini therefore yield
\[
 1=\nu_n(r_v\partial_v F)
  =\nu_n\left(r_v^6+r_v^4+s_v^2r_v^2+
       \sum_{w\sim v}J_{vw}(r_v^2-\sigma_{vw}r_vr_w)\right).
\]
The value $1$ uses reference $dr$, not $r\,dr$. This identity is applied
to the constructed continuous radial law, not asserted at a finite mesh.

Let $X=\max_v\nu_n(r_v^2)$, finite and attained. At a maximizing vertex,
Jensen, the identity above and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
 X^3\leq\nu_n(r_v^6)
 \leq1+2\sum_{w\sim v}\nu_n(r_vr_w)\leq1+10X.
\]
If $X\geq4$, then $X^3-10X=X(X^2-10)\geq24>1$, a contradiction.
Hence $\nu_n(r_v^2)\leq4$ for every $v,n$. This is a local maximum
argument, not a volume sum; it assumes no translation symmetry and retains
all frontier terms.

\section*{Uniform conditional probability away from zero}
Let $A_v=\{r_w\leq8\text{ for every }w\sim v\}$. Markov's inequality
and the degree bound give $\nu_n(A_v)\geq1-5\cdot4/64=11/16$.
Conditioning on all other amplitudes and $z$ leaves density proportional
to $e^{-V(t)}dt$, where
\[
 V(t)=\frac{t^6}{6}+\frac{t^4}{4}+\frac{A t^2}{2}-Ht,
 \quad A=s_v^2+\sum_{w\sim v}J_{vw}\in[1/4,11],
 \quad H=\sum_{w\sim v}J_{vw}\sigma_{vw}r_w.
\]
On $A_v$, $|H|\leq80$. For $1\leq t\leq2$,
\[
 V(t)\leq\frac{64}{6}+\frac{16}{4}+22+160=\frac{590}{3}.
\]
For $0\leq t\leq4$, $V(t)\geq-320$; for $t\geq4$,
$80t\leq t^6/12$ and $t\leq t^6/12$, so $V(t)\geq t$.
The conditional normalization is at most $4e^{320}+e^{-4}\leq5e^{320}$.
Therefore, on $A_v$,
\[
 \Pr_{\nu_n}\{1\leq r_v\leq2\mid r_{-v},z\}
 \geq\frac15 e^{-1550/3}.
\]
Since $b_v^2=1$ on this interval, averaging gives the stated
$\nu_n(b_v^2)\geq(11/80)e^{-1550/3}$.
The thresholds $4,8$ are auxiliary proof constants, not model or witness
changes. This conditioning is ordinary disintegration, not a new dynamics
or a refinement averaging map.
