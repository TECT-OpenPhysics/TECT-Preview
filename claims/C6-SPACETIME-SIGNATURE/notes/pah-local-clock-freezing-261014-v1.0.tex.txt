\section*{2. Source cap and exact labelled path realization}
Use only the fixed-scope full-state estimate of R-575, equations (5)--(7):
$\sum_v|\psi_v|=A=R_0q_0/m_0$, $|F_r(x)|\leq B$, and
\[
\begin{split}
 b_0={}&\lambda_s(1-\epsilon)^2/2+|m^2|A^2/2
       +|\lambda_4|A^4/4+\eta_6 A^6/6+gA^2/2,\\
 B={}&vb_0+\kappa_s e(1-\epsilon)^2/2
                 +2\kappa_D eA^2/\epsilon+2\kappa_g p/\epsilon,\qquad
 C=e^{\beta B}.
\end{split}                                                 \tag{5}
\]
The parameter domains and normalization are identical. These bounds follow
on every full tuple from $|\psi_w-U_e\psi_v|\leq2A$, $J_e,J_b\leq1/\epsilon$,
and $0\leq1-\operatorname{Re}U_b\leq2$. Every mobility is at most one;
hence $c_a(x)\leq C$, including signed mass/quartic coefficients. $C$ is
independent of $r$, not of geometry, $\epsilon$ or the other parameters.

There are $M=4(v+e)$ potential labels. Give each label an independent
Poisson proposal stream of rate $C$, with independent uniform acceptance
marks. At a proposal, apply its root with probability $c_a(x)/C$ if valid;
otherwise stay. Rejected proposals are auxiliary bookkeeping, not new source
events. Invalid roots have acceptance zero and are never evaluated outside
$\Omega_r$. The infinitesimal generator of the resulting state path is
exactly (2). Equivalently its one-proposal kernel $P$ satisfies
\[
 P f(x)-f(x)=M^{-1}\sum_{a\text{ valid}}(c_a(x)/C)[f(ax)-f(x)],
 \qquad MC(P-I)=L_r.                                        \tag{6}
\]
The proposal sum is finite on compact intervals almost surely. Conditional
on its count, the kernel is $P$, so the transition matrices are
$e^{-MCt}\sum_{n\geq0}(MCt)^nP^n/n!=e^{tL_r}$.
Independent future proposal increments give the finite Markov path law
with the stipulated initial distribution. Thus this construction represents
the original chain; it does not change rates or time. Duplicate labels
contribute their separate rates, as required by the source.

Let $N^{\rm prop}_{\rm LK}(S)$ count all LK proposals in $[0,S]$.
It is Poisson with mean $\Lambda S$, where $\Lambda=2eC$, because there are
exactly $2e$ independent signed LK proposal streams. The accepted LK events
need not be Poisson: their intensities depend on the full state.

\section*{3. Maximal path estimate and all local clocks}
PH, TR and AP leave $u_e$, hence instantaneous $W$, unchanged. They still
affect subsequent LK rates; (5)--(6) do not discard them. A single LK move
touches at most two elementary faces, so telescoping their unit holonomies
gives, with modular chord distance,
\[
 |\Delta_aW_r|\leq\frac2p|e^{2\pi i/K_r}-1|
       =\frac4p\sin(\pi/K_r)\leq d_r:=\frac{4\pi}{pK_r}.
                                                               \tag{7}
\]
This counts boundary edges safely and retains both signs at $K=2$.
Telescoping along each original path implies
\[
 \sup_{0\leq t\leq S}|W_r(X_t)-W_r(X_0)|
       \leq d_r N^{\rm prop}_{\rm LK}(S).                    \tag{8}
\]
For $N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(z)$ the generating function is
$\mathbb E q^N=e^{z(q-1)}$. Differentiating at $q=1$ yields
$\mathbb EN=z$, $\mathbb EN(N-1)=z^2$, and $\mathbb EN^2=z+z^2$.
Thus (8) has a square-integrable envelope.

Since $\epsilon^\nu\leq s_v(X_t)^\nu\leq1$, each clock in (3) is continuous,
strictly increasing and onto $[0,\infty)$, with
\[
 \epsilon^\nu t\leq\tau_v(t)\leq t,\qquad
 u\leq\sigma_v(u)\leq u/\epsilon^\nu.                       \tag{9}
\]
The inverse is causal: $\{\sigma_v(u)\leq t\}=\{\tau_v(t)\geq u\}$.
No stochastic time-change or stationarity theorem is required. For
$S=T/\epsilon^\nu$ every sampled time in (4), for every vertex, lies in the
same $[0,S]$. Consequently, without a vertex union factor,
\[
 E_r(T)\leq d_r^2\{\Lambda S+(\Lambda S)^2\}
       =\left(\frac{4\pi}{pK_r}\right)^2
          \left\{2eCS+(2eCS)^2\right\}\longrightarrow0.       \tag{10}
\]
All paths are piecewise constant with finitely many proposals on $[0,S]$;
the finite-vertex maxima and time suprema above are measurable. They are
bounded by (8) and also by $2$ in absolute displacement. The limit in (10)
uses $K_r\geq2^{r+1}\to\infty$ and fixed $C,e,p,S$. It is a second-moment
bound of order $K_r^{-2}$, not a derived relaxation time or its lower bound.

\section*{4. Independent checks of the analytic step}
An independent derivation avoids Poisson independence. Count accepted
labelled LK incidences by $N_t$; their predictable intensity obeys
$\lambda_r(X_t)\leq\Lambda$. Stop at the $m$th LK incidence and freeze the
augmented finite state/count chain. Its generator on $n$ and $n^2$ is,
respectively, $1_{n<m}\lambda_r(x)$ and
$1_{n<m}(2n+1)\lambda_r(x)$. Integrating the finite matrix exponential gives
\[
 \mathbb EN^{(m)}_t\leq\Lambda t,\qquad
 \mathbb E(N^{(m)}_t)^2
 \leq\int_0^t\Lambda(2\mathbb EN^{(m)}_s+1)\,ds
 \leq\Lambda t+\Lambda^2t^2.                                \tag{11}
\]
The full rate cap $MC$ gives nonexplosion. Monotone convergence as
$m\to\infty$ and pathwise telescoping give (10) again. No new physical
carrier is introduced: the finite stopped count is a proof device only.

A second, weaker check uses original external-time stationarity and the
per-label midpoint flux bound from R-575 equation (11):
$\sum_x\pi_r(x)c_a(x)\leq1$. For LK it follows by Cauchy--Schwarz on the
bijective labelled root from $\pi_r c_a=m_a\sqrt{\pi_r(x)\pi_r(ax)}$.
Expected total variation on $[0,S]$ is at most $2ed_rS$. Because $W\in[-1,1]$,
the squared maximum displacement is at most twice that variation. Hence
\[
 E_r(T)\leq4ed_rS\longrightarrow0.                         \tag{12}
\]
Stationarity is used here only at deterministic original times before
the pathwise interval containment, never in the sampled clock. The
positive initial Gibbs variance of $W$ in R-575 remains separate; (10)
is not a constant-readout artifact, nor a new stationary local-clock law.

\item \textbf{An arbitrary vertex defines a physical clock. DISMISSED:}
