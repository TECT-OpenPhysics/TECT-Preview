\section{Purpose and scope}
R-579 excludes a pure global minimum at each fixed cutoff. It does not
exclude a sequence of increasingly pure minima as the cutoff increases.
Here one lower bound applies to an entire conserved energy sublevel, not
just its equilibria. No distance to a ground-state orbit is estimated.

Use the unchanged EXCH-001 Hamiltonian on the side-16 periodic three-torus,
volume $V=16^3$, with exact orthogonal Fourier projections at $K$ for the
three complex order components and $2K$ for the complex reservoir.
All integers $K\ge1$ are allowed separately. Denote normalized spatial
averages by $\langle f\rangle=V^{-1}\int f$. Write
\[
 \rho=\Psi^\dagger\Psi,\quad S=\Psi^T\Psi,\quad
 m=\langle\rho\rangle,\quad n=\langle|\chi|^2\rangle,\quad
 q=m/2+n=\bar Q/V=43/432,\quad x=m/(2q).
\]
The reservoir share is $1-x=n/q=2Q_\chi/\bar Q$, not $Q_\chi/\bar Q$.
The pure-order energy is $E_P=\mu_t\bar Q$ by R-579 on this sector.
Suppose $H\le E_P-V\delta$ with $\delta>0$.
Define $s=\rho_*=43/216$, $c=\gamma/6=27/100$, $d=s+1/4$ and
$\epsilon=1/16$. Then on every original trajectory from this sublevel,
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle x(\tau)(1-x(\tau))\ \ge\
 b_\delta=\frac{\delta^2}{2\epsilon^2d q^2}\quad(\tau\in\mathbb R).}
\]
At $\delta=1/10000$ the sublevel is nonempty for every $K$, and
$b_\delta=20155392/70059765625$. Each share is at least $b_\delta$
because $0\le x\le1$. This is a sufficient bound, not an optimal one.

\paragraph{What is being excluded.}
In particular there is no sequence of states, one in each of arbitrarily
many such finite cutoffs at the same positive gap, for which one entire
component carries a vanishing charge fraction. Spatial zeros of the
density, loss of spatial coherence, or stationarity are not excluded.

\section{Energy estimate and persistence}
The pinned R-158 decomposition (1.3) gives
$F_{\rm P1}\ge\mu_t V m/2+c V R$ with
$R=\langle\rho(\rho-s)^2\rangle$. Its spectral and Class-II remainders
are nonnegative; the inequality holds off shell as well as on a plane.
In EXCH-001 the other energy terms per volume are
$n/2+\langle|\chi|^4\rangle/4-\epsilon\Re\langle\bar\chi S\rangle$.
Set $T=\langle\rho^2\rangle$. The complex triangle inequality gives
$|S|\le\rho$. Subtract $\mu_t q$, keep the direction of the energy
inequality, and apply Cauchy--Schwarz in normalized measure:
\[
 \delta+cR+(1/2-\mu_t)n
 \le \epsilon\Re\langle\bar\chi S\rangle
 \le \epsilon\sqrt{n T}.
\]
Only the nonnegative reservoir quartic was discarded in this step.
Since $d+(\rho-s)^2-\rho=(\rho-s-1/2)^2$, we have
$T\le dm+R$. Split the square root; then
\[
 \epsilon\sqrt{nT}\le\epsilon\sqrt{dmn}+
 cR+\frac{\epsilon^2}{4c}n .
\]
The coefficient left on $n$ is strictly larger than $1127/6912>0$:
use $\mu_t<1/3$ and $\epsilon^2/(4c)=25/6912$.
Consequently $\delta\le\epsilon\sqrt{dmn}$. Squaring and substituting
$mn=2q^2 x(1-x)$ proves the assertion on the sublevel. R-578 gives
complete finite-dimensional Hamiltonian flow in the original canonical
time and conservation of both $H$ and $Q$. This extends the same bound
to all $\tau$, without orbit approximation or an interchange of limits.

\section{Independent derivation and a nonempty test}
Alternatively, take square roots in $\rho\le d+(\rho-s)^2$ and
multiply by $\epsilon|\chi|\sqrt{\rho}$. Pointwise Young absorption gives
\[
 \epsilon|\chi|\rho\le
 \epsilon\sqrt d\,|\chi|\sqrt\rho+
 c\rho(\rho-s)^2+\frac{\epsilon^2}{4c}|\chi|^2.
\]
Integrate first and then apply Cauchy--Schwarz only to the remaining
$\langle|\chi|\sqrt\rho\rangle$. This recovers the same bound without
estimating a cutoff-dependent sup norm or postulating a bound on $T$.

For nonvacuity use the original R-579 plane and its doubled reservoir
mode, with $r=\epsilon s$ and $p=s-2r^2>0$. Their charge density is
$p/2+r^2=q$ at all $K\ge1$. Their energy per volume relative to $E_P/V$
is $(1/2-\mu_t)r^2+r^4/4+c p(p-s)^2-\epsilon r p$.
Replacing $\mu_t$ by its smaller bound $1/4$ gives
$-18277891107531797/157767900923764408320<-1/10000$.
The verifier independently subtracts the two full plane energies using
the bare quartic and sextic coefficients. This trial witnesses the
sublevel only: the bound does not restrict other states to that ansatz.

\section{Devil's-advocate review and verification}
\begin{itemize}
\item Could ultraviolet concentration make one fraction vanish?
The pointwise square and normalized Cauchy--Schwarz hold at arbitrary
amplitude; no grid inverse inequality or finite-dimensional compactness
constant appears. A vanishing integrated fraction contradicts the product
floor at fixed $\delta$.
\item Could an energy gap be lost by a factor of $V$ or two? The hypothesis
uses a gap per volume. The charge is $V(m/2+n)$; using either $Q_\chi$
instead of $2Q_\chi$, or replacing $V\delta$ by $\delta$, is incorrect.
\item Could the result assert persistent charge flow or spontaneous
relaxation? It bounds occupations, not their derivatives. Both may be
stationary. At $\delta=0$ the positive floor disappears; only the designated
positive gap is asserted nonempty here.
\item Could this produce a continuum solution? No compactness in space
or time, convergence of trajectories, volume estimate, physical clock
or state preparation follows. The torus and hypothetical parent are inputs.
\end{itemize}
Two analytic derivations, exact rational/symbolic checks and a separate
hostile review cover the normalization, cancellation, trial and scope.
Six partial Lean lemmas check the moment envelope, scalar split, Young
coefficient, positive remainder, absorption and final normalization;
the function-space inequalities, nonvacuity and inherited dynamics are
not formalized. No external refereeing or experimental test is claimed.
