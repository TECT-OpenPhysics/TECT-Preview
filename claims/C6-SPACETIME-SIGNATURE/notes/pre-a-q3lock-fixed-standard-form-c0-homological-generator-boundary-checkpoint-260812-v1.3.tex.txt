\section{Fixed finite faithful Gibbs standard-form theorem}

Fix one finite periodic Q3 volume, one compact source, one inverse
temperature, one declared split mesh/history, and one radius \(R_0\).  Let
\(\rho\) be its faithful Gibbs density on \(\mathcal H\), let

\begin{equation}
 \mathcal K=\mathfrak S_2(\mathcal H),
 \qquad \xi=\rho^{1/2},
 \qquad \pi(A)X=AX,
 \tag{3.1}
\end{equation}

and let \(P_L,P\) be the cutoff and exact split implementers.  Define

\begin{equation}
 e_+(L)=\|(P-P_L)\xi\|_2,
 \qquad
 e_-(L)=\|(P^*-P_L^*)\xi\|_2
       =\|\xi(P-P_L)\|_2.
 \tag{3.2}
\end{equation}

The equality in the second leg uses only invariance of the Hilbert--Schmidt
norm under adjoint.  It does not assume modular commutation.  At fixed
\(R_0\), the v2.3 opposite telescopes give separately

\begin{equation}
 e_\pm(L)
 \le {54|c|T\sqrt{M_{20}}\over\hbar}R_0^3L^{-8},
 \tag{3.3}
\end{equation}

and their paired bound is

\begin{equation}
 e_+(L)+e_-(L)
 \le {54\sqrt2|c|T\sqrt{M_{20}}\over\hbar}R_0^3L^{-8}.
 \tag{3.4}
\end{equation}

Faithfulness makes \(\xi\) cyclic for the right commutant.  More explicitly,

\begin{equation}
 \{\xi C:C\in B(\mathcal H)\}
 \quad\hbox{is dense in }\mathfrak S_2(\mathcal H),
 \tag{3.5}
\end{equation}

because finite-rank operators are dense and their left vectors can be
approximated by vectors in the dense range of \(\xi\).  On this core,

\begin{equation}
 \|(P-P_L)\xi C\|_2\le e_+(L)\|C\|,
 \qquad
 \|(P^*-P_L^*)\xi C\|_2\le e_-(L)\|C\|.
 \tag{3.6}
\end{equation}

All implementers are unitary, so uniform boundedness extends both
convergences to every vector of \(\mathfrak S_2(\mathcal H)\).  Left
multiplication by \(P_L\) therefore converges strong-star to left
multiplication by \(P\).

For fixed \(A\in B(\mathcal H)\) and fixed
\(\eta\in\mathfrak S_2(\mathcal H)\),

\begin{align}
 &(P_L^*AP_L-P^*AP)\eta \notag\\
 &\quad=P_L^*A(P_L-P)\eta+(P_L^*-P^*)AP\eta
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{3.7}
\end{align}

Replacing \(A\) by \(A^*\) gives the adjoint statement.  Hence

\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\pi(P_L^*AP_L)\longrightarrow\pi(P^*AP)
 \quad\hbox{point-strong-star for every fixed }A.}
 \tag{3.8}
\end{equation}

The quantitative claim stops at (3.3)--(3.6).  Equation (3.8) is qualitative
and is not an arbitrary-observable Gibbs-L2 channel estimate.  Moreover, the
optimized choice \(L=R^{2/5}\) changes the volume, Gibbs density, and standard
representation.  Its v2.3 \(R^{-1/5}\) implementer corridor is not a
strong-star rate on one common Hilbert space and proves no thermodynamic
common alpha.

For the exact fixed-member fixture \(R_0=32\), the per-leg coefficients at
\(L=4,8\) are \(27\) and \(27/256\), while the paired coefficients are
\(27\sqrt2\) and \(27\sqrt2/256\).  An independent two-dimensional probe uses

\begin{equation}
 \rho=\operatorname{diag}(1/5,4/5),
 \qquad
 U_n={1\over n^2+1}
 \begin{pmatrix}n^2-1&-2n\\2n&n^2-1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{3.9}
\end{equation}

For \(P=I\), both squared legs are \(4/(n^2+1)\), equal to \(2/5\) and
\(2/25\) at \(n=3,7\).  With \(A=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), the squared
weighted conjugation errors are \(16n^2/(n^2+1)^2\), equal to
\(36/25\) and \(196/625\).  This finite probe checks the two directions and
their decay; it does not prove the general theorem.

\section{Conditional bidirectional all-shape C0 completion}

Let \(\mathcal A\) be one common unital C-star algebra, let
\(\mathcal A_0\) be a norm-dense unital star subalgebra, and let
\(\mathcal D\) be the directed set of every declared finite shape and cutoff.
For each \(\lambda\in\mathcal D\), let \(\alpha_\lambda^t\) be a point-norm
continuous automorphism group of \(\mathcal A\).  For every
\(A\in\mathcal A_0\) and finite \(T\), assume that both nets

\begin{equation}
 \alpha_\lambda^t(A),
 \qquad \alpha_\lambda^{-t}(A)
 \tag{4.1}
\end{equation}

are point-norm Cauchy uniformly for \(0\le t\le T\) over the full directed
set.  This is stronger than convergence along one preferred exhaustion.

Every \(\alpha_\lambda^t\) is isometric, so the two limits extend uniquely
from \(\mathcal A_0\) to \(\mathcal A\); denote them by
\(\alpha^t\) and \(\beta^t\).  Norm limits preserve linearity, products,
stars, and the unit.  For the group law, add and subtract one fixed limiting
argument:

\begin{align}
 &\|\alpha_\lambda^s(\alpha_\lambda^t(A))
       -\alpha^s(\alpha^t(A))\| \notag\\
 &\quad\le
 \|\alpha_\lambda^t(A)-\alpha^t(A)\|
 +\|\alpha_\lambda^s(\alpha^t(A))-\alpha^s(\alpha^t(A))\|.
 \tag{4.2}
\end{align}

Both terms tend to zero.  Passing the finite-volume identities through the
norm limit gives

\begin{equation}
 \alpha^{s+t}=\alpha^s\alpha^t,
 \qquad
 \alpha^t\beta^t=\beta^t\alpha^t=\operatorname{id}.
 \tag{4.3}
\end{equation}

Thus \(\beta^t=\alpha^{-t}\), every \(\alpha^t\) is an automorphism, and the
limit is exhaustion independent.  Uniform convergence on compact time
intervals preserves continuity on \(\mathcal A_0\).  A fixed
\(\mathcal A_0\) approximation and isometry leave a
\(2\|A-A_0\|\) error for a general \(A\), so the limit is a point-norm
continuous \(C_0\) automorphism group.

For the arithmetic probe, take
\(H_n=(1+1/n)\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\) on \(M_2\).  At
\(n=10\), \(m=20\), and \(T=1\),
\(\|H_n-H_m\|=1/20\); the Duhamel automorphism bound is \(1/10\) for both time signs,
and the bound from \(n=10\) to the limiting Hamiltonian is
\(1/5\).

This theorem is conditional.  It proves no instance of (4.1) for Q3, no
identification of the abstract generator with a local derivation or its
domain, and no convergence or quotient theorem for finite-volume Gibbs/KMS
states.

\section{First local parity-equivariant homological generator}

Let \(P\) be the product rank-two low-band projection, \(Q=1-P\), and

\begin{equation}
 KP=0,
 \qquad K=\sum_xk_x\ge\Gamma Q,
 \qquad R=Q(QKQ)^{-1}Q.
 \tag{5.1}
\end{equation}

For every edge \(e\), define

\begin{equation}
 T_e=QB_eP,
 \qquad \|T_e\|\le\epsilon,
 \qquad D_e=RT_e,
 \qquad G_e=D_e-D_e^*.
 \tag{5.2}
\end{equation}

Assume that \(K\), \(P\), and every \(B_e\) commute with the declared global
parity.  Then \(G_e\) is parity equivariant and skew adjoint.  The global
resolvent \(R\) is retained in every commutator.  Locality follows because
\(K\) preserves exact onsite high-support sectors and \(T_e\) creates high
support only in \(e\); on \(\operatorname{ran}(T_e)\), the restriction
\(RT_e\) is represented by the corresponding edge-local onsite resolvent.

Relative to \(P\oplus Q\), \(G_e\) is one skew off-diagonal block matrix, so
its norm is sharp at the block level:

\begin{equation}
 \|G_e\|=\|D_e\|\le{\epsilon\over\Gamma},
 \qquad
 \boxed{\|G\|_a\le2ze^a{\epsilon\over\Gamma}}.
 \tag{5.3}
\end{equation}

Let \(\Pi_M\) preserve parity, contain \(P\), and commute with the onsite
resolvent on the declared sectors.  Put

\begin{equation}
 G_{e,M}=R\Pi_MT_e-T_e^*\Pi_MR,
 \qquad
 \tau_M=\sup_e\|(1-\Pi_M)T_e\|.
 \tag{5.4}
\end{equation}

The same block identity gives

\begin{equation}
 \|G_e-G_{e,M}\|\le{\tau_M\over\Gamma},
 \qquad
 \|G-G_M\|_a\le2ze^a{\tau_M\over\Gamma}.
 \tag{5.5}
\end{equation}

Since \(KD_e=T_e\), \(D_eK=0\), \(KD_e^*=0\), and
\(D_e^*K=T_e^*\), one obtains, for
\(G=\sum_eG_e\) and \(V_{\rm od}=\sum_e(T_e+T_e^*)\),

\begin{equation}
 [K,G]=V_{\rm od},
 \qquad [G,K]=-V_{\rm od}.
 \tag{5.6}
\end{equation}

The exact second-order low block after first-order cancellation is

\begin{equation}
 \boxed{{1\over2}P[G,V_{\rm od}]P
 =-\sum_{e,f}T_e^*RT_f,}
 \tag{5.7}
\end{equation}

which is exactly the v2.3 onsite-resolvent coefficient.

For \(z=6\), \(e^a=2\), \(\epsilon=1/100\), \(\Gamma=2\), and
\(\tau_M=1/1000\),

\begin{equation}
 \|G_e\|\le{1\over200},
 \quad \|G\|_a\le{3\over25},
 \quad \|G_e-G_{e,M}\|\le{1\over2000},
 \quad \|G-G_M\|_a\le{3\over250}.
 \tag{5.8}
\end{equation}

On the four-level parity fixture

\begin{equation}
 P=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0,0),
 \quad K=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,2,2),
 \quad \Theta=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1,-1),
 \tag{5.9}
\end{equation}

take \(T=\epsilon(|3\rangle\langle1|+|4\rangle\langle2|)\).  Direct matrix
multiplication verifies parity, skew adjointness, (5.6), and

\begin{equation}
 {1\over2}P[G,T+T^*]P
 =-{\epsilon^2\over\Gamma}P=-{1\over20000}P.
 \tag{5.10}
\end{equation}

Equation (5.7) is an algebraic coefficient identity.  It proves no
third-order coefficient, convergent BCH or Lie--Schwinger series, all-order
connected interaction, phasewise intertwiner, or oscillator GNS gap.

\section{Two exact all-order promotion boundaries}

\subsection{Disconnected spectator in a scalar Feshbach denominator}

Let subsystem \(X\) have two low and two high states, high energy
\(\Gamma=2\), and low-to-high coupling

\begin{equation}
 T_X=\epsilon\operatorname{diag}(1,2),
 \qquad \epsilon={1\over10}.
 \tag{6.1}
\end{equation}

Let the disconnected spectator \(Y\) lie entirely in the retained low block,
with \(h_Y=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\).  The original Hamiltonian is an
\(X\) term plus a \(Y\) term and has no mixed interaction.  With the global
product low projection and scalar energy \(E=0\), the raw Feshbach self-energy
is

\begin{equation}
 \Sigma(0)=\epsilon^2\operatorname{diag}(1,4)
 \otimes\operatorname{diag}(1/2,1/3).
 \tag{6.2}
\end{equation}

Using \(Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), its mixed
\(Z_X\otimes Z_Y\) coefficient is

\begin{equation}
 {\epsilon^2\over4}(1-4)(1/2-1/3)
 =-{\epsilon^2\over8}=-{1\over800}.
 \tag{6.3}
\end{equation}

Thus the raw Feshbach operator \(PHP-E-\Sigma(E)\) has mixed coefficient
\(+1/800\), despite the absence of an original \(X\)--\(Y\) interaction.  The
global scalar denominator retains the spectator energy.  This does not
contradict the v2.3 second-order onsite-resolvent coefficient, whose disjoint
ordered edge terms vanish.  It blocks only the automatic passage from that
second-order fact to connectedness of the raw all-order global scalar
Feshbach map.

\subsection{Finite Ritz boundedness is not uniform SW smallness}

Let

\begin{equation}
 \Pi_M=\sum_{n=0}^M|n\rangle\langle n|,
 \qquad q={a+a^*\over\sqrt2}.
 \tag{6.4}
\end{equation}

Each \(\Pi_Mq\Pi_M\) is a bounded finite matrix, but

\begin{equation}
 \|\Pi_Mq\Pi_M\|\ge\sqrt{M/2}.
 \tag{6.5}
\end{equation}

For the correctly compressed quadratic bond,

\begin{equation}
 \langle M,0|(q_x-q_y)^2|M,0\rangle=M+1.
 \tag{6.6}
\end{equation}

Choose

\begin{equation}
 B_M={1\over4}(\Pi_M\otimes\Pi_M)(q_x-q_y)^2
       (\Pi_M\otimes\Pi_M),
 \qquad \Gamma=2.
 \tag{6.7}
\end{equation}

Then

\begin{equation}
 {\|B_M\|\over\Gamma}\ge{M+1\over8},
 \tag{6.8}
\end{equation}

which is at least \(1/2\) at \(M=3\) and at least \(2\) at \(M=15\).
In the verifier's ASCII token form, the lower bound is \texttt{(M+1)/8}.
Finite-cutoff boundedness therefore gives no cutoff-uniform ordinary
operator-norm SW smallness at fixed \(\Gamma\).  This fixture does not reject
relative-form smallness, graph norms, a connected QPS construction, or the
resolvent-compatible Ritz-tail theorem in Section 5.
