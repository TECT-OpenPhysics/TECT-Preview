\section{Finite-Gibbs two-orientation cutoff resummation}

Let $H$ and $H_L=H-W_L$ be bounded-below finite-volume Hamiltonians on a
common form domain, and set

\[
 \rho=Z^{-1}e^{-\beta H},\qquad
 U(t)=e^{-itH/\hbar},\qquad U_L(t)=e^{-itH_L/\hbar}.       \tag{3.1}
\]

Assume the two strong Duhamel formulas on a common invariant form core and
$\rho(W_L^2)<\infty$.  Put

\[
 \epsilon_L(t)={|t|\over\hbar}\rho(W_L^2)^{1/2}.          \tag{3.2}
\]

The ordering with the full flow adjacent to $\rho^{1/2}$ on the right gives

\[
 \|(U(t)-U_L(t))\rho^{1/2}\|_2\le\epsilon_L(t),           \tag{3.3}
\]

and the opposite ordering gives

\[
 \|\rho^{1/2}(U(t)-U_L(t))\|_2\le\epsilon_L(t).           \tag{3.4}
\]

No commutation of $\rho$ with $U_L$ is used.  Factoring the density-matrix
difference into Hilbert--Schmidt products yields

\[
 \|\rho-U_L(t)\rho U_L(t)^*\|_1\le2\epsilon_L(t).         \tag{3.5}
\]

For a bounded context satisfying the half-modular identity

\[
 A\rho^{1/2}=\rho^{1/2}C_A,\qquad
 C_A=\alpha_{-i\beta\hbar/2}(A),                         \tag{3.6}
\]

the right observable estimate is

\[
 \|[\alpha_t(A)-\alpha_t^{(L)}(A)]\rho^{1/2}\|_2
 \le(\|A\|+\|C_A\|)\epsilon_L(t).                       \tag{3.7}
\]

Applying (3.6) separately to $A^*$ gives the left estimate with
$\|C_{A^*}\|$.  One narrow sufficient class is a finite Bohr-projective
decomposition $A=\sum_\omega A_\omega$ with
$[H,A_\omega]=\omega A_\omega$, $|\omega|\le\Omega$, and finite
$\sum_\omega\|A_\omega\|$.  This is not an arbitrary bounded-observable
theorem.

For the registered finite-volume Q3 hard/form cutoff pair, $H$ and $H_L$ are
closed form sums on the common quartic form domain, $W_L$ is a real quadratic
coordinate multiplier, and the existing finite-volume Gibbs moments control
$W_L^2$.  Spectral or form truncation of $W_L$, bounded Duhamel, and then
$S_2$ closure realize (3.3)--(3.5).  A separately defined smooth
clipped-coordinate cutoff requires its own form-convergence audit.

\section{Why arbitrary bounded contexts do not follow}

Then $\rho_p$ is exactly Gibbs for $H$.  At $t=t_0$, both squared weighted

\[
