\section*{3. All-index formula and fixed-data counterexample}
Fix once and for all $f_0=\mathbf1_{\{j_O=0\}}$ at $r_0=0$.
It is gauge invariant because gauge transformations leave $j$ fixed, and
anchor invariant because the allowed automorphisms fix $O$.
It has full-state sup norm one. R-571 gives, at the same $h,N$,
$j_O\mapsto\lfloor j_O/2^{s-r}\rfloor$. Thus for every $r\ge0$,
\[
 f_r=I_{0,r}f_0=\mathbf1_{\{j_O<M_r\}}.
\]
This is one fixed cylinder observable, not a cutoff-dependent selection.
The complete generator, by (2) and the individual zero increments above, is
\[
 L_r f_r(x)=\mathbf1_{\{j_O=M_r\}}-
                  \mathbf1_{\{j_O=M_r-1\}}. \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the only downward jump across the indicator discontinuity starts at
$M_r$, and the only upward jump across it starts at $M_r-1$.
The signs in (3) are respectively positive and negative. This also proves the
endpoint case $M_r=1$: the two states have generator values $-1,+1$.

For arbitrary $s>r\ge0$, set $D=2^{s-r}\ge2$, so $M_s=DM_r$.
For every fine tuple, the pullback of the positive indicator in (3) is
$\mathbf1_{\{j_O=M_s\}}$. The pullback of the negative indicator is
$\mathbf1_{\{M_s-D\le j_O<M_s\}}$. Subtracting the complete coarse
generator from the fine generator gives the exact all-state identity
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
  (L_s I_{r,s}f_r-I_{r,s}L_r f_r)(x)
  =\mathbf1_{\{M_s-D\le j_O<M_s-1\}}.} \tag{4}
\]
There is no half factor, volume factor, density correction or norm change.
The band in (4) is nonempty because $D\ge2$; it has $D-1$ integer labels.
For any such label a valid full state exists: put all occupation $Q_s=2^s$
at $O$, zero occupation elsewhere, and choose any admitted phase/link labels.
This is valid since $2^s\le4^s$. Hence on the full fine counting space
\[
 \|L_s I_{r,s}f_r-I_{r,s}L_r f_r\|_\infty=1
 \quad\text{for every }s>r\ge0. \tag{5}
\]

\begin{theorem}
The universal full-invariant-cylinder sup-norm Cauchy statement (1) is false
for the exact adopted PAH-v2 model and comparison at its allowed
$\epsilon=1$ boundary. Its GD-001 verdict is DISPROVED.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the parameters, carrier and observable specified above and $\eta_0=1$.
For every integer tail threshold $R\ge0$, choose $r=R$, $s=r+1$ and
$j_O=2^{r+1}-2$. Set all other $j$ and every phase/link label to zero and
put $\ell_O=Q_s$, all other occupations zero. This is an admitted full tuple
at every such cutoff. Formula (4) gives defect one there. Thus there cannot
be a tail on which all defects are strictly below $\eta_0$. Formula (5)
proves the stronger all-pair norm identity. No finite-table extrapolation
enters the argument.
\end{proof}

\section*{5. Devil's-advocate review and applicability}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{DISMISSED: equal displayed apertures identify the states.}
The approved v2 integer counting definition explicitly retains $j$ at
$\epsilon=1$. Quotienting it would change the model and the observable class.
\item \textbf{DISMISSED: an omitted root cancels the defect.}
All PH/TR/LK increments, and AP increments away from $O$, vanish individually.
Both signed AP moves at $O$ are included exactly when valid.
\item \textbf{DISMISSED: the factor is one half or the sign is reversed.}
The source generator has no half; only $B$ has $\sqrt{c/2}$. The definition
is fine minus coarse. At the adjacent witness the two values are $0,-1$.
\item \textbf{DISMISSED: the observable changes with the cutoff.}
It is always the same $f_0$ pulled back from $r_0=0$. Only the witness state
and the two comparison indices vary, as allowed by the exact negation.
\item \textbf{DISMISSED: this is only a small-cutoff artifact.}
The proof covers every integer $s>r\ge0$; the kernel covers every adjacent
pair through $M=2^r$, including an explicit witness beyond each threshold.
\item \textbf{UPHELD boundary: sup failure need not be Gibbs-$L^2$ failure.}
No Gibbs weight or weak convergence was evaluated. This result cannot be
used to decide either or a smaller limiting-generator domain.
\item \textbf{UPHELD boundary: one parameter boundary is not all parameters.}
The counterexample disproves the universal target, not every fixed
$0<\epsilon<1$ case or the finite consistency of R-570/R-571.
\end{itemize}
