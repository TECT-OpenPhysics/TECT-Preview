\section{Setting and two sequential rotations}

parity-preserving onsite Ritz cutoff \(M\).  Every block below is therefore a
bounded finite matrix.  Relative to a rank-two low projection \(P\) and
\(Q=1-P\), write

\begin{equation}
 H(\lambda)=K+\lambda(V_{\rm d}+V_{\rm od}),\qquad
 V_{\rm d}=A\oplus C,\qquad T=QVP.
 \tag{3.1}
\end{equation}

Normalize the low energy so that \(PKP=0\), assume
\(QKQ\ge\Gamma Q\), and put

\begin{equation}
 R=(QKQ)^{-1},\qquad D=RT,\qquad G=D-D^*.
 \tag{3.2}
\end{equation}

The inverse is extended by zero on \(P\).  The retained parity hypotheses say
that \(K\), \(P\), and \(V\) commute with global parity.  Consequently the
objects in both rotations preserve the declared parity sectors.  Direct block
multiplication gives

\begin{equation}
 [G,K]=-V_{\rm od}.
 \tag{3.3}
\end{equation}

For the first rotation,

\begin{equation}
 H_1(\lambda)=e^{\lambda G}H(\lambda)e^{-\lambda G},
 \tag{3.4}
\end{equation}

collecting powers through order three gives

\begin{align}
 H_1(\lambda)={}&K+\lambda V_{\rm d}
 +\lambda^2\left([G,V_{\rm d}]+{1\over2}[G,V_{\rm od}]\right)
 \notag\\
 &+\lambda^3\left({1\over2}[G,[G,V_{\rm d}]]
                  +{1\over3}[G,[G,V_{\rm od}]]\right)
 +O(\lambda^4).
 \tag{3.5}
\end{align}

The remaining second-order off-diagonal block is removed by defining

\begin{equation}
 Z=R(DA-CD),\qquad G_2=Z-Z^*.
 \tag{3.6}
\end{equation}

The \(QP\) block of \([G,V_{\rm d}]\) is \(DA-CD\), and another direct
block multiplication gives

\begin{equation}
 [G_2,K]=-[G,V_{\rm d}].
 \tag{3.7}
\end{equation}

Apply the second rotation

\begin{equation}
 H_2(\lambda)=e^{\lambda^2G_2}H_1(\lambda)e^{-\lambda^2G_2}.
 \tag{3.8}
\end{equation}

Equation (3.7) cancels the remaining order-two off-diagonal block.  At order
three, \([G_2,V_{\rm d}]\) is off diagonal.  Also \(G\) and
\(V_{\rm od}\) are off diagonal, so \([G,V_{\rm od}]\) is block diagonal
and \([G,[G,V_{\rm od}]]\) is off diagonal.  Projection therefore leaves
the complete low block

\begin{equation}
 \Theta^{(3)}={1\over2}P[G,[G,V_{\rm d}]]P.
 \tag{3.9}
\end{equation}

Using \(D=RT\), exact multiplication yields

\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Theta^{(3)}=T^*RCR T-{1\over2}\{A,T^*R^2T\}.}
 \tag{3.10}
\end{equation}

\section{Linked edge triples and conservative QPS control}

Resolve the local blocks as \(T=\sum_eT_e\), \(A=\sum_fA_f\), and
\(C=\sum_fC_f\).  The ordered \((e,f,g)\) term is

\begin{equation}
 \Theta^{(3)}_{efg}
 =T_e^*RC_fRT_g
 -{1\over2}\left(A_fT_e^*R^2T_g+T_e^*R^2T_gA_f\right).
 \tag{4.1}
\end{equation}

If the retained edge \(f\) is disconnected from \(e\cup g\), its high-block
operator \(C_f\) is the same retained spectator as \(A_f\) on the exact
high-support sector created by \(T_g\).  It commutes with \(R\), \(T_e\),
and \(T_g\), so

\begin{equation}
 T_e^*RC_fRT_g=A_fT_e^*R^2T_g=T_e^*R^2T_gA_f.
 \tag{4.2}
\end{equation}

Substitution into (4.1) gives exact cancellation.  If another component is
disconnected, the onsite high-support sectors make the corresponding
\(T_e^*\cdots T_g\) block orthogonal.  Thus every nonzero ordered triple has
connected union.  A connected union of three edges has at most four vertices
and diameter at most three, including repeated-edge cases.

\begin{equation}
 \rho_{M,\Lambda}=\sup_f\|R^{1/2}C_fR^{1/2}\|,

\section{Canonical compact-cylinder point-norm obstruction}

Let

\begin{equation}
 {\cal H}_x={\cal H}_y=L^2(\mathbb R^8),\qquad
 B_\delta=\exp\left({ic\delta\over\hbar}q_x\mathbin\cdot q_y\right),
 \qquad \beta_\delta=\operatorname{Ad}(B_\delta),
 \tag{6.1}
\end{equation}

where \(c\ne0\) and \(\hbar>0\).  For a nonzero compact operator \(K\) on
\({\cal H}_x\), the formal one-site cylinder is \(A=K\otimes I\).
Direct-integral decomposition over \(q_y\) gives, for every \(\delta\ne0\),

\begin{align}
 \|\beta_\delta(A)-A\|
 &=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{r\in\mathbb R^8}
 \|M_{c\delta r/\hbar}^*KM_{c\delta r/\hbar}-K\| \notag\\
 &=\sup_{s\in\mathbb R^8}\|M_s^*KM_s-K\|.
 \tag{6.2}
\end{align}

The modulations \(M_s\) converge weakly to zero as \(|s|\to\infty\).
Compactness gives \(KM_s\xi\to0\) for each fixed vector \(\xi\), and hence

\begin{equation}
 \sup_s\|M_s^*KM_s-K\|\ge\|K\|.
 \tag{6.3}
\end{equation}

Thus the multiplier action has a nonzero norm jump at every nonzero time.
For an exact rank-one fixture, set

\begin{equation}
 \psi(q)=2^{-4}\exp\left[-{1\over2}\sum_{j=1}^8|q_j|\right],
 \qquad K=P_\psi.
 \tag{6.4}
\end{equation}

The vector is normalized and

\begin{equation}
 \langle\psi,M_s\psi\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^8{1\over1+s_j^2}.
 \tag{6.5}
\end{equation}

The exact supremum in (6.2) is one.  With \(c=\hbar=1\),
\(\delta_n=1/n\), and \(r_n=(n/2)e_1\), the modulation is \(s=e_1/2\), so

\begin{equation}
 |\langle\psi,M_s\psi\rangle|={4\over5},\qquad
 \|P_{M_s\psi}-P_\psi\|={3\over5},\qquad
 \|P_{M_s\psi}-P_\psi\|^2={9\over25}.
 \tag{6.6}
\end{equation}

The unitized compacts on \({\cal H}_x\otimes{\cal H}_y\) do not contain
\(K\otimes I\): its infinite \({\cal H}_y\) multiplicity prevents
compactness modulo a scalar.  The multiplier of the nonunital compact ideal is

\begin{equation}
