\section{Finite-region local-strict topology}

Fix a finite ambient region $Y$ and put

\[
 \mathcal H_Y=L^2(\mathbb R^{8|Y|}),\qquad
 K_Y\ge1,\qquad
 \mathcal M_Y=B(\mathcal H_Y)=M(K(\mathcal H_Y)),               \tag{3.1}
\]

where $K_Y$ is any positive compact-resolvent control.  On norm-bounded
subsets of $\mathcal M_Y$, the strict topology is the strong-star topology.
For one fixed $0<s\le1/2$, define

\[
 q_{s,Y}(A)=\|AK_Y^{-s}\|+\|K_Y^{-s}A\|.                       \tag{3.2}
\]

For $E>\inf\sigma(K_Y)$ define the two-sided energy-constrained seminorm

\[
 e_{E,Y}(A)=\max\left\{
 \sup_{\substack{\|\psi\|=1\\ \langle\psi,K_Y\psi\rangle\le E}}
       \|A\psi\|,
 \sup_{\substack{\|\psi\|=1\\ \langle\psi,K_Y\psi\rangle\le E}}
       \|A^*\psi\|
 \right\}.                                                     \tag{3.3}
\]

If $\|A\|\le M$, spectral splitting gives the exact comparison bounds

\[
 e_{E,Y}(A)\le E^s
 \max\{\|AK_Y^{-s}\|,\|K_Y^{-s}A\|\},                         \tag{3.4}
\]

\[
 q_{s,Y}(A)\le2e_{E,Y}(A)+2ME^{-s}.                             \tag{3.5}
\]

Since $K_Y^{-s}$ is compact with dense range, strict convergence of a
norm-bounded net is equivalent to convergence in $q_{s,Y}$.  Equations
(3.4)--(3.5) make this equivalent to convergence in every $e_{E,Y}$.  No
constant uniform in $Y$, no exhaustion topology, and no infinite-volume
dynamics is hidden in this equivalence.

\section{Exact onsite and bond subflow controls}

For onsite evolution choose the separate compact-resolvent control
$K_Y^{\rm os}$ to be a positive weighted sum of the exact zero-source onsite
Hamiltonians.  It commutes with the finite-region tensor onsite unitary.
Onsite conjugation is therefore isometric in the versions of $q_{s,Y}$ and
$e_{E,Y}$ defined by $K_Y^{\rm os}$, preserves strict and strong-star
topology, and has a strict-$C_0$ orbit on every bounded local observable.

For the exact commuting all-bond kick write

\[
 \beta_\delta(A)=B_\delta^*AB_\delta.                           \tag{4.1}
\]

If $A$ is supported in $X$, choose one finite ambient $Y\supset N_1(X)$ and
the registered centered positive control $K_{Y,\mu}$ of R-167 v1.3.  For
$0<|\delta|\le1$, both orientations satisfy

\[
 B_{\pm\delta}^*K_{Y,\mu}B_{\pm\delta}
 \le M_\delta K_{Y,\mu},\qquad
 M_\delta=1+C_b|\delta|,                                      \tag{4.2}
\]

\[
 C_b=1+{c^2z^2e^\mu\over2\chi\sqrt\gamma}.                    \tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
 e_{E,Y}(\beta_\delta A)\le e_{M_\delta E,Y}(A),\qquad
 q_{s,Y}(\beta_\delta A)\le M_\delta^s q_{s,Y}(A).            \tag{4.4}
\]

The support statement is exact: onsite evolution fixes $X$, while the bond
kick sends it into $N_1(X)$ inside $Y$.  On the common polynomial/Schwartz
core, and only there when the unbounded commutators are invoked,

\[
 [q_x,\beta_\delta(A)]=\beta_\delta([q_x,A]),                   \tag{4.5}
\]

\[
 [p_x,\beta_\delta(A)]
 =\beta_\delta\left([p_x,A]-\delta c\sum_{y\sim x}[q_y,A]\right).
                                                                    \tag{4.6}
\]

Thus the directed family of finite-region multiplier algebras is a genuine
beta- and phase-state-independent local-strict subflow carrier.  It is a
precursor, not the sought thermodynamic dynamics: split products grow in
support and no pairwise all-shape exhaustion estimate has been proved.
Taking a supremum over all locally normal states cannot weaken the local
topology, because

\[
 \sup_\rho\operatorname{Tr}(\rho D^*D)=\|D\|^2.                \tag{4.7}
\]

The surviving route must instead use compatible energy-density or
state-tempered families across the exhaustion.

\section{Full-Q3 packet no-go for point-norm continuity}

At one exact eight-component Q3 site let

\[
 h=\sum_j{p_j^2\over2\chi}+V(q),\qquad
 K=h-\inf\sigma(h)+1,\qquad
 \delta={i\over\hbar}[h,\,\cdot\,],                            \tag{5.1}
\]

and put $G=g+3\lambda>0$,

\[
 W_a=e^{-iap_0/\hbar},\quad a\ne0,\quad
 R_0=(i+p_0)^{-1},\quad
 D_a=V(q)-V(q-ae_0),\quad F_0=\partial_0V.                     \tag{5.2}
\]

On Schwartz space the exact first and second derivations are

\[
 \delta W_a={i\over\hbar}D_aW_a,                               \tag{5.3}
\]

\[
 \delta^2W_a={i\over2\chi\hbar}
   \sum_j\{p_j,\partial_jD_a\}W_a-\hbar^{-2}D_a^2W_a,          \tag{5.4}
\]

\[
 \delta R_0=R_0F_0R_0,                                        \tag{5.5}
\]

\[
 \delta^2R_0=2R_0F_0R_0F_0R_0
 +{1\over2\chi}R_0\sum_j\{p_j,\partial_jF_0\}R_0.           \tag{5.6}
\]

The full Q3 force has leading axis coefficient $Gq_0^3$.  Global polynomial
ellipticity with anisotropic degrees one for $q$ and two for $p$ gives the
closed graph endpoint

\[
 \|\delta^2(A)K^{-3/2}\|<\infty,\qquad A\in\{W_a,R_0\}.       \tag{5.7}
\]

The onsite propagator preserves Schwartz space, so (5.3)--(5.7) support a
second-order vector Taylor formula on a common invariant core and then by
graph closure.  For a normalized Schwartz vector $\psi$, translate along the
Q3 axis by

\[
 \psi_R=e^{-iRp_0/\hbar}\psi.                                  \tag{5.8}
\]

The exact leading limits are

\[
 R^{-3}\|\delta W_a\psi_R\|\longrightarrow{|a|G\over\hbar},
 \qquad
 R^{-3}\|\delta R_0\psi_R\|\longrightarrow
 G\|R_0^2\psi\|>0,                                            \tag{5.9}
\]

while

\[
 \|K^{3/2}\psi_R\|=O(R^6).                                    \tag{5.10}
\]

Set $t_R=\tau R^{-3}$.  With

\[
 d_{W_a}={|a|G\over\hbar},\qquad d_{R_0}=G\|R_0^2\psi\|,
\quad C_A=\|\delta^2(A)K^{-3/2}\|,
\]

and

\[
 M_\psi=\limsup_{R\to\infty}R^{-6}\|K^{3/2}\psi_R\|<\infty,
\]

the Taylor remainder yields

\[
 \liminf_{R\to\infty}
 \|(\alpha_{\tau R^{-3}}(A)-A)\psi_R\|
 \ge \tau d_A-{\tau^2\over2}C_AM_\psi.                       \tag{5.11}
\]

Choose one fixed sufficiently small $\tau>0$ for each label.  The right-hand
side is positive, hence

\[
 \liminf_{R\to\infty}
 \|\alpha_{\tau R^{-3}}(A)-A\|>0.                              \tag{5.12}
\]

Since $\tau R^{-3}\to0$, any invariant concrete C-star algebra containing
either label fails point-norm $C_0$ continuity for the unsplit flow.  Equation
(5.12) proves only a positive discontinuity.  The full-flow norm jump is not
an exact jump of one or two.  Invariance of the full unsplit resolvent algebra
remains open.

\section{Pure quartic kick and resolvent-algebra scope}

The pure potential subflow has a stronger exact boundary.  Along one Q3 axis,

\[
 W_4(re_0)={G\over4}r^4,\qquad
 U_t=e^{itW_4(Q)/\hbar},\qquad
 R_\mu=(i\mu-p_0)^{-1},\qquad A_t=U_tR_\mu U_t^*.              \tag{6.1}
\]

For the configuration translation $S_s=e^{-isp_0/\hbar}$, a normalized
translated-dilated packet of width $R^{1/2}$ has momentum spread
$O(R^{-1/2})$.  After multiplication by the relative quartic phase, its
momentum center is

\[
 k_R=3tGsR^2+O(R),\qquad
 \|(p_0-k_R)\chi_R\|=O(R^{3/2})=o(|k_R|).                     \tag{6.2}
\]

One resolvent action tends to $(i\mu)^{-1}$ and the other to zero.  The
Cayley-transform upper bound matches the packet lower bound, so for every
nonzero $s,t$,

\[
 \|S_sA_tS_s^*-A_t\|={1\over|\mu|}.                            \tag{6.3}
\]

Elements of the finite-dimensional field/resolvent algebra have
norm-continuous Weyl-conjugation orbits on finite-dimensional phase-space
subspaces; see Georgescu--Iftimovici, Proposition 2.38(2),
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10026}.  Therefore

\[
 U_tR_\mu U_t^*\notin\mathcal R(\mathbb R^{16},\sigma),
 \qquad t\ne0.                                                  \tag{6.4}
\]

This resolvent algebra is unital, so its multiplier strict topology equals
its norm topology.  Strict continuity cannot repair the pure-kick route.
Equation (6.4) blocks a Trotter construction that requires the kinetic and
pure quartic subflows separately to be internal automorphisms.  It neither
proves nor disproves invariance under the unsplit kinetic-plus-quartic flow.

\section{Two-sided fixed-Gibbs character entropy}

Let $\rho=Z^{-1}e^{-\beta H}$ be one exact finite-volume Q3LOCK Gibbs density
and let $A=W_\xi=e^{i\xi\cdot q}$.  The exact momentum shift is

\[
 A^*HA-H={\hbar\over\chi}\xi\cdot p
          +{\hbar^2\over2\chi}\|\xi\|^2.                     \tag{7.1}
\]

The real Hamiltonian is time-reversal invariant, so the Gibbs momentum mean
vanishes.  Unitary covariance of logarithms then gives both orientations,

\[
 S(A\rho A^*\Vert\rho)=S(A^*\rho A\Vert\rho)
 ={\beta\hbar^2\over2\chi}\|\xi\|^2=:S_\xi.                  \tag{7.2}
\]

Gibbs invariance makes (7.2) valid also for $A_t=\alpha_t(A)$.  For a
coordinate-tail projection $E$, put

\[
 p=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho E),\quad
 q_+(t)=\operatorname{Tr}(A_t\rho A_t^*E),\quad
 q_-(t)=\operatorname{Tr}(A_t^*\rho A_tE).                     \tag{7.3}
\]

Binary data processing gives

\[
 S_\xi\ge d_{\rm bin}(q_\pm(t)\Vert p)
 \ge q_\pm(t)\log(1/p)-\log2,                                 \tag{7.4}
\]

and hence

\[
 q_\pm(t)\le\min\left\{1,{S_\xi+\log2\over\log(1/p)}\right\}.
                                                                    \tag{7.5}
\]

If the exact coordinate-tail theorem supplies

\[
 p\le M_a|S|e^{-aL^2},\qquad aL^2>\log(M_a|S|),                \tag{7.6}
\]

then

\[
 q_\pm(t)\le\min\left\{1,
 {S_\xi+\log2\over aL^2-\log(M_a|S|)}\right\}.                \tag{7.7}
\]

This is an exact finite-Gibbs, evolved-character, two-orientation theorem.
Under (7.6) it is only $O(L^{-2})$, not a Gaussian history tail.

\section{Entropy and finite moments do not force a Gaussian tail}

For $n\ge2$ and any fixed integer $m\ge3$ on $\mathbb C^2$, let

\[
 q_n=nP_1,\qquad H_n={n^4\over\beta}P_1,\qquad
 \rho_n={\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{-n^4})\over1+e^{-n^4}}.      \tag{8.1}
\]

Write $\Delta_n=p_{0,n}-p_{1,n}=\tanh(n^4/2)$ and choose

\[
 \sin^2\theta_{n,m}={1\over\Delta_n n^{2m}},\qquad
 U_{n,m}^\pm=e^{\mp i\theta_{n,m}\sigma_y},\qquad
 \rho_{n,m}^\pm=U_{n,m}^\pm\rho_nU_{n,m}^{\pm*}.              \tag{8.2}
\]

Exact two-by-two arithmetic gives, for both signs,

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{n,m}^\pm P_1)=p_{1,n}+n^{-2m},        \tag{8.3}
\]

\[
 S(\rho_{n,m}^\pm\Vert\rho_n)=n^{4-2m},\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}[(\rho_{n,m}^\pm-\rho_n)H_n]
 ={n^{4-2m}\over\beta},                                      \tag{8.4}
\]

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{n,m}^\pm q_n^r)
 =n^rp_{1,n}+n^{r-2m},\qquad 0<r\le2m.                        \tag{8.5}
\]

For $r=0$ the moment is exactly one.  The reference Gibbs state has every
Gaussian coefficient,

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_ne^{a q_n^2})\le1+e^{a^2/4},
 \qquad a>0.                                                    \tag{8.6}
\]

For any fixed $T>0$, the two rotations are exact time-$T$ flows of the
bounded auxiliary drives

\[
 K_{n,m}^\pm=\pm{\hbar\theta_{n,m}\over T}\sigma_y.           \tag{8.7}
\]

At $m=4$ the tail is exactly $p_{1,n}+n^{-8}$, the relative entropy is
$n^{-4}$, the energy excess is $1/(\beta n^4)$, and

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{n,4}^\pm q_n^8)
 =1+n^8p_{1,n}\le1+4e^{-2}.                                   \tag{8.8}
\]

More generally, after any finite moment ceiling is preregistered, one can fix
$m$ above it.  A genuine Gibbs reference, all-coefficient Gaussian reference
tails, vanishing entropy and energy excess, both orientations, and all the
requested finite tilted moments still allow a polynomial tail.  This fixture
rejects that inference only.  Its auxiliary drive is not the equilibrium
flow of $H_n$, and it does not rule out a stronger Q3LOCK orbit
quasi-invariance estimate.

\section{The remaining history corridor}

For the bounded coordinate cutoff, the safe interaction corridor is

\[
 B_{R,L}(T)=8\sqrt2|X|\|A\|e^{\nu_LT}
 { (\nu_LT)^R\over R!},\qquad
 \nu_L\le{96cL^2\over\hbar}.                                  \tag{9.1}
\]

Choosing $L=R^\alpha$ makes (9.1) vanish on compact time intervals for
$0<\alpha<1/2$, but the estimate pays $e^{CL^2}$.  Neither the
inverse-logarithmic bound (7.7) nor any fixed finite-moment version of
(8.3)--(8.8) absorbs that loss.  A Trotter telescope requires uniform control
for every partial history $P$ and its adjoint history,

\[
 \phi_0(P^*EP)+\phi_0(PEP^*),                                  \tag{9.2}
\]

together with the corresponding $(V-V_L)^2$-weighted expressions.  Static
tails and one-step energy control do not prove (9.2).  The next dynamics gate
must establish a compatible two-orientation history estimate across all
exhaustions, or replace the corridor with a state-weighted all-bond
resummation that avoids its exponential loss.

\section{Ordered ground states do not imply a GNS gap}

Let

\[
 \mathcal K_0=\mathbb C\Omega\oplus L^2((0,1),dx),\qquad
 h_0\Omega=0,\qquad (h_0f)(x)=xf(x),                            \tag{10.1}
\]

and define

\[
 \mathcal A=B(\mathcal K_0)\oplus B(\mathcal K_0),\qquad
 \alpha_t=\operatorname{Ad}(e^{ith_0/\hbar})
            \oplus\operatorname{Ad}(e^{ith_0/\hbar}).          \tag{10.2}
\]

Because $h_0$ is bounded with norm one, this is a point-norm $C_0$ C-star
dynamics.  The two component vector states

\[
 \omega_+(A\oplus B)=\langle\Omega,A\Omega\rangle,
 \qquad
 \omega_-(A\oplus B)=\langle\Omega,B\Omega\rangle             \tag{10.3}
\]

are pure, disjoint, exact ground states.  Summand-swap parity exchanges them,
the central order observable $Z=(1,-1)$ separates them, and each GNS ground
vector is simple.  Nevertheless

\[
 \sigma(h_0)=\{0\}\cup[0,1],\qquad
 \inf(\sigma(h_0)\setminus\{0\})=0.                            \tag{10.4}
\]

The positive implementing spectrum accumulates at zero.  With the repository
convention $\delta=(i/\hbar)[H,\,\cdot\,]$, a genuine sector gap requires an
independent coercive estimate on a dense invariant core,

\[
 -i\hbar\,\omega(A^*\delta(A))
 \ge\Delta\{\omega(A^*A)-|\omega(A)|^2\},\qquad \Delta>0,      \tag{10.5}
\]

separately in both selected broken-sector GNS representations.  Neither
distinct order nor the v1.6 ground-state construction proves (10.5).

\item \textbf{UPHELD as a boundary:} the two-level auxiliary rotations are
