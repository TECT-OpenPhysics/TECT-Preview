\section{Purpose and scope}
Keep the full original EXCH-001 Hamiltonian, the side-16 periodic
three-torus, the dimensionless canonical time $\tau$, and exact
rectangular Fourier projections $P_K,P_{2K}$. For one common
$(\Psi_0,\chi_0)\in H^2(\mathbb T^3;\mathbb C^3)
\times L^\infty(\mathbb T^3;\mathbb C)$ take initial data
$(P_K\Psi_0,P_{2K}\chi_0)$ as in R-584, with no normalization after
projection. Use Lebesgue measure for energy and its real $L^2$
pairing. Equivalently divide both by the same fixed volume.

For every finite $T>0$, every sequence $K\to\infty$ has a subsequence
on $I=[-T,T]$ such that $\Psi_K\to\Psi$ in $C(I;H^{15/8})$ and
$\chi_K\to\chi$ in $C(I;L^4)$. In that subsequence, the exact
projected Class-II variation satisfies
\[
 P_K R_{\rm II}(\Psi_K)\longrightarrow R_{\rm II}(\Psi)
                      \quad\text{strongly in } C(I;H^{-1}).
\]
There is no additional Class-II term in the weak equation. The pair
satisfies both original equations in distributions, has the common
initial data, and preserves the original total charge. This is
subsequence identification, not a unique limiting evolution.

\section{Exact variational input and time control}
Use the manifest's \emph{proposed reference functional}, not its
declared comparison energy or nonvariational executed residual.
Realify $\Psi$ as $z\in\mathbb R^6$ and its Hermitian generators as
real symmetric matrices $A_A$. For $d_\rho,d_M>0$ the pinned floors,
let $q_A=z^TA_Az/(|z|^2+d_\rho)$, $p_A=2A_Az$ and
$s_A=2(A_A-q_AI)z$. The density is
\[
 \tfrac12\sum_j(\partial_j z)^TB(z)\partial_j z,\qquad
 B=\sum_A\{a p_Ap_A^T+b(p_As_A^T+s_Ap_A^T)+c s_As_A^T\},
\]
where $(a,b,c)=(c_{JJ}\alpha_X^2,c_{JK}\alpha_X\beta_X,
c_{KK}\beta_X^2)/(M_X^2+d_M)$. This is A2 v2.0
Eqs.\ (4.1)--(4.3) as a \emph{static} identity; no A2 gradient-flow
theorem is used. In particular the mixed term is retained.
For a real test $h$ the exact first variation is
\[
 \langle R_{\rm II}(z),h\rangle
 =\sum_j\int (B(z)\partial_jz)\cdot\partial_jh
   +\tfrac12(\partial_jz)^T DB(z)[h]\,\partial_jz .
\]
The $\tfrac12 DB$ term includes differentiation through the density
denominator; neither it nor either floor is dropped.

\section{Strong coefficients from the Hamiltonian equation}
R-584 gives the cutoff-independent bounds on $\Psi_K$ in $H^2$
and on $\chi_K$ in $L^4$ from the R-158 static identity and
R-578 finite conservation. R-584 also gives relative compactness
of the reservoirs in $C(I;L^4)$ on this exact preparation.
The real canonical equations, written in complex notation, are
\[
 i\dot\Psi_K=P_K\bigl(R_{\rm P1}(\Psi_K)-2\epsilon\chi_K\bar\Psi_K\bigr),
 \qquad
 i\dot\chi_K=P_{2K}\bigl((\omega+u|\chi_K|^2)\chi_K
                            -\epsilon\Psi_K^T\Psi_K\bigr).
\]
Here $R_{\rm P1}$ is the real gradient, so there is no extra factor
of two from the Wirtinger convention. The $-2\epsilon$ in the
order equation is the variation of the quadratic pair source.

The fourth-order linear term maps $H^2$ to $H^{-2}$. The potential
gradient and the exchange term are bounded in $L^2$ using the
uniform $H^2\hookrightarrow L^\infty$ bound and finite volume.
For the Class-II term the variation above is uniformly bounded
in $H^{-1}$: on the bounded amplitude ball $B,DB$ are bounded,
$\nabla z_K$ is bounded in $L^2\cap L^4$, and the two factors are
paired with $\nabla h$ and $h$ in $L^2$. Orthogonal Fourier
projections contract $H^{-2}$ and commute with multiplication by
the constant $i$. Hence $\|\dot\Psi_K\|_{H^{-2}}\le C$,
without any estimate for $\dot\Psi_K$ in $L^2$.

For $-2<s<2$ interpolation bounds the $H^s$ time increment by
$C|\tau-\sigma|^{(2-s)/4}$, since the $H^{-2}$ increment is
$O(|\tau-\sigma|)$ and the $H^2$ increment is uniformly bounded.
The spatial $H^2$ ball is relatively compact in $H^s$ on the fixed
torus. Arzela--Ascoli at $s=15/8$ and then R-584 give the two
strong convergences on the same subsequence. The limit has bounded
$H^2$ norm at each time by weak lower semicontinuity.

Indeed $s>7/4$ gives both $C(I;L^\infty)$ convergence of $z_K$
and $C(I;L^4)$ convergence of its gradients. On the common
amplitude ball, $B,DB$ are Lipschitz, so the flux and the
quadratic-gradient coefficient of $h$ in the first variation
converge strongly in $C(I;L^2)$. For the latter use
$\|a_K b_K-ab\|_2\le\|a_K-a\|_4\|b_K\|_4+
\|a\|_4\|b_K-b\|_4$. Pairing with the unit $H^1$ test ball proves
strong $C(I;H^{-1})$ convergence of $R_{\rm II}$ itself.
Returning $P_K$ does not change the limit: it contracts $H^{-1}$
and converges uniformly to the identity on the compact range
$\{R_{\rm II}(\Psi(\tau)):\tau\in I\}$ by a finite-net argument.

\section{Devil's-advocate checks and weak equations}
With trigonometric spatial tests the projections on the tests
are eventually identities. The order linear term can be moved
onto those tests (or passed weakly in $H^{-2}$). The local terms
pass by the strong convergence of both fields. In particular the
reservoir cubic is continuous from $L^4$ to $L^{4/3}$:
the difference is bounded by a constant times the squared
$L^4$ norms times the $L^4$ difference. Density of trigonometric
tests then gives the full coupled distributional identities,
using smooth compactly time-supported tests. Strong continuity
and the original initial projections give the initial traces.
Strong $C(I;L^2)$ convergence of both fields transfers the exact
conserved charge $Q=\|\Psi\|_2^2/2+\|\chi\|_2^2$ in the Lebesgue
normalization. This does not require projected charges to be
identical at different $K$.

\begin{itemize}
\item The main Class-II proof uses the real matrix form. A separate
derivation differentiates the two original currents
$J_A=\nabla m_A$ and $K_A=J_A-q_A\nabla\rho$ by the quotient rule,
then passes each pairing by strong gradient convergence.
\item These are the pinned positive-floor and zero shell-bias
sources. The proof neither removes floors nor uses the finite-grid
proxy or borrows smoothing from the parabolic A2 time law.
\item Strong convergence below $H^2$ is enough for the first-order
products, but not for strong convergence of $\Delta\Psi_K$. In
particular no full energy equality is inferred from the weak
equation. No uniqueness, full-sequence convergence or group law
is inferred from having identified all subsequential equations.
\item The reservoir cubic uses R-584's strong compactness, not
just an $L^4$ bound. Unbounded $L^4$ initial reservoirs are not
covered, and the different-preparation EXCH-008 result is unchanged.
\end{itemize}
The exact audit checks source hashes, gradient, cross term, floor
derivatives, Hamiltonian factors and interpolation exponents.
The independent convergence and hostile scope checks are analytic,
not a numerical approximation or a new Lean formalization.
