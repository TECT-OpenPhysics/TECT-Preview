\section*{1. Purpose and scope}
This note constructs the non-perturbative finite-volume Gibbs measure for the
real scalar Brazovskii slice.  It is deliberately a separate claim from the
perturbative A3 correlator theorem and from the P3 PDE-discretization theorem.
The torus is
\[
 \mathbb T^3=[0,L)^3,\qquad L=16,\qquad |\mathbb T^3|=L^3.       \tag{1.1}
\]
Let $\alpha=2\pi/L$, let $P_N$ retain the Fourier modes
$n\in\mathbb Z^3$ with $|n|_\infty\leq N$, and define
\[
 K_n=m_{\rm sh}^2+Y\bigl(\alpha^2|n|^2-q_0^2\bigr)^2,
 \qquad C_n=K_n^{-1}.                                      \tag{1.2}
\]
The hypotheses are
\[
 m_{\rm sh}^2>0,\qquad Y>0,\qquad q_0\geq0,
 \qquad \lambda\in\mathbb R,\qquad \gamma>0.              \tag{1.3}
\]
For a real field $\phi$, put
\[
 V(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb T^3}
 \left(\frac{\lambda}{4}\phi^4+
       \frac{\gamma}{6}\phi^6\right)dx.                   \tag{1.4}
\]

\paragraph{Pinned-scope theorem.}
Let $\mu_0$ be the centred real Gaussian measure with covariance $C=K^{-1}$.
The covariance is trace class on real $L^2(\mathbb T^3)$.  The Galerkin laws
\[
 d\nu_N(\phi_N)=Z_N^{-1}e^{-V(\phi_N)}d\mu_{0,N}(\phi_N),
 \qquad \mu_{0,N}=(P_N)_\#\mu_0,                            \tag{1.5}
\]
are normalizable.  There is a probability measure
\[
 d\nu(\phi)=Z^{-1}e^{-V(\phi)}d\mu_0(\phi)                 \tag{1.6}
\]
such that the full sequence $\nu_N$ converges weakly to $\nu$ on real $L^2$.
The lifted densities on the common Gaussian space converge in $L^1(\mu_0)$,
equivalently in total variation, and every finite-degree smeared cylinder
polynomial has a convergent expectation.  This is cutoff removal for the
declared spectral regulator; it is not an infinite-volume limit.

\section{Trace class and Gaussian regularity}
On the max-norm shell $|n|_\infty=m$ there are exactly
\[
 (2m+1)^3-(2m-1)^3=24m^2+2                              \tag{2.1}
\]
modes.  If $m\geq m_0:=\lceil\sqrt2q_0/\alpha\rceil$, then
$\alpha^2|n|^2\geq2q_0^2$ and hence
\[
 K_n\geq \frac{Y}{4}\alpha^4m^4.                         \tag{2.2}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sum_{|n|_\infty\geq M}C_n
 \leq\frac{4}{Y\alpha^4}
 \sum_{m=M}^\infty\left(24m^{-2}+2m^{-4}\right)<\infty.  \tag{2.3}
\]
The finitely many inner modes are harmless because $K_n\geq m_{\rm sh}^2$.
Thus $C$ is trace class and $\mu_0$ is a Radon Gaussian probability measure
on real $L^2$.

With the $H^s$ weight, the shell summand is bounded by a constant times
$m^{2s-2}$.  It is summable for every $s<1/2$; negative $s$ already follows
from $L^2$ support.  Conversely, $K_n\leq c(1+|n|^4)$ at large $|n|$, so the
weighted covariance trace has the harmonic lower power at $s=1/2$.  Hence the
covariance is trace class on $H^s$ for $s<1/2$, but not on $H^{1/2}$.  This is
the sharp Gaussian Hilbert-space threshold used here.

The low Sobolev exponent does not obstruct the local polynomial.  For the
common Gaussian Fourier series, the tail
$X_N=(I-P_N)\phi$ has an $x$-independent variance
\[
 t_N=|\mathbb T^3|^{-1}\sum_{|n|_\infty>N}C_n\longrightarrow0. \tag{2.4}
\]
Since a centred real Gaussian $X$ satisfies $\mathbb E X^6=15(\mathbb E
X^2)^3$,
\[
 \mathbb E\int_{\mathbb T^3}|X_N(x)|^6dx
 =15|\mathbb T^3|t_N^3\longrightarrow0.                   \tag{2.5}
\]
Therefore $P_N\phi\to\phi$ in
$L^6(\Omega\times\mathbb T^3)$.  In particular, $\phi\in L^6$ almost surely
and (1.4) is a finite random variable.  This direct Gaussian-moment argument,
not an invalid $H^{1/2-}\to L^6$ Sobolev embedding, defines the interaction.

\section{Stability and partition functions}
Write $a=\max(-\lambda,0)$.  For $x=\phi^2\geq0$, the possibly negative part
of the potential is minimized at $x=a/\gamma$.  Direct substitution gives the
sharp pointwise bound
\[
 \frac{\lambda}{4}\phi^4+\frac{\gamma}{6}\phi^6
 \geq -\frac{a^3}{12\gamma^2}=-c_*.                       \tag{3.1}
\]
It follows immediately that
\[
 e^{-V(P_N\phi)}\leq e^{c_*|\mathbb T^3|},
 \qquad Z_N\leq e^{c_*|\mathbb T^3|}.                     \tag{3.2}
\]

Let
\[
 \sigma_*^2=|\mathbb T^3|^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}C.         \tag{3.3}
\]
Translation invariance and the real Gaussian moments
$\mathbb E X^4=3\sigma^4$, $\mathbb E X^6=15\sigma^6$ give
\[
 \mathbb E V(P_N\phi)
 \leq |\mathbb T^3|
 \left(\frac{3\lambda_+}{4}\sigma_*^4+
       \frac{5\gamma}{2}\sigma_*^6\right)=B_*<\infty.   \tag{3.4}
\]
Jensen's inequality has the required direction:
\[
 Z_N=\mathbb E e^{-V(P_N\phi)}
 \geq e^{-\mathbb E V(P_N\phi)}\geq e^{-B_*}>0.           \tag{3.5}
\]
Equations (3.2) and (3.5) uniformly enclose every Galerkin partition function.

\section{Full-sequence regulator removal}
The elementary polynomial difference inequality, Holder's inequality on
$\Omega\times\mathbb T^3$, and (2.5) yield
\[
 \mathbb E|V(P_N\phi)-V(\phi)|\longrightarrow0.            \tag{4.1}
\]
For example, the sextic term is bounded by a constant times
\[
 \bigl(\|P_N\phi\|_{L^6}^5+\|\phi\|_{L^6}^5\bigr)
 \|P_N\phi-\phi\|_{L^6},                                  \tag{4.2}
\]
and the quartic term is easier on the finite product space.  Thus
$V(P_N\phi)\to V(\phi)$ in probability.  By (3.1), all weights are bounded by
the same deterministic constant.  Bounded convergence in probability gives
\[
 w_N:=e^{-V(P_N\phi)}\longrightarrow w:=e^{-V(\phi)}
 \quad\hbox{in }L^1(\mu_0).                                \tag{4.3}
\]
Hence $Z_N\to Z=\mathbb Ew$.  The limit is strictly positive because $w>0$
almost surely; equivalently, (3.5) passes to the limit.

Define the lifted probability measure
\[
 d\widetilde\nu_N=Z_N^{-1}w_Nd\mu_0.                      \tag{4.4}
\]
Normalization and (4.3) imply
\[
 \left\|\frac{w_N}{Z_N}-\frac{w}{Z}\right\|_{L^1(\mu_0)}
 \longrightarrow0,                                        \tag{4.5}
\]
which is total-variation convergence of the lifted laws.  The actual
finite-dimensional law is $(P_N)_\#\widetilde\nu_N=\nu_N$.  For every bounded
continuous $F$ on $L^2$, $P_N\phi\to\phi$ almost surely and the normalized
densities are uniformly bounded by (3.2)--(3.5).  Therefore
\[
 \int F(\phi_N)d\nu_N
 =\int F(P_N\phi)d\widetilde\nu_N
 \longrightarrow\int F(\phi)d\nu.                         \tag{4.6}
\]
This identifies the full sequence, so no unnamed subsequence or uniqueness
assumption is hidden in the cutoff removal.  Weak convergence also supplies
tightness on the Polish space $L^2$.

The total-variation statement in (4.5) is only for the common-Gaussian lifted
laws.  The embedded finite-dimensional laws have different supports and are
not claimed to converge in total variation.

\section{Declared correlations}
Let $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r$ be continuous real linear functionals on $L^2$ and
let $Q$ be a finite-degree polynomial.  The observable
\[
 \mathcal O(\phi)=Q(\ell_1(\phi),\ldots,\ell_r(\phi))       \tag{5.1}
\]
has Gaussian moments of every order.  The uniform density bound from
(3.2)--(3.5) transfers uniform integrability to $\widetilde\nu_N$.  Since
$\ell_j(P_N\phi)\to\ell_j(\phi)$ in every finite Gaussian moment,
\[
 \int Q(\ell_1(\phi_N),\ldots,\ell_r(\phi_N))d\nu_N
 \longrightarrow \int\mathcal O(\phi)d\nu.                \tag{5.2}
\]
Thus smeared cylinder correlations have regulator removal.  Unsmeared
coincident-point products, composite-operator renormalization, and infinite-
volume correlation decay are not statements of this theorem.

\section{Devil's-advocate review}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Trace class alone defines the local sextic interaction.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  Equation (2.5) separately proves Gaussian $L^6$
convergence and almost-sure integrability.
\item \textbf{$H^{1/2-}$ embeds into $L^6$ in three dimensions.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  No such embedding is used; the pointwise Gaussian
sixth-moment identity is the load-bearing route.
\item \textbf{A negative quartic makes the Gibbs weight unbounded.}
\textbf{DISMISSED under the stated hypotheses.}  The positive sextic gives
the exact global lower bound (3.1), hence the uniform weight bound (3.2).
\item \textbf{Jensen's inequality could have been used in the wrong
direction.} \textbf{DISMISSED.}  The convex function is $e^{-x}$, giving
$\mathbb E e^{-V}\geq e^{-\mathbb EV}$ as displayed in (3.5).
\item \textbf{Tightness only gives an unidentified subsequence.}
\textbf{UPHELD as insufficient by itself.}  The $L^1$ density limit (4.5)
identifies the full sequence before tightness is cited.
\item \textbf{The finite-dimensional measures converge in total variation
after zero-extension.} \textbf{UPHELD as false.}  Only the lifted laws converge
in total variation; the actual Galerkin laws converge weakly on $L^2$.
\par\smallskip
\item \textbf{Every local or composite correlation is covered.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  Section 5 is restricted to finite-degree smeared
cylinder polynomials.
\item \textbf{The same proof covers derivative Class-II terms.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  Typical fields have only the covariance regularity
of Section 2; derivative-current squares require a separate definition and
possibly renormalization.
\item \textbf{Finite volume establishes a phase transition.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  No thermodynamic limit, symmetry breaking, or BCC
selection is included.
\item \textbf{Large finite constants are practical error bars.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  The bounds certify existence and convergence, not
useful cutoff sizes or Monte Carlo efficiency.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Falsifier and no-overclaim boundary}
The claim is falsified by a non-trace-class covariance in the declared
three-dimensional positive-mass $q^4$ scope; failure of (2.5), (3.1), or the
uniform partition bounds; a failure of $L^1$ weight convergence; two Galerkin
subsequences with different weak limits; a declared smeared cylinder
correlation that does not converge; or failure of the 31-assertion verifier.

This result does not construct the full three-complex-component derivative
Class-II Gibbs measure.  It also excludes $\eta_{\rm shell}\ne0$, zero or
negative shell mass, infinite volume, phase transition, a genuine finite-
difference Route B limit, BCC existence or selection, and the future P5
Sector-A synthesis.  Independent operator execution is required before the
separate T6 conditional-theorem review.
