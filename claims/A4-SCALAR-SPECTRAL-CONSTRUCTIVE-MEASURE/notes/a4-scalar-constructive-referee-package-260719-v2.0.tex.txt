\section*{1. Purpose and scope}

This package gives a single-file proof and reproduction map for the
finite-volume real-scalar constructive measure in Sector A.  It consolidates
the previously approved analytic proof and scoped T6 enactment without adding
a theorem or enlarging the scope.  The torus, regulator, covariance,
interaction, topology of convergence, and admissible observables are all fixed
below so that the result can be attacked without consulting a sibling note.

The underlying theorem was promoted to T6 after Jusang Lee independently ran
the hash-bound verifier on 2026-07-18.  The current document is the exact entry
candidate for the eventual reproduction bundle.  Repository policy requires
the operator to confirm this integrated document before packaging; until that
confirmation, no A4 PUBLISHED bundle is claimed.

The result is deliberately scalar.  It is not a construction of the full
three-complex-component derivative Class-II measure and it proves neither an
infinite-volume phase transition nor BCC existence or selection.

\section{Definitions and theorem}

Let
\[
 \mathbb T_L^3=[0,L)^3,\qquad L=16,\qquad
 \alpha=\frac{2\pi}{L},\qquad |\mathbb T_L^3|=L^3.       \tag{2.1}
\]
Use the orthonormal real Fourier representation, with the usual conjugacy
constraint, and let $P_N$ retain precisely the modes
$n\in\mathbb Z^3$ satisfying $|n|_\infty\leq N$.  For
\[
 m_{\rm sh}^2>0,\qquad Y>0,\qquad q_0\geq0,              \tag{2.2}
\]
define
\[
 K_n=m_{\rm sh}^2+Y\bigl(\alpha^2|n|^2-q_0^2\bigr)^2,
 \qquad C_n=K_n^{-1}.                                   \tag{2.3}
\]
Let $\mu_0$ be the centred real Gaussian probability measure with covariance
$C=K^{-1}$.  Its projected law is $\mu_{0,N}=(P_N)_\#\mu_0$.

For $\lambda\in\mathbb R$ and $\gamma>0$, set
\[
 V(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb T_L^3}
 \left(\frac{\lambda}{4}\phi(x)^4+
       \frac{\gamma}{6}\phi(x)^6\right)dx.             \tag{2.4}
\]
The finite-dimensional Galerkin Gibbs law is
\[
 d\nu_N(\phi_N)=Z_N^{-1}e^{-V(\phi_N)}d\mu_{0,N}(\phi_N).
                                                               \tag{2.5}
\]

\paragraph{Theorem (finite-volume scalar spectral constructive measure).}
Under (2.2) and $\lambda\in\mathbb R$, $\gamma>0$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $C=K^{-1}$ is trace class on real $L^2(\mathbb T_L^3)$ and defines a
Radon Gaussian probability measure $\mu_0$;
\item $V(\phi)$ is finite for $\mu_0$-almost every $\phi$, every $Z_N$ is
finite and strictly positive, and the bounds are uniform in $N$;
\item with
\[
 d\nu(\phi)=Z^{-1}e^{-V(\phi)}d\mu_0(\phi),              \tag{2.6}
\]
the full sequence $\nu_N$ converges weakly to $\nu$ on real $L^2$;
\item on the common Gaussian space, the lifted normalized densities converge
in $L^1(\mu_0)$, equivalently in total variation; and
\item expectations of every finite-degree polynomial in finitely many
continuous real linear functionals on $L^2$ converge.
\end{enumerate}
This is a T6 conditional theorem under the named hypotheses
\texttt{A1-SHELL-POSITIVITY} and
\texttt{A2-H2-SEXTIC-COERCIVITY}.  The hard analytic dependency is
\texttt{A1-SCALAR-ANALYTIC-BRANCH}.

\section{Trace class and the sharp Gaussian Hilbert threshold}

The number of lattice points on the max-norm shell $|n|_\infty=m$ is exactly
\[
 (2m+1)^3-(2m-1)^3=24m^2+2.                              \tag{3.1}
\]
Let $m_0=\lceil\sqrt2q_0/\alpha\rceil$.  If $m\geq m_0$ and
$|n|_\infty=m$, then $\alpha^2|n|^2\geq2q_0^2$ and
\[
 K_n\geq \frac{Y}{4}\alpha^4m^4.                        \tag{3.2}
\]
Therefore, for $M\geq m_0$,
\[
 \sum_{|n|_\infty\geq M}C_n
 \leq\frac{4}{Y\alpha^4}
 \sum_{m=M}^\infty\left(24m^{-2}+2m^{-4}\right)<\infty. \tag{3.3}
\]
The inner modes are finite in number and $K_n\geq m_{\rm sh}^2$, so
$\operatorname{Tr}C<\infty$.  Hence $\mu_0$ is a Radon Gaussian measure on
$L^2$.

With the $H^s$ weight, the shell contribution is asymptotic to
$m^{2s-2}$.  Thus the covariance is trace class on $H^s$ for every
$s<1/2$.  Conversely, the upper estimate $K_n\leq c(1+|n|^4)$ at large
$|n|$ gives the harmonic lower behaviour at $s=1/2$, so the threshold is
sharp.  This regularity fact is used as a warning, not as a Sobolev route to
the local sextic term.

\section{The local interaction: direct Gaussian $L^6$ control}

Let $X_N=(I-P_N)\phi$.  Translation invariance gives the pointwise variance
\[
 t_N=|\mathbb T_L^3|^{-1}
     \sum_{|n|_\infty>N}C_n\longrightarrow0.             \tag{4.1}
\]
For a centred real Gaussian random variable $X$,
$\mathbb E X^6=15(\mathbb E X^2)^3$.  Tonelli's theorem therefore yields
\[
 \mathbb E\int_{\mathbb T_L^3}|X_N(x)|^6dx
 =15|\mathbb T_L^3|t_N^3\longrightarrow0.                \tag{4.2}
\]
Consequently $P_N\phi\to\phi$ in
$L^6(\Omega\times\mathbb T_L^3)$, $\phi\in L^6$ almost surely, and the
quartic and sextic integrals in (2.4) are finite.  This is the load-bearing
construction of $V$; the false embedding $H^{1/2-}\hookrightarrow L^6$ in
three dimensions is never used.

The polynomial difference identity and Holder's inequality on the finite
measure product space imply
\[
 \mathbb E|V(P_N\phi)-V(\phi)|\longrightarrow0.           \tag{4.3}
\]
For the sextic part one uses
\[
 |a^6-b^6|\leq6(|a|^5+|b|^5)|a-b|                       \tag{4.4}
\]
with the $L^6$ convergence and uniform Gaussian $L^6$ norms; the quartic
term follows identically with lower powers.

\section{Stability and uniform partition bounds}

Put $a=\max(-\lambda,0)$ and $x=\phi^2$.  Direct minimization on $x\geq0$
gives
\[
 \frac{\lambda}{4}\phi^4+\frac{\gamma}{6}\phi^6
 \geq-\frac{a^3}{12\gamma^2}=:-c_*.                     \tag{5.1}
\]
Therefore
\[
 0<e^{-V(P_N\phi)}\leq e^{c_*|\mathbb T_L^3|},
 \qquad Z_N\leq e^{c_*|\mathbb T_L^3|}.                 \tag{5.2}
\]

Let $\sigma_*^2=|\mathbb T_L^3|^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}C$ and
$\lambda_+=\max(\lambda,0)$.  The real Gaussian moments
$\mathbb E X^4=3\sigma^4$ and $\mathbb E X^6=15\sigma^6$ give
\[
 \mathbb EV(P_N\phi)
 \leq |\mathbb T_L^3|
 \left(\frac{3\lambda_+}{4}\sigma_*^4+
       \frac{5\gamma}{2}\sigma_*^6\right)=:B_*<\infty. \tag{5.3}
\]
Since $x\mapsto e^{-x}$ is convex, Jensen's inequality has the direction
\[
 Z_N=\mathbb E e^{-V(P_N\phi)}
 \geq e^{-\mathbb EV(P_N\phi)}\geq e^{-B_*}>0.           \tag{5.4}
\]
Equations (5.2)--(5.4) enclose every Galerkin partition function uniformly.

\section{Full-sequence cutoff removal}

Write $w_N=e^{-V(P_N\phi)}$ and $w=e^{-V(\phi)}$.  Equation (4.3) implies
$V(P_N\phi)\to V(\phi)$ in probability.  The deterministic ceiling in
(5.2) makes the weights uniformly bounded, and bounded convergence in
probability gives
\[
 w_N\longrightarrow w\quad\hbox{in }L^1(\mu_0),
 \qquad Z_N=\mathbb Ew_N\longrightarrow Z=\mathbb Ew>0. \tag{6.1}
\]
Define the lifted law
\[
 d\widetilde\nu_N=Z_N^{-1}w_Nd\mu_0.                    \tag{6.2}
\]
Then
\[
 \left\|\frac{w_N}{Z_N}-\frac{w}{Z}\right\|_{L^1(\mu_0)}
 \longrightarrow0.                                      \tag{6.3}
\]
This is total-variation convergence only on the common Gaussian space.

The actual Galerkin law satisfies
$\nu_N=(P_N)_\#\widetilde\nu_N$.  For bounded continuous $F$ on $L^2$,
$P_N\phi\to\phi$ almost surely and (5.2)--(5.4) uniformly bound the
normalized densities.  Combining this fact with (6.3) gives
\[
 \int F(\phi_N)d\nu_N
 =\int F(P_N\phi)d\widetilde\nu_N
 \longrightarrow\int F(\phi)d\nu.                      \tag{6.4}
\]
Thus the full sequence converges weakly; no unnamed subsequence remains.
The embedded finite-dimensional laws have different supports and are not
claimed to converge in total variation.

\section{Declared correlations}

Let $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r$ be continuous real linear functionals on $L^2$
and $Q$ a finite-degree polynomial.  The random variables
$Q(\ell_1(P_N\phi),\ldots,\ell_r(P_N\phi))$ converge in every finite
Gaussian moment to the corresponding unprojected polynomial.  The uniform
density ceiling following from (5.2)--(5.4) transfers uniform integrability
to the lifted Gibbs laws.  Hence
\[
 \int Q(\ell_1(\phi_N),\ldots,\ell_r(\phi_N))d\nu_N
 \longrightarrow
 \int Q(\ell_1(\phi),\ldots,\ell_r(\phi))d\nu.           \tag{7.1}
\]
Unsmeared coincident-point products and composite-operator renormalization
are not included.

\section{Devil's-advocate review}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Trace class alone defines the local sextic.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  The separate Gaussian sixth-moment estimate (4.2)
is load-bearing.
\item \textbf{The proof silently uses $H^{1/2-}\hookrightarrow L^6$.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  That embedding is unavailable in three dimensions;
the proof uses direct $L^6(\Omega\times\mathbb T^3)$ convergence.
\item \textbf{A negative quartic makes the Gibbs weight uncontrollable.}
\textbf{DISMISSED under the hypotheses.}  The positive sextic gives the exact
global lower bound (5.1).
\item \textbf{Jensen's inequality has the wrong direction.}
\textbf{DISMISSED.}  Convexity of $e^{-x}$ gives the lower bound (5.4).
\item \textbf{Tightness identifies only a subsequence.}
\textbf{UPHELD as insufficient by itself.}  The common-space $L^1$ density
limit identifies the full sequence before weak convergence is concluded.
\item \textbf{The finite-dimensional laws converge in total variation after
zero extension.} \textbf{UPHELD as false.}  Total variation is asserted only
for the lifted laws; the projected laws converge weakly.
\item \textbf{Identical deterministic JSON hashes prove a copied operator
artifact.} \textbf{DISMISSED.}  The execution provenance and subprocess
returns are distinct; a non-importing second algorithm supplies diversity.
\item \textbf{The theorem constructs derivative Class-II currents.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  Typical Gaussian regularity does not automatically
define those terms; they require a separate construction or renormalization.
\item \textbf{Finite volume proves a phase transition or BCC selection.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  Neither a thermodynamic limit nor a selection
argument occurs here.
\item \textbf{The large explicit constants are usable numerical error bars.}
\textbf{UPHELD as false.}  They certify integrability and reproduction only.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Falsifier and honesty boundary}

The theorem fails if the covariance trace diverges in the declared positive
$q^4$ scope; the Gaussian $L^6$ tail does not vanish; the exact stability or
uniform partition bounds fail; the common-space densities do not converge in
$L^1$; two cutoff subsequences have different weak limits; a declared smeared
cylinder polynomial fails to converge; a source/evidence hash drifts; or one
of the two executable routes fails.

The theorem excludes the full three-component derivative Class-II Gibbs
measure, $\eta_{\rm shell}\ne0$, zero or negative shell mass, unsmeared
composite operators, infinite volume, phase transition, finite-difference
Route B and regulator independence, BCC existence or selection, T7, and any
claim that the scalar branch shares the full-production parameter anchor.
