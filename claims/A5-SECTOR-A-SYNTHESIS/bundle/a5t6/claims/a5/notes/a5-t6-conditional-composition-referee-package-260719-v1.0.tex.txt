\section*{1. Purpose and scope: exact conditional theorem}
This package asks whether the already published Sector-A results can be
composed into one mathematically exact, branch-aware statement without
silently identifying their parameters or fields.  The answer is the following
conditional theorem.  It is a logical composition theorem, not a new
construction of field dynamics or measures.

\paragraph{Theorem A5-T6.}
On the fixed spectral three-torus with periods $16$, assume the seven named
hypotheses in Section 3 and the six exact, content-addressed premises in
Section 4.  Then both of the following statements hold, simultaneously but on
their explicitly separate branches.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The canonical three-complex-component production functional generates
the unique global $H^2$ gradient flow supplied by A2, including its exact
energy law, continuous dependence, and positive-time smoothing.  On each
declared positive-time solution ball, the restarted exact-Galerkin trajectories
of full-production A3 converge with the residual and trajectory bounds proved
there.
\item For the declared positive real-scalar shell-mass class, scalar A3 has
order-by-order spectral cutoff removal at each fixed perturbative order and
external momentum, while A4 has full-sequence finite-volume sharp-spectral
Gibbs convergence, lifted-density $L^1$/total-variation convergence, and the
declared smeared cylinder-polynomial correlation convergence.
\end{enumerate}

The first item is an implication chain.  The second is a conjunction of two
independent scalar theorems; neither proves the other.  Shared geometry does
not imply equality of shell masses, state spaces, or Gibbs measures.  The
current claim remains T5 until this exact source and PDF are independently
executed and confirmed by the operator.

\[

stored $Z$ values agree within the declared $10^{-9}$ kernel tolerance.  This
is compatibility of theorem statements on one declared geometry.  It is not
equality of field spaces: the full branch uses three complex components and
the scalar branch uses one real field.

\section{Full-production composition lemma}
Let $F$ be the canonical full functional exposed by
\texttt{A2-H3-CANONICAL-PRODUCTION-FUNCTIONAL}.  A2 proves that its gradient
flow has a unique global $H^2$ solution, exact energy dissipation, finite-time
continuous dependence, and smoothing for every $t>0$.  The full-production A3
card names both this functional and A2 as hard dependencies.  Its continuum
trajectory is therefore that same unique A2 solution.

For each A3-declared radius, $0<\tau\leq t\leq T$, and exact nonlinear
projection, application of the A3 theorem gives the recorded solution-ball
residual and trajectory bounds.  This is ordinary implication composition:
\[
 \text{canonical }F \Longrightarrow \text{A2 flow}
 \Longrightarrow \text{A3 exact-Galerkin approximation}.       \tag{6.1}
\]
No new $t=0$ rate follows for merely $H^2$ data, and (6.1) supplies no practical
error bar for historical N32/N64/N128 solver trajectories. \hfill$\square$

\section{Scalar-continuum conjunction lemma}
Under the kernel, shell-positivity, sextic-coercivity, $d=3$ fourth-order
ultraviolet, and infrared-positivity hypotheses, the scalar A3 and A4 theorem
statements both apply to the declared positive-mass real-scalar spectral
family.  Scalar A3 gives fixed-order perturbative amplitudes after cutoff
removal.  A4 separately constructs the full finite-volume Gibbs cutoff limit.

There is no hard dependency arrow between these cards.  Consequently their
valid joint conclusion is the conjunction
\[
 (\text{fixed-order perturbative convergence})\quad\wedge\quad
 (\text{finite-volume constructive convergence}),              \tag{7.1}
\]
not a proof of resummation and not a derivation of A4 from perturbation theory.
The constructive statement covers smeared cylinder-polynomial observables,
not unsmeared composite fields. \hfill$\square$
