\section*{2. Functional and theorem}

Let $\rho=\Psi^\dagger\Psi$, let $T_A$ be the three P1 Hermitian generators, and define
\[
 m_A=\Psi^\dagger T_A\Psi,\qquad J_A=\nabla m_A,\qquad
 K_A=J_A-{m_A\over\rho+\epsilon_\rho}\nabla\rho,
 \qquad \epsilon_\rho=10^{-12}.
\]
The functional is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal F(\Psi)={}&{1\over2}\langle\Psi,L\Psi\rangle_{\mathbb R}
 +\int_{\mathbb T^3}\left({\lambda\over4}\rho^2+{\gamma\over6}\rho^3\right)dx\\
 &+\sum_A\int_{\mathbb T^3}\left({a\over2}|J_A|^2
 +b\operatorname{Re}(J_A^\dagger K_A)+{c\over2}|K_A|^2\right)dx,
 \end{aligned}                                                    \tag{2.1}
\]
where
\[
 L=(r+Z(-\Delta)+Y(-\Delta)^2)I_3+M_{\rm fam}
 +k_{\rm lock}(I_3-P_0),                                      \tag{2.2}
\]
and
\[
 a={c_{JJ}\alpha_X^2\over M_X^2+\epsilon_M},\quad
 b={c_{JK}\alpha_X\beta_X\over M_X^2+\epsilon_M},\quad
 c={c_{KK}\beta_X^2\over M_X^2+\epsilon_M},\qquad
 \epsilon_M=10^{-12}.                                         \tag{2.3}
\]

\paragraph{Theorem.}
Assume \texttt{A2-H3-CANONICAL-PRODUCTION-FUNCTIONAL}. For every
$\Psi_0\in H^2(\mathbb T^3;\mathbb C^3)$, the real-$L^2$ gradient flow
\[
 \partial_t\Psi=-\nabla_{\mathbb R}\mathcal F(\Psi),\qquad \Psi(0)=\Psi_0, \tag{2.4}
\]
has a unique global solution
\[
 \Psi\in C([0,\infty);H^2)\cap L^2_{\rm loc}((0,\infty);H^4),
 \qquad \partial_t\Psi\in L^2_{\rm loc}((0,\infty);L^2).       \tag{2.5}
\]
On every finite interval the solution depends continuously on the initial datum in $H^2$. It satisfies the exact energy identity
\[
 \mathcal F(\Psi(t))+\int_s^t\|\partial_\tau\Psi(\tau)\|_{L^2}^2d\tau
 =\mathcal F(\Psi(s)),\qquad 0\le s\le t,                       \tag{2.6}
\]
and is $C^\infty$ in space and time for every $t>0$.

The family matrix is positive semidefinite, while $P_0$ is an orthogonal projector and $k_{\rm lock}(I-P_0)$ is positive semidefinite. Thus $L$ is positive self-adjoint on $H^4$, its form domain is $H^2$, and $D(L^{1/2})=H^2$ with equivalent norms.

Hence the Class-II energy is nonnegative. Finally $\lambda=-0.43$ and $\gamma=1.62>0$, and Young's inequality gives

\section*{4. Full nonlinear map and local solution}

Realify $\Psi$ as $u\in\mathbb R^6$. For each generator let $A_A$ be its real symmetric realification and define
\[
 p_A(u)=2A_Au,\qquad q_A(u)={u^TA_Au\over u^Tu+\epsilon_\rho},\qquad
 s_A(u)=2(A_A-q_A(u)I)u.                                       \tag{4.1}
\]
The Class-II density is exactly
\[
 G_{II}(u,\nabla u)={1\over2}\sum_j(\partial_ju)^TB(u)(\partial_ju), \tag{4.2}
\]
where
\[
 B(u)=\sum_A\{a p_Ap_A^T+b(p_As_A^T+s_Ap_A^T)+c s_As_A^T\}.    \tag{4.3}
\]
Because $\epsilon_\rho>0$, $B$ is smooth on all of $\mathbb R^6$. Direct Euler--Lagrange differentiation gives only
\[
 N_{II}(u)=B(u)\nabla^2u+C(u)[\nabla u,\nabla u],               \tag{4.4}
\]
with smooth $C$; no derivative above order two occurs. On a fixed three-torus,
$H^2\hookrightarrow L^\infty\cap W^{1,6}$ and $L^3\hookrightarrow L^2$. Product and difference estimates therefore yield
\[
 \|N(u)-N(v)\|_{L^2}\le C_R\|u-v\|_{H^2}
 \quad\text{on every bounded }H^2\text{ ball}.                 \tag{4.5}
\]
The quartic and sextic terms obey the same bound because $H^2$ is an algebra.

The positive operator $L$ generates an analytic semigroup and
\[
 \|L^{1/2}e^{-tL}\|_{L^2\to L^2}\le Ct^{-1/2}.                 \tag{4.6}
\]
The kernel is integrable. The mild equation and (4.5)--(4.6) give a contraction in $C([0,T];H^2)$, hence a unique local solution and the continuation alternative that finite maximal time requires the $H^2$ norm to diverge.

\section*{5. Energy identity and global continuation}

Let $P_n$ be the real $L^2$-orthogonal Fourier projection and solve
\[
 \partial_tu_n=-P_n(Lu_n+N(u_n)).                              \tag{5.1}
\]
This is the exact finite-dimensional real gradient flow of $\mathcal F|_{P_nH}$, so
\[
 {d\over dt}\mathcal F(u_n)=-\|\partial_tu_n\|_{L^2}^2.        \tag{5.2}
\]
The coercive bound gives $u_n$ uniformly in $L^\infty(0,T;H^2)$, while (5.2) bounds $\partial_tu_n$ in $L^2(0,T;L^2)$. Since
$Lu_n=-\partial_tu_n-P_nN(u_n)$, ellipticity also bounds $u_n$ in $L^2(0,T;H^4)$. Aubin--Lions gives strong convergence in $L^2(0,T;H^2)$; (4.5) gives strong convergence of $N(u_n)$ in $L^2(0,T;L^2)$.

For the nonlinear potential $\Phi$, the identity $D\Phi(u)[h]=\langle N(u),h\rangle_{\mathbb R}$ and the $H^2\to L^2$ local Lipschitz bound give the chain rule
\[
 \Phi(u(t))-\Phi(u(s))=\int_s^t\langle N(u),\partial_tu\rangle_{\mathbb R}dt. \tag{5.3}
\]
Combining (5.3) with the spectral quadratic-form chain rule proves (2.6), not merely an inequality. Equations (2.6) and (3.5) prevent the $H^2$ continuation alternative, proving global existence.

\section*{6. Continuous dependence and positive-time smoothing}

For two solutions $u,v$ in a common energy-bounded $H^2$ ball, the mild difference equation gives
\[
 \|u(t)-v(t)\|_{H^2}\le C\|u_0-v_0\|_{H^2}
 +C_R\int_0^t(t-s)^{-1/2}\|u(s)-v(s)\|_{H^2}ds.               \tag{6.1}
\]
Weakly singular Gronwall proves continuous dependence uniformly on every finite interval.

For each $\alpha<1$, analytic-semigroup estimates first give $u(t)\in D(L^\alpha)=H^{4\alpha}$ for $t>0$. Choosing $\alpha>1/2$ makes $u$ Holder continuous in $H^2$ away from zero; then $f(t)=N(u(t))$ is Holder continuous into $L^2$. The endpoint $H^4$ gain follows from the cancellation
\[
 L\int_a^t e^{-(t-s)L}f(s)ds
 =(I-e^{-(t-a)L})f(t)
 +\int_a^tLe^{-(t-s)L}[f(s)-f(t)]ds.                           \tag{6.2}
\]
The last integrand is bounded by $C(t-s)^{\theta-1}$ for some $\theta>0$ and is therefore integrable. Hence $u(t)\in D(L)=H^4$ for every $t>0$.

The positive regularisers make all coefficient functions smooth, and the nonlinear map has differential order two. Periodic Moser estimates give
\[
 N:H^m\to H^{m-2}\quad\text{locally Lipschitz for every integer }m\ge2. \tag{6.3}
\]
Repeating the endpoint argument on the base space $H^{m-2}$ gains two derivatives at each positive-time step. Thus $u(t)$ lies in every Sobolev space for $t>0$; the equation then supplies all time derivatives. This proves the stated $C^\infty$ regularity.

\section*{8. Tier decision in plain terms}

T5 would say only that a pinned calculation is closed. The present result is stronger: it establishes a theorem about every $H^2$ initial field in the declared periodic domain, rather than a finite list of runs. T6 is therefore the correct tier. It remains conditional because the physical identification of the hash-pinned P1 reference functional as the production functional is a T5 definitional input. T7 is excluded because the theorem does not cover the full physical domain: it omits the historical backend, nonzero shell bias, infinite volume, negative shell mass, and vacuum/BCC selection, and it has not undergone the T7 dual external-domain audit.

In plain language, the theorem says: once the pinned three-component energy rule is accepted, its time evolution is mathematically safe. A valid starting field cannot split into two different futures or blow up in finite time; nearby starting fields stay nearby over finite times; the energy can only decrease; and every initially rough $H^2$ field becomes smooth immediately after positive time. It does not say which vacuum Nature chooses or that the universe must form a BCC phase.
