\section*{1. Purpose and scope}
The P1 backend supplies a discrete variational functional and its real
gradient. P2 proves well-posedness of the corresponding continuum
full-production gradient flow on a fixed three-torus. Neither result by itself
proves that an N32, N64, or N128 nonlinear solver trajectory approximates that
continuum flow. P3 is the separate discretization-to-continuum package.

This v1.0 note freezes the numerical objects and closes only the first spatial
baseline. Finite-time flow convergence, Hessian/Ritz convergence, and
hardware/precision agreement remain open.

\section{Continuum and discrete objects}
Let $R(\Psi)=\nabla_{\mathbb R}F(\Psi)$ denote the real-$L^2$ gradient of the
P1 reference functional, in the eta-shell-zero scope of P2. Let $S_N$ be the
three-component trigonometric polynomial space represented by the cubic FFT
grid and let $P_N$ be the real-$L^2$ Fourier projector.

The exact spectral Galerkin vector field is
\[
 R_N^G(\Psi_N)=P_NR(\Psi_N).
\]
The hash-pinned backend instead differentiates the periodic collocation energy;
call the result $R_N^C$. Spectral differentiation is exact on $S_N$, but
pointwise nonlinear products need not remain in $S_N$. Therefore
$R_N^C=R_N^G$ is not assumed. The measured aliasing/consistency quantity is
\[
 e_N^R=\frac{\lVert R_N^C-P_NR_{N_{\rm ref}}^C\rVert_{L^2}}
 {\max(\lVert P_NR_{N_{\rm ref}}^C\rVert_{L^2},10^{-30})}.
\]
Two same-backend reference grids are used, and their projected difference is
reported separately. This prevents a single under-resolved reference from
defining its own apparent convergence. It does not provide an independent
implementation of the continuum Euler--Lagrange residual; that remains a
load-bearing closure item.

For the collocation functional $F_N^C$, the discrete real pairing is
\[
 \langle u,v\rangle_{N,\mathbb R}
 =\Delta V\,\mathrm{Re}\sum_{a,x}\overline{u_a(x)}v_a(x).
\]
The energy-gradient audit verifies a centred finite-difference form of
$DF_N^C(\Psi_N)v=\langle R_N^C(\Psi_N),v\rangle_{N,\mathbb R}$.

\section{Manufactured spatial field}
Each complex component is a nonzero constant plus several low Fourier modes
with distinct phases and directions. The construction keeps the density far
above the positive rho floor and activates every pinned Class-II coefficient.
It is analytic, so spectral convergence is expected rather than a fixed
low-order finite-difference rate. The acceptance criterion asks for at least
fourth algebraic order over the declared grid sequence; the measured rate is
also retained rather than replaced by the threshold.

\section{Executed result}
The grids are $N=8,12,16$ and the same-backend oversampling references are
$N=24,32$. The result is:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
N&e_N^R&e_N^{\rm ref}&e_N^F&e_N^{DF}\\\hline
8&5.3301196\,10^{-6}&5.5885294\,10^{-16}&1.7564784\,10^{-13}&1.0210053\,10^{-8}\\
12&4.2482236\,10^{-10}&3.6469415\,10^{-15}&0&3.9197285\,10^{-9}\\
16&6.6678548\,10^{-12}&2.3350092\,10^{-14}&2.2533398\,10^{-16}&3.9197706\,10^{-9}
\end{array}
\]
The two observed residual orders are 23.2750 and 14.4408. All 13 executable
assertions pass. Energy monotonicity is interpreted above the declared
$10^{-14}$ relative roundoff floor; values below that floor are machine-level
agreement and are not ordered artificially.

\section{Stage 2 finite-time result}
Temporal and spatial errors are separated. On N8, the manufactured trajectory
uses the semidiscrete forcing
$S_N(t)=\partial_t\Psi_N^\star+R_N^C(\Psi_N^\star)$, so the experiment measures
the classical RK4 time error rather than spatial aliasing. At final time 0.2,
steps $0.04,0.02,0.01$ give relative errors
$5.6334\,10^{-11}$, $3.4821\,10^{-12}$, and $2.1644\,10^{-13}$, hence observed
orders 4.0160 and 4.0079. These errors are in the truncation regime rather than
at machine zero.

Separately, the unforced full-production gradient flow is integrated to time
0.005 with step $5\,10^{-4}$. N8, N12, and N16 are compared with projected N20
and N24 trajectories. Relative errors against N24 are $1.3063\,10^{-8}$,
$1.0239\,10^{-12}$, and $1.3118\,10^{-14}$, with observed rates 23.3164 and
15.1465. The energy decreases at every recorded step on all five grids. This
is same-backend trajectory self-convergence, not yet an independent continuum
flow implementation.

\section{Devil's-advocate review}
First, Fourier differentiation alone does not remove nonlinear aliasing; this
objection is valid and is mitigated by keeping $R_N^C$ and $R_N^G$ distinct.
Second, one smooth manufactured field does not give a uniform theorem for all
P2 solutions; this objection is upheld, and the claim remains T3. Third, energy
convergence does not imply eigenvalue convergence; the isolated Ritz-cluster
gate remains open. Fourth, a missing CUDA device cannot be reported as a
cross-device pass; the hardware matrix must distinguish PASS, FAIL, and
UNAVAILABLE.

\section{Mandatory adversarial code review}
\textbf{Sign.} Fourier derivatives use $+ik_j$ and the Laplacian uses
$-|k|^2$; the spatial audit does not change the canonical backend signs.
\textbf{Factor and convention.} FFT coefficients are divided by the grid-point
count, inverse reconstruction multiplies by the target count, and the real
pairing includes the cell volume. Centered crops use same-parity grids, so no
Nyquist half-weight is silently introduced. \textbf{Units.} All values use the
pinned dimensionless P1 normalization and the fixed length-16 torus; the table
does not claim a dimensional laboratory error. \textbf{Convergence.} Two
reference grids and three target grids are retained, with the reference
projection uncertainty reported independently of the measured target error.
The time-order rerun was deliberately moved away from machine precision after
the first otherwise-passing run exposed roundoff-sensitive terminal errors.
\textbf{Hardcode masking.} No measured residual, rate, or energy value is an
input to the executable. Manufactured Fourier coefficients are declared test
inputs; acceptance thresholds live in the manifest. \textbf{Limit cases.} A
nonzero-density analytic field is intentional for this baseline and does not
test removal of the rho floor, rough H2 data at time zero, or a Nyquist-loaded
field. Those cases cannot be inferred from this table. External review of the
Fourier normalization, same-backend-reference limitation, and later
manufactured forcing is explicitly invited.
