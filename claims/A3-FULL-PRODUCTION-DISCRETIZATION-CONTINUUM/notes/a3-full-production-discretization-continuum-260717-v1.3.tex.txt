\section{Devil's-advocate and mandatory adversarial review}
\textbf{Sign and factor.} The portable implementation is independently
evaluated against the canonical CPU complex128 residual, so a sign or real-pairing
factor mismatch would appear in the residual comparison. \textbf{Convention.}
The evidence names the canonical backend hash and the review checks it against
the frozen authority. \textbf{Units.} Both implementations use the fixed-torus
P1 normalization and manifest thresholds. \textbf{Convergence.} This is a
cross-device/precision agreement test, not a grid-convergence theorem; its N8
scope is explicit. \textbf{Hardcode masking.} The reviewed energy and residual
errors are produced by the audit and checked against manifest limits, not copied
into the code as targets. \textbf{Limit cases.} One GPU, two fields, and one
grid do not prove all-device equivalence, a solution-ball error bound, or
historical-solver continuum validity. External hardware reproduction remains
welcome.
