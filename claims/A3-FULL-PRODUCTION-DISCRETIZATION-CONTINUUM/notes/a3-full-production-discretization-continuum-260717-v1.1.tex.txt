\section*{1. Purpose and scope}
This supplement records P3 stages 3 and 4. The domain, functional, positive
floors, eta-shell-zero rule, and real pairing are unchanged from v1.0. Stage 3
asks which conditions make a finite-grid Ritz value interpretable as a Hessian
spectral value. Stage 4 compares an independent device/dtype-variable
implementation with the canonical CPU complex128 backend.

\section{Hessian/Ritz conditions and result}
At a homogeneous nonzero Class-II-active background, translation invariance
makes the cosine/sine block for Fourier index $(1,1,0)$ invariant when all six
internal real coordinates are retained. The real block dimension is 12. If a
Ritz cluster has gap $g>0$ and residual norm $r<g/2$, the standard residual
bound localises true spectrum within distance $r$ and keeps the cluster
separated from its complement. The audit measures both quantities rather than
inferring eigenvalue validity from a symmetric small matrix.

Across N8, N12, N16 and N20, the block has maximum invariance defect
$5.45\,10^{-8}$ and relative antisymmetry below $7.50\,10^{-11}$. The lowest
cluster has multiplicity two, gap $g=0.1368657$, maximum residual
$1.377\,10^{-9}$, and $r/g=1.0061\,10^{-8}$. A separate nonuniform stress field
with Fourier indices through three exposes aliasing at N8. Its fixed eight-real
dimensional Ritz errors against N20 are $1.5199\,10^{-3}$,
$4.7888\,10^{-7}$, and $2.7136\,10^{-8}$, with observed orders 19.89 and 9.98.
The nonuniform subspace is not invariant and its values are not labelled as
full-spectrum eigenvalues.

\section{Hardware and precision result}
The portable implementation reproduces the functional independently of the P1
backend and makes device and complex dtype explicit. On homogeneous and
nonuniform Class-II-active N8 fields, CPU complex128 agrees exactly with the
canonical energy and residual. CPU complex64 has maximum relative energy error
$9.8340\,10^{-8}$ and residual error $2.8775\,10^{-7}$, below the frozen
$5\,10^{-6}$ and $5\,10^{-5}$ limits.

The installed runtime is Torch 2.11.0+cpu, with no CUDA device exposed. CUDA
complex128 and complex64 are therefore recorded as UNAVAILABLE, not PASS. The
same executable automatically runs those rows when a CUDA-enabled Torch build
and device are present.

\section{Devil's-advocate and mandatory adversarial review}
\textbf{Sign and factor.} The Hessian action is the centred derivative of the
canonical real gradient, and the Ritz matrix uses the same cell-volume real
pairing. Antisymmetry is measured before symmetrisation. \textbf{Convention.}
All six internal real directions and both cosine/sine profiles are retained;
dropping either would create a false invariant block. \textbf{Units.} Ritz
values use the pinned dimensionless fixed-torus normalization. \textbf{Convergence.}
The first stress field placed all grids at the finite-difference floor and was
rejected despite 11/13 passing; a higher-bandwidth declared test input exposes
measurable convergence without relaxing thresholds. \textbf{Hardcode masking.}
No measured eigenvalue, gap, residual, error, or rate is an input. Modes and
thresholds are declared test inputs in the manifest. \textbf{Limit cases.}
This does not cover a BCC candidate Hessian, a non-isolated cluster, spectral
pollution for arbitrary expanding subspaces, rho-floor removal, or unavailable
CUDA hardware. External review of the invariant-block argument and the
portable complex-gradient convention is invited.
