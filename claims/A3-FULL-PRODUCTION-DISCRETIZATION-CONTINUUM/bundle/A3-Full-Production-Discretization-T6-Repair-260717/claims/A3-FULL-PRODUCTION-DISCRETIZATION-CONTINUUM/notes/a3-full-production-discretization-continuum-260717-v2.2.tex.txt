\paragraph{Corrected theorem.}
Assume (H3), let $R\in\{0.5,1,2\}$, and let
$\|u_0\|_{H^2}\leq R$.  For $\tau=0.02$ and $T=0.2$ the P2 solution satisfies
\[
 \sup_{\tau\leq t\leq T}\|u(t)\|_{H^6}\leq B_6(R,\tau,T)<\infty. \tag{1.2}
\]
For every even Fourier grid size $N$, with $P_N$ the real-$L^2$ Fourier
projector and $R_N^C$ the pinned collocation residual,
\[
 \sup_{\tau\leq t\leq T}
 \|P_NR(u(t))-R_N^C(P_Nu(t))\|_{L^2}
 \leq C(R,\tau,T)N^{-2}.                                  \tag{1.3}
\]
Let $u_N$ solve the exact Galerkin equation
\[
 \partial_tu_N+Lu_N+P_NN(u_N)=0,\qquad u_N(\tau)=P_Nu(\tau).
                                                                    \tag{1.4}
\]
Then an explicit finite $E(R,\tau,T)$ satisfies
\[
 \sup_{\tau\leq t\leq T}\|u_N(t)-P_Nu(t)\|_{L^2}
 \leq E(R,\tau,T)N^{-4}.                                  \tag{1.5}
\]
Equations (1.2)--(1.5) are a positive-time, restarted, exact-Galerkin
conditional theorem.  They do not assert an algebraic rate from $t=0$ for
merely $H^2$ data and do not identify finite oversampling with exact Galerkin
for the rational Class-II term.

$\delta=\tau/4$.  The mild formula first bounds $u$ in $D(L^{3/4})$.
The endpoint cancellation
\[

\section{Repaired exact-Galerkin evolution estimate}
Put $e=u_N-P_Nu$.  The continuum projection forcing is bounded on the
continuum ball, while $N(u_N)-N(P_Nu)$ is bounded on the common ball (4.4).
Coercivity and Young's inequality give
\[
 {d\over dt}\|e\|_{L^2}\leq a_G\|e\|_{L^2}+F_RN^{-4},
 \qquad a_G={L_0(M_*)^2\over2c_L},                        \tag{6.1}
\]
where $F_R=L_0(M_2)(L_{\max}/\pi)^4B_6$.  Since $e(\tau)=0$,
\[
 \|e(t)\|_{L^2}\leq
 F_R{e^{a_G(t-\tau)}-1\over a_G}N^{-4}.                  \tag{6.2}
\]
For $d=T-\tau=0.18\leq1$,
$(e^{a_Gd}-1)/a_G\leq d e^{a_Gd}\leq e^{a_Gd}$.  Therefore the stored
logarithmic enclosure is computed without a direction-sensitive subtraction:
\[
 \log_{10}E\leq\log_{10}F_R+{a_Gd\over\log 10}.          \tag{6.3}
\]
The outward stored values of this logarithm are
$3.32936730872633\,10^{152}$, $5.97917890507636\,10^{152}$, and
$6.99562180830297\,10^{153}$.  Their extreme size makes the theorem
