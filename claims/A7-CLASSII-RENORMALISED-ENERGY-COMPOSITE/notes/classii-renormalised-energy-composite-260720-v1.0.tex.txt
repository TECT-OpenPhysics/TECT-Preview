\section*{1. Purpose and scope}
The preceding A6 packages fixed two facts.  The full production Gaussian has a
positive linear derivative contraction, and the fixed-floor geometric current
$K_A$ has a canonical common-even spectral lift.  Neither fact defines
$J_AK_A$, $|K_A|^2$, or an interacting Gibbs weight.  This note closes the
next, narrower question: the full Class-II \emph{energy density as a Gaussian
composite distribution}.

The result uses the fixed $L=16$ torus, the hash-pinned A1 production
quadratic symbol, three complex components (six real components), embedded
Pauli generators, fixed
\[
 \varepsilon_\rho=10^{-12}>0,
\]
one common real-even uniformly bounded scalar Fourier multiplier on every
real component, pointwise convergence of that multiplier to one, compact
support or a uniform Schwartz tail, and exact Fourier convolution or
sufficient de-aliasing.  The theorem is regulator independent only inside
that class.  The proof below uses regulator-independent Fourier majorants;
it does not silently assume a pointwise kernel bound.

The result does not construct the interacting measure.  In particular,
$L^2$ convergence of an action is not uniform integrability of its
exponential.  The missing estimate is isolated in Section~8.

\section*{2. Exact finite-cutoff algebra}
Realify the field as $X:\mathbb T_L^3\to\mathbb R^6$ and each Hermitian
generator as a real symmetric matrix $S_A$.  Put
\[
 \rho=X^TX,\qquad m_A=X^TS_AX,\qquad
 q_A={m_A\over \rho+\varepsilon_\rho},
\]
\[
 p_A(X)=2S_AX,\qquad
 v_A(X)=2(S_A-q_AI)X.
\]
For $Y_i=\partial_iX$, the currents are
\[
 J_{A,i}=p_A^TY_i,\qquad K_{A,i}=v_A^TY_i.
\]
Writing
\[
 Q_{\rm II}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{pmatrix}>0,
\]
define the symmetric six-real matrix
\[
 B(X)=\sum_A\left\{
 a\,p_Ap_A^T+b(p_Av_A^T+v_Ap_A^T)+c\,v_Av_A^T
 \right\}.
\]
Then the exact smooth cutoff density is
\[
 e_{{\rm II},\Lambda}(x)
 ={1\over2}\sum_{i=1}^3
 Y_{\Lambda,i}(x)^TB(X_\Lambda(x))Y_{\Lambda,i}(x).
 \tag{2.1}
\]
This identity includes all $J^2$, $J K$, and $K^2$ coefficients and is
checked against the direct complex-current implementation.

Let
\[
 \Gamma_{\Lambda,i}
 =\mathbb E\!\left[
 \partial_iX_\Lambda(x)\partial_iX_\Lambda(x)^T
 \right]
 \tag{2.2}
\]
be the \emph{six-real} derivative covariance.  Common evenness gives
\[
 \mathbb E[X_\Lambda(x)\partial_iX_\Lambda(x)^T]=0.
 \tag{2.3}
\]
The two jointly Gaussian vectors are therefore independent.  The exact
finite-cutoff conditional contraction is
\[
 {\cal C}_\Lambda(X)
 ={1\over2}\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}
 [B(X)\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}]
 =\mathbb E[e_{{\rm II},\Lambda}(x)\mid X_\Lambda(x)=X].
 \tag{2.4}
\]
Equation (2.4), rather than only its leading asymptotic term, is the
renormalisation condition.

In complex notation let
\[
 U_A=T_A\Psi,\qquad V_A=(T_A-q_AI)\Psi,\qquad
 D_{\Lambda,i}
 =\mathbb E[\partial_i\Psi_\Lambda
             \partial_i\Psi_\Lambda^\dagger].
\]
Since $\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}=\frac12\operatorname{realify}
(D_{\Lambda,i})$, (2.4) is equivalently
\[
 {\cal C}_\Lambda(\Psi)
 =\sum_{i,A}\left[
 a\,U_A^\dagger D_{\Lambda,i}U_A
 +2b\,\operatorname{Re}(U_A^\dagger D_{\Lambda,i}V_A)
 +c\,V_A^\dagger D_{\Lambda,i}V_A
 \right].
 \tag{2.5}
\]
The explicit one-half in the real covariance is load-bearing: inserting the
complex $D_{\Lambda,i}$ directly into the real trace formula would double the
subtraction.

\begin{definition}
For every generator and spatial direction define jointly
\begin{align*}
 [J_{A,i}^2]_\Lambda^{\rm ren}
 &=J_{A,i}^2-p_A^T\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}p_A,\\
 [J_{A,i}K_{A,i}]_\Lambda^{\rm ren}
 &=J_{A,i}K_{A,i}-p_A^T\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}v_A,\\
 [K_{A,i}^2]_\Lambda^{\rm ren}
 &=K_{A,i}^2-v_A^T\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}v_A.
\end{align*}
Their $Q_{\rm II}$ combination is
\[
 e_{{\rm II},\Lambda}^{\rm ren}
 =e_{{\rm II},\Lambda}-{\cal C}_\Lambda(X_\Lambda).
 \tag{2.6}
\]
\end{definition}

\section*{3. Composite convergence theorem}
\begin{theorem}[fixed-floor covariance-normal closure]
For every $\kappa>0$ the three products in Definition~1, and hence
$e_{{\rm II},\Lambda}^{\rm ren}$, converge jointly as
\[
 e_{{\rm II},\Lambda}^{\rm ren}
 \longrightarrow e_{\rm II}^{\rm ren}
 \quad\hbox{in}\quad
 L^2(\Omega;H^{-1-\kappa}(\mathbb T_L^3)).
 \tag{3.1}
\]
The limit is common to every regulator in the class of Section~1.  In
particular,
\[
 {\cal V}_\Lambda^{\rm ren}
 =\int_{\mathbb T_L^3}e_{{\rm II},\Lambda}^{\rm ren}(x)\,dx
 \longrightarrow
 {\cal V}^{\rm ren}
 \quad\hbox{in }L^2(\Omega),
 \tag{3.2}
\]
and $\mathbb E{\cal V}^{\rm ren}=0$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{3.1 Gaussian regularity and coefficient bounds}
The production symbol satisfies, uniformly at high momentum,
\[
 c\langle k\rangle^4I\le {\cal A}(k)
 \le C\langle k\rangle^4I.
 \tag{3.3}
\]
Thus every real covariance entry is bounded by
$C\langle k\rangle^{-4}$.  The value field has regularity below one half,
whereas a derivative has regularity below minus one half.

At fixed positive floor, $q_A$, $p_A$, $v_A$, and $B$ are smooth functions of
$X$.  Since $|m_A|\le\rho$, one has $|q_A|\le1$ and
\[
 |p_A(X)|+|v_A(X)|\le C|X|,\qquad |B(X)|\le C|X|^2.
 \tag{3.4}
\]
Every derivative of $B$ has polynomial growth with a constant depending on
the fixed floor.  The point covariance converges, so all required Gaussian
Sobolev norms of $B(X_\Lambda)$ are uniform in $\Lambda$.

\subsection*{3.2 Exact finite Gaussian-IBP covariance lemma}
The rational coefficient is handled without an infinite Hermite truncation.
We use the following finite lemma.  Let $(X,Y,\widetilde X,\widetilde Y)$ be
jointly Gaussian finite-dimensional vectors with
\[
 \mathbb E[X\otimes Y]=0,\qquad
 \mathbb E[\widetilde X\otimes\widetilde Y]=0,
\]
and write
\[
 P=\max\{\|\mathbb E[Y\otimes\widetilde X]\|,
          \|\mathbb E[X\otimes\widetilde Y]\|\},\qquad
 Q=\|\mathbb E[Y\otimes\widetilde Y]\|.
\]
For symmetric matrix functions $A,D$ with derivatives through order two of
polynomial growth, put
\[
 \Phi_A={1\over2}A_{ab}(X)
   (Y_aY_b-\mathbb E[Y_aY_b]),\qquad
 \widetilde\Phi_D={1\over2}D_{cd}(\widetilde X)
   (\widetilde Y_c\widetilde Y_d-
    \mathbb E[\widetilde Y_c\widetilde Y_d]).
\]
Uniformly over Gaussian value covariances in a compact set,
\[
 |\mathbb E[\Phi_A\widetilde\Phi_D]|
 \le C_{A,D}\{Q^2+QP^2+P^4\}.
 \tag{3.5}
\]
Here is the exact tensor identity behind that bound.  Put
$P_{a\beta}=\mathbb E[Y_a\widetilde X_\beta]$,
$R_{c\alpha}=\mathbb E[\widetilde Y_cX_\alpha]$,
$Q_{ac}=\mathbb E[Y_a\widetilde Y_c]$,
$\Gamma=\mathbb E[Y\otimes Y]$, and
$\widetilde\Gamma=\mathbb E[\widetilde Y\otimes\widetilde Y]$.  Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E\{A_{ab}(X)(Y_aY_b-\Gamma_{ab})
D_{cd}(\widetilde X)
(\widetilde Y_c\widetilde Y_d-\widetilde\Gamma_{cd})\}\\
={}&(Q_{ac}Q_{bd}+Q_{ad}Q_{bc})\,
\mathbb E[A_{ab}(X)D_{cd}(\widetilde X)]\\
&+\{P_{a\beta}(Q_{bc}R_{d\alpha}+Q_{bd}R_{c\alpha})
+P_{b\beta}(Q_{ac}R_{d\alpha}+Q_{ad}R_{c\alpha})\}\\
&\hspace{16mm}\times
\mathbb E[(\partial_\alpha A_{ab})(X)
(\partial_\beta D_{cd})(\widetilde X)]\\
&+P_{a\beta}P_{b\gamma}R_{c\alpha}R_{d\delta}\,
\mathbb E[(\partial_{\alpha\delta}A_{ab})(X)
(\partial_{\beta\gamma}D_{cd})(\widetilde X)].
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5a}
\]

To prove (3.5), apply Gaussian integration by parts successively to the four
displayed derivative variables.  The two conditional-centering terms cancel
every same-vertex derivative pair.  Each surviving derivative leg therefore
either pairs with a derivative at the opposite vertex, contributing $Q$, or
differentiates the opposite coefficient through a value--derivative
covariance, contributing $P$.  There are exactly three connection types:
\[
 (Q^2,P^0),\qquad (Q^1,P^2),\qquad (Q^0,P^4).
 \tag{3.6}
\]
No coefficient is differentiated beyond order two.  H\"older's inequality and the
Gaussian Sobolev norms of $A$ and $D$ give (3.5).  This is a finite identity
and bound; it leaves no Hermite-series tail.  Applied with $A=D=B$ it covers
the complete rational coefficient, and applied to the three separate
coefficient matrices it covers $J^2$, $JK$, and $K^2$ jointly.

The same argument identifies the only divergent \emph{local contraction in
this Gaussian two-point composite construction}.  Before centering, pairing
the two derivative legs at one vertex gives
\[
 \sum_{|k|\lesssim\Lambda}|k|^2\langle k\rangle^{-4}
 =\Theta(\Lambda),
 \tag{3.7}
\]
and (2.4) removes it exactly while keeping $B$ unexpanded.  A same-point
value--derivative contraction is zero by (2.3).  We make no claim here about
counterterms in an interacting perturbation expansion.

\subsection*{3.3 Bessel-weighted Fourier domination}
Couple all cutoffs through the same underlying Gaussian Fourier modes.  If
$a_r(k)=\langle k\rangle^{-r}$, the production symbol and the common bounded
scalar multipliers give, uniformly in both cutoffs,
\[
 |\widehat C_{\Lambda M}(k)|\le Ca_4(k),\qquad
 |\widehat P_{\Lambda M}(k)|\le Ca_3(k),\qquad
 |\widehat Q_{\Lambda M}(k)|\le Ca_2(k).
 \tag{3.8}
\]
The first sequence is summable in dimension three, so the value covariances
converge uniformly.  Polynomial growth and uniform Gaussian moments then make
all coefficient expectations in (3.5a) bounded and continuous under a change
of cutoff.

The needed discrete convolutions are, for every $\eta>0$,
\[
 a_2*a_2\lesssim a_1,\qquad
 a_2*a_3*a_3\lesssim_\eta a_{2-\eta},\qquad
 a_3*a_3*a_3*a_3\lesssim_\eta a_{3-\eta}.
 \tag{3.9}
\]
They follow by splitting each lattice sum into the regions where one input
frequency is at most half the output frequency and its complement.  The
first estimate is sharp.  It corresponds to the two derivative--derivative
bridges; the other two are the $QPR$ and $P^2R^2$ lines of (3.5a).

The executable connection-pattern audit enumerates exactly these three
families rather than declaring a hand-written multivertex graph table.  Its
frozen-gradient variance sum is only a numerical check of the worst $Q^2$
scaling; the proof of the full rational coefficient is the exact identity
(3.5a) together with (3.8)--(3.9).

\subsection*{3.4 Sobolev Cauchy convergence and scheme independence}
Put $s=1+\kappa$ and let $G_s$ be the positive periodic Bessel kernel with
Fourier coefficients $\langle k\rangle^{-2s}$.  Positivity follows from its
heat-kernel representation.  From (3.9),
\[
 \int G_s(z)|Q_{\Lambda M}(z)|^2\,dz
 \lesssim\sum_k\langle k\rangle^{-2s-1}<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s>1.
 \tag{3.10}
\]
The $P^2R^2$ term is strictly better by the last estimate in (3.9), and the
$QPR$ term is bounded by Cauchy--Schwarz in the positive measure
$G_s(z)\,dz$.  Thus (3.5a) is uniformly integrable against $G_s$ for every
$s>1$.

Pointwise multiplier convergence and the same absolutely summable
convolution majorants imply convergence of each $G_s$-weighted covariance
term.  Uniform convergence of the value covariance handles the coefficient
expectations in (3.5a): truncate the Gaussian values at fixed radius, use
continuity in the finite-dimensional covariance there, and then remove the
truncation with the uniform polynomial moments.

For
$R_{\Lambda M}=e_{{\rm II},\Lambda}^{\rm ren}
-e_{{\rm II},M}^{\rm ren}$, stationarity and Parseval give
\[
 \mathbb E\|R_{\Lambda M}\|_{H^{-s}}^2
 =\int_{\mathbb T_L^3}G_s(z)
   \mathbb E[R_{\Lambda M}(z)R_{\Lambda M}(0)]\,dz.
 \tag{3.11}
\]
Polarising the four cross-cutoff covariances and applying the preceding
dominated-convergence argument makes (3.11) tend to zero.  This proves the
common Cauchy limit in $L^2(\Omega;H^{-1-\kappa})$ for every $\kappa>0$.
At $s=1$ the sharp $Q^2$ sum in (3.10) is logarithmic, so the $H^{-1}$
endpoint is not claimed.  Pairing with the constant test function gives
(3.2), and conditional centering in (2.4) gives zero mean at every cutoff
and in the limit.

This direct Fourier--Wick proof is the pinned specialisation used here.
The general BPHZ framework and stochastic model convergence are developed by
Chandra and Hairer,
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.08138}; the present proof does not import a
black-box interacting-measure conclusion from that work.

\section*{4. Leading contraction and exact finite scheme}
For the sharp cube, spatial symmetry makes all complex derivative
covariances equal to the A6 matrix $D_N$.  The A6 limit gives
\[
 {D_N\over N}\longrightarrow\delta_{\rm cube}I_3,
 \qquad
 \delta_{\rm cube}=0.01619898645075695.
 \tag{4.1}
\]
Substituting (4.1) into (2.5) recovers
\[
 {{\cal C}_N(\Psi)\over N}
 \longrightarrow\delta_{\rm cube}W_\varepsilon(\Psi),
 \tag{4.2}
\]
where, with $s=|\Psi_1|^2+|\Psi_2|^2$,
\[
 W_\varepsilon
 =9(a+2b+c)s
 -{6bs^2\over\rho+\varepsilon_\rho}
 -{3cs^2(\rho+2\varepsilon_\rho)
       \over(\rho+\varepsilon_\rho)^2}.
 \tag{4.3}
\]
Equation (4.2) is an asymptotic check, not the definition.  Exact
$D_{\Lambda,i}$ subtraction removes the finite regulator-dependent part and
pins one covariance-normal renormalisation condition.

\section*{5. Frozen-background determinant check}
Fix a deterministic background $u$ and freeze $B(X)$ at $B(u)$.  Relative to
the Gaussian quadratic operator, the derivative perturbation defines a
positive operator $T_{u,\Lambda}$ whose high-mode eigenvalues are
$O(|k|^{-2})$.  Hence
\[
 \sup_\Lambda\|T_{u,\Lambda}\|_{\rm HS}^2
 \lesssim\sum_k\langle k\rangle^{-4}<\infty.
 \tag{5.1}
\]
The exactly centered quadratic partition factor is
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\left\{
 -{1\over2}\langle X,L_uX\rangle
 +{1\over2}\operatorname{Tr}T_{u,\Lambda}
 \right\}
 =\det{}_2(I+T_{u,\Lambda})^{-1/2},
 \tag{5.2}
\]
and
\[
 0\le {1\over2}\sum_j
 [\lambda_j-\log(1+\lambda_j)]
 \le {1\over4}\sum_j\lambda_j^2.
 \tag{5.3}
\]
This proves a convergent frozen-coefficient determinant and checks that the
remaining diagrams are Hilbert--Schmidt.  It is not an estimate for the
self-coupled random coefficient $B(X)$ and is not used to claim a Gibbs
measure.

\section*{6. Sharp pointwise running-mass threshold}
At the production coefficients,
\[
 a=0.0045,\qquad b=0.001875,\qquad c=0.00234375.
\]
Put
\[
 h=9(a+2b+c)=0.09534375,\qquad
 d=6b+3c=0.01828125.
 \tag{6.1}
\]
Equation (4.3) gives the exact identity
\[
 hs-W_\varepsilon
 ={6bs^2\over\rho+\varepsilon_\rho}
 +{3cs^2(\rho+2\varepsilon_\rho)
       \over(\rho+\varepsilon_\rho)^2}\ge0.
 \tag{6.2}
\]
Moreover $W_\varepsilon/s\to h$ as $s/\rho\to0$ with a nonzero third
component.  Therefore
\[
 \sup_{\Psi:s>0}{W_\varepsilon(\Psi)\over s}=h.
 \tag{6.3}
\]

For the leading subtraction $-t_NW_\varepsilon$, a pointwise family-mass
repair $t_Nms$ is nonnegative for every field exactly when $m\ge h$.  Since
the production family energy is $\frac12\Delta\mu_Ns$, the leading sharp
shift is
\[
 \Delta\mu_N\ge2h\delta_{\rm cube}N+O(1)
 =0.00308894422882794\,N+O(1).
 \tag{6.4}
\]
If $m<h$, choose a fixed small orientation $x=s/\rho$ such that
\[
 A_x=(h-m)x-dx^2>0.
\]
A homogeneous amplitude then has asymptotic potential
\[
 {\gamma\over6}\rho^3-t_NA_x\rho+O(\rho^2),
\]
whose minimum is
\[
 -{2^{3/2}\over3\sqrt\gamma}
 (t_NA_x)^{3/2}+O(t_N).
 \tag{6.5}
\]
Thus every subcritical pointwise mass repair retains the raw
$-\Theta(N^{3/2})$ escape.  At the sharp value, (6.2) produces a divergent
positive orientation penalty and can collapse the first doublet under
additional uniform-energy and tightness assumptions.  This explains why the
pointwise-coercive repair is not adopted as the physical nondegenerate
renormalisation condition.

\section*{7. Bare-route negative control}
The zero set of the conditional contraction is not the zero set of the
pathwise Class-II energy.  For
\[
 \Psi(x)=e^{ik\cdot x}u
 \tag{7.1}
\]
with constant $u\in\mathbb C^3$, both $\rho$ and every $m_A$ are constant.
Therefore $J_A=\nabla m_A=0$ and $K_A=0$, even if the first doublet of $u$ is
nonzero.  But (4.3) gives $W_\varepsilon(u)>0$ for such a field.

More generally, positive definiteness of $Q_{\rm II}$ implies that
$F_{\rm II}=0$ exactly when every $J_A$ and $K_A$ vanishes.  This includes
fixed-density, fixed-Bloch-direction phase branches, not only the pure third
component.  Consequently the conditional Gaussian mean cannot be used as a
deterministic rate function for the bare Gibbs law.  Any bare-concentration
proof must compare the entropy and free energy of all null branches.

\section*{8. Interacting-measure boundary}
Let $U_\Lambda$ denote the local quartic--sextic part relative to the
production Gaussian.  Since
\[
 \mathbb E{\cal V}_\Lambda^{\rm ren}=0
\]
and the Gaussian local moments are uniformly bounded, Jensen gives a
uniform positive lower bound on the finite-cutoff partition functions:
\[
 Z_\Lambda
 =\mathbb E\exp[-U_\Lambda-{\cal V}_\Lambda^{\rm ren}]
 \ge
 \exp[-\mathbb E U_\Lambda]>z_*>0.
 \tag{8.1}
\]
The positive sextic gives finite-dimensional normalisability.  Neither fact
gives uniform integrability of the weights.

The missing constructive estimate is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \sup_\Lambda
 \mathbb E\exp\{-p[U_\Lambda+
 {\cal V}_\Lambda^{\rm ren}]\}<\infty
 \quad\hbox{for some }p>1.
 \quad}
 \tag{8.2}
\]
If (8.2) is proved, (3.2), Vitali convergence, and the common-Gaussian
construction yield $L^1$ convergence of densities, partition convergence,
tightness, a full-sequence finite-volume Gibbs limit, and convergence of
smeared correlations.  This is the named
\texttt{A7-CLASSII-NELSON-EXPONENTIAL-BOUND} gate.  It is not discharged in
this note.

\section*{10. Devil's-advocate}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{DISMISSED:} the A6 complex derivative covariance can be inserted
unchanged into the six-real trace.  It cannot; the real covariance is one
half of the realification.  Both implementations test the factor.

\item \textbf{VALID-with-mitigation:} subtracting only
$\delta_{\rm cube}NW_\varepsilon$ can leave a finite scheme term.  The
definition uses each regulator's exact $\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}$ and thereby fixes
the covariance-normal finite renormalisation condition.

\item \textbf{DISMISSED in the Gaussian-composite scope:} the rational
coefficient creates an uncontrolled Hermite tail.  Exact identity (3.5a)
acts directly on $B$ and uses only its first two derivatives.  Conditional
centering removes the local derivative pair, evenness removes the local mixed
pair, and the three surviving two-point connection families are controlled
by (3.9).  This does not assert convergence of an interacting perturbation
series.

\item \textbf{VALID-with-mitigation:} arbitrary component-split or asymmetric
regulators may carry a first-level or area anomaly.  They are excluded.  The
theorem inherits the full common real-even scalar multiplier and
exact/dealiased-product boundary of A6.

\item \textbf{UPHELD:} composite $L^2$ convergence is not a constructive Gibbs
theorem.  Uniform negative-exponential control (8.2) is separately named and
remains open.

\item \textbf{VALID-with-mitigation:} the frozen-background determinant might
be mistaken for the self-coupled measure.  Section~5 states and uses it only
for deterministic $B(u)$; it is an audit of the Hilbert--Schmidt remainder.

\item \textbf{UPHELD:} a pointwise running mass at the sharp threshold can
produce a degenerate first-doublet limit.  The result is recorded as a
stability boundary, not selected as the physical prescription.

\item \textbf{UPHELD:} $W_\varepsilon^{-1}(0)$ does not classify the bare
pathwise zero set.  The exact plane-wave counterexample prevents a
pure-third concentration overclaim.

\item \textbf{VALID-with-mitigation:} constants can blow up as
$\varepsilon_\rho\downarrow0$.  The floor is fixed; no uniform floor-removal
statement is made.
\end{enumerate}
