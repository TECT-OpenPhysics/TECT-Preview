\section*{1. Purpose and scope}
The previous ultraviolet calculation identified a positive linear contraction
in the derivative Class-II energy, but it deliberately left two logically
different questions open.  First, does the current $K_A$ itself have a
canonical continuum definition?  Second, can the leading energy contraction
be subtracted while preserving stability, or can an unsubtracted measure
concentrate on its zero set?

This note closes the first question at fixed positive density floor and inside
a declared symmetry-preserving spectral regulator class.  It then reviews the
other questions separately.  The literal leading-$W_\varepsilon$ subtraction
is ruled out as a cutoff-uniform fixed-parameter coercivity route.  Two local
bare-concentration proxies are solved exactly, but no conclusion is drawn for
the spatially correlated Gibbs field.

The domain is the three-torus of side $L=16$.  The covariance and all
production parameters are imported from the content-addressed A1 production
manifest.  The density floor is fixed at
$\varepsilon_\rho=10^{-12}$.  Floor removal, infinite volume, a phase
transition, and BCC existence or selection are outside scope.

\section*{2. Statement}
Realify the three-component complex field as
$X:\mathbb T_L^3\to\mathbb R^6$.  Let $S_A$ be the real symmetric
representation of an embedded Pauli generator and set
\[
 \rho(X)=X^{\mathsf T}X,\qquad
 m_A(X)=X^{\mathsf T}S_AX,\qquad
 q_A(X)={m_A(X)\over\rho(X)+\varepsilon_\rho}.
\]
The relevant one-form is
\[
 \omega_A=dm_A-q_A,d\rho
          =(\rho+\varepsilon_\rho)dq_A,
\qquad
 \omega_{A,a}(X)=2\{(S_AX)_a-q_A(X)X_a\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-floor canonical spectral current]
Suppose the centered stationary six-real Gaussian field has an even symmetric
Fourier covariance $Q(k)$ satisfying
\[
 c\langle k\rangle^{-4}I_6\le Q(k)
 \le C\langle k\rangle^{-4}I_6.
\]
Let $R_\Lambda$ be a scalar Fourier multiplier common to all six coordinates,
real and even, uniformly bounded, converging pointwise to one, and having
compact spectral support or a uniform Schwartz tail.  Let
$X_\Lambda=R_\Lambda X$ and evaluate nonlinear products by exact convolution
or sufficient de-aliasing.  For every $p<\infty$ and $\kappa>0$,
\[
 K_{A,i}^{(\Lambda)}
 =\omega_A(X_\Lambda)\,\partial_iX_\Lambda
 \longrightarrow K_{A,i}
 \quad\hbox{in }L^p(\Omega;\mathcal C^{-1/2-\kappa}).
\]
The limit is independent of the multiplier inside this class and is the
canonical geometric, equivalently symmetric-Stratonovich, reconstruction of
the one-form current.  Along a dyadic subsequence the convergence is almost
sure.  No divergent $K_A$-level subtraction occurs.
\end{theorem}

The limit identities are written
\[
 K_A=\nabla m_A-q_A\diamond\nabla\rho
     =(\rho+\varepsilon_\rho)\diamond\nabla q_A,
\]
where $\diamond$ denotes the canonical geometric product.  It is not an
ordinary product of arbitrary distributions.  The theorem defines $K_A$ but
does not define $J_AK_A$, $|K_A|^2$, or the Class-II energy.

\section*{3. Setting and proof of the composite theorem}
\subsection*{3.1 Gaussian regularity}
The production symbol has leading part $Y|k|^4I_6$.  On a dyadic shell,
\[
 \mathbb E|\Delta_jX(x)|^2
 \lesssim\sum_{|k|\asymp2^j}\langle k\rangle^{-4}
 \lesssim2^{-j}.
\]
Gaussian moment equivalence, Bernstein estimates, and the usual dyadic
summation imply
\[
 X\in\bigcap_{\eta>0}\mathcal C^{1/2-\eta}
 \quad\hbox{almost surely and in every finite }L^p(\Omega),
\]
while $\partial_iX\in\mathcal C^{-1/2-\eta}$.  The endpoint
$H^{1/2}$ is not claimed.

\subsection*{3.2 The canonical second level}
Fix $\alpha\in(1/3,1/2)$.  The only nonclassical input required by the
one-form current is the resonant product
\[
 \mathbb X_i^{ab,\Lambda}
 =X_\Lambda^a\circ\partial_iX_\Lambda^b.
\]
The resonant restriction has $|k|\asymp|\ell|$.  Wick's identity and the
covariance bound give, uniformly in the admitted multipliers,
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E\left|
  \widehat{\mathbb X_i^{ab,\Lambda}}(p)
 \right|^2
 &\lesssim
 \sum_{\substack{k+\ell=p\\|k|\asymp|\ell|}}
 \langle k\rangle^{-4}\ell_i^2\langle\ell\rangle^{-4}\\
 &\lesssim \langle p\rangle^{-3}.
\end{align*}
The last estimate is obtained by splitting $|k|\lesssim|p|$ from
$|k|>|p|$.  The first part has $O(|p|^3)$ summands of size
$O(|p|^{-6})$; the second is bounded by
$\sum_{|k|>|p|}O(|k|^{-6})=O(|p|^{-3})$.

At fixed output mode the cutoff tail obeys
\[
 \mathbb E\left|
  \widehat{\mathbb X_i^{ab,\Lambda}
              -\mathbb X_i^{ab}}(p)
 \right|^2
 \lesssim\sum_{|k|>c\Lambda}|k|^{-6}
 =O(\Lambda^{-3}).
\]
Dominated convergence handles two different admitted multipliers coupled to
the same Gaussian modes.  Littlewood-Paley summation and hypercontractivity of
the second chaos yield convergence in
$L^p(\Omega;\mathcal C^{-\eta})$ for every $p<\infty$ and $\eta>0$.
Summability of dyadic cutoff increments then gives almost-sure convergence
along dyadic subsequences.

For every admitted regulator,
\[
 \mathbb E[X_\Lambda^a\partial_iX_\Lambda^b]
 =\partial_iC_\Lambda^{ab}(0)=0,
\]
because the covariance and common multiplier are even.  Thus the second level
has no local divergent contraction to subtract.  Its symmetric part is fixed
by
\[
 X^a\diamond\partial_iX^b
 +X^b\diamond\partial_iX^a
 =\partial_i(X^aX^b).
\]

\subsection*{3.3 Nonlinear reconstruction}
The positive floor makes every derivative of $\omega_A$ smooth with at most
polynomial growth controlled by powers of $\varepsilon_\rho^{-1}$.  First
order paralinearisation gives
\[
 \omega_{A,a}(X)
 =T_{\partial_b\omega_{A,a}(X)}X^b+R_{A,a},
 \qquad R_{A,a}\in\mathcal C^{2\alpha}.
\]
All paraproducts with $\partial_iX^a$ are classical except the resonant term,
which is reconstructed from
\[
 \partial_b\omega_{A,a}(X)\,
 \mathbb X_i^{ba}.
\]
The coefficient is in $\mathcal C^\alpha$ and the second level is in
$\mathcal C^{-\eta}$, so this product is classical for $\eta<\alpha$.
Moreover,
\[
 R_{A,a}\circ\partial_iX^a
 \in\mathcal C^{3\alpha-1},
\]
which is well defined because $3\alpha-1>0$.  The reconstruction is a
continuous map of the enhanced Gaussian field $(X,\mathbb X)$.  Convergence
and scheme independence of the enhancement therefore imply the asserted
convergence of $K_A$.  The least regular low-high paraproduct has regularity
$\alpha-1$, giving the declared $\mathcal C^{-1/2-\kappa}$ target.

This is the same second-level mechanism used in paracontrolled calculus; see
Gubinelli, Imkeller, and Perkowski,
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.2684}.  Canonical Gaussian rough lifts and
approximation convergence are developed by Friz and Victoir,
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.0668}.  The equality of Gaussian rough and
symmetric-Stratonovich integrals under suitable regularity is treated by
Ohashi and Russo, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06865}; correction formulas
relative to Skorohod integration are discussed by Cass and Lim,
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06846}.  The Fourier estimates above are the
specialised argument used for the pinned torus field.

\subsection*{3.4 Why the enhancement is load-bearing}
Direct differentiation gives
\[
 (d\omega_A)_{ab}
 ={4\over\rho+\varepsilon_\rho}
 \{X_a(S_AX)_b-X_b(S_AX)_a\}.
\]
For the real two-coordinate slice of $S_3=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$,
\[
 \rho=x^2+y^2,\qquad m_3=x^2-y^2,
\]
and
\[
 d\omega_3=-{8xy\over x^2+y^2+\varepsilon_\rho}\,dx\wedge dy.
\]
This is generically nonzero.  Two enhancements differing by antisymmetric
area $\Gamma_i^{ab}$ can change the current by
\[
 \Delta K_{A,i}
 ={1\over2}\sum_{a,b}(d\omega_A)_{ab}(X)\Gamma_i^{ab}.
\]
The common-even spectral class fixes $\Gamma=0$ relative to the canonical
Gaussian lift.  A component-split asymmetric approximation is outside this
class and can have a finite anomaly.  Therefore the theorem is scheme
controlled, not an unrestricted universality claim.

\section*{4. Counterterm and bare-concentration split}
\subsection*{4.1 Exact $W_\varepsilon$ bounds}
Write
\[
 Q_{II}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 s=|\Psi_1|^2+|\Psi_2|^2,
\]
and let $v_A=(T_A-q_AI)\Psi$.  The leading contraction is
\[
 W_\varepsilon
 =3\sum_A
 \begin{pmatrix}T_A\Psi\\v_A\end{pmatrix}^{\!*}
 Q_{II}
 \begin{pmatrix}T_A\Psi\\v_A\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since $Q_{II}>0$ and $\sum_A|T_A\Psi|^2=3s$,
\[
 W_\varepsilon\ge9\lambda_{\min}(Q_{II})s.
\]
The exact Fierz form is
\[
 W_\varepsilon
 =9(a+2b+c)s-{6bs^2\over\rho+\varepsilon_\rho}
 -{3cs^2(\rho+2\varepsilon_\rho)
       \over(\rho+\varepsilon_\rho)^2},
\]
so, because $b,c>0$,
\[
 W_\varepsilon\le9(a+2b+c)s.
\]
It follows that $W_\varepsilon=0$ exactly when
$\Psi_1=\Psi_2=0$.  This is an algebraic statement about the conditional
leading contraction, not a pathwise lower bound on the full derivative
energy.

\subsection*{4.2 No-go for literal fixed-parameter subtraction}
Let $t_N=\delta_{\rm cube}N$ and consider
\[
 F_N^{\rm naive}=F_{\rm core}+F_{{\rm II},N}
 -t_N\int W_\varepsilon(\Psi)\,dx.
\]
For a homogeneous field in the first component, every derivative current
vanishes.  Put $\rho=|\Psi_1|^2$.  Then
\[
 W_\varepsilon(\sqrt\rho,e_1)
 =w_\infty\rho+O(\varepsilon_\rho),
 \qquad w_\infty=9a+12b+6c>0,
\]
while
\[
 {F_{\rm core}\over|\mathbb T^3|}
 ={\gamma\over6}\rho^3+{\lambda\over4}\rho^2+O(\rho).
\]
Choose
\[
 \rho_N=\sqrt{{2t_Nw_\infty\over\gamma}}.
\]
Substitution gives
\[
 {F_N^{\rm naive}(\sqrt{\rho_N}e_1)\over|\mathbb T^3|}
 =-{2\sqrt2\over3\sqrt\gamma}
   w_\infty^{3/2}t_N^{3/2}+O(t_N),
\]
hence there is no cutoff-uniform raw lower bound.  In $N$ variables,
\[
 {|\Psi_N|\over N^{1/4}}
 \longrightarrow
 \left({2\delta_{\rm cube}w_\infty\over\gamma}\right)^{1/4},
\]
and the energy density divided by $N^{3/2}$ tends to a strictly negative
constant.  A vacuum shift changes the displayed minimum but not the normalised
measure or the escaping amplitude.  Sextic coercivity proves finite-cutoff
normalisability only.

Adding the running relevant term $t_Nh\int s$ with
$h=9(a+2b+c)$ makes $t_N(hs-W_\varepsilon)\ge0$.  This is a possible stability
repair, but it introduces a new renormalisation condition and favours collapse
toward $s=0$.  It is not counterterm closure.

\subsection*{4.3 Mean-contraction local proxy}
Let $z=(\Psi_1,\Psi_2)\in\mathbb C^2$, let $w=\Psi_3$, and let $\nu$ be a
nondegenerate local Gaussian value law.  Define
\[
 d\mu_t^W=Z_t^{-1}e^{-tW_\varepsilon(z,w)}d\nu(z,w).
\]
For $z=y/\sqrt t$, fixed positive floor gives
\[
 tW_\varepsilon(y/\sqrt t,w)\longrightarrow h|y|^2,
 \qquad h=9(a+2b+c).
\]
The lower bound $W_\varepsilon\ge9\lambda_{\min}s$ supplies domination.
Laplace scaling in four real dimensions yields
\[
 Z_t\sim t^{-2}{\pi^2p_z(0)\over h^2},
\]
and
\[
 (\sqrt t\,z,w)\Longrightarrow(Y,W_0),
 \qquad {d\Pr_Y\over dy}=\left({h\over\pi}\right)^2e^{-h|y|^2}.
\]
Thus
\[
 t|z|^2\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Gamma}(2,\operatorname{rate}=h),
 \qquad \mathbb E[t|z|^2]\longrightarrow{2\over h}.
\]

\subsection*{4.4 Exact derivative-integrated local proxy}
The preceding proxy exponentiates the conditional mean and is not exact
gradient integration.  Instead let each of the three local complex gradient
vectors have covariance $tI_3$ and define
\[
 G_t(\Psi)=\mathbb E_{\nabla\Psi}
 \exp\{-e_{II}(\Psi,\nabla\Psi)\}.
\]
At $z=y/\sqrt t$, the floor makes $K_A-J_A\to0$.  The embedded Pauli identity
gives, for one spatial direction,
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(J_A,J_B)=2|y|^2\delta_{AB}.
\]
With $g=a+2b+c$, a Gaussian determinant in three current coordinates and
three spatial directions gives
\[
 G_t(y/\sqrt t,w)\longrightarrow
 G_\infty(y)=(1+2g|y|^2)^{-9/2}.
\]
The lower eigenvalue of $Q_{II}$ gives an integrable determinant bound.  Hence
the rescaled radial variable has density
\[
 f_R(r)=35g^2r(1+2gr)^{-9/2},\qquad r\ge0,
\]
with
\[
 \mathbb ER={2\over3g},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}R={14\over9g^2}.
\]
This proxy also concentrates locally on $z=0$, but with a different limiting
law and a mean three times the mean-contraction proxy after substituting
$h=9g$.  Neither local statement controls spatial entropy, mode coupling,
partition functions, or tightness of the third component.

\section*{6. Devil's-advocate}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{VALID-with-mitigation:} deterministic Holder counting alone
does not prove that the product diverges or is ambiguous.  The closure instead
uses the special Fourier-Wick second level and the one-form reconstruction.
\item \textbf{DISMISSED:} the rational denominator creates a hidden
$K$-level subtraction.  At fixed positive floor the one-form is smooth, and
the only local second-level contraction vanishes by evenness.
\item \textbf{VALID-with-mitigation:} arbitrary regulator independence is
false.  The theorem is restricted to common real-even scalar multipliers.  A
component-split asymmetric negative control exhibits an anomaly.
\item \textbf{DISMISSED:} sharp spectral cutoffs invalidate the limit.  The
proof uses bounded pointwise-convergent multipliers and dominated Fourier-Wick
sums, so smoothness of the multiplier is not required when convolution is
exact.
\item \textbf{VALID-with-mitigation:} collocation aliasing can imitate scheme
dependence.  The scope requires exact convolution or sufficient de-aliasing;
an aliased product is a falsifier, not an admitted implementation.
\item \textbf{DISMISSED:} defining $K_A$ defines $|K_A|^2$.  Squaring a
$\mathcal C^{-1/2-}$ distribution is a separate renormalisation problem and
is explicitly excluded.
\item \textbf{UPHELD:} literal subtraction of
$\delta_{\rm cube}NW_\varepsilon$ with fixed lower-order coefficients is not
uniformly coercive.  The exact homogeneous trial has an escaping
$N^{1/4}$ amplitude and negative $N^{3/2}$ energy.
\item \textbf{VALID-with-mitigation:} $W_\varepsilon^{-1}(0)$ does not prove
full Gibbs concentration.  Only two local proxies are closed; the full-field
problem is a separate gate.
\item \textbf{VALID-with-mitigation:} the constants are asymptotic and the
couplings are weak, so existing $N=32,64,128$ simulations need not display
the local proxy limits.  No practical crossover claim is made.
\item \textbf{VALID-with-mitigation:} no estimate is uniform as
$\varepsilon_\rho\downarrow0$.  The floor is pinned at a positive value and
removal is excluded.
\end{enumerate}
