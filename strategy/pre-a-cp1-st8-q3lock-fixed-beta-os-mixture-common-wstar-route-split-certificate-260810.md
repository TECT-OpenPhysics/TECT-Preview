## 1. Purpose and scope

`EXP-000790` reconstructed the two ordered Euclidean DLR phases separately as
stochastically positive beta-KMS systems.  It deliberately did not call the
two phasewise real-time groups one common dynamics.  `R-167 v1.2--v1.3`
subsequently isolated the topology needed to identify a thermodynamic
Hamiltonian dynamics and rejected several raw norm, fixed-power and fixed
Leibniz routes.

This checkpoint answers a narrower but exact question first:

> If both phase measures already live on the same Euclidean path space and
> use the same full analytic cylinder-word operations, does their canonical
> mixture reconstruct one W-star system in which both phases are normal KMS
> states?

The answer is yes at every externally fixed beta.  The construction is not an
arbitrary direct sum.  It starts from the convex DLR mixture on the registered
common path space, performs one OS reconstruction, and identifies its cyclic
space with the common-word cyclic subspace of the two phase reconstructions.

The result is still not the phase-, state- and beta-independent thermodynamic
Hamiltonian automorphism required before algebraic ground-state selection.
Three exact no-gos sharpen that remaining step:

1. sharp-time OS Gram data alone do not determine the real-time
   functoriality;
2. a bounded finite-support class with both full-Gibbs half-modular endpoint
   conjugates bounded is scalar;
3. a single frequency-blind site-influence rung cannot have a uniformly small
   bond-transfer coefficient.

No C6, CP1, Sector-A or Pre-A claim changes.

## 3. The canonical mixture reflection form

Choose

\[
  0<\lambda_+,\lambda_-<1,\qquad
  \lambda_++\lambda_-=1,
\]

and define

\[
  \mu_0=\lambda_+\mu_++\lambda_-\mu_- .                 \tag{3.1}
\]

The DLR equation, time translation, beta periodicity and temporal reflection
positivity are linear in the measure, so `mu_0` has the same periodic OS
interface.  For `F,G in D_+`, set

\[
 q_\sigma(F,G)=\int \overline{F(\Theta\omega)}G(\omega)
                 d\mu_\sigma(\omega),
 \qquad \sigma\in\{0,+,-\}.                              \tag{3.2}
\]

Then

\[
 q_0=\lambda_+q_++\lambda_-q_- ,                         \tag{3.3}
\]

and in particular

\[
 q_\sigma(F,F)\leq \lambda_\sigma^{-1}q_0(F,F).          \tag{3.4}

Because both weights are strictly positive, the null ideals obey the exact
identity `N_0=N_+ intersect N_-`.

Let `H_sigma` be the quotient completion for `q_sigma`, and write `[F]_sigma`
for the class of `F`.

## 4. Common-word functoriality and the mixture reconstruction

Define

\[
 V:H_0\longrightarrow H_+\oplus H_-,\qquad
 V[F]_0=\bigl(\sqrt{\lambda_+}[F]_+,
              \sqrt{\lambda_-}[F]_-\bigr).              \tag{4.1}
\]

Equation (3.3) proves that `V` is an isometry.  Its range is

\[
 K=\overline{\{(\sqrt{\lambda_+}\pi_+(X)\Omega_+,
                    \sqrt{\lambda_-}\pi_-(X)\Omega_-):
                    X\in W_\beta\}} .                    \tag{4.2}
\]

This is the closed common-word cyclic subspace.  It need not be the complete
direct sum; surjectivity would require phase-separating approximants and is
not assumed.

Because `W_beta` contains products and star, `K` reduces the direct-sum word
representation.  Because it contains both real shift orientations and the
same formal time-shift action, `K` also reduces the direct-sum reconstructed
unitary group.  Therefore uniqueness of periodic reconstruction identifies
the mixture word representation and real-time group with the restrictions to
`K`:

\[
  V\pi_0(X)=\bigl(\pi_+(X)\oplus\pi_-(X)\bigr)V,
  \qquad
  VU_0(t)=\bigl(U_+(t)\oplus U_-(t)\bigr)V .              \tag{4.3}
\]

This full-word intertwining is the load-bearing statement.  It is stronger
than equality or domination of a form at one reflection plane.

## 8. Full-Gibbs half-modular local scalarity

Let `H_Lambda(h)` be the exact finite-volume ST8/Q3LOCK Schrodinger
Hamiltonian, with `c,chi,hbar>0`.  Let `X` be a finite set with one lattice
collar inside `Lambda`, and let `A=A_X tensor I` be bounded.  Put
`s=beta/2`.  Assume the densely defined operators

\[
 e^{sH}Ae^{-sH},\qquad e^{-sH}Ae^{sH}                   \tag{8.1}
\]

have bounded closures.  Then `A` is scalar.

### 8.1 Strip lemma

Shift `H` by a scalar so that `H>=0`.  On compact spectral subspaces, the
matrix elements of

\[
 F(z)=e^{zH}Ae^{-zH}                                     \tag{8.2}
\]

are analytic for `|Re z|<s`.  On the two boundary lines, imaginary shifts are
unitary and the assumed endpoint closures bound the compressions by

\[
 M=\max\{\|e^{sH}Ae^{-sH}\|,
          \|e^{-sH}Ae^{sH}\|\}.                         \tag{8.3}
\]

Three-lines and Cauchy on every disk of radius `r<s` give
`||F'(0)||<=M/r`.  Passing the compact spectral cutoffs and then `r` upward to
`s` shows that

\[
 B=[H,A]\in B({\cal H}),
 \qquad \|B\|\leq\frac{M}{s}=\frac{2M}{\beta}.           \tag{8.4}
\]

All following commutators are first form identities on the common polynomial
core and then bounded extensions.

### 8.2 Extreme-site induction

Choose a site `x` of maximal first coordinate in the current support and let
`y=x+e_1`.  The collar condition ensures `y in Lambda` and no other neighbor
of `y` is in the current support.  Apply a configuration translation of
`q_y` by `Rv`.  The translated
onsite terms at `y` and all other `y-z` bonds commute with `A`, while the
`xy` bond gives

\[
 T_y(Rv)^*BT_y(Rv)-B=-cR[v\mathbin\cdot q_x,A].          \tag{8.5}
\]

The left side has norm at most `2||B||`; divide by `R` and let `R` tend to
infinity.  Thus `[q_x,A]=0` componentwise.

Now apply a momentum boost of `p_x` by `hbar Rv`.  Since `A` commutes with `q_x`, the boost
commutes with `A`, the potential is unchanged, and the kinetic term gives

\[
 S_x(Rv)^*BS_x(Rv)-B
 =\frac{\hbar R}{\chi}[v\mathbin\cdot p_x,A].            \tag{8.6}
\]

Again division by `R` proves `[p_x,A]=0`.  Stone--von Neumann irreducibility of the one-site
Schrodinger representation makes `A` the identity on site `x`.  Removing
`x` and repeating proves scalarity.

The elementary cross-bond witness is already visible in

\[
 e^{s(-cq_xq_y)}e^{-iap_x/\hbar}e^{-s(-cq_xq_y)}
 =e^{-iap_x/\hbar}e^{-sca q_y},                          \tag{8.7}
\]

which is unbounded for `sca!=0`.

This proves

`NG-2026-08-10-PRE-A-ST8-Q3LOCK-FULL-GIBBS-HALF-MODULAR-LOCAL-SEPARATING-CLASS`.

Finite-volume global spectral analytic elements are not excluded.  The
theorem rejects a nontrivial bounded finite-support separating class with the
two endpoint bounds, not direct D/delta-D estimates or the existence of a
weaker dynamics.

## 9. A single site-influence rung cannot transfer uniformly

Consider one exact bond kick on neighboring sites `x,y`, and use the v1.3
orientation `beta_delta(A)=B_delta^*AB_delta`.  For

\[
 A=W_a(x)=e^{-iap_x/\hbar}
\]

the exact shear gives

\[
 \beta_\delta(A)=A e^{-ic\delta a q_y/\hbar}.             \tag{9.1}
\]

Fix `b!=0` and normalize the graph-admissible test

\[
 C_b=\frac{W_b(y)}{G_y(W_b)}.                             \tag{9.2}
\]

In the symmetric energy-constrained strong-star convention,

\[
 p_E([\beta_\delta(A),C_b])
 =\frac{2\sqrt2}{G_y(W_b)}
   \left|\sin\frac{c\delta ab}{2\hbar}\right|.           \tag{9.3}
\]

Initially the `y` influence is zero and the `x` influence is uniformly at
most `2sqrt(2)`, independently of `a`.  For every nonzero `delta`, choose

\[
 a=\frac{\pi\hbar}{c|\delta|b}.                          \tag{9.4}
\]

Then (9.3) equals `2sqrt(2)/G_y(W_b)`.  Hence no recurrence

\[
 L_y(\beta_\delta A)
 \leq L_y(A)+\varepsilon(\delta)L_x(A),
 \qquad \varepsilon(\delta)\longrightarrow0,            \tag{9.5}
\]

can hold uniformly over source frequency if the seminorm records only one
site-influence rung.

This proves

`NG-2026-08-10-PRE-A-ST8-Q3LOCK-SINGLE-RUNG-ENERGY-CONSTRAINED-SITEWISE-INFLUENCE-RECURRENCE`.

The exact centered critical graph Banach norm and energy-constrained
strong-star topology still propagate through one bond kick.  A Weyl-Fourier
analytic hierarchy also has an exact shear/radius recurrence.  The no-go says
that a viable locality proof must retain that frequency or analytic-rung
profile.  Quartic onsite invariance of the needed all-moment orbit-Frechet
scale remains open.
