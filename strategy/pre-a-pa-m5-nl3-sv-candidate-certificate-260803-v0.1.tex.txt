\section*{1. Purpose and scope: decision boundary}

This note tests the new-hypothesis candidate \texttt{PA-M5-NL3-SV-v0}
introduced in EXP-000687.  It is not a registered TECT action and inherits no
A1, A2, A7, or A13 theorem.  The calculation has a two-sided conclusion:
\begin{quote}
The candidate is retained as a coherent static screened-shell mathematical
mechanism, but its bare form is rejected as a joint T-053
Lorentz-gauge-critical survivor.
\end{quote}
The rejection is scoped to an isotropic nonzero soft shell with an ordinary
positive inertial completion.  It does not refute M5, nonlocal models, or
emergent structure in general.  A genuine gauge connection, isolated
symmetry-protected nodes, or a separate light sector defines a materially new
candidate and must rerun the reference-state theorem below.

On the cubic torus $\cT_L^3$, let
\[
 \Psi:\cT_L^3\longrightarrow\mathbb C^3,
 \qquad
 A_i:\cT_L^3\longrightarrow\mathbb C^3,\quad i=1,2,3,
 \qquad \rho=\Psi^\dagger\Psi .                  \tag{1.1}
\]
For a Hermitian $3$ by $3$ matrix $M$, declare
\[
\begin{split}
 \cF[\Psi,A]={}&{1\over2}
 \langle\Psi,(r\Id+M-c\Delta)\Psi\rangle_{\mathbb R}
 +{1\over2}\sum_i\langle A_i,(-\Delta+\sigma)A_i\rangle_{\mathbb R}
 \\
 &-\sqrt g\sum_i\langle A_i,\partial_i\Psi\rangle_{\mathbb R}
 +\int_{\cT_L^3}\left({u\over4}\rho^2+{v\over6}\rho^3\right)\dd x .
\end{split}                                                     \tag{1.2}
\]
The parameter domain is
\[
 c>0,\qquad \sigma>0,\qquad g>0,\qquad v>0,
 \qquad r,u\in\mathbb R.                           \tag{1.3}
\]
Equation (1.2), its boundary conditions, and its ordinary inertial extension
are hypotheses.  No numerical values, microscopic derivation, cooling map,
fit, or measured-observable map is supplied.

\section*{2. Exact auxiliary elimination and coercivity}

Put $L_\sigma=-\Delta+\sigma$.  Since $\sigma>0$, it is invertible and
\[
 A_i^*=\sqrt g\,L_\sigma^{-1}\partial_i\Psi.       \tag{2.1}
\]
Completing the square with the repository real pairing gives
\[
\begin{split}
 \cF[\Psi,A]={}&{1\over2}\sum_i
 \left\|L_\sigma^{1/2}(A_i-A_i^*)\right\|_2^2
 +{1\over2}\left\langle\Psi,
 \left(r\Id+M-c\Delta-g(-\Delta)L_\sigma^{-1}\right)\Psi
 \right\rangle_{\mathbb R}
 \\
 &+\int_{\cT_L^3}\left({u\over4}\rho^2+{v\over6}\rho^3\right)\dd x .
\end{split}                                                     \tag{2.2}
\]
Thus, if $Mq_j=\lambda_jq_j$ and $s=|k|^2$, the reduced quadratic symbol is
\[
 \boxed{K_j(s)=r+\lambda_j+cs-{gs\over s+\sigma}.} \tag{2.3}
\]
There is no missing factor two: both quadratic terms in (1.2) carry one half,
and the cross term carries exactly $-\sqrt g$.

Let $\lambda_{\min}$ be the lowest eigenvalue of $M$ and set
$\alpha=r+\lambda_{\min}-g$.  Since
\[
 K_j(s)\ge cs+r+\lambda_j-g,                       \tag{2.4}
\]
and the scalar polynomial
\[
 {\alpha x\over2}+{ux^2\over4}+{vx^3\over12}
\]
has a finite lower bound on $x\ge0$, (2.2) yields
\[
 \cF\ge {1\over2}\sum_i\|L_\sigma^{1/2}(A_i-A_i^*)\|_2^2
 +{c\over2}\|\nabla\Psi\|_2^2+{v\over12}\|\Psi\|_6^6
 -|\cT_L^3|C_{\rm pol}.                            \tag{2.5}
\]
Moreover
\[
 \sum_i\|L_\sigma^{-1}\partial_i\Psi\|_{H^1}^2
 \le \sup_{s\ge0}{(1+s)s\over(s+\sigma)^2}\|\Psi\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 \sup_{s\ge0}{(1+s)s\over(s+\sigma)^2}
 \le\max\{1,\sigma^{-1}\}.                       \tag{2.6}
\]
Therefore finite-torus sublevel sets are bounded in
$H^1(\cT_L^3;\mathbb C^3)\times H^1(\cT_L^3;\mathbb C^9)$.
Weak compactness, compact $H^1\hookrightarrow L^4$, and lower
semicontinuity of the positive quadratic and sextic terms give a global
static minimizer.  This is a static well-posedness statement, not a dynamical
or causal theorem.

\section*{3. Continuous and finite-torus shell gates are different}

Direct differentiation of (2.3) gives
\[
 K_j'(s)=c-{g\sigma\over(s+\sigma)^2},
 \qquad
 K_j''(s)={2g\sigma\over(s+\sigma)^3}>0.           \tag{3.1}
\]
Hence the continuous radial minimum is nonzero exactly when
\[
 g>c\sigma,\qquad
 s_*=\sqrt{g\sigma/c}-\sigma>0,                    \tag{3.2}
\]
and then
\[
 K_j(s_*)=r+\lambda_j-(\sqrt g-\sqrt{c\sigma})^2, \tag{3.3}
\]
with the useful exact identity
\[
 K_j(s)-K_j(s_*)={c(s-s_*)^2\over s+\sigma}.       \tag{3.4}
\]
Condition (3.2) selects a radial minimum; it does not assert that this mode is
soft or unstable.  Those cases require respectively $K_j(s_*)=0$ or
$K_j(s_*)<0$.

On the finite cubic torus,
\[
 s_n=\left({2\pi\over L}\right)^2|n|^2,
 \qquad s_1=\left({2\pi\over L}\right)^2.          \tag{3.5}
\]
Since
\[
 K_j(s)-K_j(0)=s\left(c-{g\over s+\sigma}\right), \tag{3.6}
\]
some nonzero lattice mode lies strictly below zero momentum if and only if
\[
 \boxed{g>c(\sigma+s_1).}                          \tag{3.7}
\]
At equality the zero mode and first shell are degenerate.  The exact
counterfixture $c=\sigma=1$, $g=3/2$, $L=2\pi$ satisfies the continuous
condition (3.2), but every nonzero torus mode has $s\ge1$ and lies above the
zero mode.  This corrects any use of $g>c\sigma$ as a finite-volume shell
selection theorem.

\section*{4. Exact neutral-reference theorem and morphology}

Let
\[
 \kappa_L=\min_{n\in\mathbb Z^3,\,j}K_j(s_n),
 \qquad u_-:=\max\{-u,0\},\qquad u_+:=\max\{u,0\}. \tag{4.1}
\]
For $x\ge0$, exact completion gives
\[
\begin{split}
 {\kappa_Lx\over2}+{ux^2\over4}+{vx^3\over6}
 =x\left[
 {1\over2}\left(\kappa_L-{3u_-^2\over16v}\right)
 +{u_+x\over4}
 +{v\over6}\left(x-{3u_-\over4v}\right)^2
 \right].
\end{split}                                                     \tag{4.2}
\]
The spectral lower bound and (4.2), together with a minimum-mode plane wave
that saturates both, prove
\[
 \boxed{
  \Psi=0\hbox{ is a global minimizer}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \kappa_L\ge {3u_-^2\over16v}.}                  \tag{4.3}
\]
If the inequality is strict, zero is the unique global minimizer.  If it is
violated, coercivity supplies a nonzero global minimizer.  When $u<0$, equality
gives first-order coexistence at
\[
 \rho_t={3u_-\over4v},\qquad
 \kappa_L={3u_-^2\over16v}>0.                      \tag{4.4}
\]
Thus the zero phase is still quadratically gapped at the actual coexistence
boundary.  The massless shell at $\kappa_L=0$ is a later spinodal, not the
first-order transition.  If $u\ge0$, the continuous onset can instead occur
at $\kappa_L=0$, but Section 5 then fires.

The inherited A1 family-lock matrix is
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}
 1/10&-1/20&-1/20\\
 -1/20&13/100&-1/20\\
 -1/20&-1/20&17/100
 \end{pmatrix}.                                    \tag{4.5}
\]
Its characteristic polynomial and discriminant are
\[
 t^3-{2\over5}t^2+{223\over5000}t-{3\over3125},
 \qquad {32233\over31250000000}>0,                 \tag{4.6}
\]
so the lowest eigendirection is simple.  In the nonzero-minimum regime,
equality in (2.2) and (4.2) forces $A=A^*$, Fourier support on the minimum
shell in that eigendirection, and constant $|\Psi|$.  The remaining finite
Fourier sum is a Laurent polynomial $P$ satisfying
$P(z)P^*(z)=\rho_t$.  It is therefore a unit of
$\mathbb C[z_1^{\pm1},z_2^{\pm1},z_3^{\pm1}]$, whose only units are
$az^n$.  Every nonzero global minimizer is consequently a single plane wave
\[
 \Psi(x)=\sqrt{\rho_*}\,q_{\min}e^{ik_{\min}\cdot x}, \tag{4.7}
\]
up to phase and the discrete shell degeneracy.  This is a uniform-density
phase twist with an unselected direction, not a density lattice or BCC state.
The finite-volume level crossing in (4.3) is not yet a thermodynamic or
statistical phase transition; robustness in $L$ and an infinite-volume Gibbs
analysis remain open.

\section*{5. Ordinary inertial completion and the causal no-go}

A causal question exists only after separately hypothesising positive kinetic
terms
\[
 {\chi_\Psi\over2}\|\dot\Psi\|_2^2
 +{\chi_A\over2}\sum_i\|\dot A_i\|_2^2,
 \qquad \chi_\Psi,\chi_A>0.                        \tag{5.1}
\]
For the longitudinal $A$ component, the exact characteristic equation is
\[
 (D_j(s)-\chi_\Psi\omega^2)
 (L(s)-\chi_A\omega^2)-gs=0,                       \tag{5.2}
\]
where $D_j=r+\lambda_j+cs$ and $L=s+\sigma$.  The two transverse
auxiliary-vector branches satisfy
\[
 \omega_T^2={s+\sigma\over\chi_A}.                 \tag{5.3}
\]
At high momentum, the two principal speeds are
\[
 \sqrt{c/\chi_\Psi},\qquad 1/\sqrt{\chi_A}.        \tag{5.4}
\]
One ultraviolet cone therefore requires the unprotected tuning
\[
 c\chi_A=\chi_\Psi.                                \tag{5.5}
\]

More decisively, tune one continuous shell critical so that
$K_{j_*}(s_*)=0$ and write $k=k_*+p$, $|k_*|^2=s_*$,
$p=p_\parallel\widehat k_*+p_\perp$.  With
\[
 B_*:=\chi_A D_{j_*}(s_*)+\chi_\Psi(s_*+\sigma)>0,
\]
equations (3.4) and (5.2) give
\[
 \boxed{
 \omega_-^2(k_*+p)
 ={c\over B_*}
 \left(2\sqrt{s_*}\,p_\parallel+p_\parallel^2+|p_\perp|^2\right)^2
 +O(|s-s_*|^3).}                                   \tag{5.6}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \omega_-\asymp|p_\parallel|\quad\hbox{radially},
 \qquad
 \omega_-\asymp|p_\perp|^2\quad\hbox{tangentially}. \tag{5.7}
\]
The spatial quadratic Hessian has rank one, not rank three.  Equation (5.6)
is a curved-shell square: paths that remain on $|k|^2=s_*$ stay exactly soft.
It must not be replaced by a false global comparison with
$p_\parallel^2+|p_\perp|^4$.

No choice of $c,g,\sigma$ and no high-frequency tuning (5.5) creates the
missing tangential $p_\perp^2$ term.  Therefore a rotationally invariant
nonzero soft sphere with regular positive inertia cannot supply a positive
isotropic Lorentz cone.  When $u<0$, the actual first-order boundary is gapped
by (4.4); when $u\ge0$, the massless boundary has the rank-one shell defect.
The bare candidate fails in both cases.

Also, $\sigma>0$ makes (5.3) gapped at zero momentum.  Taking $\sigma=0$
removes invertibility and coercive control of the $A$ zero mode, while the
nonzero shell collapses toward zero momentum.  The same bare $A_i$ cannot both
generate the screened finite shell and act as a massless photon.

\section*{6. Gauge, symmetry, and observable audit}

The fields $A_i\in\mathbb C^3$ transform as vector matter, not as a gauge
connection.  Under a local common phase multiplication, derivatives of the
phase enter both $\partial_i\Psi$ and $\nabla A_i$; (1.2) is not locally
$U(1)$ invariant.  It contains no $A_0$, Gauss constraint, inhomogeneous
connection transformation, Maxwell curvature, or gauge quotient.

If $A$ remains auxiliary, (2.1) is an instantaneous Yukawa-type spatial
response and supplies no microscopic finite-propagation theorem.  If (5.1)
is added, $A$ is no longer auxiliary and contributes extra propagating modes
and generally a second cone.  Either interpretation blocks identifying this
screened vector with a photon.

Because (4.6) is simple, the unitary commutant of literal $M$ is generically
$U(1)^3$, not $U(3)$.  No $\mathbb{CP}^2$ quotient follows.  A global phase
circle can support a limited winding discussion inside a nonzero-amplitude
sector, but amplitude zeros can unwind it, and no flux quantum, charge,
$\hbar$, or $e$ is derived.

For (4.7), $\rho$, internal bilinears, and $|A|^2$ are uniform.  The phase
gradient and tensors such as
$\partial_i\Psi^\dagger\partial_j\Psi=\rho k_i k_j$ expose a preferred
direction.  Under a future local gauge completion that phase gradient may
instead be gauge equivalent to a uniform state.  In neither interpretation
does the present candidate supply a derived gauge-invariant density crystal
or measured-observable map.
