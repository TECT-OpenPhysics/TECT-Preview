# Pre-A Evidence-First Model Selection Charter

**Recorded:** 2026-08-02  
**Authority:** T0 strategy charter only; no theorem, tier, or physical-vacuum claim  
**Bears on:** Sector A, T-052, T-053, C2-LORENTZ-EMERGENT,
C6-SPACETIME-SIGNATURE, D2-GAUGE-FORCING, E2-HBAR-ORIGIN,
F3-INFLATION-CMB

## 1. Founding question and operator directive

The founding TECT picture is that a high-energy precursor cools through a
phase transition into a highly constrained ordered structure, whose propagating
fluctuations and topologically stable distortions are observed at low energy as
light, matter, and ultimately effective space-time. This is a research ontology,
not a registered theorem.

The directive of this charter is:

> Start from strong, independently reproducible empirical constraints; do not
> start by assuming the present TECT equation. Let the same preregistered tests
> reject, retain, or distinguish the current functional and genuinely different
> candidate dynamics.

The current production functional is therefore one candidate implementation.
Its mathematical results remain valid at their recorded scopes, but its use as
the microscopic law must be earned by evidence that was not used to construct
or tune it.

## 2. Why a Pre-A layer is needed

Sector A answers a conditional mathematical question: once fields, kernel,
regularisation, counterterms, and dynamics are fixed, is the resulting theory
well defined and internally controlled? Completing that question for one fixed
functional does not establish that nature selected that functional.

Pre-A answers the logically earlier physical-selection question:

> Which minimal microscopic structures and dynamical classes can jointly
> account for the strongest observed constraints, and which candidate equation
> should enter a full Sector-A verification programme?

Thus Pre-A is logically prior to the *physical interpretation* of Sector A,
but it is not a retroactive dependency of already proved conditional
mathematics. Work may proceed in parallel. No candidate-selection or physical
foundation claim may, however, be inferred merely from completing the current
Sector-A branch.

If a different candidate survives the Pre-A tests, it requires its own
Sector-A package: definition, well-posedness, continuum control, stability, and
reproducible numerical implementation. The existing package is a benchmark,
not a monopoly.

## 3. Evidence is a constraint, not an interpretation

An observation is not automatically evidence for the full founding ontology.
For example, a shared limiting speed constrains candidate kinetic structures;
it does not by itself prove an underlying rigid medium. Flux quantisation
constrains compact phase and winding structure in a superconductor; it does not
prove that the vacuum is a superconductor. A cosmological thermal history
constrains any cooling scenario; it does not select a Brazovskii transition.

Each Pre-A evidence entry must therefore separate:

1. the directly measured observable;
2. its uncertainty, units, calibration, and primary provenance;
3. the minimum model-independent or weakly model-dependent constraint;
4. the additional interpretation proposed by a candidate;
5. known alternative explanations and systematic limitations; and
6. whether the datum belongs to the discovery set or a frozen holdout set.

Only items with primary or independently reproducible provenance may enter the
strong-evidence register. Analogies, aesthetic unification, historical TECT
fits, and candidate-generated targets belong in a separate clue register.

## 4. Initial evidence-anchor families

The following are intake families, not yet a frozen evidence registry and not
evidence for one unique microscopic equation.

| Empirical anchor family | Minimum structural constraint | What it does not license |
|---|---|---|
| Local Lorentz tests and common causal propagation | Severe suppression of preferred-frame effects; a viable low-energy kinetic sector must possess one protected causal cone to the measured accuracy | Assuming a literal mechanical ether or declaring the microscopic theory Lorentzian |
| Quantum interference, phase coherence, and discrete conserved quantum numbers | A viable candidate must recover complex phase relations, composition rules, and stable quantised sectors without inserting the outputs after the fact | Calling every classical wave a quantum amplitude |
| Stable particle species, masses, charges, and long-lived topological or symmetry sectors | The spectrum must contain the observed gapless and gapped sectors with the correct stability and selection rules | Identifying an arbitrary classical knot with a particle |
| Universal gravitational response, clock redshift, and causal geometry | Matter and clocks must couple to a common effective geometry within observational bounds | Assuming that time or gravity has already emerged from the candidate |
| Cosmic expansion, thermal relics, and primordial correlation structure | A cooling/transition history must be dynamically defined and compatible with cosmological observations | Treating a temperature-like coefficient as the physical cosmic temperature without a derived map |
| Laboratory phase transitions, critical scaling, compact winding, and defect formation | They establish that collective order, universality, and topology can generate abrupt low-energy properties and provide controlled mechanism tests | Treating a material analogue as direct vacuum evidence |

The first Pre-A deliverable is to replace this table with a versioned evidence
register containing exact sources, datasets, uncertainty models, independence
groups, and frozen observables. Until then, the word `strong` refers to an
admission standard, not to a completed TECT evidentiary result.

## 5. Model-independent structural chain to be tested

The founding picture must be decomposed into separately falsifiable links:

```text
microscopic state space
  -> high-energy symmetric or disordered regime
  -> derived cooling / expansion / control-parameter map
  -> genuine thermodynamic or quantum phase transition
  -> stable ordered state below the declared reference state
  -> complete fluctuation spectrum and protected common causal cone
  -> compact topological sectors and stable finite-energy defects
  -> effective quantum, gauge, mass, space-time, and gravitational relations
  -> cosmological and laboratory predictions not used in fitting
```

No arrow may be supplied by verbal analogy. In particular:

- a temperature-like coefficient is not a cosmological cooling law;
- local curvature is not global vacuum selection;
- a zero eigenvalue is not automatically a physical photon;
- a mathematical defect is not automatically massive matter;
- `z=1` is not automatically a derived Lorentzian metric; and
- matching `c`, `hbar`, `e`, or a mass after inserting it is not prediction.

Failure of one arrow refutes or narrows that implementation, not necessarily
the broader possibility of emergent structure.

## 6. Candidate-neutral equation registry

Pre-A must compare declared model families under one benchmark, rather than
searching only deformations of the current equation. The initial non-exhaustive
registry is:

| Local label | Candidate family | Required declaration |
|---|---|---|
| M0 | Established low-energy effective baseline | Which observations it fits and which microscopic-origin questions it leaves open |
| M1 | Current TECT production functional | Exact upstream inputs, reference state, finite-temperature/control map, and all constants already inserted |
| M2 | General local finite-wave-number or Lifshitz/Brazovskii dynamics | Kernel order, field content, stable phase diagram, and whether finite-wave-number order is selected rather than assumed |
| M3 | Compact order-parameter and gauge-coupled dynamics | Compact configuration space, constraints, winding sectors, gauge redundancy, and physical zero modes |
| M4 | Elastic, geometric, or topological-medium dynamics | Microscopic degrees of freedom, defect-energy law, causal dynamics, and restoration of low-energy isotropy |
| M5 | Nonlocal, spectral, network, or other pre-geometric dynamics | Locality/causality limit, continuum map, observable algebra, and regulator-independent predictions |

These labels are local to this charter and are not claim IDs. A new family may
be added before the evidence and test freeze. After the freeze, additions are
reported as a new comparison round rather than silently changing the field.

Every candidate must publish the same minimum manifest before testing:

- degrees of freedom and state/configuration space;
- action, energy, or evolution law and admissible parameter domain;
- reference state and boundary conditions;
- map from microscopic variables to measured observables;
- inserted constants, fitted constants, and predicted outputs;
- mathematical and numerical well-posedness status; and
- preregistered failure conditions.

## 7. Common tournament and anti-fitting rules

1. **Freeze evidence first.** Register discovery data, holdout data,
   uncertainties, and independence groups before candidate comparison.
2. **Run null and baseline checks.** A candidate must outperform a simpler
   effective or phenomenological explanation on a declared joint target, not
   merely reproduce one known number.
3. **Use common reference states.** Energies and actions must share
   normalisation, volume, regulator, and boundary conventions.
4. **Demand joint derivation.** Priority goes to one protected mechanism that
   yields several independent constraints together: for example zero-mode
   count, common speed, winding class, and critical exponent.
5. **Penalise inserted outputs.** A quantity used to set the kernel, units,
   critical point, or matching map cannot reappear as an independent
   prediction.
6. **Test robustness.** Conclusions must survive resolution, volume,
   regulator removal, admissible perturbations, and independent
   implementations at a declared grade.
7. **Hold out predictions.** At least one dimensionless relation, spectrum, or
   scaling law is tested only after the inputs and candidate are frozen.
8. **Report non-selection honestly.** If several candidates remain
   observationally equivalent, Pre-A returns an equivalence class and the
   next discriminator; it does not force a winner.

The first shared high-information benchmark is the boundary package already
defined by T-053: full quadratic kinetic tensor, physical zero-mode count,
all limiting speeds, compact winding data, and critical exponents on both
sides of one preregistered transition. T-052 supplies a candidate-specific
phase-selection diagnostic for M1. Neither task becomes evidence for M1 until
the common evidence register and comparison rules are frozen.

## 8. Entry and exit conditions

### Pre-A entry condition

- the evidence item is reproducible and its interpretation boundary is explicit;
- the candidate manifest exposes every input and fit target; and
- the falsifier and holdout are registered before the decisive calculation.

### Pre-A survival condition

A candidate survives a round only if it passes all hard consistency gates and
explains at least one preregistered joint structure without reusing the target
as input. Survival is not truth and does not promote a TECT claim.

### Pre-A exit condition

Pre-A may hand a candidate to Sector A only when there is:

1. a frozen evidence and clue registry;
2. a reproducible candidate manifest;
3. a common discriminator matrix including baselines and alternatives;
4. at least one successful holdout prediction or new discriminating relation;
5. a documented robustness envelope and failed-candidate record; and
6. an explicit statement of what remains underdetermined.

No unique winner is required. A justified shortlist or a documented
non-selection result is a valid exit.

   clue, and interpretation fields kept separate.
2. Convert the founding chain in Section 5 into a discriminator matrix with
   pass, fail, and not-tested states.
3. Register manifests for M0 and M1, then at least two structurally different
   alternatives before freezing the first comparison round.
4. Execute the T-053 boundary package across the registered candidates.
5. Use T-052 only as the M1 phase-selection branch of the common comparison.
6. Decide whether the evidence selects one candidate, a class, or no candidate;
   only then define the next candidate-specific Sector-A package.
